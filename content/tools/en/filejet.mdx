## What it is

Filejet is entity-compliance software with a registered agent attached. It covers the recurring Secretary of State work in [entity management](/en/learn/entity-management/): registered agent service in all 50 states, annual reports, business license and permit renewals, DBA renewals, and the one-off filings in between (formations, amendments, foreign qualifications, good standing certificates, dissolutions). Each entity's deadlines, filed documents, and service-of-process notices sit in one portal, with reminders that sync to your calendar.

The company grew out of Allen Corporation Supply, a seller of physical corporate kits and seals that CEO Andy White acquired in 2015 and rebuilt as a digital compliance product. Funding records disagree: aggregators list either about $1.4M in a single 2023 round or $2.9M across three rounds through 2024. Treat it as a small, lightly funded company. Filejet says it has served 3,335+ clients and shows logos including Substack, Wastequip, ShiftMed, and Moov. It also claims that 66% of new clients were out of compliance somewhere when they onboarded, and 44% had entities they did not know about.

The question Filejet answers is narrow, and that is its value: do you need a governance platform, or do you need someone to file annual reports in 30 states on time? If it is the second, Filejet is built for that.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **Filing, not just a reminder.** On Professional, Filejet prepares and submits the annual reports itself and tracks the deadlines. Most spreadsheets and many governance tools only tell you a report is due.
- **Transactional filings are bundled.** The pricing page lists formations, amendments, good standing certificates, change of agent, conversions, dissolutions, foreign qualifications, and reinstatements as free service on paid plans. At other agents, those are $50-$150 each.
- **A law-firm product exists.** Filejet ProFirm (launched 2025) is a white-labeled portal that law and accounting firms use to resell registered agent and annual report service to their own clients, with a firm-branded client view. Few competitors sell this channel.
- **International reach through a partner.** An October 2025 partnership with Klea (owned by Quantios) extends compliance coverage to 90+ countries as a managed service, so non-US entities can go on the same order form.
- **Reviewers praise the service team.** Capterra shows 4.8/5 from 28 reviews, and G2 about 4.6/5 from roughly 10. The repeated praise is account-manager responsiveness and a cleaner switch away from CSC. Both review counts are small.

## Pricing reality

The unit is **entity registrations per year, not users**. A Delaware corporation qualified in California and Texas is three registrations.

- **Standard, $150/registration/year.** Digital registered agent, alerts, and document visibility in the portal. Annual report filing is not included at this tier, and the comparison table lists one user (one mobile view says unlimited; confirm in writing).
- **Professional, $350/registration/year.** Registered agent plus automated annual report filing and deadline tracking, with unlimited users. This is the plan most buyers actually need.
- **Premium, custom quote.** Build-your-own packages for complex portfolios: corporate secretarial work, independent directors, charitable solicitation reports, managed entity management, SSO, and bulk upload. Older third-party listings show Premium from $500; the vendor page now says "inquire for quote".
- **Law and accounting firms**: custom, volume-based pricing.

No setup fee. The pricing page does not say whether state filing fees are passed through at cost or marked up, so ask before you sign.

Worked example: a 40-entity group averaging three registrations each is 120 registrations. That is $42,000/year at Professional list price, well inside "volume discount" territory, so negotiate. For comparison, [Discern](/en/tools/discern/) charges a flat $350 per registration with annual reports included, and Harbor Compliance starts registered agent service at $99 for the first year and $159 on renewal, with filings billed separately. Filejet's Professional tier is priced like Discern's; its Standard tier is priced against plain registered agents.

## Best for

Controllers, paralegals, and legal ops leads at US companies with 10-300 registrations, where the work is keeping entities in good standing and the governance record (minute books, cap tables, board consents) lives elsewhere or does not need software. It is also the strongest pick for a law or accounting firm that wants to sell entity maintenance to its clients under its own brand.

For one Delaware C-corp with no foreign qualifications, it is overkill: a $99-$159/year agent and a calendar reminder do the job.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[CSC Entity Management](/en/tools/csc-entity-management/)** — one of the two incumbents with the largest share of enterprise registered agent work. Pick it when you have many non-US subsidiaries and want CSC's own offices in 140+ jurisdictions under a single contract.
- **CT Corporation** (Wolters Kluwer) — the other incumbent. Pick it when outside counsel already routes litigation service of process through CT.
- **[Discern](/en/tools/discern/)** — the fastest-growing entrant (a $10M Series A in July 2026, 800+ clients). Pick it for fund managers and PE groups that need each entity paying fees from its own bank account and Delaware franchise tax calculated on the cheaper method.
- **Harbor Compliance** — pick it below roughly 10 registrations when cost comes first, or for nonprofits filing charitable solicitations in many states.
- **[Athennian](/en/tools/athennian/)** or **[Diligent Entities](/en/tools/diligent-entities/)** — pick one when the job is governance: structure charts, minute books, D&O appointments, and UBO tracking. Either can sit on top of Filejet as the filing layer.

If none of these fit, consolidate onto one registered agent across every state, keep a register of every entity × state registration with an owner and due date, and check good standing on each state's lookup every quarter.

## Watch-outs

- **Standard is not the filing product.** At $150, you get the agent and alerts; the filings start at $350. Guard: price every registration at Professional unless you are certain your team will file every annual report itself.
- **Service of process can land with the wrong agent while you switch.** Guard: change agents state by state, keep the old agent until each state confirms, and send a test notice through to the named contacts before you cancel anything.
- **No public API or MCP server.** Filejet offers integrations through partner agreements, not developer docs, so pushing entity data to a warehouse or an AI workspace means a custom deal. Guard: get the export format and API access terms in the contract, and run a full CSV export during the trial. Background: [MCP server explained](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).
- **AI features are minor.** "AI Document Summary" and "Intelligent Filing Automation" sit in the feature table, with no public detail on how they work. Guard: score Filejet on filing accuracy and service response time, not on AI.
- **A small vendor in a statutory role.** If your registered agent fails, you file a change of agent in every state. Guard: export every registration, agent address, and filing receipt each quarter, and require transfer-out cooperation in writing.
- **The review sample is thin.** Fewer than 40 public reviews across G2 and Capterra. Guard: ask for two references at your registration count and state mix before you move the portfolio.
