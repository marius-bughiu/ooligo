## What it is

Juicebox (branded PeopleGPT) is the AI-native sourcing tool that replaces Boolean search with natural-language queries against an 800M+ profile database compiled from 30+ public sources — LinkedIn, GitHub, conference attendee lists, patent and paper authorship, and other signals. Launched in 2023, it was the first widely-adopted example of LLM-native recruiting tooling, built around a generative-AI primitive rather than retrofitting it onto a legacy product.

As of 2026 it is no longer just a search box. Juicebox now spans the full source → outreach → shortlist loop: PeopleGPT search, multi-step personalized email sequences, and — the headline addition — **AI Agents** that run search, outreach, and follow-up end-to-end for a continuous pipeline against a standing req. That shift moves Juicebox out of the "better search" bucket and into the autonomous-sourcing conversation alongside [SeekOut](/en/tools/seekout/) and [goperfect](/en/tools/goperfect/).

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Natural-language sourcing.** "Find me senior Python engineers in Berlin who have contributed to open-source ML libraries and worked at fintech startups" produces a ranked candidate list in seconds. The Boolean queries that used to take a senior sourcer 30 minutes to construct are now a single sentence.
- **Multi-source candidate signal.** Pulls from LinkedIn (where most candidates live) plus GitHub (depth on technical roles), patent databases, conference speaker lists, and paper authorship — sources a Boolean LinkedIn search can't reach — and surfaces achievement-level signal, not just titles.
- **Autonomous agents for standing reqs.** An Agent searches, drafts, sends, and follows up on its own, learning from which profiles get shortlisted. This is the "always-on pipeline" play for evergreen or high-volume technical roles.
- **Built for AI-first workflows.** Pairs naturally with [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) for outreach personalization and [n8n](/en/tools/n8n/) for ATS sync; the assumption is that the recruiter's stack is itself AI-augmented.

## Pricing

Flat per-seat, billed monthly or annually. The annual toggle is advertised at 15% off, but the realized discount differs by plan — Starter drops 17%, Growth only 10%:

- **Free** — $0, limited trial searches, 1 seat, 5 one-off contact credits
- **Starter** — $119 per seat/month, or **$99 per seat/month billed annually**; 1 seat, unlimited searches, 500 contact credits (email + phone) and 500 export credits per month, 1 mailbox
- **Growth** — $199 per seat/month, or $179 per seat/month annually; add up to 5 seats, each paid per seat; 1,500 contact credits and 1,500 export credits per seat/month, talent insights, hiring-manager collaboration, 3 mailboxes per seat
- **Business** — custom, annual only; unlimited contact credits, ATS/CRM integrations, SSO, usage analytics, network sourcing, priority support, 6 mailboxes per seat
- **AI Agents add-on** — $199 per agent/month, on any paid plan; unlimited contact + email credits, auto-email or auto-shortlist

**Real-world cost:** the "$99" headline is one seat with no automation. A solo recruiter running Juicebox the way it's marketed — one seat plus one always-on Agent — is closer to **$300–320/mo** ($99–119 seat + $199 agent). Budget for the agent line item, not the base price, if the pitch that sold you was autonomous pipeline. ATS/CRM sync sits on the custom Business tier, so integration-dependent teams should price that in early.
## Best for

- Growth-stage tech companies hiring engineers, designers, and ML researchers — technical roles where deep, achievement-level signal beats keyword matching
- Recruiting teams already running AI in adjacent workflows ([sourcing](/en/learn/ai-sourcing/), screening, interview) who want natural-language search plus an autonomous top-of-funnel
- Teams reducing LinkedIn Recruiter lock-in by moving primary sourcing off the LinkedIn graph

## Watch-outs

- **Established players out-integrate it on the funnel.** [hireEZ](/en/tools/hireez/) and [Gem](/en/tools/gem/) — the two incumbents most teams already run — carry deeper ATS/CRM workflow and reporting; Juicebox's edge is the natural-language UX and the agent layer. *Guard:* keep your ATS as the system of record and treat Juicebox as the discovery + first-touch layer, not a CRM replacement.
- **Sparse-signal roles underperform.** Sales, ops, and traditional management candidates leave thin public footprints, so search quality drops. *Guard:* reserve Juicebox for roles with strong online signal (engineering, research, design) and keep a referral or agency channel for the rest.
- **Database depth varies by geography.** US/UK/EU coverage is strongest; some APAC and LATAM markets are thinner. *Guard:* run a 20-candidate test search in your actual target region before committing a seat, and compare recall against [Findem](/en/tools/findem/) or your current tool.
- **Autonomous agents send real email on your domain.** An auto-email Agent can burn sender reputation or misfire on a bad query. *Guard:* start any Agent in auto-shortlist mode, review its output for a week, and only switch to auto-email once its shortlists match what you'd have picked by hand.

## Alternatives

- **hireEZ / Gem** — pick these when ATS-native workflow, sequencing, and diversity/pipeline reporting matter more than search UX. They're the incumbents; Juicebox wins on natural-language discovery, they win on downstream workflow.
- **[SeekOut](/en/tools/seekout/)** — the agentic-slate alternative when you want a vendor-delivered shortlist service (Spot) rather than running the search yourself.
- **[goperfect](/en/tools/goperfect/)** — the fastest-moving autonomous-sourcing entrant; weigh it when continuous, multi-channel autopilot outreach across many ATSes is the core need and you're comparing agent-first products head-to-head.

Not a replacement for an [ATS](/en/learn/ats-vs-recruiting-crm/) — pair with [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/), [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), or another ATS for funnel tracking.
