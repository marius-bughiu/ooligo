The old way to frame [Avature](/en/tools/avature/) vs [Beamery](/en/tools/beamery/) was two enterprise talent CRMs: Avature as the configurable one that also runs your ATS, Beamery as the AI-first one that sits on top of the ATS you already have. That frame is out of date on both sides.

Beamery no longer sells itself as a talent CRM. Its site now leads with "The AI Platform For Workforce Transformation" and ships three layers: a Data Platform (Skills Intelligence, Task Intelligence, Talent Market Insights), a Workforce Intelligence Suite for scenario modelling across automation, reskilling, and hiring, and an Execution Suite that still contains Talent CRM next to sourcing, matching, talent marketing, and career sites. Ray, its agentic AI consultant, runs across all three. Avature, for its part, has moved into Beamery's old ground. It now sells a Workforce Planning module with an AI model builder covering permanent and contingent workers, a VMS, and a new set of AI agents with an agent builder for customers' own agents, announced alongside its second consecutive Leader placement in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Talent Acquisition (Recruiting) Suites (8 May 2026).

So the real split is this. **Avature is the system that runs hiring, with planning and agents added. Beamery is a workforce-planning and skills-data layer, with a CRM included.** Which one you need depends on whether your problem is process or planning.

## Where Avature wins

**It runs the whole process, not just the top of it.** Avature's Talent Acquisition Suite covers ATS, candidate CRM, high-volume, early careers, executive search, agency management, contingent workforce, interview scheduling, and video interview. Its Talent Management Suite adds onboarding, internal mobility, performance, succession, and learning. One data model holds candidates, employees, and contractors, so a campus hire becomes an employee and later an internal-mobility candidate without re-keying anything. Beamery has no ATS, and its roadmap points toward planning, not process. Every Beamery deployment assumes another system owns requisitions, offers, and compliance steps.

**Configurability for hiring that doesn't fit a template.** Avature's core advantage is still that its workflow engine and data model bend to the organization. A bank running executive search, graduate intake, and contractor onboarding through three different approval chains can build all three without vendor engineering. This is why it shows up in regulated industries and in multi-business-unit groups where every division hires differently.

**Footprint and procurement fit.** Avature reports use by 110 Fortune 500 companies across 164 countries and 32 languages, private-cloud hosting in US, European, and Asian data centres, and a FedRAMP-certified public-sector offering. Gartner placed it as a Leader in the 2026 recruiting-suites Magic Quadrant, one of 12 vendors evaluated. For a buyer whose security review asks for data residency and a US federal authorization, Avature arrives with the paperwork.

**Vendor stability.** Avature is privately held and has been operating since 2005. Beamery cut roughly 12% of staff in January 2023 and a further 25% in November 2023, and third-party trackers put its headcount near 200. It has announced no funding round since its 2022 Series D. Beamery is still shipping, but a multi-year, partner-led rollout on Beamery is a bet on a smaller company than the one that won those enterprise logos.

## Where Beamery wins

**Task-level workforce planning.** Beamery's Task Intelligence breaks roles into tasks, scores each one by frequency and effort, and estimates the time and cost saved by automating it, then maps tasks back to skills and roles. It answers "which of these 40 roles is mostly automatable, and what do those people do next", which is the question a 2026 workforce plan starts with. Beamery says the models draw on 20B+ talent data points, 600M+ talent profiles, and 300M+ normalized job descriptions. Avature's new Workforce Planning module models headcount and mix. It does not decompose work into tasks, and it does not ship an external skills and market dataset of comparable scale.

**An AI governance record you can hand to a regulator.** Beamery holds ISO 42001 (AI management systems) on top of ISO 27001 and SOC 2. Its talent-matching model also has a continuously running third-party bias audit by Warden AI, with results published live rather than once a year. Talent-matching AI already falls under NYC LL 144 and Illinois HB 3773, and enters EU AI Act Annex III scope in December 2027. A live audit dashboard is what procurement and works councils are starting to ask for. Avature lets you switch AI on or off feature by feature, which is useful, but it has not published an equivalent third-party audit.

**It layers onto Workday or SAP instead of replacing them.** Beamery's Workday integration is certified for both CRM and Recruiting, and SAP SuccessFactors connects through its Talent Intelligence Hub. If your HCM decision is final and the ATS is part of it, Beamery adds intelligence without a rip-and-replace. Avature can integrate with Workday too, but you buy it to be the recruiting system of record, which means displacing whatever currently plays that role.

## Pricing reality

Neither vendor publishes prices; both are quote-only.

For Beamery, Vendr's aggregated contract data (as recorded on our [Beamery entry](/en/tools/beamery/)) puts the median annual contract at **$76,797**, with a range of **$24,717** to **$204,153**. The low end is a single-module Talent CRM, and the high end is the Data Platform plus Workforce Intelligence at global-enterprise headcount. Implementation runs through partners (Accenture, Carahsoft, The Cloud Connectors, Version 1) and is priced separately.

Avature has no comparable public contract data. Our estimate, from its packaging and deployment pattern, is high five figures a year at the mid-market entry point, rising to seven figures for a global enterprise taking both suites, plus 90–180 days of partner-led configuration. Treat that as an estimate, not a quote.

The ratio that matters is scope, not list price. A Beamery CRM-only deal sits around one-third of the median Beamery contract. A full Avature deployment replaces the ATS, CRM, and onboarding contracts, so it is likely 2–5× a mid-band Beamery contract on its own (estimate). Measured against the stack it retires, it can come out flat. Compare Avature against the sum of the contracts it would retire, and compare Beamery against the cost of the planning work it replaces.

## Implementation effort

Avature is a configuration project. Expect 90–180 days with a partner before go-live on a complex global setup, and a permanent TA ops or HRIS owner afterwards. Its power is proportional to how well it is configured. An under-resourced rollout produces a highly configurable system that nobody configured, which feels like an expensive, dated ATS. Recruiters coming from lighter tools will notice the extra clicks.

Beamery is an integration and data project. The HCM connectors are pre-built, but Task and Skills Intelligence are only as good as the employee and job data you load and the ontology tuning you do. Task Intelligence outputs are inferred from job descriptions and market signal, not observed from real work, and Beamery's own figure of 90% relevance on first review still leaves one inference in ten wrong. Validate the task map against manager interviews for two or three role families before any headcount decision runs off it.

## Verdict

**Pick Avature** when the problem is process: your recruiting runs on fragmented or outgrown systems across business units or countries, you have non-standard hiring tracks (campus, executive, contingent, regulated roles), and you want CRM, ATS, onboarding, and internal mobility on one configurable data model. It is also the pick when vendor longevity and FedRAMP or data-residency requirements sit high on the procurement scorecard.

**Pick Beamery** when the problem is planning: your HCM and ATS are settled (Workday or SAP SuccessFactors), you have a funded skills-based or AI-automation workforce programme with an executive owner, and you need task-level modelling plus an auditable AI trail. Cap the first term at two years and sign data-export terms with the licence.

**Pick neither** if you are below roughly 2,000 employees or 1,000 hires a year. Both need enterprise-scale implementation and ongoing ownership to pay back. [Gem](/en/tools/gem/) or [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) will deliver most of the CRM and pipeline value for a fraction of the contract. If matching accuracy across a very large internal population is the whole job, look at [Eightfold AI](/en/tools/eightfold/). If the mandate is purely an internal talent marketplace, [Gloat](/en/tools/gloat/) does that narrower job better than either.

If you are choosing in a vacuum without the conditions above, pick Avature. It runs the process today, it now covers planning well enough for most programmes, and it comes from the more stable vendor. Add Beamery later if task-level workforce planning becomes a board mandate. It is built to sit on top of a system of record, and Avature can be that system.
