## What it is

**Plum is no longer an independent vendor.** [Phenom](/en/tools/phenom/) announced on April 28, 2026 that it had acquired Plum, with terms undisclosed. As of November 5, 2026, plum.io and plum.io/contact both 301-redirect to Phenom's acquisition post, so there is no standalone product page, sign-up or pricing contact left. Phenom describes Plum's science as "now part of Phenom's platform." Neither the press release nor the blog post says whether Plum will keep selling outside Phenom. This page stays up for historical context and to route existing customers, and anyone shortlisting psychometric assessment, to the products that now do the job.

Plum was a psychometric talent-assessment platform from Kitchener, Ontario, led by co-founder and CEO Caitlin MacGregor and funded to roughly $12.1M. It measured *who* a candidate is (problem-solving style, motivation, social style, adaptability) rather than *what* they can already do, which put it in a different category from skills testing like [HackerRank](/en/tools/hackerrank/), [TestGorilla](/en/tools/testgorilla/) or [Vervoe](/en/tools/vervoe/). Its Role Model technology maps a behavioral profile against more than 40,000 real-world jobs, and Plum says every assessment is independently audited for adverse impact across demographic groups. The "four times more accurate than résumé screening" figure in the launch material is Plum's own claim, not an independent study.

Inside Phenom, Plum is one of two assessment anchors. Be Applied, bought in February 2026, supplies cognitive and situational-judgement tests; Plum supplies the behavioral layer. Both are delivered through Phenom's AI Interviewer, Voice Assessment Agent and Simulation Agent, and a Phenom WorkOps "Assessment Creation Agent" builds role blueprints. Phenom's pitch for the bundle is candidate fraud: behavior that an AI-written résumé cannot fake.

## Why it showed up in Recruiting stacks

- **Behavioral signal next to skills signal.** Teams that already ran skills tests added Plum for roles where work style and motivation drove retention, mostly customer-facing and early-career knowledge work.
- **One assessment, many roles.** A candidate took Plum once and was scored against every open role profile, which cut the assessment fatigue that hurts [candidate experience](/en/learn/candidate-experience/).
- **The profile outlived the hire.** The same data fed internal mobility, team composition and succession planning, which is the part Phenom now sells as onboarding, career pathing and manager coaching.

## Pricing (historical)

Plum was quote-only, priced per employee or per assessment on annual contracts, with an estimated entry point in the mid-five figures a year for mid-market buyers and 30-60 day implementations. There is no current standalone price: the contact page redirects to Phenom. Plum is now bought as part of a Phenom contract, which is also quote-only; Vendr's median Phenom deal is $98,313 a year, so a buyer who only wanted assessments is now pricing a talent-acquisition suite.

## What it was best for

Mid-market and enterprise TA teams that treated behavioral fit as a stated hiring criterion, wanted one assessment reused across many roles, and planned to reuse the profiles for internal mobility. It was a poor fit for engineering hiring and for high-volume hourly hiring, where skills tests or game-based assessments carried more signal.

## Migration path: where Plum customers go now

- **Already on Phenom, or planning to be:** take Plum inside Phenom. That is the only place the Role Model library is still developed, and it removes the separate assessment login.
- **Running another ATS and needing a psychometric personality layer at enterprise scale:** [SHL](/en/tools/shl/). The OPQ and Motivational Questionnaire are the closest like-for-like replacement, sold independently of any ATS.
- **Mid-market, wanting cognitive aptitude and personality in one cheaper contract:** [Criteria](/en/tools/criteria-corp/), with the CCAT plus personality and emotional-intelligence inventories.
- **High-volume hourly or frontline hiring:** [Harver](/en/tools/harver/), whose Pymetrics-derived behavioral games are built for that population.
- **Small teams that want self-serve pricing and a personality test in a broad library:** [TestGorilla](/en/tools/testgorilla/).
- **Mainly using Plum profiles for internal mobility rather than hiring:** price Phenom's talent-marketplace modules against [Eightfold](/en/tools/eightfold/).

## Watch-outs

- **Standalone continuity is unannounced.** Nothing public says whether Plum's connectors to Workday, Greenhouse, Lever and other ATSs stay maintained for non-Phenom customers. Guard: before your next renewal, get written terms from Phenom on contract continuity, the connector support end date, and a full export of candidate profiles and custom role models in a documented format; take that export now, while the integration still works.
- **Validation does not transfer automatically.** An adverse-impact audit of the standalone product says little about the same assessment delivered through a voice agent or simulation. Guard: ask for the validation study and audit as the assessment runs inside Phenom, then run your own four-fifths-rule check on the first hiring cohort before scaling. See [AI screening bias](/en/learn/ai-screening-bias/).
- **Switching tools restarts compliance work.** If you score candidates in New York City, [NYC LL 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/) requires a bias audit within one year before an automated employment decision tool is used, plus candidate notice; the [EU AI Act](/en/learn/eu-ai-act-for-legal-teams/) treats hiring AI as high-risk. Guard: schedule the replacement tool's audit and update notices before cutover; consult counsel on how these rules apply to your hiring footprint.
- **Personality data is moving to a new processor.** Stored Plum profiles now sit with Phenom. Guard: confirm your data-processing agreement names Phenom, check retention periods, and delete profiles of rejected candidates you have no basis to keep under GDPR.
- **Personality scores were always a secondary signal.** Meta-analyses give personality measures modest predictive power next to [structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) and work samples. Guard: whichever replacement you pick, cap its weight in the decision and never use it as a single knockout.
