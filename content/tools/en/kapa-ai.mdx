## What it is

kapa.ai is a managed retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) service that answers technical product questions from your own content: documentation sites, API specs, PDFs, GitHub, community forums, Zendesk tickets, and internal wikis such as Confluence and Notion. You point it at your sources, it crawls and keeps them in sync, and it serves cited answers through prebuilt surfaces: an "Ask AI" widget on your docs, a support-form deflector that answers before a ticket is filed, Slack and Discord bots, a Zendesk app that drafts replies from previously solved tickets, and an internal assistant for support and solutions engineers. For developers it exposes a Chat API, a Retrieval API, an Agent SDK, and a hosted MCP server, so a customer's coding agent in Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex can query your docs directly.

The company came out of Y Combinator's Summer 2023 batch, was founded by Emil Soerensen (CEO) and Finn Bauer (CTO), announced a $3.2M round in October 2024, and lists a team of 22 on its YC profile. It says 200+ companies use it; named customers include OpenAI, Docker, monday.com, Nokia, Logitech, and Raspberry Pi. In mid-2026 it repositioned from "AI docs assistant" toward "the knowledge layer your agents run on," which is where the Retrieval API and MCP server come from.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Answer quality on technical content is the product.** kapa does one job: grounded answers with citations, and a refusal when the sources don't cover the question. Reviewers on G2 (4.9 out of 5, a small sample of about 40 reviews) and in independent write-ups consistently put answer accuracy and the "I don't know" guardrail first. If your tickets are "why does this SDK call time out," that matters more than workflow breadth.
- **It deflects at the point of question, not after the ticket.** The docs widget and support-form deflector catch the question before it becomes a ticket, and the Zendesk app helps the agent who gets the ones that slip through. Vendor-published case studies claim 88% self-serve at Raspberry Pi and 300k+ queries answered at Nokia; treat those as ceilings, not forecasts.
- **Documentation teams get a gap report.** Conversation analytics flag questions the docs couldn't answer, so the assistant doubles as a backlog for your technical writers. That loop is the main reason DevRel and docs teams, not just support, end up owning the contract.

## Pricing reality

kapa publishes no paid price. The pricing page has three tiers: a 14-day free trial (1 index, 5,000 pages crawled, 1,000 answers, no credit card; the workspace pauses when it ends), **Growth** (a platform fee plus a committed volume of answers per month, with continuous sync, zero data retention, PII masking, and gap reports), and **Enterprise** (SSO, SCIM, role-based access, EU or US hosting, SLA).

What buyers actually pay: Vendr's purchase data, based on 30 deals, shows a median of about $24,600 a year, with a range of roughly $12,000 to $83,000. Read that as about $1,000 a month at the floor and $2,000 a month at the median, on an annual contract. The single biggest cost variable is the **overage rate** on answers above your committed volume; Vendr buyers report it is hard to forecast question volume and that kapa negotiated both overage (about 20% off) and platform fee (about 30% off) when pushed.

## Best for

Support, DevRel, and documentation leads at developer-tool, API, hardware, and B2B SaaS companies whose questions are answered by docs, specs, and past tickets, and who want a managed assistant live across docs, Slack or Discord community, and Zendesk within weeks, without building or hosting a RAG pipeline themselves.

Not for an ecommerce or consumer support team whose tickets are order status, refunds, and account changes: kapa answers questions, it does not take actions in your backend or close tickets.

## Watch-outs

- **Answer-based pricing with opaque overage.** A launch, a breaking change, or a community spike can push answers well past your committed volume. **Guard:** get the overage rate per 1,000 answers and an annual cap in the order form, and size the commit from 3 months of real ticket plus community question volume, not a guess.
- **It is a question-answering layer, not a helpdesk agent.** The Zendesk app drafts replies for a human to send; it does not resolve or route tickets on its own. **Guard:** if your goal is autonomous resolution, measure it against per-resolution agents like [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) Fin, and keep kapa scoped to deflection and rep assist.
- **Quality is capped by your docs.** Stale or missing docs produce confident refusals or thin answers. **Guard:** assign a docs owner to the gap report with a weekly review, and run a 3-week pilot on your 200 hardest real questions before signing, as Vendr buyers recommend.
- **Gated content needs mirrored permissions.** If parts of your docs are behind login or plan tiers, the assistant only respects that if you replicate the access rules in its configuration. **Guard:** keep gated and public content in separate indexes and test with a logged-out user before launch.
- **Small vendor.** $3.2M disclosed funding and a team of about 22 is light next to the support-agent platforms. **Guard:** request the SOC 2 Type II report and confirm data-export terms, and keep your docs as the system of record so the answer layer stays replaceable.

## Alternatives

[Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) Fin and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/)'s AI agents are the market-share leaders in AI support; pick them when the job is resolving a mixed ticket queue inside the helpdesk you already pay for, billed per resolution. [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) and [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) are the fastest-growing entrants; pick them for high-volume, action-taking agents with custom enterprise pricing. [Inkeep](/en/tools/inkeep/) is the closest direct rival on technical docs; pick it when you want the agent defined in your own TypeScript repo and need background automation agents, not just Q&A. [eesel AI](/en/tools/eesel-ai/) is the cheaper, helpdesk-first pick for teams whose answers live in past tickets inside Zendesk or Freshdesk. If your docs platform already bundles an assistant (Mintlify, Fern) and your volume is low, start there and buy kapa only when answer quality or multi-surface coverage becomes the bottleneck. Pick kapa.ai when your product is technical, accuracy on docs-grounded answers is the deciding metric, and you want it managed rather than built.
