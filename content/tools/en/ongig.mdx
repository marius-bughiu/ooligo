## What it is

Ongig is job description management software from Ongig, Inc., an independent company founded by Rob Kelly. It keeps every job description in one cloud library and flags exclusionary language and readability problems. It scores titles for search, drafts and edits with AI, and pushes finished posts back to the ATS. It started as a "Text Analyzer" (the login still lives at text-analyzer.ongig.com) and grew into a governance layer: version history, approval workflows, pay-transparency checks and, on the top tier, translation. Logos on its client page include Intel, Disney, GoDaddy, Yelp and Elastic.

The question this page answers: you have 300 or 3,000 job descriptions spread across an ATS, shared drives and hiring managers' inboxes. Do you need a dedicated tool to govern them, and is Ongig's $4,900 Lite plan enough to start?

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **The library is the product, not the writer.** Any LLM drafts a decent post. Ongig's value is one versioned source of truth for every role, with a job content dashboard showing which posts are stale, missing a pay range or out of sync with the ATS. That is a different job from writing one good post.
- **It connects to the ATS you already run.** The integrations page names [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), [Workday](/en/tools/workday/), [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/), [SAP SuccessFactors](/en/tools/successfactors-recruiting/), [Lever](/en/tools/lever/), [SmartRecruiters](/en/tools/smartrecruiters/), [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/), [BambooHR](/en/tools/bamboohr/), [Workable](/en/tools/workable/), [Jobvite](/en/tools/jobvite/), Oracle Taleo, UKG and others, plus an integration API for a homegrown ATS.
- **Translation sits inside the governed library.** The Enterprise tier translates job content, so a German or Japanese post goes through the same approval and versioning as the English original instead of living in a translator's email.

## Pricing

Ongig publishes its prices, which is rare in this category. Three tiers, each priced per year:

- **Lite — $4,900/year.** Up to 3 users and 100 jobs. Cloud job library, Word or copy-paste import, Word export, SEO title scoring, readability and language suggestions, email support. **No ATS import and no AI writing.**
- **Professional — from $14,900/year.** Unlimited users and jobs. Adds one-way ATS/HRIS import, AI writing and templates, version history, PDF and HTML export, the job content dashboard, pay-transparency compliance and a named success rep.
- **Enterprise — from $39,900/year.** Adds two-way ATS/HRIS sync, custom workflow automation and alerts, custom roles and reporting, SSO, out-of-sync alerts and job language translation.

There are also one-off services: a JD bias audit of the whole library from $6,900, a bulk rewrite from $9,900 and a career site build from $12,900. Ongig says it discounts for small businesses and non-profits.

Lite works out to about $136 per user per month. That is steep for a tool without ATS import or AI drafting. For most teams the real entry point is Professional at $14,900. That is about 60% of Textio's $24,405 median contract (Vendr, February 2026) and at the top of the $10,000–15,000 band we see for [Datapeople](/en/tools/datapeople/).

## Best for

TA operations leads and HR compliance owners at employers with a few hundred to a few thousand recurring roles. Hiring managers rewrite posts by hand, pay-range laws apply in several jurisdictions, and nobody can say which version of a job description is current. Enterprise suits multinationals that post in several languages and need translated posts approved like the English ones.

**Not for you** if one or two recruiters write every post and you hire in one state with no salary-range law. A Claude prompt with your style guide (see the [JD writer skill](/en/workflows/jd-writer-claude-skill/)) plus your ATS's built-in generator covers that for $0 marginal cost. Lite is also the wrong buy if you mainly want AI drafting, because Lite doesn't include it.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Textio](/en/tools/textio/)** — the category's best-known name. Its score predicts who will apply, including the demographic skew of the applicant pool, from real hiring outcomes. Pick Textio when applicant mix is the metric you report on and you hire mostly in English. Pick Ongig when the problem is governing a large library at a known price.
- **[Datapeople](/en/tools/datapeople/) (Payscale)** — strongest on audited pay-transparency posting across jurisdictions, with Payscale market data feeding the range. Pick it when you already buy Payscale. Pick Ongig when you need translation or want to see the price before the demo.
- **Your ATS's native AI plus an LLM** — the fastest-growing substitute. Greenhouse and Workday both draft job descriptions in the editor. Pick this when volume is low. None of them keeps a cross-role library, version history or an audit trail of what was posted.

## Watch-outs

- **The tier ladder hides the features you came for.** Lite has no ATS import and no AI writing. Pay-transparency checks start at Professional, and two-way sync, SSO and translation only come with Enterprise. *Guard:* list your must-haves before the demo, map each to a tier on the pricing page, and get the tier named in the order form.
- **Exclusionary-language checking is sold two ways.** The homepage lists exclusionary-language detection as a feature, while the full-library bias audit is a separate service from $6,900. *Guard:* ask in writing whether live flagging is included in your tier, and only buy the audit if you're cleaning up a legacy library in bulk.
- **Independent evidence is thin.** At the time of writing, Ongig's G2 and TrustRadius profiles show zero reviews, so there is no third-party read on support or integration reliability. *Guard:* ask for two references at your ATS and job volume, and run a 30-day pilot on 20 live reqs before you commit to an annual term.
- **One-way sync drifts.** On Professional, edits made in the ATS don't flow back to Ongig, so the library and the live post diverge. *Guard:* lock job-description editing to Ongig in the ATS permissions, or budget for Enterprise's two-way sync and out-of-sync alerts.
- **Compliance flags are not legal advice.** The tool flags missing pay ranges; it doesn't certify that you comply with each state's law or the EU Pay Transparency Directive, whose transposition deadline passed in June 2026. *Guard:* ask which jurisdictions the rules cover and how often they update, and keep counsel's sign-off on the posting template.
