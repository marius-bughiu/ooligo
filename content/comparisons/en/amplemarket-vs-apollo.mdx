The old way to frame [Amplemarket](/en/tools/amplemarket/) vs [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) was premium deliverability and contact-level signals against a cheap database with a basic sequencer. That frame is out of date. During 2026 Apollo closed most of the infrastructure gap: every paid plan now includes a deliverability suite with email warmup and domain and mailbox purchasing, a parallel dialer is a $119/month team add-on, and its MCP server works on every plan, free included. Amplemarket moved the other way. It now leads with "Agents build your pipeline. Your team closes it." and sells a set of Duo agents on top of its data, sequencing, and dialer.

Both are now AI-led outbound platforms with their own data, and both can be driven from Claude or ChatGPT over MCP. The split that remains is this. **Apollo is a self-serve, per-seat platform with a public price list. Amplemarket is a sales-led, multichannel platform with automated LinkedIn, WhatsApp, and SMS steps, priced per team.** If your outbound is email plus phone, most of the gap between them closes, and Apollo costs a fraction as much.

## Where Amplemarket wins

- **More channels, all automated.** Amplemarket sequence steps include email, calls, LinkedIn connection requests, profile visits and post likes, WhatsApp, SMS and iMessage, and voice messages, all in one workflow. Apollo sequences email and calls natively. Its LinkedIn work runs through the Chrome extension and manual tasks, and it has no WhatsApp or SMS step. If your buyers answer on LinkedIn or WhatsApp, this is the deciding feature.
- **Agents that act, not just a writing assistant.** Amplemarket's Duo line covers account research (Duo Accounts), campaign building (Copilot), copy (Copywriter), competitive intelligence, and buying signals on every plan. Duo Voice comes with Growth and Elite, and Duo Inbox, which works replies, is on Elite. Apollo has agents and an AI Assistant too, but the assistant is still labelled "introductory" and capped at 5 chats on the free plan. Amplemarket's agents are the product. Apollo's are a layer on a database and sequencer.
- **Contact-level signals.** Amplemarket tracks job changes (500 to 2,000 contacts by plan) and person-level buying signals, and its agents act on them. Apollo's intent works by topic and account: 6 intent topics on Basic and Professional, 12 on Organization. That tells you which company is researching a category, not which person to call.
- **A CSM on every plan.** Startup gets a scaled CSM and community onboarding. Growth and Elite get a dedicated CSM and personalized onboarding. Apollo is self-serve below its Custom plan.
- **Bounce rate.** Amplemarket advertises a 3% or lower average bounce rate on 200M+ verified contacts, refreshed with 70M+ updates a week. That's a vendor claim, not an independent audit, but it's the figure to hold them to in a pilot.

## Where Apollo wins

- **Price.** Apollo Professional is $79 per seat per month billed annually. Amplemarket's cheapest plan works out to $300 per user per month. More on this below.
- **Public prices and self-serve.** Apollo publishes every tier, offers a 14-day trial, and lets one person buy a seat today. Amplemarket publishes only its Startup price and sells Growth and Elite through a sales call.
- **More credits per seat.** Professional grants 48,000 credits per seat per year. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 8, so that's up to 48,000 emails or 6,000 phone numbers. Amplemarket Startup grants 13,500 email credits and 600 phone credits per user per year. Even Elite's 9,600 phone credits are only modestly more than what a Professional seat could buy.
- **Database breadth.** Apollo lists 240M+ contacts and 30M+ companies, plus waterfall enrichment from partner sources on every paid plan.
- **Inbound and CRM enrichment in the same tool.** Apollo's Inbound add-on (Essentials $119, Max $639 per team per month, billed annually) adds website visitor identification, lead routing, and form enrichment. Automated CRM enrichment is included from Basic up. Amplemarket is an outbound tool.
- **Ecosystem.** Apollo reports 600,000+ companies and 9,690 reviews at 4.7/5. Integrations, templates, and how-to content for edge cases are easier to find.

## Pricing reality

Apollo's annual list prices: Free ($0, 900 credits per seat per year), Basic $49, Professional $79, and Organization $119 per seat per month (minimum 3 seats). Annual billing saves 24% over monthly. Credits are pooled across the team and don't roll over. All seats on an account must be on the same plan. The Advanced Dialer add-on (parallel, power, international, and local presence dialing) is $119 per team per month.

Amplemarket: Startup is $600/month on an annual term with 2 users included, which is $7,200/year. Growth (4 users) and Elite (10 users) are custom-quoted. More users can be bought on any plan.

- **2 reps:** Apollo Professional costs $1,896/year, or $3,324 with the Advanced Dialer. Amplemarket Startup costs $7,200. That's **about 2.2× to 3.8× more** for Amplemarket.
- **10 reps:** Apollo Organization costs $14,280/year, or $15,708 with the dialer. Amplemarket doesn't publish Elite. If you plan on Startup's $300 per user per month, 10 users comes to $36,000, **roughly 2.3× Apollo's top published tier**. Treat that as a planning number to negotiate against, not a quote.

Apollo's real cost runs above list price when credits run out. Add-on credits are charged immediately and renew every cycle until you remove them. Amplemarket's real cost is the quote. Ask for the per-seat price, credit limits, and Duo Voice and Duo Inbox terms in writing before you sign.

## Implementation effort

Apollo: a rep can connect a mailbox, import a list, and launch a sequence the same day. If you buy new sending domains through Apollo, budget two to three weeks of warmup before you send at volume. That's the same as on any platform.

Amplemarket: plan on two to four weeks. Domain health setup and warmup take most of that. Configuring your ICP for the Duo agents, connecting LinkedIn and WhatsApp, and syncing your CRM take the rest. Growth and Elite include personalized onboarding. On Startup you get community onboarding, so a RevOps owner has to drive it.

Whichever you pick, the slow part is the first 60 days of tuning ICP and copy, not setup. Agent-written copy that nobody reviews reads as generic on either platform.

## Verdict

- **Pick Amplemarket when** your buyers answer on LinkedIn, WhatsApp, or SMS and you want those steps automated, you want agents running research, campaigns, and reply handling rather than an assistant, you have roughly 4 or more full-time outbound reps, and you want a CSM in the rollout.
- **Pick Apollo when** your outbound is mostly email plus phone, you have fewer than about 10 reps, you need to buy seats this week without a sales call, or you also want inbound routing and CRM enrichment in the same tool.
- **Pick neither when** you're 1 to 3 people running low-volume, research-heavy outbound. [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) for enrichment plus [Instantly](/en/tools/instantly/) or [Smartlead](/en/tools/smartlead/) for sending gives you more control for less money. If you want an AI BDR to run outbound for you rather than a platform for your reps, compare [11x](/en/tools/11x/) and [Artisan](/en/tools/artisan/) instead.

**The pick:** if you can't decide, start with Apollo Professional. Its email, dialer, deliverability, and MCP coverage now match what most teams use Amplemarket for, at roughly a quarter of the per-seat cost. Move to Amplemarket when automated LinkedIn or WhatsApp steps, or agents that work replies, would book meetings Apollo can't.
