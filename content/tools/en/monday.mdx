## What it is

monday.com started as a visual work-management board and now sells four products on one platform: work management, monday CRM, monday service (ticketing and service desk) and monday dev. Every product is built from the same boards, columns, automations and dashboards, so a team that already runs projects in monday can add a sales pipeline or a ticket queue without learning a new tool or moving data. On 6 May 2026 the company repositioned itself from a work-management vendor to an "AI Work Platform", with 250,000 customers at the time: no-code agents that any board owner can configure, the Sidekick assistant, the monday vibe app builder, an AI Platform Gateway for choosing models, and one-click connectors to [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) and Microsoft 365 Copilot.

It is a public company (NASDAQ: MNDY) with real scale. Q2 2026 revenue was $364.6M, up 22% year on year, ARR crossed $1.5B in July, and management guides $1,466-1,474M for the year. On the Q2 call it said monday CRM had passed $100M ARR, strongest in SMB, while net new ARR from CRM, dev and service was softer as the company moved sales effort upmarket. It also cut about 20% of its workforce in the same restructuring.

## What changed in 2026

On 11 March monday opened the platform to outside AI agents: a dedicated agent sign-up flow, MCP support and instant GraphQL API keys, free on every plan. The first native agents shipped inside monday CRM for SDR work — lead sourcing and outreach. The 6 May relaunch extended agents to every product and changed pricing: new customers since that date buy AI credits alongside seats, at $0.01 per credit on annual plans. In May monday also agreed to acquire OneAI to bring voice agents in-house.

## The fork it resolves

The question this page answers: *we run monday for projects — is monday CRM plus monday service good enough to replace [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/)?*

For a team under roughly 30 sellers and 15 support agents whose process is simple, yes. You get one data model, one permission model and one automation engine across the handoff from closed deal to onboarding project to support ticket, and that handoff is where two-vendor stacks usually break. You lose depth. monday CRM has no lead scoring, no landing pages and no marketing automation, and its reporting and forecasting sit well below HubSpot Sales Hub Professional. monday service has AI triage, a customer portal and email intake, but no telephony and no per-resolution AI agent at Zendesk's maturity. If marketing attribution or multi-channel support at volume drives the decision, keep HubSpot or Zendesk and connect them to monday through the native integrations.

## A use case worth buying it for

A 40-person B2B services firm selling fixed-scope implementations. Deals live on a monday CRM board; when a deal moves to Won, an automation creates the delivery project on a work-management board with the scoped hours, the account manager and the kickoff date copied over. The client gets a monday service portal for change requests, and each ticket links back to both the project and the original deal. An SDR agent sources and qualifies leads into the pipeline. The ops lead builds all of it without a developer, and the dashboard showing sold hours against delivered hours per client is one board query, not a warehouse job.

## Pricing reality

monday CRM costs $12 (Basic), $17 (Standard) and $28 (Pro) per seat per month billed annually, or $18, $25 and $41 month to month; Ultimate is quoted. Email sequences and mass email start at Pro, and Standard caps custom automations at 250 a month against 25,000 on Pro, so Pro is the real floor for a sales team. monday service lists Standard at $31 and Pro at $45 per seat per month billed annually, with Enterprise quoted.

Two details move the budget. Seats are sold in buckets starting at 3, so a 6-person team pays for 10. And AI is now metered: plans include a monthly credit allowance, agent tasks draw 10 to 250+ credits each, and new customers buy extra credits in blocks. A 10-seat Pro CRM plus 10 seats of Pro service comes to about $8,800 a year before extra credits, against about $10,800 for 10 HubSpot Sales Hub Professional seats alone, before its $1,500 onboarding fee.

## Best for

The ops or RevOps lead at a 20-200 person company that already runs delivery or projects in monday and wants sales, onboarding and support on the same boards, owned by ops, not by a CRM admin.

## Not for

A sales org that lives on forecasting accuracy, multi-touch attribution or territory management, or a support team handling thousands of tickets a day across chat, voice and social.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/)** — the market-share leader. Pick it when you need territory models, CPQ, complex forecasting and an admin team to run it.
- **[HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/)** — the other market-share leader, and the pick when marketing and sales share one funnel and attribution matters.
- **[Attio](/en/tools/attio/)** — the fastest-growing AI-native CRM we cover. Pick it when the team wants a relationship-data CRM that enriches itself and does not need project or ticket boards.
- **[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/)** — pick it over monday service once support is its own department with volume, channels and SLAs to match.
- **[Airtable](/en/tools/airtable/)** — pick it when you need a flexible relational database more than a ready-made CRM or service product.

## Watch-outs

- **The seat buckets overcharge odd-sized teams.** Six users pay for ten, eleven pay for fifteen. Guard: count seats per product before you buy, give occasional users viewer or guest access, and negotiate a custom seat count on annual contracts above 20 seats.
- **Agent costs are variable.** A complex agent task can use 250+ credits, and credits are now a line item for new customers. Guard: run each agent on one board for a full billing cycle, read the credit consumption report, and cap credit purchases before you enable agents account-wide.
- **The CRM and service roadmap follows the company's upmarket shift.** Management reported softer net new ARR for CRM and service in Q2 and gave both products dedicated teams and budgets. Guard: take a monthly or one-year term on the new modules, and write an export path for boards and ticket history into the contract.
- **Reporting will hit a ceiling.** Dashboards combine a limited number of boards per plan, and there is no native cohort or attribution analysis. Guard: sync monday boards to your warehouse or BI tool from day one if leadership reports on pipeline conversion or support cost per ticket.
