Apollo and [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/) still aren't direct competitors, but the reason changed in 2026. The old split was capability: Apollo the cheap all-in-one, Outreach the enterprise sequencer. Both have since shipped an agent layer and a native MCP server, so the question a growing team faces is no longer "when do we graduate to a real platform." It's whether Outreach's seat floor and consumption pricing buy anything Apollo's bundle doesn't.

## What changed since the last version of this page

Two things, both in Q1 2026, and both narrowed the gap.

- **Apollo shipped an agent layer.** The Apollo AI Assistant went GA on 4 March 2026 — natural-language execution across prospecting, research, and sequencing rather than a copy-drafting sidebar. Apollo reports nearly 20,000 weekly active beta users and 2.3× more meetings booked among them; that is vendor-reported, not independently measured. It is currently free as an introductory offer on Basic, Professional, and Organization, with free-plan users capped at 5 chats.
- **Both now ship a native MCP server, gated differently.** Apollo's landed in Claude's connector directory on 24 February 2026, authenticates over OAuth with no API key, and works on **any plan, including free**. Outreach announced GA the same month and is the first revenue platform shipping both an MCP server and a client — but using it requires a licensed seat *and* Amplify enabled with active credits.

That gating asymmetry is the sharpest new line on this page. If your reason for buying is "our own agents query the system of record," Apollo gives you that at $0 and Outreach sells it to you inside a credit tier.

## Where Apollo wins

- **Published prices.** Basic $49, Professional $79, Organization $119 per user/month on annual terms ($59 / $99 / $149 monthly; Organization requires 3 seats). Outreach publishes nothing. You can size an Apollo deployment in an afternoon.
- **Database included.** Contact data sits in the SKU, so there is no separate ZoomInfo line. This is the largest cost difference between the two and it never shows up in a per-seat comparison.
- **Ungated API and MCP.** All paid plans expose the API; the MCP server works on every plan.
- **Time to first sequence.** An hour, against 4-8 weeks for a full Outreach rollout.
- **You can buy it at 5 seats.** Outreach usually won't quote a 10-rep team at all.

## Where Outreach wins

- **Sequence and reporting depth.** Branching on opens, clicks, and replies, send-time A/B testing, and activity-to-pipeline attribution that survives a board review. Apollo's reporting is shallower and stays shallower at its top tier.
- **The agent surface is broader and administered.** Omni in Slack and mobile, Agent Studio for assembling agents on a canvas, plus Research, Revenue, Personalization, Deal, and Meeting Prep agents, AI coach cards, and deal health alerts. Apollo has one assistant; Outreach has a portfolio with permissions around it.
- **Salesforce depth.** Heavy customization — custom objects, complex permission models, audit trail — is where Apollo's bi-directional sync starts losing edge cases.
- **Scale.** 200+ reps, multiple regions, structured territories. Apollo's UI becomes the constraint well before its data does.
- **Forecasting.** Scenario planner, forecast rollups, territory management, and forecast AI assist at the Pro tier. Apollo has no equivalent.

## Pricing reality

Outreach is quote-gated across all four Amplify tiers — Essentials, Core, Plus, and Pro, carrying 10,000 / 25,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 AI credits — and describes its own model as seat-based combined with consumption-based. Reported per-seat figures land near $100 / $130 / $160 for Core / Plus / Pro; those are procurement estimates, not list prices.

At 50 seats:

- **Apollo Professional** — 50 × $79 × 12 ≈ **$47K/year**, data included.
- **Outreach Amplify Core**, fully loaded — **roughly $150-165K in year one**, including $5,000-25,000+ implementation and a $2,000-5,000 annual platform fee.

That is about **3-3.5×**, and the multiplier holds because Apollo's number absorbs the data spend. At 30 seats the gap narrows to roughly 1.5-2.3× before any credit consumption. Below 25 seats there is no comparison to run: Outreach's seat minimum typically sits at 25-50, with volume breaks starting around 20-25 users.

Both sides now carry a consumption line, and that is where the budget actually leaks.

- Apollo credits expire at the end of the billing cycle with no rollover, overages run $0.20 each with a 250-credit ($50) minimum purchase, and a phone reveal costs 8× an email reveal. **Guard:** model one real month of phone reveals and dialer minutes before picking a tier. Third-party estimates put heavy-outbound teams at $150-400 per user/month once overages and add-ons land.
- Outreach's AI credits are negotiated and its overage rates aren't published. **Guard:** get the overage rate and a consumption cap into the original contract. Renewal uplifts ran 8-12% in Q1 2026 unless capped, on annual-only terms with 30-60 day cancellation notice.

## Implementation effort

Apollo: an hour to a first sequence, a week to a clean CRM sync. Outreach: 2-4 weeks of technical implementation covering CRM integration, sequence migration, and provisioning, then another 2-4 weeks before new reps are productive — 4-8 weeks realistically. Budget the professional-services line inside the year-one number rather than treating it as a rounding error.

## Verdict

- **Pick Apollo** if you're under 30 reps, you're paying separately for data and engagement today, or you want your own agents hitting the system of record without buying into a credit tier.
- **Pick Outreach** if you're 50+ reps with Salesforce customization Apollo's sync can't hold, you need forecasting and activity-to-pipeline reporting a CRO defends externally, or you want an administered agent portfolio instead of one assistant.
- **Pick neither** if you're 10-40 reps and Outreach's seat floor is the only thing blocking you. [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/) quotes at 10-15 seats with comparable engagement depth. And if the real complaint is record quality rather than sequencer depth, add [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) to Apollo — the data jump is bigger than the sequencer jump for most teams, at a fraction of the migration cost.
- **If you're choosing in a vacuum**, pick Apollo. It's published, it's cancellable, its MCP surface is ungated, and moving to Outreach later is a migration you can afford at 30 reps. Going the other direction at 100 reps is not.

The mistake to avoid hasn't changed, only its wording: don't buy Outreach because Apollo "feels" outgrown. Name the constraint first — seat count, a Salesforce edge case, or a forecast your CRO has to defend. If you can't name it, the upgrade is a re-platform with no thesis behind it.
