## What it is

Champify watches the contacts already sitting in a firm's Salesforce org and fires an event when one of those relationships turns into pipeline somewhere else. The core event is the job change: a contact the firm sold to, lost to, or supported takes a role at a different company. Around that core, Champify layers past-customer re-engagement, closed-lost alumni who now work at target accounts, executive hires at accounts already under coverage, and contact-record completion assembled from calendar invites, call transcripts, and email.

The design decision that defines the product is delivery. Signals land as records and fields inside Salesforce through a managed package, not in a separate console with its own login. Sits in [signal-based selling](/en/learn/signal-based-selling/) next to [UserGems](/en/tools/usergems/) and [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/), and it is deliberately the narrowest of the three.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Salesforce-native by construction.** The managed package carries unlimited users on every tier. Signals write to the objects that routing, forecasting, and reporting already read, so a [LeanData](/en/tools/leandata/) assignment flow or a [Clari](/en/tools/clari/) forecast consumes them with no middle layer and no second seat count.
- **First-party relationships, not a purchased list.** The tracked population is the firm's own CRM contacts. That keeps the signal defensible in a way a bought intent feed is not, and it makes the [champion-builder](/en/learn/champion-builder/) motion measurable instead of anecdotal.
- **Renewal-risk detection on the same feed.** A champion leaving a customer account is an NRR event, not a prospecting event. Because both flow through Salesforce, the same subscription serves the AE and the CSM.

## Pricing reality

Published tiers, checked 2026-09-03: **Core from $2,000/month** with 15,000 tracked contacts; **Pro from $3,000/month** with 40,000 contacts, Insider AI on 2,000 accounts, and calendar sync for 50 users; **Enterprise from $6,000/month** with 150,000 contacts, Insider AI on 5,000 accounts, and CRM Clean over 200,000 records. At the floor that is $24K, $36K, and $72K a year. Contact add-ons extend to 150K, 200K, and 2M records — the bill tracks contact and account volume, not headcount. Core carries a 2x ROI guarantee.

Champify's own FAQ sets the viability threshold at 10,000 CRM contacts. Below that the tracked population produces too few monthly job-change events to clear a $24K floor, and the honest answer is to stay on manual tracking.

Against the category leader the gap is real but narrower than the folklore. UserGems publishes $40,000/year for Core plus a $3,000 implementation fee — $43,000 in year one against Champify's $24,000, roughly 1.8x. At the top of both price books, Champify Enterprise starts at $72,000 while UserGems Elite is $150,000 plus $10,000 implementation, about 2.2x. Anyone still quoting a 5-10x spread is working from pre-2026 numbers.

Champify's marketing puts past-customer conversion at 6-22x other outbound signals, and its case pages credit Jellyfish with 200% growth in enterprise outbound ACV. Both are vendor figures — useful as a directional claim to test in a pilot, not as a benchmark to underwrite a business case.

## Best for

RevOps and sales leaders at Salesforce-standardized B2B SaaS companies in the $20-200M ARR band, running mid-market or enterprise deals where named champions decide outcomes, with at least 10,000 CRM contacts worth tracking. The sharpest scoped use case: a customer-success team that needs the champion-departure alert to reach the owning CSM within days of the move, wired to a defined renewal-risk play rather than to an inbox.

## Versus the alternatives

- **vs [UserGems](/en/tools/usergems/).** UserGems is the segment leader and supports Salesforce and HubSpot on every tier. Champify's FAQ is explicit that there is no native HubSpot integration; Champify data reaches HubSpot only by routing through Salesforce first. Pick UserGems if the firm runs HubSpot, or if it wants the wider signal and AI-action surface that the higher price buys. Pick Champify if the firm is Salesforce-only and wants the same core event for roughly half the year-one cost.
- **vs [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/).** The largest data vendor in the segment bundles a job-change feed with the contact database the firm already buys. Pick ZoomInfo if a bundled feed covers the need and no separate line item can be defended. Pick Champify when the job-change signal has to arrive as a routed, owned Salesforce record with a play attached rather than as a list to export.
- **vs [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/).** The fastest-growing multi-signal entrant treats job change as one input among community, product, and social signal, at a $2,500/month floor. Pick Common Room for product-led motions with developer or community signal. Pick Champify when relationship movement is the only signal that matters and the stack is contact-and-account-centric.
- **vs [Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/) and the status quo.** Sales Navigator surfaces job changes to the individual rep and stops there. Under about 5,000 tracked contacts that is workable. The predictable failure above it: nobody notices the champion left, and the renewal call is where the firm finds out.

## Watch-outs

- **Signal without an execution layer.** The most repeated critique in third-party reviews is that Champify reports the move and leaves the follow-up entirely to the team — no sequencer, no dialer, no prioritization of which of this week's 40 movers to work first. **Guard:** treat the Salesforce field as a trigger, not a deliverable. Wire it into an [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/) sequence and a named play before launch, and measure adoption on plays fired, not alerts delivered.
- **Salesforce is a hard dependency.** A CRM migration or a HubSpot-first business unit strands the subscription. **Guard:** confirm the Salesforce commitment outlasts the Champify term, and if a HubSpot move is on the two-year roadmap, price UserGems now instead of paying a migration penalty later.
- **Yield is a function of CRM hygiene.** Contacts with dead emails and missing titles produce no matches, and contact-volume tiers mean the firm pays to track them anyway. **Guard:** run a suppression and dedupe pass before the contract is sized, so the tier is bought against live contacts.
- **Narrow scope invites churn at 6-12 months.** Third-party reviews report teams outgrowing the tool once they want multi-signal scoring. **Guard:** negotiate a 12-month initial term rather than a multi-year, and set the renewal review against a written list of the signals the firm expected to add.
- **Tracking people carries a privacy posture.** Job-change monitoring on named individuals has GDPR implications for EU-resident contacts. **Guard:** clear the scope with the DPO before rollout; the common resolution is restricting tracked populations by region.
