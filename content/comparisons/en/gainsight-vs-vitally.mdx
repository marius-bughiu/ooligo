[Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) and [Vitally](/en/tools/vitally/) are both built for Customer Success teams, but they differ on what "built for CS" actually means in practice. Gainsight is a configurable enterprise platform with the deepest health-scoring and playbook engine on the market, plus an optional Product Experience bundle. Vitally is a modern CS platform with a Notion-adjacent collaboration surface — docs live next to account data, views are flexible, and a non-admin user can build their own dashboard. The routing question comes down to this: do you need maximum configurability and the PX bundle, or do you need your entire CS team — not just your admin — to actually use the tool daily?

## Where Gainsight wins

- **Health-score configurability.** Gainsight's scorecard builder lets you weight signals from virtually any data source: product events, support history, billing cadence, NPS, community activity. Vitally's health scoring is solid but more opinionated — fewer custom dimensions, less flexibility in weighting and threshold logic. For enterprise CS-Ops teams building multi-product health models, Gainsight's depth is hard to match.
- **Gainsight PX bundle.** Gainsight sells CS + Product Experience as a combined contract. PX adds in-app engagement (guides, announcements, onboarding checklists, adoption surveys) woven into the same health record that CS sees. No other platform in this comparison offers that combination natively. If you're running both a CS motion and an in-app product engagement program, the bundle — typically 15-25% cheaper than buying both separately — is a genuine structural advantage.
- **Journey Orchestrator and Rules Engine.** Gainsight splits automation across two systems — Rules Engine for data-driven triggers and Journey Orchestrator for multi-step lifecycle programs. That split is often criticized as complexity, but for large CS teams with dozens of concurrent programs, the explicit separation between data-triggered automations and human-managed journeys is operationally useful.
- **Enterprise governance.** Gainsight ships SSO, role-based access controls, team-level segmentation, and audit logging at every tier. For publicly traded companies or those under SOC 2 audit, the governance layer matters.

## Where Vitally wins

- **CSM-facing UX.** The single most-cited Gainsight complaint is that non-admin users find the platform hard to navigate without coaching. Vitally's UI design gives CSMs flexibility to build their own dashboards and views without touching the admin layer. A CSM new to the platform can be productive inside a week; Gainsight typically requires structured onboarding.
- **Automation architecture.** Vitally's playbook system uses a single interface with 7 trigger types and 13 possible actions, covering CSM assignment, project creation, email delivery, and task completion with branching logic. Gainsight's split between Rules Engine and Journey Orchestrator achieves similar outcomes but requires twice the configuration surface. One Apollo CS-Ops manager reported reducing their Gainsight admin team from 8 to 1 equivalent after moving to Vitally.
- **Collaborative docs.** Vitally's native Docs feature lets CSMs build living documents alongside account data — QBR prep, success plans, meeting notes — with real-time customer data embedded. Gainsight requires third-party tools (Confluence, Google Docs, Notion) for the same workflows. For teams that want their account plans to live in the CS platform rather than scattered across external tools, Vitally's native docs remove a real operational seam.
- **Time to value.** Vitally's average go-live is approximately 2 months. Gainsight's realistic enterprise implementation runs 8-16 weeks and typically requires a dedicated internal owner plus ongoing certified admin support. For teams that cannot afford a 6-month runway before the platform is fully operational, Vitally is the more honest commitment.
- **All features on every plan.** Vitally's pricing model includes full feature access on every tier — no gated modules, no premium add-ons for core functionality. Gainsight's AI features (Horizon AI) are sold separately, and advanced playbook capabilities require higher tiers.

## Pricing reality

Vitally's pricing is engagement-model-based (Tech-Touch, Hybrid-Touch, High-Touch tiers) and quote-only, but publicly available market data puts mid-market deployments at $18,000-50,000/year for a 10-15 person CS team. Gainsight's typical mid-market range is $50,000-100,000/year before the PX bundle or Horizon AI; complex enterprise deployments with both modules regularly exceed $200,000 annually. The gap at comparable scope is roughly 2-3×. If you're considering the PX bundle specifically, the combined Gainsight contract is still typically cheaper than Gainsight CS + a separate product analytics tool, but that advantage only materializes if you're actually running an in-app engagement program — not if you're paying for PX as a hedge. Neither platform publishes list pricing.

## Implementation effort

Vitally: ~2 months average go-live, all features available on day one, one admin or part-time CS ops resource sufficient to maintain. Gainsight: 8-16 weeks for a real deployment, dedicated project owner during setup, certified admin required ongoing. The admin-ratio difference is concrete: the one reported ratio above (8:1 Gainsight admin equivalents to Vitally admin equivalents for comparable CS motions) is the outlier, but the directional claim — Gainsight costs more admin hours per CSM served — is consistent with G2 satisfaction data.

## Verdict

- **Pick Gainsight** if you need the PX bundle (in-app guides + CS health in one contract), have a dedicated CS-Ops admin, run 300+ accounts on Salesforce, and are building health models that pull from more than three distinct data sources.
- **Pick Vitally** if your priority is getting your whole CS team to actually use the platform daily, you want to remove third-party tools for QBR docs and success plans, and your CS-Ops team is lean (1-2 people or fewer).
- **Pick neither** if you're pre-CS-function — fewer than 15 accounts managed by non-dedicated CSMs. A Salesforce or HubSpot native view with a shared Notion workspace gets you further than either platform at that scale.

If you're choosing in a vacuum, pick Vitally. Gainsight's configurability advantage only converts into business outcomes when you have the admin capacity to configure it. Most teams buying Gainsight at the $60-100K tier end up using 30-40% of the platform's capability. Switch to Gainsight when PX becomes a genuine use case or when your CS-Ops team is large enough to maintain two separate automation systems.
