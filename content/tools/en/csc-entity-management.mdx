## What it is

CSC Entity Management is the [entity management](/en/learn/entity-management/) software sold by CSC, the company founded in Wilmington, Delaware in 1899 as Corporation Service Company. The software is the smaller half of the offer. CSC's real business is the service underneath it: registered-agent coverage in all 50 US states and DC, annual-report and state filings, and corporate-secretarial work in more than 140 jurisdictions, mostly with its own staff. CSC says it uses "no affiliates, no networked partners." That footprint came largely from the 2022 Intertrust acquisition. CSC is privately owned, employs more than 8,500 people, and says it serves 90% of the Fortune 500 and more than 75% of the PEI 300 private-equity firms.

The software keeps one record per legal entity: officers and directors, ownership and share data, DBAs and prior names, merger history, and an electronic minute book. Structure charts are generated from that record, and you can model pending ownership changes before they close. The point of buying it from CSC instead of a software-only vendor is that the record updates when CSC files something for you, so the system and the filing history do not drift apart.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **One vendor for the filing and the record.** When CSC is already the registered agent, entity data and governance documents sync from CSC's service work into the software. Every competitor without a filing arm needs a person or an integration to close that loop.
- **Global coverage under one contract.** 140+ jurisdictions, served by CSC's own offices, is the main thing Discern, Filejet and the US-only filing startups cannot match.
- **Compliance calendar across US and global deadlines.** Annual reports and key filings are tracked automatically. Discern's August 2026 review reports that the international side of the calendar is available only to clients who also buy CSC's Global Subsidiary Management service. Confirm that in your quote.
- **Workday, DocuSign and regulator e-filing.** A Workday integration pushes officer and director changes from HR data. DocuSign handles signature routing. Prebuilt connections file electronically to Companies House and to the SEC for Section 16 filings. CSC also advertises bi-directional APIs.
- **AI for the document pile, not for the decisions.** The shipped AI covers bulk document import, automatic routing of documents to the right entity and folder, and summaries of key content. That clears a minute-book backlog. It does not reason about compliance.

## Pricing reality

CSC publishes no prices for the software, the registered-agent service, or filings. Its own pricing line is "one flat fee, unlimited users," with training and support included. Unlimited users is a real advantage over per-seat tools when tax, treasury and HR all need read access.

Budget from the service side, because that is most of the bill. Third-party estimates put CSC's registered-agent fee at roughly $300-450 per state per year at low volume, falling to $200-300 per state for large portfolios. That puts a 50-state US footprint at about $15,000-25,000 a year before any software. Discern's comparison lists CSC at $350 or more per state on quote. Annual-report filings are billed as a service fee plus state fees, each quoted separately. Treat all of these as indicative. They come from competitors and aggregators, not from CSC.

For reference, Athennian publishes $25,000/year for 100 entities as software only, and Discern publishes $350 per state registration per year with the agent service and annual-report filing included.

## Best for

Corporate secretaries and legal ops leads at groups with 100+ entities spread across several countries, who already use CSC as their registered agent or corporate service provider and want the filing provider and the entity record to be the same system. It also fits PE and fund groups that need entities formed and maintained in many jurisdictions on short notice.

It is a weak pick for a US-only group of 10-50 entities that mainly needs annual reports filed on time. A flat-rate filing service does that job at a published price.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **CT Corporation (Wolters Kluwer)** is the other century-old incumbent. Discern's founder puts CT and CSC together at about 90% of the enterprise market. Pick CT when outside counsel already routes litigation service of process through it.
- **[Diligent Entities](/en/tools/diligent-entities/)** is the software-first volume incumbent. Pick it when board workflows already run on Diligent, or when you want an MCP connection for Claude or ChatGPT. CSC offers no MCP server.
- **[Athennian](/en/tools/athennian/)** is the stronger product when entity data is the primary job and you are happy to keep any registered agent. It has a published starting price and a larger review base.
- **[Discern](/en/tools/discern/)** is the fast-growing US challenger with flat per-state pricing. Pick it when your entities are all in the US.
- **DiliTrust** is the pick when you want entities, contracts and board work in one AI-led platform.

CSC's own 2026 General Counsel Barometer, a survey of 350 in-house leaders, found that 83% use more than one corporate service provider and only 7% call their global entities fully compliant. Splitting the software from the agent is normal. You do not have to buy the bundle to use CSC as an agent.

## Watch-outs

- **The review record is thin and mixed.** G2 shows 3.3 from 3 reviews and Capterra 3.0 from 2. One Capterra reviewer wrote in December 2023 that CSC "continually missed filing reports and licenses on time." Guard: ask for two reference customers with a similar entity count and jurisdiction mix, and write filing-timeliness commitments, with credits, into the service agreement.
- **Billing complexity across service lines.** Software, agent fees, filings and state fees arrive as separate charges. A June 2025 BBB complaint described invoices for companies that had already been dissolved. Guard: require a per-entity, per-service-line fee schedule in writing before signing, and reconcile billed entities against your register every quarter.
- **The sync that helps you also ties you in.** Records stay current because CSC does the filing. If you later move the agent work to CT or Discern, that update stream stops. Guard: get export terms for the full register, minute books and structure-chart data in the contract, and run a test export during evaluation.
- **No MCP server and only document-level AI.** Querying entity and officer data from an AI workspace means building on the API. Guard: check during the trial that the API exposes appointments and ownership, not just documents. Background: [MCP server explained](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).
