[Phenom](/en/tools/phenom/) and [Beamery](/en/tools/beamery/) used to split cleanly: Phenom owned the candidate, Beamery owned the sourcer and the skills data. 2026 blurred that line from both sides. Phenom bought Included (people analytics, January 14), Be Applied (assessments, February 10) and [Plum](/en/tools/plum/) (psychometric assessments, April 28), and now sells a Workforce Intelligence product with skills inference, gap views and a Workforce Planning Agent. Beamery, for its part, lists Career Sites & Chatbot inside its Execution Suite. Both vendors now claim both halves of the problem, so the old routing rule ("Phenom for candidates, Beamery for strategy, Phenom does not do skills") no longer holds.

What still separates them is where each is strongest and how much company stands behind it. Phenom is built to convert visitors and employees into applicants at volume, and is buying its way into assessment and analytics. Beamery is built to model work itself, down to the task, and to prove its AI is fair. Pick by the metric that is failing.

## Where Phenom wins

**Candidate conversion at volume.** Phenom's Intelligent Career Site, chatbot, personalized job matching, SMS messaging and automated interview scheduling run on one candidate record, and the same matching engine powers the internal Talent Marketplace. Its published customer results come from high-volume employers: Southwest Airlines reports $7M in annual savings and Varsity Brands 17,000 recruiter hours saved, both vendor-published. Beamery does list a career site and chatbot, but it sells them as one component among seven in its execution layer. Phenom's product line is designed around them.

**Screening and assessment in one contract.** Since the Be Applied and Plum purchases, Phenom pairs its AI Interviewer and Voice Screening Agent with in-platform cognitive, situational-judgement and behavioral assessments. Plum maps behavioral profiles against more than 40,000 jobs and is independently audited for adverse impact. Beamery has no assessment product; a Beamery buyer still contracts [SHL](/en/tools/shl/), [HireVue](/en/tools/hirevue/) or [TestGorilla](/en/tools/testgorilla/) separately.

**Internal mobility that employees actually use.** Phenom's Talent Marketplace is an employee-facing surface: personalized roles, gigs and career paths, plus Career Coach and Profile Builder agents. Beamery folded internal mobility into a redeployment use case inside its Workforce Intelligence Suite, which serves the planner rather than the employee. If the goal is employees browsing and applying for internal roles, Phenom is the product; if it is only that, [Gloat](/en/tools/gloat/) belongs on the shortlist too.

**ServiceNow-native hiring.** Phenom's hiring agents run inside the ServiceNow AI Platform, built on A2A and MCP and sold through the ServiceNow Store. For a [ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/)-standardized enterprise, hiring-manager intake, screening and interview scheduling happen without leaving ServiceNow. Beamery has no equivalent.

**A buyer that is investing.** Three acquisitions in fifteen weeks, Everest Group's highest-designated Leader in its 2026 Skills Intelligence Platforms PEAK Matrix (April 20, 2026), and a Visionary placement in Gartner's 2026 Magic Quadrant for Talent Acquisition Suites. That last one is not a Leader position, but the vendor is clearly expanding.

## Where Beamery wins

**Task-level workforce modeling.** Phenom now answers skills questions, so the old line that "Phenom does not" was wrong as of January. The gap that remains is one level down. Beamery's Task Intelligence breaks roles into tasks, scores each by frequency and effort, and estimates the time and cost saved by automating it before anything is built, then maps tasks back to skills and roles. That answers "which of these 40 roles is mostly automatable, and what do those people do next." Phenom Workforce Intelligence works at the skill and role level: coverage gaps, rising and fading skills, succession benches, flight risk. It does not publish a task-decomposition model. If the CHRO's question is automation exposure, Beamery answers it and Phenom does not yet.

**Auditable AI as a public artifact.** Beamery was the first HR-tech vendor to undergo a third-party bias audit, in 2022, and its matching model is now continuously audited by Warden AI with results on a live public page. Phenom's Plum assessments carry an adverse-impact audit, but Phenom does not publish an equivalent for its matching engine or AI Interviewer. Under [NYC Local Law 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/) the employer owns the bias audit either way, but a vendor dashboard you can hand to procurement or a works council shortens that conversation.

**Proactive sourcing for long-cycle roles.** Beamery's Talent CRM, Sourcing & Matching and Talent Marketing are built for nurture: deep segmentation, conditional campaigns and pipelines for executive, specialist-technical and campus hiring where the cycle runs six months or longer. Phenom's CRM is tuned to convert inbound traffic quickly. For a sourcer pool that needs to stay warm for a year, Beamery is the better tool.

**Lower disruption over an existing stack.** Beamery sits as a layer over Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Taleo, Greenhouse or SmartRecruiters, with a Workday-certified integration, and does not require you to replace your career site. Buying Phenom usually means rebuilding the career site, the most visible and slowest part of the deployment.

## Pricing reality

Neither vendor publishes prices. Vendr contract data puts the **Phenom median at $98,313 a year** (range $37,500 to $112,278) and the **Beamery median at $76,797 a year** (range $24,717 to $204,153). At the median Phenom costs about 1.3× Beamery. Both bands mostly describe single-module or two-module deals, not full platforms. Broker estimates for a full Phenom candidate-plus-employee deployment run $7 to $13 per employee per month, which is $420,000 to $780,000 a year at 5,000 employees. Beamery's top end, around $200,000, corresponds to the Data Platform plus Workforce Intelligence at global-enterprise scale.

Both vendors license modules line by line, so module mix decides the bill. Phenom's newly acquired assessments do not come free with the career site. Beamery's sales motion pushes toward the Data Platform, which leaves a CRM-only buyer paying for platform overhead. Phenom can offset part of its cost if you decommission a separate career-site vendor and scheduling tool. Beamery offsets nothing it replaces, because it replaces nothing.

## Implementation effort

Phenom's critical path is the career site rebuild: content migration, employer brand, ATS integration and SEO preservation. A single module such as scheduling or the chatbot can reach production well before the full site. The 2026 assessments are still being folded into the platform, so pilot them on one job family and ask for validation studies as they run inside Phenom, not as they ran on the standalone products.

Beamery deploys through partners (Accenture, Carahsoft, The Cloud Connectors and Version 1 are named), which means multi-quarter projects priced outside the license. The CRM comes up first. Skills and Task Intelligence need your skills vocabulary configured, your employee and candidate data loaded, and a TA team that changes from filling reqs to managing talent pools. Without that operating change, Beamery is an expensive CRM.

Vendor scale belongs in the effort estimate. Beamery has raised no priced round since its $1B Series D in December 2022, cut staff twice in 2023, and third-party trackers put headcount near 190. Phenom is acquiring. Neither fact is a reason to rule a vendor out, but for Beamery cap the first term at two years and sign a data-export clause with the license.

Neither ships an official MCP server. Phenom exposes a public REST API and MCP access through ServiceNow. Beamery exposes a REST API with a public Swagger spec. Confirm the objects your own agents need are readable before signing either.

## Verdict

**Pick Phenom** when the failing metric is conversion: career-site apply rates, time to schedule, high-volume or hourly hiring in retail, healthcare, hospitality or manufacturing, or an internal-mobility program employees should use themselves. Also pick Phenom if you want screening, assessment and scheduling under one contract, or if your company runs on ServiceNow.

**Pick Beamery** when your ATS and career site work and the question is workforce strategy at task level: which work automates, who can be redeployed, and what to hire for the gap, with an AI audit trail you can show a regulator. Also pick it for long-cycle proactive sourcing over Workday or SuccessFactors, where replacing the career site is off the table.

**Pick neither** if your ATS is the problem or the process breaks at the recruiter-to-hiring-manager handoff. Fix that with [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) or [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) first. Below roughly 2,000 employees, [Gem](/en/tools/gem/) for CRM and [SmartRecruiters](/en/tools/smartrecruiters/) for ATS and career site cover most of what either platform sells at a fraction of the contract. If the actual purchase is skills matching across a very large workforce, put [Eightfold](/en/tools/eightfold/) in the evaluation.

If you are choosing without any of the conditions above, pick Phenom. Most TA teams have a conversion problem before a workforce-strategy problem, Phenom's results show up in funnel metrics within a quarter, and since the Included purchase it covers enough skills analytics to serve a first workforce-planning conversation. Move to Beamery, or add it, when the question becomes task-level automation exposure. Before signing either, have both vendors demo the same skills-gap question on your data.
