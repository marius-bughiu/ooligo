## What you're leaving

The old reason to open this page was price. That still happens. But the bigger change is that the thing you'd be leaving is no longer the company you bought from.

Workday closed its acquisition of [Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/) on 1 October 2025, and the integration since has not been cosmetic. On 8 January 2026 the **Paradox Conversational ATS** launched through Workday: Olivia is no longer a chat layer you bolt onto whatever applicant tracking system you already run — it is the applicant tracking system. Candidate Experience Agent, Conversational Career Sites, Conversational Apply, Conversational Scheduling, Conversational CRM, and Conversational Events all sit under that line now. Workday's own numbers for the launch: a 72% average application completion rate, an average time-to-hire of three and a half days, and a 95% candidate satisfaction rating in 2025, with 7-Eleven, Ace Hardware, and Valvoline named as references.

The signal worth weighing is on the front page rather than in the press release. Paradox's homepage now names three integrations — [Workday](/en/tools/workday/), SAP SuccessFactors, and Indeed. iCIMS, Oracle HCM, and Greenhouse, all of which mattered when Paradox sold itself as ATS-agnostic, are not among them. Nothing has been announced as discontinued and existing connections still run, so do not read this as a sunset. Read it as where the roadmap points.

That reframes the question. It is not "is Olivia still good at hourly screening" — it is, and none of the three below beats it head-to-head at top-of-funnel conversational screening. The question is whether you are comfortable with your frontline hiring layer being a Workday product line. If you run Workday HCM, the answer just got better. If you run iCIMS or SuccessFactors and had a five-year horizon on Paradox, it got worse.

## Sense

The nearest peer on conversational engagement, and the only vendor here that publishes prices. That alone changes the evaluation: you can build the comparison before the first sales call.

[Sense](/en/tools/sense/) lists the Sense Platform for Talent Acquisition from $7,000 per month, the staffing-side platforms from $2,000 per month, **Sense AI Recruiter** — its agent roster, packaged as its own SKU — from $3,000 per month, and SMS/WhatsApp Messaging and Interview Scheduling from $500 per month each. Every package includes a dedicated customer success manager, an implementation manager, three months of hypercare, and 24/7 support. Third-party procurement trackers put Paradox contracts somewhere between roughly $25K and $150K+ per year for mid-to-large deployments — vendor-unconfirmed, since Paradox publishes nothing — which means Sense's corporate TA floor at $84K/year lands mid-band, while a two-module entry at $12K/year lands roughly an order of magnitude below it.

Where it pulls ahead of Paradox is the record, not the chat. Sense keeps CRM-style candidate history, runs multi-channel nurture across SMS, WhatsApp, email, and voice, and has an actual redeployment motion for re-engaging past placements. Paradox treats the conversation as the unit; Sense treats the candidate as the unit. For staffing firms and anyone whose real asset is a database of people they have already placed, that difference compounds.

**Watch-out:** the $7,000/month platform figure is a floor, and modules price separately on top of it. **Guard:** price your exact module list — Messaging plus Scheduling plus AI Recruiter is $4,000/month before the platform line — and get the platform floor confirmed in writing before you set it against your Paradox renewal.

## Fountain

The independent option, and the one that has moved fastest. [Fountain](/en/tools/fountain/) was already built for the segment Paradox owns — hourly, frontline, and gig hiring, where turnover is measured per quarter rather than per year — and it has spent 2026 rebuilding around agents rather than adding them at the edges.

In January 2026 it shipped Pool and Source for talent rediscovery and sourcing. On 14 April 2026 it launched **Cue**, which it positions as autonomous frontline operations: building and updating hiring workflows from performance data, sourcing and screening under oversight, detecting and filling shift gaps, and flagging underperforming locations. Its launch metric — 12,847 applications processed and 847 roles filled in 14 hours — is a throughput claim, so treat it as a scale demonstration rather than a quality one. Alongside Cue sit three named agents: Anna for screening, Emma for pre-start support, Sam for satisfaction. The module set spans Source, CRM, ATS, Onboarding, and Shift & Scheduling; the company reports 500+ frontline employers, with Bojangles, LSG Sky Chefs, CLEAR, Stitch Fix, and Fetch named publicly.

Fountain is the right shape for the reader who liked Paradox's segment focus and specifically does not want a Workday product. It also reaches past hiring into shift scheduling, which matters if your actual problem is that a location is short three people on Saturday, not that applications convert badly.

**Watch-out:** Fountain quote-gates pricing, and third-party estimates describe an annual platform fee in the low-to-mid five figures plus a per-hire component — a bill that grows with hiring volume, which is the opposite of how your Paradox seat renewal behaves. **Guard:** model it at your peak seasonal month rather than your annual average, and negotiate a cap or a volume band on the per-hire line before signing.

## Phenom

The enterprise answer, and the one to shortlist when the conversational layer is only part of what you need. [Phenom](/en/tools/phenom/) sits above the ATS rather than replacing it, owning career site, application flow, candidate portal, and internal mobility, with X+ Agents across all of them. Its Voice Screening Agent runs live phone and desktop screens around the clock and adapts by role type — the closest thing here to what Olivia does, arriving from the opposite direction. Its 2026 releases extended the frontline lifecycle into shift scheduling, internal mobility, referrals, and alumni engagement.

Pick Phenom when you are a large employer running both corporate and frontline hiring and you want one experience layer over both, keeping your existing ATS. Do not pick it when high-volume hourly screening is the entire job — you would be buying a platform to use one part of it, and Paradox or Fountain will do that part better for less.

**Watch-out:** Phenom prices by employee count, per-user license, or volume, and bills a flat annual fee for each third-party integration. **Guard:** enumerate every system you will connect — HCM, payroll, assessment, background check, scheduling — before you take the quote, because that list is what moves the number.

## What's not on this list

[Gem](/en/tools/gem/) was named as an alternative on the previous version of this page, and it should not have been. Gem is a professional-sourcing Talent CRM; it publishes only a startup price ($130/month billed annually for 1-10 FTEs) and quotes everything above that on headcount. It is a fine tool and the wrong shape — if you adopted Paradox for corporate sourcing, you bought the wrong product, and Gem is the fix for that mistake rather than a migration path off frontline hiring.

[Harver](/en/tools/harver/) is likewise absent by design: it is assessment for high-volume hiring, which sits next to the conversational layer rather than replacing it.

## Match rules

- **Switch to Sense** when your asset is a candidate database rather than a job board funnel, you run redeployment or multi-channel nurture, or you need to build a defensible cost comparison before the first call. Its published prices are the only ones here you can model without sales.
- **Switch to Fountain** when your motion is squarely hourly and frontline, you want an independent vendor, or your real constraint is shift coverage rather than application volume.
- **Switch to Phenom** when you run corporate and frontline hiring together, want one candidate-facing layer over both, and intend to keep your current [ATS](/en/learn/ats-vs-recruiting-crm/).
- **If you cannot separate these conditions, default to Fountain.** It matches Paradox's segment most closely, it is the only one of the three whose independence is not in question, and switching later costs less than the alternative of discovering in year three that you standardised on a suite you do not otherwise run.

## When staying on Paradox is right

Stay when you run Workday HCM — the acquisition made your integration story better, not worse, and buying the Conversational ATS through Workday is now the shortest path. Stay when top-of-funnel hourly screening is the load-bearing capability and nothing else about the stack is broken; Olivia is still the strongest conversational experience in that segment, and none of the three above changes that. Stay when your ATS-specific event wiring is deep — every renewal cycle that passes makes that harder to unpick, not easier.

The one bad reason to leave: the chat scripts feel stale. Rewrite the scripts. The platform is not the problem.

## Migration cost

This is the part readers underestimate, and the acquisition made it worse rather than better. Conversational scripts do not port between vendors — the flows, the knockout logic, and the tone are rebuilt from scratch, which for a multi-brand employer is weeks of work before the first candidate sees anything. Your ATS event wiring is a second rebuild. Career-site conversion is a third if you run Paradox's Conversational Career Sites, because the surface candidates apply through is the thing being replaced.

Budget two quarters for a multi-location deployment and run the two systems in parallel for at least one hiring peak. Any vendor telling you it is a six-week cutover is quoting the integration, not the migration. Run this through [vendor due diligence](/en/learn/vendor-due-diligence-workflow/) with the ownership question stated explicitly — for Paradox the roadmap owner is now Workday, and that belongs in the evaluation whether you stay or go.
