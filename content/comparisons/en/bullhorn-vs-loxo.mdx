[Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) and [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) both sell an ATS plus a recruiting CRM to agencies, and in 2026 both rebuilt themselves around AI agents. The difference is what each one assumes your firm does all day. Bullhorn assumes you place contractors and temps with enterprise clients and then have to track their time, bill for them, and stay compliant: its agents (Amplify Digital Workers) now reach into the back office, with skills that audit pay and pull timesheet and credentialing data. Loxo assumes your bottleneck is finding people: it bundles its own 850M+ profile graph with an agent workforce that writes the job, builds the longlist, ranks the shortlist, and runs outreach.

So the split is not legacy versus modern any more. **Bullhorn is the system for firms that place and then manage workers. Loxo is the system for firms whose revenue depends on sourcing speed.** Contract and temp desks belong on the first. Direct-hire and executive search desks lean toward the second.

## Where Bullhorn wins

**VMS and back office.** Bullhorn's VMS Automation add-on connects to more than 110 vendor management systems and imports job records and pushes submittals in real time. Bullhorn One and the separately sold Middle Office add timesheets, time interpretation, invoicing, and pay-and-bill automation. If your margin comes from contingent-workforce programmes at large clients, this is the reason to choose Bullhorn, and Loxo has nothing equivalent. Loxo is a front-office platform. It does not run payroll, timesheets, or invoicing.

**Agents that touch money and compliance.** Bullhorn's Amplify page lists ten digital-worker skills, and five of them are new since Engage 2026 (May 28). Three matter for staffing. Audit catches pay and compliance errors. Verify flags potentially fraudulent candidate records. Extract pulls timesheet and credentialing data out of documents. Loxo's agents stop at the placement. For a contract desk, the errors that cost money happen after the placement, so Bullhorn has the stronger AI where it counts for that model.

**Ecosystem and scale.** Bullhorn lists 300+ pre-integrated marketplace partners (Core tier and up), covering background checks, payroll, onboarding, and assessments. It also runs a second product line, Bullhorn Recruitment Cloud, on Salesforce for firms that have standardised on that platform. Salesforce named Bullhorn a 2027 Partner of the Year in September 2026. For a 50+ recruiter firm with a bespoke stack, breadth of integration beats elegance of any single feature.

**The cheapest published entry seat.** Starter is $99 per user per month and Core is $165, both with free implementation. If all you need today is a staffing-shaped ATS with client and placement objects, Bullhorn is the lowest listed price of the two.

## Where Loxo wins

**Sourcing is in the seat.** Loxo Source searches an 850M+ professional graph together with your own database in one query, and Professional seats return contact information with shortlists when it is available. A Bullhorn firm doing the same work usually signs a second contract with [hireEZ](/en/tools/hireez/), [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/), or [Lusha](/en/tools/lusha/), and then reconciles two candidate sets. For a direct-hire desk, removing that contract is the main saving.

**The agent workforce is built for search work.** Loxo's Professional plan puts every agent on every seat. Job Intake and Write JD turn an intake call into a spec. Longlist screens up to 10,000 candidates in a full-market search. Shortlist ranks them across seven dimensions. Outreach + Pursuit runs the sequence. Data Agents keep records current. Every action is logged in an Agent Impact Center, and that log is what you need when a candidate asks who contacted them. Bullhorn's equivalent agentic features sit on Max, a quote-only tier that does not include free implementation.

**MCP on the entry tier.** Loxo lists its MCP connector on Core, so recruiters can drive Loxo from Claude or another MCP client without buying the top plan. Bullhorn publishes developer and API documentation but does not mention an MCP server on its pricing or Amplify pages as of 24 November 2026.

**A real trial.** Loxo offers a 7-day trial of the full Professional platform with no credit card. Bullhorn offers demos and quotes, not a trial. You can test Loxo on a live requisition before you sign. You can only watch Bullhorn being demonstrated.

## Pricing reality

Both vendors now publish an entry price and hide the price of AI.

**Bullhorn** (bullhorn.com/pricing, checked 24 November 2026): Starter $99 and Core $165 per user per month, with free implementation, and no AI on either. Pro is quote-only and is the first tier with Amplify (AI search and match, Amplify Chat, enrichment, transcription). Max is quote-only, adds the agentic Digital Workers, and does not list free implementation. Larger agencies buy Front Office Growth, Front Office Enterprise, or Bullhorn One, all by quote. VMS Automation, Middle Office, Onboarding, Messaging, and Analytics are separate add-ons. As recorded on our Bullhorn entry, third-party TCO trackers put a ten-seat first year at $34,000–$46,100, with renewal uplift reported near 20% a year.

**Loxo** (loxo.co/pricing, checked 24 November 2026): Core starts at $149 per user per month, billed annually and paid up front. It includes the ATS, CRM, AI search, and MCP, but no Loxo Source, agents, credits, or API. Professional no longer shows a price: in September 2026 the page listed it at $199, and today the card reads "Book a demo to see pricing". It includes 2,500 credits per seat per month, pooled and reset monthly. Boost tiers (5,000 or 12,500 credits per seat) are quoted. Enterprise is custom and is the only tier with SSO.

The quantified comparison is the entry seat. Bullhorn Starter costs $99 against Loxo Core at $149, so Loxo is about 1.5× the price. Bullhorn Core costs $165, about 1.1× Loxo Core. At ten seats, that is $11,880 a year for Bullhorn Starter, $17,880 for Loxo Core, and $19,800 for Bullhorn Core. Above the entry tiers, neither vendor gives you a number, so ask both for written year-one and year-two prices on the tier you will actually run. For Bullhorn that means Pro or Max plus any add-ons. For Loxo it means Professional plus Boost. Loxo Professional was $199 at list in September, so treat anything near that as the floor rather than the expected price.

## Implementation effort

Bullhorn implementations at agencies with more than 20 recruiters run in months, not weeks. The work is data migration from the legacy ATS, workflow configuration, marketplace integrations, and, for contract desks, the VMS and back-office connections. Starter, Core, and Pro include implementation. Max and the larger-agency tiers scope it into the quote. Budget recruiter training separately, because the interface is the most common complaint in user reviews.

Loxo is a weeks-scale rollout. Core includes free migration for teams of three or more seats, and Professional adds agent setup. The real effort is calibration. Credits cover agent runs, outreach, and AI Chat, so run one full-market search on a live role during the trial and count the credits it consumes before you size seats. Loxo's own pricing page says its capacity figures are "figurative".

## Verdict

**Pick Bullhorn** if contract or temp placements make up a meaningful share of revenue, if you work enterprise clients through a VMS, or if pay-and-bill and compliance errors cost you real money. Also pick it if you are a 50+ recruiter firm that needs a deep integration ecosystem or a Salesforce-native option. Price Max only after Pro's AI has proven useful on your desks.

**Pick Loxo** if you run direct hire, retained, or executive search, your bottleneck is sourcing throughput, and you would otherwise pay for a separate sourcing or contact-data tool. The right range is roughly 2–50 recruiters. Buy Professional, not Core, because the agents and Loxo Source are the reason to choose Loxo.

**Pick neither** if you are a solo recruiter or a two-person shop without a growth plan. [Manatal](/en/tools/manatal/) starts at $15 per user per month, and [Crelate](/en/tools/crelate/) at $85, and either covers the ATS and CRM job. If you want an AI-led agency CRM focused on client business development rather than sourcing, look at [Recruiterflow](/en/tools/recruiterflow/) instead.

If none of the conditions above separates them, pick Loxo. A mixed or direct-hire agency gets more from built-in sourcing and agents than from back-office depth it does not use yet, and the 7-day trial lets you test that claim before paying. Move to Bullhorn when contract volume and VMS clients make pay-and-bill the binding constraint.
