## What it is

SourceWhale is a recruitment-agency outreach platform that has grown into a full recruiter workspace. It started as the sequencing layer agencies bolted onto [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) or [JobAdder](/en/tools/jobadder/), and today the module list reads Sourcing, Data, Outreach, Inbox Agent, Dialer, Notetaker, Data Refresh and CRM & ATS. The CRM & ATS module launched on 10 November 2025 under the name "Projects," which is what older reviews still call it. SourceWhale Ltd was founded in 2019 (CB Insights), lists offices in London and New York, and claims "thousands of recruitment agencies" as customers, with Harnham, Morson, Impellam Group and Daversa among the logos on its site.

The question it answers is narrow and common: an agency already has a system of record, and recruiters are still copying candidates into a separate email tool, logging calls by hand and chasing clients from their own inbox. SourceWhale runs candidate and client outreach from the recruiter's own mailbox, records and transcribes calls, and writes all of it back to the ATS.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Write-back is the product.** The integrations page lists two-way connectors for more than 60 CRMs and ATSs, including Bullhorn, JobAdder, [Vincere](/en/tools/vincere/), [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/), [Crelate](/en/tools/crelate/), [Recruiterflow](/en/tools/recruiterflow/), [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) and [Lever](/en/tools/lever/), plus about 30 contact-data providers ([Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/), [Cognism](/en/tools/cognism/), [Lusha](/en/tools/lusha/), [ContactOut](/en/tools/contactout/) among them) chained as a waterfall. Agencies buy it because the ATS stays the record and the outreach history still lands in it.
- **Client outreach sits next to candidate outreach.** Agency recruiters sell both sides of the placement. The same sequences, dialer and notetaker cover business development, which is the main thing in-house sourcing tools leave out.
- **Calls become records.** The Dialer records, transcribes, summarises and logs every call to the CRM. For a 360 desk, that is usually the biggest admin cost.
- **A first-party MCP server, launched 1 October 2026.** It exposes candidates, clients, roles, calls, meetings, notes, resumes and full call transcripts to [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) and other MCP clients, and it can take supported actions back in SourceWhale. Each user signs in with their own account and existing permissions apply. The page does not publish a tool list or say whether MCP costs extra. StackOne also sells a separate, third-party SourceWhale MCP with six actions. Don't confuse the two.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price list, and the pricing URL opens a demo-booking form. Third-party figures roughly agree: SoftwareFinder lists **$200-$290 per user per month**, HeroHunt reports roughly **$2,500-$3,500 per user per year**, and Vendr shows an average contract value of **$11,604** across 5 deals. Read that as about $3,000 per recruiter per year for the outreach-plus-dialer core. Ten recruiters come to around $30,000 a year before data credits and the CRM module, so price both into the quote. SourceWhale's own homepage claims £3k saved per recruiter per year from replacing up to 10 tools. That is a vendor number, but it shows the pitch: consolidation, not a cheaper sequencer.

## Best for

Agency owners and ops leads at 10-200 recruiter contingent or retained firms who sell to clients as well as candidates, keep Bullhorn, JobAdder or Vincere as the system of record, and lose recruiter hours to calls, notes and copy-paste logging. It is the strongest pick when the ATS stays and everything around it gets consolidated.

Skip it for in-house TA. Gem or Pin fit an employer's funnel better. Skip it for solo recruiters on a budget: a seat at roughly $250 a month is hard to justify when your ATS already sequences.

## Do you still need it if your ATS has sequences?

Run the test before the demo. If your ATS's native automation already sends from recruiters' own mailboxes, logs replies, and you don't need recorded call transcripts or client BD sequences, you probably don't. Loxo bundles sourcing, outreach and an ATS from $149 per seat, and Bullhorn sells its own automation layer. SourceWhale earns its seat when calls, client outreach and multi-provider data enrichment all need to flow into one record. Native sequences rarely cover all three.

## Versus the alternatives

[Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) is the consolidation rival. Pick Loxo when you are willing to replace the ATS too and want one published seat price. Pick SourceWhale when the ATS migration is the thing you won't do.

[Gem](/en/tools/gem/) is the market-share leader in recruiting CRM, but its model is in-house TA with pipeline reporting across a large team. Pick Gem for an employer; pick SourceWhale for an agency selling to clients.

[Pin](/en/tools/pin/) is the fastest-growing entrant, with published prices from $99 per user and an agent that sources, ranks and sends. Pick Pin when finding candidates is the bottleneck and you have no client BD motion. Pick SourceWhale when the bottleneck is everything recruiters do after the candidate is found.

If none fit because the real problem is your ATS data quality, fix the record first. Every tool here writes into it, and enriching a broken database just spreads the mess further.

## Watch-outs

- **Quote-only pricing with wide spread.** Third-party bands run from about $200 to $290 per user per month, and module bundling moves the number further. *Guard:* get a written per-module quote for your seat count, including data credits and CRM & ATS, and compare it against your current sequencer, dialer and notetaker spend line by line.
- **Channel coverage is narrower than older reviews suggest.** The current site names email, SMS and WhatsApp from your own mailbox. LinkedIn steps are not on the module list, and LinkedIn restricts automation in its terms. *Guard:* ask in the demo exactly which LinkedIn actions are supported and how they are executed. Don't plan a sequence around one you haven't seen running.
- **Recorded calls carry consent obligations.** Call recording and transcription across the UK, EU and US states triggers notice and consent rules under GDPR and two-party-consent laws. *Guard:* confirm the dialer's consent prompt and retention settings with your DPO before rollout. Note that SourceWhale displays SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001 badges, and pull the reports from its trust centre.
- **MCP widens who can read transcripts.** Anything a recruiter can see, their AI client can now query. *Guard:* audit role permissions in SourceWhale before enabling MCP, since the server inherits them rather than adding its own scopes.
- **Vendor ROI numbers are self-reported.** The "17 hours back per recruiter per week" claim has no published method. *Guard:* run a 30-day pilot on one desk and measure calls logged, replies and submittals against a matched desk without it.
