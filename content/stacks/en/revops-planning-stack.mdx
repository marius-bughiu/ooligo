The annual GTM plan is one chain with four links: carve the territories, set the quotas, fund the comp plan, commit to a forecast. Most companies run it as four projects, owned by four people, in four systems. The seams are where the money leaks — a rep paid on a territory they no longer own, a quota table that sums to 118% of the board number, a forecast rolled up against targets nobody re-synced after the March re-carve.

This stack closes the seams. Salesforce holds the transaction record. Fullcast holds the policy — territory, quota, capacity, routing. CaptivateIQ turns attainment into a payable, explainable number. Clari Forecast holds the commitment and measures it against the quota Fullcast published. Four systems, one roster, one set of quota records.

Three things changed in 2026 that this page now reflects. Clari stopped being a company: on September 2, 2026 the merged Clari + Salesloft business took the single name Salesloft, and Clari Forecast is the only product that kept the Clari name. Fullcast, now at fullcast.ai, sells its own forecasting product, so it overlaps the commitment layer as well as the pay layer. And Salesforce's top Sales Cloud edition now bundles its own commission and planning tools. Every boundary in this stack is contestable. Do not buy it because the diagram looks tidy.

## How the pieces fit

- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) is the system of record for the transaction.** Accounts, opportunities, closed-won, product line items. Every other tool here reads from it and writes back to it. It is not the planning system — native territory management holds a static hierarchy, not continuous policy — but it is the arbiter of what actually closed, and every number downstream traces to an opportunity ID living here.

- **[Fullcast](/en/tools/fullcast/) is the policy layer.** Fullcast Plan puts territory design, quota assignment, headcount and capacity modeling, and lead and account routing in one object model. The distinction that earns its place: rules evaluate continuously and write assignments through to Salesforce, so an account created in July lands in the right book on creation rather than at the next carve. Its roster and territory sync keeps assignments current when a rep transfers or leaves. Fullcast's MCP server, live since August 2025, exposes territory, routing, targets and compensation data to Claude and other AI clients.

- **[CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/) is the calculation and explanation layer.** Closed-won lands from Salesforce, quota from Fullcast, roster from Workday, BambooHR or ADP. The SmartGrid engine runs the plan logic and produces the two outputs that matter: a payable number finance can sign, and a statement the rep can interrogate line by line. The second one prevents the dispute cycle. The Comp Builder Agent, generally available since October 2026, drafts plan changes inside the same model, which shortens the cycle CaptivateIQ's own 2026 survey puts at one to two months for 39% of organizations.

- **[Clari Forecast](/en/tools/clari/) is the commitment layer.** Rep commits roll to manager, manager to CRO, and the rollup is measured against the quota Fullcast published rather than a spreadsheet copy of it. It is now sold and supported by Salesloft: `clari.com` redirects to `salesloft.com`, Inspect carries the Salesloft name, and from inside Forecast a manager can create a Salesloft task or AI follow-up email on the deals carrying a gap.

### Named handoffs

1. **Carve approved → assignment live.** Territory rules published in Fullcast → account owner and territory fields written to Salesforce on record create, not at the next batch job.
2. **Quota published → two downstream reads.** Fullcast quota records → CaptivateIQ as the attainment denominator, and → Clari Forecast as the target the rollup is measured against. One publish, two consumers, no re-keying.
3. **Roster change → credit change.** HRIS termination or transfer → Fullcast roster and territory sync → CaptivateIQ payee seat and crediting. This is the handoff that breaks first when the stack is assembled loosely, and it is the one that produces overpayments.
4. **Closed-won → payable.** Salesforce opportunity closes → CaptivateIQ credits the payee per plan → statement available to the rep before the payout run, not after the dispute.
5. **Commit gap → coverage action.** Clari Forecast shows a segment committing under quota → because that quota traces to a Fullcast territory, the remediation is a modeled coverage or capacity change instead of a spreadsheet argument.

## The overlaps you have to resolve first

Every vendor in this stack now sells at least one other vendor's job.

- **Fullcast sells all three layers.** Its platform is three suites — Plan, Revenue Intelligence (forecasting, built on the Ebsta acquisition of August 2025) and Pay (commissions, built on Commissionly, upgraded on May 5, 2026). It has also absorbed Copy.ai and, on September 4, 2026, AskElephant's conversation intelligence.
- **CaptivateIQ sells planning.** CaptivateIQ Planning covers territories and quotas, and its Rev Planning Agent reached general availability in October 2026 alongside the Comp Builder Agent.
- **Salesforce sells commissions and planning.** The Max edition bundles Salesforce Spiff and Sales Planning into the CRM contract.

Buying overlapping products without a written boundary produces two quota tables, two rosters, two forecasts, and a quarterly argument about which one finance trusts. The boundary that holds:

- **Fullcast owns the plan and the roster.** Territory, quota, capacity, routing, and who sits on which team as of which date. It is the upstream publisher.
- **CaptivateIQ owns the calculation and the payee-facing artifact.** Plan logic, crediting mechanics, statements, ASC 606 reporting, and the audit trail finance signs.
- **Clari Forecast owns the commitment.** Categories, submission history and the rollup against quota.
- **Neither owns the transaction.** Salesforce does.

Write that down before any contract is signed, and enforce it by making quota records read-only in CaptivateIQ and Clari Forecast.

**If you cannot defend four vendors, run fewer.** The honest default for most companies is one vendor across plan and pay. Run Fullcast and CaptivateIQ together only when comp plan complexity and territory policy churn are *both* binding constraints — multi-component plans with split and omni-role crediting, plus a carve that moves more than twice a year. That pairing is a mid-market-and-up problem, not a Series B problem.

## Cost reality

Salesforce is the only published number in the stack. Sales Cloud lists at $25 (Starter Suite), $100 (Pro Suite), $195 (Core), $395 (Advanced) and $550 (Max) per user per month billed annually; Core, Advanced and Max replaced Enterprise and Unlimited for new contracts in September 2026. A 150-seat org on Core is $351K/year list. Existing customers keep legacy Enterprise pricing ($175) until they change editions, so check which one your renewal is quoted on. It is already in the budget before this project starts, so treat it as the platform you are extending rather than a line this stack adds.

The three planning layers are all custom-quoted:

- **Fullcast** prices each suite on its own meter: Plan on users, Pay on payee count and plan complexity, routing on volume and policy complexity. Vendr's marketplace data puts the median buyer at $49,960/year across 11 purchases, in a $31,900-$75,000 range; its buyer guide reports an $84,264 average across 8 deals with outliers up to about $250,000. Both samples are small, so read the median rather than the average.
- **CaptivateIQ** prices per seat, where a seat is every admin plus every payee, plus a one-time setup fee. ASC 606 reporting is an add-on subscription. Vendr reports a median annual contract of $37,542 across 312 purchases, a $12,090-$100,278 range, and average savings of 22% off the opening quote.
- **Clari Forecast** is quoted through Salesloft, whose pricing page is a "Talk to Sales" form. Docket estimated it at about $100-$120 per user per month on annual billing in April 2026, and Vendr's data across 291 Clari purchases puts the median annual contract near $76,000, in a $19,065-$415,001 range. Expect the account team to price Salesloft's engagement product against it.

For a 150-payee org the three layers land at roughly **$160K-$200K/year** using the median and average anchors above, on top of whatever Salesforce already costs. That band assumes a Clari Forecast contract near the Vendr median, which buys seats for managers and forecasters rather than every payee. If all 150 payees take a Clari Forecast seat at Docket's per-user rate, the Clari line alone is $180K-$216K and the three layers land at roughly $265K-$340K. Both ends of the ranges are live: a lean configuration near the floors reaches about $65K, an intricate one clears $450K.

Implementation is the cost nobody budgets. A first-year carve migration plus comp plan build is a 10-16 week project, and the roster-sync handoff alone earns a named integration owner for the duration.

## Common variations

- **Drop CaptivateIQ; run Fullcast Pay.** One vendor, one roster, no boundary document to maintain. The swap rule: take it when plans are single- or two-component and dispute volume is low. Fullcast Pay's decisive case is revenue that pays out long after booking — payouts release as products ship, including shipments up to 24 months after the booking — so hardware, milestone-billed and usage-billed businesses should evaluate it head-on rather than assuming the compensation specialist wins by default.

- **Drop Clari Forecast; forecast in Fullcast.** Take it when Fullcast already owns plan and pay and you want the commit measured in the same system that published the quota. What you give up is Clari Forecast's longer track record and submission-history depth; what you avoid is a Salesloft contract when Salesloft is not your sequencer.

- **Drop Fullcast; run CaptivateIQ Planning plus native Salesforce territory management.** Take it when the carve is stable (annual, geographic or named-account) and comp complexity is the hard half of the problem. What you give up is continuous routing, which native Salesforce territory management does not do — assignment becomes a batch event again.

- **Collapse into Salesforce Max.** Spiff and Sales Planning ride the CRM contract at $550 per user per month. Take it when you are already pricing Max for Agentforce and plans are simple enough for Spiff. Run the math per seat: Max costs $355 per user per month more than Core, which for 150 users is about $639K/year, far above the specialist layers it replaces unless you also need the rest of the edition.

- **Enterprise substitution: [Anaplan](/en/tools/anaplan/) or [Varicent](/en/tools/varicent/).** At $1B+ scale where FP&A already owns a connected-planning platform and the comp team reports into finance, quota and comp consolidate there instead of arriving as two more vendors. The rule: evaluate the incumbent planning platform before signing either specialist.

## What this stack does NOT replace

- **The plan itself.** Segmentation, coverage model, and the board number are decisions. These tools execute a strategy and measure it; they do not choose one. See [territory design](/en/learn/territory-design/) and [quota coverage](/en/learn/quota-coverage/).
- **FP&A's headcount and budget model.** Fullcast models selling capacity; it does not own the P&L, and [capacity planning](/en/learn/capacity-planning/) still starts with a number finance sets.
- **The HRIS.** Workday, BambooHR or ADP stays the roster system of record. Fullcast syncs it, CaptivateIQ consumes it, neither replaces it.
- **Data hygiene in Salesforce.** Territory rules evaluate the fields you actually have; a null industry or employee-count field routes an account to the wrong book silently and no dashboard flags it.
- **Legal review of the comp plan.** The statement CaptivateIQ generates is not the contract the rep signed, and a plan drafted by the Comp Builder Agent still needs the same review.
- **Sales methodology and enablement.** Nothing here makes a forecast honest. It makes one measurable, which is a different thing.

## Match rules

**Use this stack when:**

- You pay 100+ commission-eligible people and the carve changes more than once a year. Both halves matter — headcount alone justifies compensation software, not a planning layer.
- A payout correction or quota dispute has already cost you a quarter of RevOps time. That is the trigger event to buy on.
- Salesforce is the CRM and will stay the CRM. Fullcast's continuous write-back is the assumption the whole chain rests on.
- Someone owns the boundaries between plan, pay and forecast. A stack with overlapping vendors and no named owner regresses to spreadsheets within two quarters.

**Do not use this stack when:**

- You are under $20M ARR with one sales team and an annual carve. The planning layer costs more than the error it prevents; run [compensation plans](/en/learn/compensation-plans/) off a maintained model instead.
- HubSpot is your CRM. The vendors support it, but the depth argument here — continuous write-back, custom object modeling, opportunity-level crediting — is a Salesforce argument.
- Comp is flat-rate commission with no accelerators, tiers or split credit. CaptivateIQ's calculation depth goes unused and Fullcast Pay covers the case.
- The roster handoff is unowned. Buying the stack before HRIS-to-roster sync has an owner reproduces the overpayment problem at higher cost, with more systems to reconcile.
