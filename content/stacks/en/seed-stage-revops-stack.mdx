The cheapest RevOps stack published on ooligo is [gtm-engineering-stack](/en/stacks/gtm-engineering-stack/), and it states **~$40K-$65K per year** in its own cost section. For a 25-person company that is not a line item, it is the entire GTM tooling budget plus the marketing budget. So the first RevOps hire at a seed or Series-A company reads every stack page, finds nothing copyable, and rebuilds the same five workflows from scratch.

This is that stack, priced for the company that actually exists: **20 people, three sellers, roughly $470 a month.**

The shape: [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) Sales Hub Starter is the system of record, [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) runs outbound and pays for the contact data, [Fathom](/en/tools/fathom/) captures every call, [n8n](/en/tools/n8n/) connects the four, and [Slack](/en/tools/slack/) is where the output gets seen. Four of the five have a free tier that does real work. One of them has an AI story you should ignore.

## How the pieces fit

- **HubSpot Starter buys the object model, not the AI.** List price is **$20/seat/month**, currently discounted to **$7** for new customers on a limited-time 65%-off promotion — budget the $20, because the promo is the surprise on renewal, not the savings. Sales Hub Free caps you at 2 users, which is why this stack starts at Starter. Starter includes **500 HubSpot Credits a month**, and three properties of those credits decide the argument: they expire monthly, they do not roll over, and they are **not additive across hubs** — a multi-product customer gets the single highest allotment, not the sum. HubSpot's Prospecting Agent burns **100 credits per recommended outreach for one lead**. That is five leads a month before you are buying credits at **$9 per 1,000** on annual billing. Do the prospecting in Apollo and let HubSpot be the database.

- **Apollo is the outbound engine and the data budget on one invoice.** Basic is **$49/seat/month** annual ($69 month-to-month), Professional **$79** ($99). Apollo has collapsed its old separate email, mobile, and export buckets into one credit: Basic grants **30,000 credits per seat per year** upfront, Professional 48,000. An email address costs 1 credit, a phone number 8, and the US dialer meters at **2 credits per minute**. Credits do not roll over. Basic gives one mailbox per user and **250 sends per day**, which at three sellers is a deliverability guardrail rather than a ceiling.

- **Fathom's free tier is unusually load-bearing, and one cap decides your tier.** Team is **$15/user/month** annual with a 2-user minimum — cheaper than the individual Premium SKU at $16, which is worth a second read before you buy the wrong one. Free gives unlimited recording and transcription, with advanced summaries capped at the **first 5 calls each month**. The constraint that matters: **CRM sync is limited to 3 users per email domain on Free, Premium, and Team alike**, lifting only at Business (**$25/user/month** annual). Writing summaries into named CRM fields is Business-only.

- **n8n is the part that turns four subscriptions into a stack.** Cloud Starter is **€20/month billed annually** (€24 month-to-month) for 2,500 executions, 5 concurrent runs, unlimited seats, and unlimited active workflows. An execution is one run of an entire workflow no matter how many steps it has. Self-hosted Community is free indefinitely under the Sustainable Use License, which permits internal business use. Check the node inventory before you plan: HubSpot and Slack have official nodes **and** official triggers; Apollo is a *verified community node* (`n8n-nodes-apollo.apollo`) that an instance owner must install and an admin can disable; **Fathom has no node at all.**

- **Slack is the review surface, and its free tier is where this stack breaks first.** Pro is **$7.25/user/month** billed annually. Free retains only **90 days** of history and allows **10 app installations** — HubSpot, Fathom, n8n, and Apollo is four of them before anyone adds a calendar bot. Workflow Builder is paid-plans-only. Conversation summaries come with any paid plan; the advanced AI set starts at Business+ (**$15/user/month** annual).

### Named handoffs

1. **Apollo sequence gets a positive reply → HubSpot contact and deal are created or updated** through Apollo's native sync, so the CRM is not a second data-entry job for the rep who booked the meeting.
2. **Meeting ends → Fathom fires its "New meeting content ready" webhook → n8n receives it.** Build this one first. Fathom's tiering makes every other route awkward: Zapier and Make are personal-use-only on Free and Premium, but **API keys and webhooks are included on every plan**, so the n8n path works from the free tier upward and ignores the automation gate entirely.
3. **n8n writes the summary and action items onto the HubSpot deal → then posts the same summary into a `#deals` channel.** Both steps use official nodes. Fathom's own CRM sync covers half of this, but not past three users per domain.
4. **Monday scheduled run → n8n queries HubSpot for deals with no next activity or a close date in the past → posts a named-owner digest to Slack.** Hygiene that arrives in the channel gets fixed; hygiene that lives on a dashboard does not. See [weekly-pipeline-report-skill](/en/workflows/weekly-pipeline-report-skill/) and [crm-hygiene](/en/learn/crm-hygiene/).
5. **Rep replies in the Slack thread (Slack Trigger node) → n8n updates the deal field.** The loop closes in the tool the rep already has open.

At 2,500 executions, n8n Starter gives you about 80 workflow runs a day. Three sellers taking 15 meetings a week, plus a Monday digest and reply handling, uses a fraction of it.

## Cost baseline

A 20-person company, three sellers, five CRM seats, everything on annual billing:

- HubSpot Sales Hub Starter, 5 seats — **$100/month** at list ($35 on the new-customer promo)
- Apollo Basic, 3 seats — **$147/month**
- Fathom Team, 5 seats — **$75/month**
- n8n Cloud Starter — **€20/month** (n8n publishes EUR only; there is no USD price on its pricing page)
- Slack Pro, 20 users — **$145/month**

That is **≈ $467/month plus €20, or roughly $5,900 a year** — against the $40K-$65K the gtm-engineering-stack costs the same-sized team. Slack is the largest line, and note what drives it: headcount, not revenue motion. Every new engineer raises your RevOps bill.

Hidden costs to plan for: **8-15 hours** of n8n build time for the five handoffs above and 2-3 hours a month of maintenance; Apollo credit burn if reps dial, at 2 credits a minute; HubSpot credit overages at $9 per 1,000.

## Variations, and the rule for each swap

- **Self-host n8n instead of Cloud.** Community edition is free with no execution quota and no concurrency cap, which saves €240 a year. *Rule:* take it only if a named person owns the container and the upgrade cadence. If the answer is "the RevOps hire will sort it out," pay the €20 — an automation that fails silently costs more than the invoice. Do not reach for n8n's Start-up Plan here either; at €333/month it is discounted Business, and it is eight times this stack's entire automation line.
- **Fathom Business instead of Team.** *Rule:* the trigger is the **fourth CRM-syncing user on your email domain**, not team size. Below that, route the extra people through the n8n webhook path, which has no per-user cap.
- **Apollo Professional instead of Basic.** Professional lifts the 250-a-day send cap and unlocks unlimited Google and Microsoft mailboxes plus 5 SMTP. *Rule:* if you are adding domains and inboxes to raise volume, you have outgrown this stack's outbound line — that is [cold-email-infrastructure-stack](/en/stacks/cold-email-infrastructure-stack/), not an upgrade path.
- **HubSpot Professional.** $90/seat/month **plus a required one-time $1,500 onboarding fee**. *Rule:* that fee alone is three months of this entire stack, so move only when a named requirement forces it — HubSpot's Customer Agent, for one, needs Professional.

## What this stack does not replace

- **An enrichment layer.** Apollo's database *is* your enrichment. No waterfall, no research agents, no fallback provider. When you need those, you are shopping [gtm-engineering-stack](/en/stacks/gtm-engineering-stack/).
- **Cold-email infrastructure.** Secondary domains, warmup, and inbox rotation are a separate build with separate economics.
- **A forecast.** HubSpot Starter gives you a pipeline, not a number that survives a board meeting — see [revenue-forecasting-stack](/en/stacks/revenue-forecasting-stack/).
- **A dial floor.** There is no parallel dialing here. Apollo's power dialer is not in any base tier; it is the Advanced Dialer add-on at **$119/team/month** annual, and a staffed dial floor is [outbound-calling-stack](/en/stacks/outbound-calling-stack/).
- **A warehouse or CDP.** If the source of truth needs to sit outside the CRM, that is [warehouse-native-gtm-stack](/en/stacks/warehouse-native-gtm-stack/).
- **The hire.** Five n8n workflows with no owner become five silent failures within a quarter. This stack assumes one person whose name is on it.

## When this is the right pick — and when it isn't

**Right:** 10-40 employees, two to five sellers, one deal motion, no Salesforce contract, a first RevOps hire who can build in a visual tool but is not an engineer, and a tooling budget under $1,000 a month.

**Wrong:** you already run Salesforce, where migration cost dwarfs the savings; product usage is your primary buying signal, which is [pls-product-led-sales-stack](/en/stacks/pls-product-led-sales-stack/); procurement or a security review will be run against your vendors, because Slack Pro and HubSpot Starter are not built to clear one; or you have more than roughly ten sellers, at which point the per-seat lines stop being the cheap part.

If you are still deciding what the first hire should own before they pick tools at all, start with [what-is-revops](/en/learn/what-is-revops/) and [revops-tech-stack](/en/learn/revops-tech-stack/).
