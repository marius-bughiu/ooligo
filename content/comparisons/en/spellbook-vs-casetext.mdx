Neither half of this pairing is what it was. Thomson Reuters shut [Casetext](/en/tools/casetext/) down on April 1, 2025, so one side cannot be bought at any price. [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) is still for sale and still growing, but it withdrew its published pricing, moved from spellbook.legal to spellbook.com, and now sells an agent rather than a sidebar. The trade that brought most people here — a $99 Word add-in against a $200 research subscription — describes two products that no longer exist in that form.

The question underneath survives, and it is the one worth answering: drafting or research, and what does either cost now that both self-serve price points are gone.

## What each side did

Casetext's shutdown is documented on the [Casetext](/en/tools/casetext/) entry and does not need re-litigating here. Thomson Reuters bought it in 2023 for $650M, began redirecting casetext.com on February 1, 2025, and retired the standalone platform two months later. The CoCounsel skills ship today inside [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/), sold per attorney against the Westlaw corpus.

Spellbook changed shape without changing owners. It raised a $50M Series B led by Khosla Ventures in October 2025 at a $350M post-money valuation, taking total funding past $80M, then added a $40M debt facility from RBCx in March 2026 earmarked for acquisitions. The product surface grew with the balance sheet. Where this catalog's tool entry describes a Word sidebar that suggests redlines, Spellbook now sells six named pieces — Review, Draft, Compare, Ask, Playbooks, and Associate — with Associate positioned as an agent for multi-document drafting and review rather than a suggestion engine. It reaches past Word into email, Slack, and Salesforce, and reports 4,500+ legal teams across 80+ countries.

## Neither one is the cheap self-serve option now

This is the part that inverts the old verdict. Spellbook's whole argument in the previous draft of this page was per-seat economics: predictable monthly cost for a ten-lawyer transactional team, no procurement cycle. Spellbook no longer publishes a number. Its pricing page states that pricing is determined by the number of team members on a license, calls the result custom pricing, and routes every visitor to a demo — a 7-day trial for qualified legal professionals is the only self-serve door left.

Third-party trackers have filled the vacuum, and they do not agree with each other. Published estimates put the entry seat anywhere from roughly $20 to $99 per user per month, team tiers from roughly $40 to $149, and enterprise from roughly $199 to $350 per user per month with a 10-seat minimum and a reported six-month minimum term. A 5x spread on the entry tier is not a price band. It is evidence that nobody outside Spellbook's sales team knows, and the $99 figure still circulating in comparison articles — including this catalog's own Spellbook entry, pending its own refresh — should be treated as unverified.

The direction is the part that is safe to act on. The reported enterprise seat sits near $350 against the $179 Spellbook once published for Team. It went up and went quiet in the same twelve months.

## What replaced Casetext costs about 3.2x Casetext

Casetext's real product was never the model. It was the checkout: a solo attorney put in a card and was working that afternoon, with no Westlaw contract and no seat minimum. It sold from roughly $200/user/month, and that access is the thing nothing in this comparison inherits.

The closest live equivalent is Westlaw Advantage with CoCounsel Essentials, which Thomson Reuters prices at **$639.20/user/month** for a solo attorney on All States & Federal coverage at a one-year term, with published term discounts of 12% at two years and 18% at three. Online purchase is open only to firms of up to 10 attorneys. Above that it is a sales cycle.

So the research side went from $200 self-serve to $639.20 with a firm-size ceiling on the checkout, and the drafting side went from a published $99 to a demo form. Both halves lost the property that made this pairing interesting to small firms in the first place.

## The workflows still barely overlap

The one thing the old page got right survives intact. Spellbook draws its authority from the contract in front of you; CoCounsel draws its authority from a case-law corpus. Associate widens Spellbook's reach — it works a term sheet up into a full financing set, or runs inconsistency checks across hundreds of documents — but every document in that set is one you supplied. It does not tell you whether a case is still good law, and Spellbook does not claim it does.

That boundary is why a full-service firm still buys both, and why the double-spend objection never applied. What changed is the bill. Two seats that once totalled roughly $300/month now start near $990 if the reported Spellbook enterprise seat holds, before any minimum-seat commitment on either side.

## Verdict

**Pick Spellbook** if the work is contract drafting, review, and playbook compliance — corporate, commercial, employment, in-house GC. Go in knowing you are entering a sales conversation rather than a checkout, and that the number you are quoted will not match the ones in circulation.

**Do not pick Casetext.** It has not been purchasable since April 1, 2025. Any shortlist, renewal model, or budget line still carrying a $200 Casetext seat is more than a year stale.

**If research is the actual need, buy Thomson Reuters CoCounsel**, and read [Harvey vs Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/vs/harvey-vs-thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) before signing. That is the live platform bake-off this pairing used to stand in for.

**If you cannot decide, buy Spellbook first.** Drafting volume is where a transactional team's hours actually go, the trial runs in days rather than a procurement cycle, and a research seat can be added later without rework. Reverse that order only if your matters already route through Westlaw.

**If neither fits** — the quote comes back above budget, or the firm has standardized on Google Docs — [Ivo](/en/tools/ivo/), [Definely](/en/tools/definely/), and [DraftWise](/en/tools/draftwise/) attack contract review and drafting from different angles, and [Spellbook vs DraftWise](/en/vs/spellbook-vs-draftwise/) works that trade in detail. Guard: none of the three does primary-law research with citation validation, so anything headed for a filing still needs a KeyCite or Shepard's check outside the tool.

## Two things to watch

- **Spellbook's published prices are gone but not forgotten.** Review sites, procurement spreadsheets, and this catalog's own tool entry still carry $99 and $179 tiers that Spellbook stopped publishing. Guard: get the quote in writing before modelling seats, and re-check it at renewal — the reported move from $179 to a ~$350 enterprise seat happened inside a year.
- **ACM is announced, not shipped.** Spellbook lists an early-access waitlist for a system it describes as powering contracts end to end. Guard: buy Review, Draft, Compare, Ask, Playbooks, and Associate on what they do today, and keep any ACM capability out of the business case until it has a GA date.
