Start with the objection, because it is the right one. [Canny](/en/tools/canny/) and [Productboard](/en/tools/productboard/) now read the same sources. Canny's Autopilot pulls feedback from Zendesk, Intercom, Slack, Gong, Help Scout, Freshdesk, Zoom and tl;dv, plus public reviews on G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, the App Store and Google Play. Productboard ingests Zendesk, Intercom, Slack, Gong, G2, the App Store and Google Play too. Both cluster what they find with AI and both deduplicate. Buying both looks like buying the same ingestion engine twice.

The part that does not overlap is the reason this stack exists. Canny runs a board your customers log into, post on, and vote on, and a changelog that tells every voter when the thing shipped. Productboard's Portal publishes a roadmap; it is a broadcast surface, not a voting community. Productboard, in exchange, holds the prioritization apparatus — scoring against objectives, feature definition, and the handoff into engineering. One tool is where customers argue about what matters. The other is where your product team decides. If you only need one of those, buy one of them, and this page is not for you.

That second half got more expensive in autumn 2026. Productboard dropped its Free and Plus plans and now sells only Business and Enterprise, both demo-gated and both with a 5-maker minimum. The cheap way to try the full stack is gone, which changes who it fits.

## What each tool does here

**Zendesk** is the volume source, not the feedback system. Most feature requests arrive as the second paragraph of a support ticket about something else, and they die there. Zendesk's job in this stack is to keep existing — the agent resolves the ticket and does not change their workflow at all.

**Canny** is the customer-facing layer and the deduplication point. Autopilot reads the Zendesk conversation, extracts the request, merges it into an existing post if one exists, and files it to a product area. The public board is where the request becomes visible and countable, and the changelog is how the loop closes.

**Productboard** is the internal ranking layer. It ingests its own copy of the support and sales conversations, attaches them to features, scores those features against objectives, and pushes committed work into Jira, Azure DevOps, GitHub, Trello or Shortcut. Spark, the AI layer across all of it, is included on both plans and rationed by credits. Productboard also ships an official MCP server, so an agent can query features and insights without a seat in the UI.

**Slack** is where the loop becomes visible to humans who will not open either tool. Canny posts notifications for new posts, comments, vote milestones and status changes; both Canny and Productboard treat Slack as a capture source as well, so a request typed into `#customer-feedback` does not evaporate.

## The handoffs

1. A ticket lands in **Zendesk** containing a feature request → Canny Autopilot captures it, deduplicates against existing posts, and files it to a product area.
2. The request appears on the **Canny** board → other customers vote; vote milestones and status changes push to **Slack**.
3. A post reaches the threshold you set → it is pushed to **Jira or Linear** with two-way status sync.
4. **Productboard** ingests the same Zendesk and Gong conversations directly, attaches them to features, and ranks against objectives.
5. Work ships → the Jira or Linear status syncs back to the Canny post → the **Canny changelog** notifies every customer who voted.

Step 4 is where this stack has a hole, and pretending otherwise would make the rest of the page useless. **Canny and Productboard have no native integration in either direction.** Canny's integration directory does not list Productboard, and Productboard's does not list Canny. Checked again 9 November 2026.

The guard is to stop trying to connect them directly and make the issue tracker the shared spine. Both tools sync natively to Jira, and both attach to the same issue, so a Jira key becomes the join between the public post and the internal feature. Two limits on that guard. It needs Jira specifically: Canny syncs natively to Linear, but Productboard reaches Linear only through a Spark MCP connector, not through its development-planning sync. And Productboard Business caps the Jira integration at 5 connections, so a company with many Jira projects should map them before signing. The alternatives are worse: a Zapier or Make bridge you now own and maintain, or the Canny API against Productboard's, which is a small internal integration with no owner by month three.

## Cost baseline

Checked 9 November 2026. Take a team with 8 support agents and 4 people who need to write in the roadmap tool.

Productboard no longer publishes a price. The last list price it published for Business was $59/maker/month on annual billing ($75 monthly), in its own billing FAQ dated September 2026; treat it as a reference point, not a quote. The 5-maker minimum means the 4-person team pays for 5 makers: $295/month, or $3,540/year.

A lean configuration: Zendesk Suite Team at $55/agent/month annually ($440), Canny Pro at $79/month billed yearly, and Productboard Business at the 5-maker minimum ($295). That is $814/month, about $9,768/year.

A fuller one: Zendesk Suite Professional at $115/agent/month annually ($920) plus the Copilot add-on at $50/agent/month paid yearly ($400), Canny Pro at $79, and Productboard Business at $295. That is $1,694/month, about $20,328/year. Copilot's agent-assist features are add-ons on Professional and are not offered on Suite Team. Zendesk's AI agents are billed per automated resolution rather than per seat: Suite Team includes 5 resolutions per agent per month and Professional 10, and extra resolutions cost $1.50 committed or $2.00 pay-as-you-go.

The feedback layer is now close to half of the lean bill ($374 of $814) and about a fifth of the fuller one. In August 2026, before Plus disappeared, it was roughly a quarter of both. On a small support team, the Productboard minimum is now the line to negotiate.

Two metering details decide whether those numbers hold. Canny does not price on team seats — Free covers 25 tracked users and 5 managers, Pro covers 100 or more tracked users and 10 managers, and Business starts at 5,000. Growth in the number of end users you track moves you up a tier, not growth in your team; a tracked user is anyone associated with feedback, including users Autopilot finds for you. Productboard meters Spark credits: Business includes 500 per maker per month, pooled across the workspace, and Enterprise includes 1,500 base credits plus 800 per additional maker. Top-ups cost $5 per 50 credits on monthly plans or $60/year per 600 credits on annual ones. Check credit burn during the demo-led trial before you size the maker count.

## Variations

**Drop Canny when the board is not public.** If customers never see or vote on requests, Canny's non-overlapping half is gone and Productboard's Portal covers roadmap publishing. That saves $948/year and removes the connector seam. The rule: if you cannot name the customers who will log in and vote, you are buying a deduplication engine you already have.

**Drop Productboard when fewer than 5 people write in the roadmap.** The minimum makes you pay for seats nobody uses, and under about ten people prioritization fits in Jira or Linear with a scoring field. Canny plus the issue tracker is then the whole loop, at $79/month for the feedback layer instead of $374.

**Swap Zendesk for Intercom or Pylon.** Both Canny and Productboard ingest Intercom natively, so the handoffs survive the substitution intact.

## What this stack does not replace

It does not tell you what customers *did*, only what they *asked for*. Behavior belongs to the [product adoption stack](/en/stacks/product-adoption-stack/). It does not deflect tickets — that is the [AI support agent stack](/en/stacks/ai-support-agent-stack/). It is not a discovery practice: vote counts measure the enthusiasm of the customers who bother to vote, which correlates with how loud a segment is rather than how much revenue it holds. And it is not a CS health or renewal system.

Two watch-outs with their guards. **Both tools ingesting Zendesk independently produces two different clusterings of the same tickets**, and the first time PM and CS bring different counts for the same request to the same meeting, trust in both numbers is gone; the guard is to give each source one system of record, letting Canny own anything customer-visible and pointing Productboard at sales calls and internal notes. **Productboard is still mid-pivot** — Hubert Palan announced on 15 April 2026 that the company was going AI-only and parting with more than 30% of the team, and the September 2026 repricing removed the self-serve tiers; the guard is to keep raw verbatims in Canny and Zendesk, where you can export them, and treat Productboard as a ranking layer over a corpus it does not solely own.

## When this is the right stack

Pick it when you have enough customers that a public board gets real votes, support volume high enough that requests get buried in tickets, at least 5 people who will actually write in the roadmap tool, a PM and a CS lead who currently disagree about priorities with no shared ranked list, and someone who will maintain the changelog.

Skip it when your customer count is small enough to just call them — a voting board across 30 enterprise accounts produces noise and a false mandate — or when nobody owns the loop. A public board showing a request as "under review" for two years is worse than never having opened one, because it is a durable, indexed, customer-visible record that you do not answer.
