## What it is

Salesforce Spiff is incentive compensation management (ICM) sold by Salesforce: it takes closed deals, runs them through your commission plans, and gives every rep a live statement that traces each dollar back to the record it came from. Salesforce completed its acquisition of Spiff on February 1, 2024 and now sells it inside the Sales Performance Management (SPM) line of Sales Cloud, next to Sales Planning. The category leaders it most resembles are [Xactly](/en/tools/xactly/) and [CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/). The difference is where it lives, not how it calculates.

The pricing page lists Spiff Designer for building plans, a Commission Estimator, tracing and audit trails, effective dating, foreign currency conversion, commission expensing, dispute resolution, approvals, automated payouts and a mobile app. Salesforce data syncs directly. Everything else comes in through what Salesforce calls "over 50" connectors.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Salesforce runs its own comp on it.** A Salesforce post dated June 11, 2026 says the company moved more than 30,000 commissionable employees, 120 compensation plans and 3.5 billion commission data points onto Spiff, off third-party software it had used for more than 17 years. It reports at least a 75% improvement in calculation time, with crediting and payouts running in three to four hours. That is self-reported, but it is the strongest public evidence that Spiff scales past the mid-market it was built for.
- **Commissions at quote time.** The same post describes a [Revenue Cloud](/en/tools/salesforce-revenue-cloud/) integration, stood up in 24 hours, that shows sellers their estimated commission while they build a quote. For a team already on Revenue Cloud, that is the one feature no standalone ICM vendor can match natively.
- **It is already on your Salesforce paper.** Spiff is included in the Agentforce 1 Sales edition (see [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) for that bundle), and for everyone else it is an add-on line on the existing contract. No new vendor security review is often worth a quarter of procurement time on its own.

## Pricing reality

The list price is published: $75 per user per month, billed annually, on an annual contract. Salesforce offers no trial licences for Spiff. Two charges sit on top. Each one-way connector to a system other than Salesforce costs $250 a month, and Premium Support costs 30% of the net licence price.

Run the math at list for a 100-payee sales org that pulls roster data from Workday and payment data from NetSuite: $90,000 in seats, $6,000 in connectors, and about $28,800 if you add Premium Support on the undiscounted price. That is roughly $125,000 in year one before implementation, which one third-party listing estimates at $10,000 to $100,000 or more in services. For reference, Vendr medians reported on our other ICM pages are about $28,000 for Varicent, $37,000 for CaptivateIQ, $41,000 for Everstage and $80,000 for Xactly.

The list price is not what large Salesforce customers pay, because Spiff is negotiated inside the whole Salesforce account. What moves the price is timing: quote Spiff into a Sales Cloud renewal or an edition upgrade, not as a standalone mid-term add-on.

## Best for

RevOps and sales-compensation teams at Salesforce-standardized companies with roughly 50 to 1,000 payees, where deals, quotas and splits already live in Salesforce and the plan has real moving parts: tiers, accelerators, splits and clawbacks. It is the strongest pick when the company already holds Agentforce 1 Sales licences or runs Revenue Cloud, and when consolidating onto the existing contract is worth more than the deepest standalone modeling engine.

Do not buy it if your CRM is HubSpot or Pipedrive, if comp inputs live in four or more non-Salesforce systems, or if you have 15 reps on a flat-rate plan. A spreadsheet handles the last case. Get the [plan design](/en/learn/compensation-plans/) right before you automate it.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Xactly](/en/tools/xactly/)** is one of the two enterprise share leaders and a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Sales Performance Management. Pick Xactly when you want its benchmark data on pay and quota design, or when the CRM is not only Salesforce.
- **[Varicent](/en/tools/varicent/)** is the other share leader, a Magic Quadrant Leader for the eighth consecutive year. Pick it for 500 or more payees where territory and quota planning run as one annual program with comp.
- **[Everstage](/en/tools/everstage/)** is the fastest-growing entrant, a 2026 Magic Quadrant Challenger that sells ICM and CPQ together. Pick it when comp inputs arrive from NetSuite, Workday or a warehouse as often as from the CRM, where Spiff's connector fees add up.
- **[CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/)** fits comp admins who think in spreadsheet formulas and want a deeper modeling layer than Spiff Designer.
- **[QuotaPath](/en/tools/quotapath/)** costs $35 per user per month plus a $525 monthly platform fee on Growth. Pick it for small teams or any CRM other than Salesforce. From about nine users up, Spiff costs more at list.

## Watch-outs

- **The AI story is partly a roadmap.** The June 2026 post says commission data "will soon be available" through MCP and a headless approach, and that natural-language comp questions and AI case resolution are coming. The product page already advertises Agentforce and connections to Slack and Claude. *Guard:* have the account team demo the exact AI feature on your own data in your org, and put anything load-bearing in the order form with a delivery date.
- **Connector fees scale with your source systems, not your payees.** At $250 a month each, six non-Salesforce sources cost $18,000 a year before a single seat. *Guard:* list every system that feeds comp (HRIS roster, ERP payments, billing, warehouse) before the quote, and where you can, land that data in Salesforce or one warehouse first so Spiff reads from one place.
- **The seat definition decides the bill.** Pricing is per user, and public sources do not settle whether managers, approvers and finance reviewers count as users. *Guard:* get the user definition in writing and model cost at your 18-month headcount plan.
- **No trial means the first real test is the implementation.** ICM projects fail on data plumbing: CRM deals, HRIS roster and ERP payments disagree at every company. *Guard:* write a paid proof-of-concept into the statement of work that recalculates one past quarter of payouts, and do not sign the full term until Spiff's number matches yours.
- **Comp lock-in compounds Salesforce lock-in.** Once commissions sit on the same contract as the CRM, walking away from either gets harder at renewal. *Guard:* price Spiff as a separate line item, and negotiate export terms that cover plan logic and statement history, not only payout totals.
