## What it is

Clarify is a Seattle CRM that bills for AI work instead of seats. Every plan carries unlimited users; what you buy is a pool of credits that the product's agent — named Rep — and any custom agents you build spend on meeting prep, meeting summaries, deal detection, field updates, task creation, workflow runs, and campaign sends. Records assemble themselves from Gmail, Outlook, calendar, and recorded calls, so the manual-entry tax a per-seat CRM charges is the exact thing the pricing model is aimed at.

The company raised $22.5M across its seed and Series A and went generally available on 25 June 2025. Founders Patrick Thompson and Ondrej Hrebicek previously built Iteratively, which Amplitude acquired in 2021. On 21 July 2026 Clarify bought San Francisco's Seam AI, an ABM product that tracked funding events, hiring plans, and champion moves, to build Clarify Signals; in mid-November 2026 Signals is still a waitlist and shows as "Coming" on the pricing page. The September and October 2026 releases restructured pricing — stepped Starter tiers, a $1,000/mo Growth floor, a new Enterprise tier — and opened HubSpot two-way sync to an early-access group.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **The agent surface is unusually deep for a CRM this young.** The hosted MCP server at `https://api.clarify.ai/mcp` takes OAuth for interactive clients, or an `api-key` header plus an `X-Clarify-Workspace` header for unattended runs, and works with Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT (as an official app), and Codex. It is not a read-only shim: alongside `query-data`, `query-analytics`, `get-transcript`, and `find-leads` against a built-in database of 28M+ companies and 175M+ people, it exposes `create-or-update-records`, `merge-records`, `send-email`, `publish-campaign`, `create-or-update-agent`, and `create-or-update-fields`. An agent can define a custom field and backfill it without a human opening the app. A Clarify CLI shipped on 13 November 2026.
- **Seat count stops being a budget line.** Adding a sales engineer, a CSM, or a founder who only reads pipeline costs nothing. On a per-seat CRM each of those is a recurring charge whether or not the person ever writes a record.
- **Capture sits outside the meter.** Meeting recording, standard enrichment, merging duplicates, and chatting with Rep are free; actions Rep takes for you cost the same as running them yourself. Exhausting credits degrades the AI layer without stopping data capture.

## Pricing

Checked against the pricing page, the September 2026 changelog, and the billing help article on 14 November 2026:

- **Free** — $0/mo. Unlimited seats, 1,000 credits/mo, 20k records, 3 objects, 1 active campaign, standard enrichment. No on-demand credits, no annual billing.
- **Starter** — $50/mo for 5,000 credits, stepping to $100 (10k), $200 (20k), and $400 (40k); 20% off on annual billing; monthly credits with no rollover. 250k records, 5 objects, 5 formula fields, 5 active campaigns, LinkedIn integration, advanced enrichment capped at 1,000 enrichments per period.
- **Growth** — from $1,000/mo, annual only, 100k+ credits as an annual pool. 750k records, 10 objects, 20 campaigns, workflows, workspace agents, delegated sending, advanced permissions, warehouse access, Slack support, a dedicated CSM, and 5,000 enrichments per period. CRM migration is quoted separately.
- **Enterprise** — custom. SAML/OIDC SSO, SCIM provisioning, security review, priority support, and a forward-deployed GTM partner.

On-demand credits past the allowance cost $0.015 each from the September 2026 billing cycle, up from $0.01. They are off by default and take a dollar spend cap. A BAA for PHI is available on Growth and Enterprise.

Published credit rates: meeting prep 30, meeting summary 30, deal summary 20 (charged only when the summary changes), deal creation 20 per suggested deal whether or not you accept it, field-update suggestions 10, meeting task creation 10, AI autofill 3 to 30, workflow blocks 1, campaign messages 1, Lead Finder 1 per net-new person. Custom agents bill at actual AI usage: Clarify's own examples put a post-meeting CRM update near 50 credits per run and a weekly pipeline digest near 100.

The number that decides the deal is what a working team burns. Six reps at 25 external meetings a week is roughly 108 meetings a month: 6,480 credits on prep and summaries, about 800 on 80 meeting tasks, 400 on 20 suggested deals, 800 on 40 deal summaries, and 1,500 on 150 field-update suggestions. That lands near 10,000 credits — the $100/mo Starter tier, $80 on annual. Add a post-meeting agent on every call (about 5,400 credits) and you are on the $200 tier. Against that: $474/mo for six seats of [Attio](/en/tools/attio/) Pro on annual billing, and $540/mo for six seats of [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) Sales Hub Professional on annual plus a required $1,500 onboarding fee.

## Best for

RevOps leaders at 5–25 person GTM orgs who want the CRM and the agent layer on one bill, whose org has more people who need to read pipeline than people who work it daily, and whose security review does not yet require SSO.

## Not for

Teams whose security review opens with SSO — SAML, OIDC, and SCIM are now Enterprise-only, so a 12-person startup with a questionnaire jumps from a $50–400/mo Starter tier past the $1,000/mo Growth floor to a custom contract. Salesforce shops that need the two systems in sync this quarter. Teams that need territory management, approval chains, or a partner app ecosystem. And anyone whose business case rests on Signals.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/)** — pick it when governance, territory rules, and a data model shared with finance and support decide the evaluation. Clarify wins on what a two-person ops team can run without an admin; Salesforce wins on what survives 200 users and an audit.
- **[HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/)** — Sales Hub Professional is $90/seat/mo annual against Clarify's flat workspace tiers, so the gap widens with every non-selling headcount. Pick HubSpot when marketing automation has to sit on the same bill as CRM; pick Clarify when the spend you want to meter is AI work. Once the two-way sync leaves early access, Clarify can also run as the rep-facing layer on top of a HubSpot marketing stack.
- **[Day.ai](/en/tools/day-ai/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in AI-native CRM, and the closest philosophical match. Day.ai meters agents at $24/$60/$200 with no usage overages; Clarify meters the work itself. Pick Day.ai when you want a fixed, predictable invoice; pick Clarify when volume is uneven month to month and you would rather pay for what ran.
- **[Attio](/en/tools/attio/)** — pick Attio when the object model is the purchase: Pro carries 12 objects and a schema you shape yourself. Clarify caps Starter at 5 objects and Growth at 10.

## Watch-outs

- **The incumbent syncs are not general availability.** Salesforce sync is still marked "Coming soon"; HubSpot two-way sync is early access through a waitlist, deletions never sync, and syncing custom objects is Enterprise-only. Guard: if you cannot rip out the incumbent this quarter, scope Clarify to one team as its own system of record, and get sync access and migration terms in writing before signing an annual Growth contract.
- **Growth is a step function, not a slope.** Starter tops out at $400 for 40k credits; the next plan is $1,000/mo billed annually, a $12,000 commitment. A team burning 50k credits a month pays about $550 on Starter with on-demand credits. Guard: stay on Starter with on-demand on until you need a Growth-only feature — workflows, workspace agents, delegated sending, warehouse access — and size the Growth pool from measured burn, not a headcount estimate.
- **The meter's price can move under you.** The overage rate rose 50% with one billing cycle's notice in September 2026. Guard: set the dollar cap and the 80/90/100% alerts, use per-member credit limits on Growth, and ask for the on-demand rate to be fixed for the contract term.
- **A zero balance pauses the AI mid-month.** Summaries and tasks missed while the pool is empty are not re-run when credits return. Guard: enable on-demand credits under Settings, Workspace, Billing before quarter-end, with a cap, so the failure mode is a known overage rather than a gap in the record.
- **Agent write access inherits a human's permissions.** Any member can create a personal API key that acts with their access, and the write tools include `delete-records` and `manage-access`. Guard: run unattended agents on a dedicated Clarify user with scoped record-level permissions, or an admin-managed workspace key, never an admin's personal key.
- **Signals was announced, not delivered.** Guard: if buying-signal detection is a requirement, keep the incumbent signal tool on contract through the current term rather than budgeting Clarify to replace it.
