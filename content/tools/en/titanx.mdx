## What it is

TitanX sells cold-calling infrastructure built on one idea: most of a B2B market never picks up an unknown number, so the cheapest connect is the call you don't place. Its core product, **Phone Intent**, scores the phone numbers you already have, whether they came from ZoomInfo, Apollo, Clay, Cognism or your own lists, by how likely the person is to answer. Contacts land in tiers from P1 (high intent) down to NA (bad data). TitanX says the scores combine 40+ signals drawn from 400M+ analyzed calls, with some human verification. Scores come in two speeds: Lightning returns in about 24 hours and favors people who answered recently, while Titan takes about 5 days and finds more P1s. TitanX doesn't sell raw contacts. It scores and waterfall-enriches the records you bring.

In February 2026 TitanX bought the power dialer FrontSpin in an eight-figure deal. A month earlier it had raised a $27M Series A led by Updata Partners. FrontSpin has since been absorbed: as of October 2026, frontspin.com redirects to titanx.io. The combined product is the **Precision Dialer**, a **single-line** dialer by design, so a rep places every call and nothing is parallel-dialed. Around it sit Smart Stream (a queue ranked by Phone Intent that sets the next task from each disposition), live transcription, AI call summaries, voicemail drop, and **Titan Defense**, which assigns, registers, rotates and repairs each rep's caller IDs so a flagged number leaves rotation before it ruins a list.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **It attacks the denominator, not the dial rate.** Parallel dialers like [Orum](/en/tools/orum/) and [Nooks](/en/tools/nooks/) raise connects per hour by ringing several numbers at once. TitanX tries to stop reps dialing the roughly 80% of a market that, by TitanX's own estimate, never answers cold calls. TitanX's headline claim is 3x more conversations from 50% fewer dials. The connect rates it publishes are customer-reported: 20-35% depending on list and market, against the 3-8% that stale lists tend to produce. One named customer reports 84 dials per meeting, down from about 320. All of these are vendor numbers, but the method is simple to test: run scored and unscored lists side by side.
- **Scores land where RevOps already reports.** Each dial, disposition, Phone Intent score and best-number pick writes back to the Salesforce or HubSpot record. That lets you segment connect rate by P-tier in your own CRM rather than trusting a vendor dashboard. An API covers scoring and phone enrichment for teams that want to push scores into Clay or a warehouse.
- **Number reputation is managed for you.** Reps can't pick or burn caller IDs. Numbers carry STIR/SHAKEN attestation and are warmed before cutover. A single-line, human-initiated dialer also keeps you clear of the dead-air hangups that parallel dialing produces.

## Pricing reality

TitanX's pricing page, read in October 2026 by a competing dialer vendor and matching TitanX's own November 2026 blog, lists **$475 per seat per month** for the Precision Dialer (SDR or full-cycle AE package) and **$375 per seat per month** for Phone Intent alone, which you'd buy to keep your current dialer. Both are **billed annually with a 3-rep minimum**. The smallest commitment is therefore $13,500 a year for scoring only and $17,100 a year for the dialer. Each seat includes a pooled monthly allotment of Phone Intent credits, and you buy extra credits when large lists run through them. International seats and numbers are quoted by country. The pricing page doesn't say how many calling minutes a seat includes.

At 10 reps the dialer package comes to about $57,000 a year before extra credits, slightly above the $40,000-$50,000 commonly reported for Nooks at that size and roughly double Orum's Launch list price. There is no self-serve trial. TitanX runs a 30-day pilot, free when you share an export of your current dialer's call logs and join an executive alignment call. A third-party review describes a 300% connect-lift guarantee with a $10,000 payout. TitanX's own pages don't repeat that guarantee, so get it in the order form or don't count on it.

## Best for

- **SDR and full-cycle AE teams of 5-30 reps selling to hard-to-reach buyers** (finance, IT, operations leaders) whose connect rates sit under 5% even with good data. Here, removing dead dials matters more than dialing faster.
- **RevOps leaders who are locked into a dialer contract** and want better list quality without switching. The $375 Phone Intent seat scores lists that your existing dialer or sales engagement platform then works.

Don't buy TitanX for 1-2 reps, because the 3-seat annual minimum bills you for seats nobody uses. Skip it if your team lives in Outreach or Salesloft and needs dispositions written back there today: TitanX lists both integrations as "soon," and only Salesforce and HubSpot are live. And skip it if your list is small and already well-worked, since there's nothing left to score away.

## Versus the alternatives

**Orum** and **Nooks** are the two category leaders, and both multiply volume with parallel lines. Pick Orum when you want the cheapest serious way to multiply dials (about $250 per user per month on Launch) and your lists are large enough to absorb lower connect quality. Pick Nooks when you want AI coaching, a virtual salesfloor and an AI prospector bundled with the dial. Pick TitanX when your lists are finite and expensive, every wasted dial burns a contact you can't replace, and you want reps having one-to-one conversations without parallel-dial drops. [Koncert](/en/tools/koncert/) is the other established multi-line option to quote if you want parallel volume from a third vendor. You can also combine approaches: buy Phone Intent alone, score your lists, and work the P1s in a parallel dialer. On the data side, [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/), [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) and [Cognism](/en/tools/cognism/) supply the numbers but don't rank them by answer likelihood. TitanX sits on top of them, not in their place.

## Watch-outs

- **Connect-rate claims are vendor-published.** The 20-35% figures are customer case studies on TitanX's own site, and ranges shift between its pages. Guard: during the pilot, split a single list into scored and unscored halves and work both on the same days with the same reps. Compare connects per 100 dials and meetings per 100 dials in your CRM before you sign.
- **Scores aren't real-time.** Turnaround is 24 hours (Lightning) to 5 days (Titan), so a list pulled this morning can't be scored for this afternoon's call block. Guard: schedule scoring a week ahead of each campaign, and keep a rolling pre-scored backlog so reps never fall back to unscored dials.
- **FrontSpin customers are now on TitanX terms.** The standalone product redirects to TitanX, and the TitanX dialer is single-line. Guard: if you're a FrontSpin customer, get the renewal plan, pricing and any feature changes in writing before your renewal notice window. If you depended on multi-line dialing, quote Orum or Koncert in parallel.
- **Integration gaps outside the CRM.** No live Outreach or Salesloft sync, and no MCP server. Guard: confirm the CRM field mapping during the pilot, and if your sequences live in a sales engagement platform, plan to trigger them from CRM fields until the native sync ships.
- **The bill grows with list volume, not just seats.** Credits are pooled per seat, and large campaigns can use them up. Guard: estimate how many records you'll score per month before you sign, and negotiate the extra-credit rate into the order form.
