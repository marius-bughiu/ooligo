## What you're actually leaving

Two triggers for moving off [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) hold up, and one doesn't.

The ones that hold up: you need something Claude does not ship at all — image generation, video, a consumer voice product — or your organization is consolidating on one AI vendor and Claude loses that fight on surface area rather than on quality. Those are structural. No release fixes them for you.

The one that doesn't hold up: a benchmark moved. Public evaluation deltas swing in both directions with every model release, and they rarely match the delta a team feels in its own work. If the complaint in the room is "we heard the other one is smarter now," that is not a migration thesis.

One argument that used to belong on this page is now gone. MCP is no longer a reason to stay on Claude. ChatGPT has full read/write MCP client access through developer mode, and Perplexity publishes its own server. Claude still has the better default — connectors are a first-class surface rather than a developer setting, which matters when an ops manager rather than an engineer maintains them — but "we'd lose MCP" is no longer true.

## ChatGPT — surface area, and the only route to image and video

[ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) wins on breadth, and breadth is a real product decision rather than a marketing one. Sora for video, native image generation, agent mode's virtual browser, and the plugin directory cover four jobs that no single alternative covers together. Claude has none of the first two. If a designer, a marketer, or a recruiter on your team produces images weekly, Claude cannot replace that seat — no configuration closes that gap.

The entry price is also lower at the bottom of the range. ChatGPT Go is $8/month; the cheapest paid Claude seat is $17/month on annual billing. At roughly half the price, Go is the only tier in this comparison built for casual users you don't want to pay full freight for.

**Switch to ChatGPT when:** creative production is in the weekly workflow, you want one vendor across coding, writing, images, and video, or you're standardized on Microsoft and OpenAI infrastructure where the integration path is shorter.

**Don't switch when:** your high-value hours go into long-document synthesis or written output quality. Those are the two dimensions where Claude's edge has been consistent since 2023, and the ChatGPT version of that work will read as a downgrade to the people doing it.

## Perplexity — cited research, and something your assistant can call

[Perplexity](/en/tools/perplexity/) is an answer engine, not a general assistant, and buying it as a Claude replacement is the standard mistake on this page. What it does better than either general assistant is narrow and valuable: web-grounded answers with inline citations you can audit in one click and forward to a stakeholder. For competitive research, regulatory checks, and account or candidate diligence, that beats a better-reasoning model with no source trail.

The change worth acting on is that Perplexity is now callable. The official `@perplexity-ai/mcp-server` package exposes four tools — `perplexity_search`, `perplexity_ask`, `perplexity_research`, and `perplexity_reasoning` — over stdio to any MCP client, Claude included. That converts the decision from "which seat do we buy" to "which layer does this belong in." Teams weighing Claude against Perplexity increasingly keep Claude as the front end and run Perplexity as the grounded-retrieval tool behind it.

**Switch to Perplexity when:** current-information research dominates your usage and every answer needs a citation attached.

**Don't switch when:** you write, code, or reason inside the assistant. Moving those motions to Perplexity is a downgrade, and the MCP server means you don't have to choose.

## When staying on Claude is the right answer

- Your highest-value work is long-context: a 300-page diligence set, a full contract stack, a quarter of support transcripts. Coherence over very long inputs is the edge that has held.
- Written output quality is the deliverable rather than a draft someone rewrites.
- You're building agent workflows over your own systems and the person maintaining them is non-technical. Connectors on by default is worth more than connectors behind a developer flag.
- Your team runs Claude Code, Skills, or Projects that are load-bearing. Those have no direct equivalent to port to.
- The stated reason to leave is model quality in the abstract with no named job attached.

## What the switch actually costs

Seat prices are close enough that they should not decide this. Claude Pro is $20/month, or $17/month billed annually at $200 up front. ChatGPT Plus is $20/month. At team scale, Claude Team standard seats are $25/month or $20 billed annually, against ChatGPT Business at $25 per user/month billed monthly. Claude Team requires a minimum of two members — low enough that it rules almost nobody out.

The power tiers are where money leaks. Claude Max is $100/month for 5x Pro usage and $200/month for 20x, monthly billing only. ChatGPT Pro mirrors that with $100 and $200 tiers. Perplexity has the widest spread: Pro is $20/month ($200/year), Max is $200/month ($2,000/year) with no cheaper power tier in between, and enterprise seats run $40/month for Enterprise Pro ($400/year) or $325/month for Enterprise Max ($3,250/year). That top seat is more than 16x a Claude Team standard seat. Buy it only for named individuals, never as an org-wide default.

Two costs people underestimate. First, Claude Enterprise is $20/seat for access with model usage billed separately at API rates, so a heavy Enterprise deployment is a metered bill rather than a flat one — compare it against ChatGPT Enterprise on projected usage, not on the seat line. Second, the migration itself: a prompt library, Projects, and wired-up MCP connectors are a rebuild project measured in weeks, not a settings change. Teams that skip this estimate are the ones that switch, stall, and switch back.

## The pick

**If Claude is the status quo and nothing on the "switch when" lists applies, stay.** Undifferentiated dissatisfaction is not a reason to absorb a rebuild.

**Switch the default to ChatGPT** if you can name a recurring job that needs image or video generation, or if single-vendor consolidation is a decision already made above you. One named job is enough; a general preference for breadth is not.

**Add Perplexity rather than switching to it** — $20/month for the people doing research — and wire its MCP server into Claude. It is a layer, not a replacement, and treating it as a replacement is the most common way to get this page wrong.

**If none of the three fit**, the problem is almost never the model. It's that the work needs your own data in the loop: the CRM, the contract repository, the ATS. The fix is an assistant plus connectors into those systems, or a vertical tool that already owns the data — not a fourth general assistant. Re-run the evaluation once the connectors exist, because the answer changes.

The last remaining way to get this wrong is to treat AI vendor choice as a loyalty question. Serious teams in 2026 pay for two and route by job.
