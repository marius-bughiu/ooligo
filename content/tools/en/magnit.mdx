## What it is

Magnit, trading as Magnit Global, is a contingent workforce company that sells two things most buyers purchase separately: the **vendor management system (VMS)**, the software that requests, approves, onboards, time-tracks and invoices non-employee workers, and the **managed service provider (MSP)** team that runs the program on top of it. It was PRO Unlimited until September 2022, when it renamed itself after a run of acquisitions that included Workforce Logiq. EQT Private Equity agreed to buy the company from Harvest Partners and Investcorp in June 2021, with Harvest staying on as a large minority shareholder. It is headquartered in Folsom, California.

The product set around the core **Magnit VMS** (formerly Wand VMS) covers direct sourcing with private talent pools, services procurement, employer of record (EOR) and, through its RightSourcing business, healthcare staffing. Magnit's VMS page claims 600+ customers, 1,800+ integrations, coverage in 130+ countries, and SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certification. The question Magnit answers: **can one vendor both run our contingent labor program and supply the system it runs on, without the program grading its own homework?**

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks

TA operations inherits contingent labor the moment a company starts direct sourcing or needs hiring managers to request contractors through the same front door as employees. Magnit's pitch is that you get one accountable firm instead of a VMS contract, an MSP contract and an EOR contract stitched together.

Two 2026 launches move it closer to the hiring manager. **Magnit Pulse**, launched on 23 June 2026, is a conversational layer that lets managers raise requisitions, read AI candidate summaries, ask spend questions in plain language and approve requests without logging into the VMS. It is live in Microsoft Teams and via API; Slack is not in the launch FAQ. **Magnit Gateway**, launched on 21 May 2026, faces the other way: an AI-assisted analytics product for staffing suppliers, built on cross-program performance data and priced by how many programs a supplier serves. For healthcare, **Magnit Shift** puts internal float pools and external per diem labor in the same VMS.

Analysts rate it in the top tier on both halves. Everest Group named Magnit a Leader in its 2026 CWM/MSP PEAK Matrix, assessed against 41 providers, and Magnit says it has been a Leader in Everest's VMS PEAK Matrix five years running through 2025. Ardent Partners named it a Market Leader in its 2026 Digital Staffing and Direct Sourcing Technology Advisor in March 2026.

## Pricing reality

Magnit publishes no price list for the VMS or the MSP; Pulse is sold as a tiered annual subscription, also unpriced. Budget by the category's percent-of-spend norms. Industry pricing guides citing Staffing Industry Analysts put MSP fees at roughly 1.5% to 3.5% of contingent spend, and VMS technology fees at about 0.5% to 1.5%. Beeline's published funding example combines a 1% VMS fee and a 2% MSP fee for a 3% total, which is a fair midpoint for a bundled Magnit quote: about $1.5M a year on a $50M program.

Most programs in North America are **supplier-funded**: the fee comes off each staffing supplier's invoice, so you never receive a software bill but you pay it inside bill rates. **Client-funded** deals put the fee on your own budget, which makes it visible and easier to benchmark. With one vendor providing both layers, ask for the VMS and MSP fees as separate lines; a single blended rate makes it impossible to compare either half against the market.

## Best for

Contingent workforce program owners and TA or procurement operations leads at enterprises spending $25M+ a year on temps, consultants and contractors who want to outsource program management and would rather hold one firm accountable for both software and service. Healthcare systems managing per diem and float labor are a specific fit through RightSourcing and Shift. Microsoft Teams shops get the most from Pulse today.

Skip it if procurement wants to keep the program team and the software independent of each other, or if you engage only a handful of freelancers: an EOR or contractor platform such as [Deel](/en/tools/deel/) is cheaper and faster. It is not an applicant tracking system either; employee hiring stays in [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/) or [Workday](/en/tools/workday/).

## Versus the alternatives

**[Beeline](/en/tools/beeline/)** and **[SAP Fieldglass](/en/tools/sap-fieldglass/)** are the two largest VMS vendors, and neither runs your program for you; an MSP of your choice sits on top. Pick Beeline when HR and finance run on mixed or non-SAP systems and you want an MCP endpoint for agents now. Pick Fieldglass when procurement owns the program on SAP S/4HANA and Ariba. Pick either over Magnit when separating software from service is a governance requirement, so you can replace the MSP at renewal without migrating the VMS.

**SimplifyVMS** is the fastest-rising independent VMS, an Everest Leader and Star Performer in both 2025 and 2026. Pick it for a vendor-neutral VMS without a services arm attached. **Workday VNDLY** fits when Workday HCM is the system of record and you want contractors in the same worker record as employees.

Pick Magnit when you have decided to outsource program management anyway and value one contract, one data model and one accountable vendor over the ability to swap layers independently.

## Watch-outs

- **The program team controls the system that measures it.** When the MSP and the VMS come from the same vendor, the supplier scorecards, fill-rate reports and savings claims that judge the MSP are produced by its own platform. *Guard:* contract direct, read-only data access and a full export of requisition, rate and invoice data, and have procurement or finance compute the MSP KPIs from the raw data each quarter.
- **Leadership is in transition.** Chandra Dhandapani, appointed CEO in December 2024, was quoted as CEO in the June 2026 Pulse release; Magnit's leadership page now lists Jim Bradley, the former COO, as interim CEO. *Guard:* put roadmap items you are buying for (Pulse integrations, Slack support, reporting changes) into the contract with dates, rather than relying on a sales deck.
- **Acquired platforms are still visible.** The login menu still lists Wand VMS and Workforce Logiq VMS alongside Magnit VMS. *Guard:* ask in writing which VMS instance your program will run on, and whether a migration is scheduled during your term.
- **Support responsiveness draws complaints.** Review volume is thin (G2 lists 12 reviews averaging 4.0), and one Gartner Peer Insights reviewer called customer service "a problem" with slow tech support. *Guard:* write response and resolution SLAs for P1 and configuration tickets into the contract, with service credits, and reference-check two programs of your size.
- **Pulse is new and Teams-first.** It launched in June 2026 with Microsoft Teams and API access only. *Guard:* pilot it with one business unit and confirm that approvals made in chat follow the same delegation limits as approvals made in the VMS.
