## What it is

Kayako is a long-running helpdesk that has been repackaged as an AI-first product. The platform is now sold as **Kayako One**: a shared inbox, ticketing, a public and private multilingual help center, live chat, automation rules, SLAs per channel, and reporting. On top sits **Kay**, an AI agent that triages, drafts replies, answers customers in 100+ languages, and, according to Kayako, can create users, issue refunds and update records before closing the case. Kay learns from your knowledge base and your past tickets, and rolls out in three phases: triage, answers, then continuous learning. Kayako also sells Kay alone on top of Zendesk, Freshdesk, Intercom or Salesforce.

It most resembles [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) or [Freshdesk](/en/tools/freshdesk/) with an Intercom-style per-resolution AI agent bolted in. Kayako has been owned by ESW Capital (the group behind Trilogy and Aurea) since March 2018; the 2026 site footer reads "ESW Operations, LLC". We found no acquisition, shutdown or sunset news in the last 12 months. The self-hosted **Kayako Classic** still exists and still gets quarterly packages, but Kayako's own page pushes Classic customers to migrate to the cloud.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Outcome pricing on the AI agent.** Kay costs $1 per ticket it fully resolves without a human. Escalations and partial answers are not billed, and a simple deflection does not count as a resolution.
- **Helpdesk and agent from one vendor.** Smaller teams that don't want to stitch Fin onto Zendesk get the inbox, help center and AI agent on one contract, with white-glove migration Kayako puts at 2-4 weeks.
- **A cheap exit from legacy Kayako.** Classic and old cloud customers can move to Kayako One without re-platforming to another vendor.

## Pricing reality

Kayako's own pages disagree, so read the contract, not the website. The main pricing page shows only **$1 per resolved ticket** with "no seat fees" and routes everything else to sales; the AI page calls the platform fee "custom base pricing". Other Kayako landing pages advertise **Kayako One at $79/mo plus $1 per AI-resolved ticket** with a 14-day trial, without saying whether the $79 is per agent or flat. Some third parties quote $79 per agent per month; we could not confirm that on kayako.com.

On the AI line, $1 per resolution is level with [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) Fin at $0.99 per outcome and Gorgias at roughly $0.90-$1.00 per automated interaction, cheaper than Zendesk's $1.50 committed or $2.00 pay-as-you-go automated resolution, and dearer than Help Scout's $0.75. At 1,000 AI-resolved tickets a month, Kay costs about $12,000 a year before the platform fee. Budget on the assumption that the $79 is per agent until the quote says otherwise: for a 10-agent team that puts the platform near $9,500 a year.

## Best for

Support leads at SMB and lower mid-market SaaS or ecommerce companies, roughly 3-25 agents and 500-5,000 tickets a month, who want a helpdesk and an outcome-priced AI agent on one bill and whose volume is mostly repetitive how-to and account questions. It also suits existing Kayako Classic customers who need a cloud path. It is the wrong pick for enterprises that need a SOC 2 report or a deep app marketplace, for teams that already run Zendesk, Freshdesk or Intercom well (add their native agent instead), and for buyers who can't absorb vendor-support risk.

## Watch-outs

- **The price you see is not the price you'll sign.** The pricing page, the AI page and the product page give three different framings of the platform fee. The guard: get a written quote that states the platform fee per agent or flat, monthly vs annual, the exact definition of a billable resolution, and a cap on renewal increases.
- **Vendor support and billing complaints.** Trustpilot shows 4.3 on only 22 reviews, but 68% are one-star, citing unanswered support, invoices after cancellation and large renewal hikes; G2 and Capterra sit around 4.0 (secondhand figures). Many complaint roundups come from competitors, so weigh them, but don't ignore the pattern. The guard: keep the first term to 12 months, put cancellation and notice terms in writing, and name a support contact with response times.
- **No official MCP server, and an unconfirmed SOC 2 status.** Kayako's help center states it has no official MCP connector; the only community server is read-only and works with the Classic API. The guard: if you need agents in Claude or ChatGPT to act on tickets, budget for REST API work, and ask for the SOC 2 report or security questionnaire before procurement starts.
- **Kayako's "80%+ deflection in 90 days" is vendor marketing.** The guard: pilot Kay on one queue for 30 days and measure resolved-without-reopen rate, not deflection.

## Versus the alternatives

[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) and [Freshdesk](/en/tools/freshdesk/) hold the most helpdesk market share. Pick Zendesk (Suite Team from $55 per agent per month annual) when you need a large app marketplace, enterprise compliance and room to grow past 50 agents. Pick Freshdesk (Growth from $19 per agent per month) when price per seat matters most and Freddy's roughly $0.49 session pricing suits your volume. [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) Fin is the fastest-growing AI-first entrant; pick it if chat is your main channel or you want the agent on top of an existing helpdesk, since Fin sells standalone. [Help Scout](/en/tools/help-scout/) is the pick for small teams wanting email-first simplicity and the cheapest AI resolution at $0.75. For heavy action-taking at enterprise scale, look at [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/). If none fit because volume is low, a shared inbox plus a help center will do until ticket volume justifies per-resolution AI.
