## What it is

ContractSafe is a contract repository that has grown into a mid-market CLM. Upload your executed agreements, and it extracts dates, parties and terms, sends renewal and expiration reminders, and makes the whole archive searchable. The two upper plans add approval workflows, redlining in Word, built-in e-signature, intake forms and AI contract review. It was founded in 2015 by Randy Bishop and Ken Button, who still run it, and took its first institutional money in 2023: a $27.5 million growth investment from Five Elms Capital. It is independent, not part of a legal-tech roll-up, and says it serves 1,900+ organizations.

The category leader it most resembles is [ContractWorks](/en/tools/contractworks/): a repository-first product with unlimited users, sold to companies whose legal team is small and whose contract users are many. What separates it in 2026 is the 26 August 2026 release, which ContractSafe calls its largest product expansion to date: "Ask AI" chat over a contract, AI extraction of 29 standard fields on every plan, AI Contract Review playbooks, configurable approval workflows, lifecycle tracking and Microsoft Word redlining. The AI is deliberately assistive. It flags and suggests; a person decides. That keeps this entry at `ai_native: false`.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **The price ignores headcount.** Every plan includes unlimited users. The bill moves with the number of active contracts in the repository, in bands from "100 or fewer" up to "10,000+". Archived documents and attachments do not count, so a contract with five drafts attached is one contract. That suits a company where sales, procurement, HR and finance all need to look things up, but legal is one or two people.
- **It publishes a list price.** Most CLMs in this catalog ([Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/), [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/), [Juro](/en/tools/juro/), [DocuSign IAM](/en/tools/docusign-iam/)) are quote-only. ContractSafe prints its starting prices, and the pricing page shows the price for each volume band from a dropdown, so you can build a budget line before a sales call.
- **Migration is included.** Onboarding, training, data and document migration and a dedicated customer success manager come with every plan, with no implementation fee. For a team moving off a shared drive, that removes the line item that usually stalls a first CLM purchase.

## Pricing reality

Three plans, all with unlimited users. The starting prices below are for the lowest contract-volume band, prepaid annually:

- **Organize — $450/month** ($5,400/year; $540/month if paid month to month). Repository, search, unlimited reminders and tagging, AI extraction of the 29 standard fields, Ask AI, custom fields, dashboards, custom roles, audit trail. No integrations and no e-signature.
- **Finalize — $660/month** ($7,920/year). Adds approval workflows, redlining, commenting, Word integration, unlimited built-in e-signature, an optional DocuSign connection, SSO (Okta, Active Directory or any SAML 2.0 provider) and Zapier.
- **Maximize — $815/month** ($9,780/year). Adds intake forms, templates, lifecycle tracking, AI Contract Review, AI extraction into custom fields, the Salesforce integration, the API and IP allowlisting.

What teams actually pay depends on two things: the contract band and the plan. Most mid-market buyers who want a CLM rather than a filing cabinet need Finalize, because signing and approvals live there. A company that wants AI review or a Salesforce link needs Maximize. Price moves up with each volume band, so check the dropdown at your expected 24-month repository size, not today's count. The seat math is where ContractSafe wins. [Concord](/en/tools/concord/) Essentials is $499/month for 5 users plus $49 for each extra user: at 25 users that is $1,479/month, against $660 for ContractSafe Finalize at the lowest band with the same 25 people, or 250. An optional "Jump Start" service, where paralegals extract data from legacy contracts, costs from $2.50 per contract.

## Best for

A General Counsel or Legal Ops lead at a 50-1,000-employee company with one to five legal staff, a few hundred to a few thousand contracts, and 15 or more people outside legal who need to find, approve or sign agreements. ContractSafe's own customer data fits that band: the median customer has about 250 employees and has invited 15 users.

**Do not buy ContractSafe** if your contract process is the hard part, not your contract archive. Multi-business-unit approval logic, Salesforce-native quote-to-contract flows at volume, or a clause library that drives self-serve drafting by sales all push you to a heavier CLM. Also skip it if you run fewer than about 50 live contracts with one or two users: a shared drive with naming rules plus a signing tool costs less than the $5,400/year floor.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/)** — the default comparison for mid-market and enterprise in-house teams. Pick it when workflow design across many teams justifies $50K-$500K+ a year and a multi-month implementation. ContractSafe Maximize at the lowest band costs under $10K a year, with migration included.
- **[DocuSign IAM](/en/tools/docusign-iam/)** — pick it when the company already standardizes on DocuSign and wants agreement data and signing on one vendor contract. ContractSafe's own signing runs on Dropbox Sign, with DocuSign as an optional connection.
- **[SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/)** — the fastest-growing AI-first entrant. Pick it when AI review inside Word against your playbook is the main job and a $15K-$35K entry contract is acceptable. ContractSafe's AI review exists but sits only on Maximize and is assistive by design.
- **[Concord](/en/tools/concord/)** — pick it when eight or fewer people will touch contracts and you want a GA MCP server for Claude or ChatGPT (it needs a Horizon-enabled plan, so get that named in the order form). Concord Essentials includes signing at $499 for 5 users, while ContractSafe needs Finalize at $660 for it; from the ninth user on, ContractSafe's unlimited users win on cost.
- **[ContractWorks](/en/tools/contractworks/)** — the closest like-for-like. Both sell unlimited users priced by document volume. Pick ContractWorks if you already buy from Onit. Pick ContractSafe if you want a published price and the 2026 AI and workflow features.

## Watch-outs

- **Organize is a repository, not a CLM.** It has no integrations, no SSO and no e-signature. *Guard:* if anyone will sign or approve inside the tool, budget Finalize ($660/month) from day one rather than discovering the gap after rollout.
- **Every active contract counts, and you can only downgrade at the end of a term.** *Guard:* archive expired and superseded agreements before migration, and size your band on the contracts you will hold in 24 months. Upgrades mid-term are prorated, so starting one band low costs little.
- **"Native" e-signature runs on Dropbox Sign.** *Guard:* send Dropbox Sign to your security review as a sub-processor before you sign, or connect your existing DocuSign account instead.
- **Review aggregates flag limited customization, missing bulk actions and uneven search and AI results** (G2, about 4.7/5 from about 130 reviews; Capterra, about 4.8/5). *Guard:* use the 7-day trial on 50 of your real contracts, not the demo set, and check extraction accuracy on the fields you care about. Extraction into custom fields is Maximize-only.
- **Billing terms read two ways.** Plan cards say "Prepaid Annually"; the FAQ says there is no annual commitment and quotes a $540 month-to-month Organize rate. *Guard:* get the term, renewal date and band in the order form.
- **No MCP server and an API only on Maximize.** *Guard:* if contract data has to flow into a warehouse, an AI assistant or a CPQ, price Maximize, or pick Concord for MCP access on a Horizon-enabled plan.
