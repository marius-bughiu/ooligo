## What it is

Avionté sells AviontéBOLD, a front- and back-office platform for US staffing firms: applicant tracking, client CRM, onboarding with e-signature, time entry, payroll, and client billing in one system. The company is based in Minnesota and has been backed by Serent Capital since 2014; Serent co-founder Kevin Frick chairs its board. It has grown by acquisition. WorkN, a mobile talent app, is now sold as Avionté Mobile. SimpleVMS, a vendor management system, was bought in 2023. AkkenCloud, a staffing-software competitor, was acquired in August 2024.

The payroll side is the point of difference. In June 2024 Avionté said almost 800 staffing agencies used its back office and that it issued more than 4 million W-2s in 2023. That same month it began selling AviontéBOLD Back Office (time entry, payroll, and billing) on its own, connected to other ATSs through its API, with ADP's payroll tax service as an integration.

The AI layer arrived late and is still thin. In March 2025 Avionté announced a job-description generator for all BOLD users and a candidate-screening chatbot built on Anthropic's Claude for customers on the AviontéPIXEL upgrade. The Summer 2026 release, published 26 August 2026, replaced the old auto-matcher with an **AI Matching Agent**. When a job order is created, it ranks candidates from the agency's own database, lets recruiters weight skills, pay, location, and work history, and shows the reasoning behind each match. Leadership also changed. CEO Rishabh Mehrotra died in July 2025, and Dirk Izzo, formerly Fullsteam's president and COO, took over on 15 June 2026.

This page answers one question: **should a mid-market staffing firm buy an all-in-one with payroll built in, or Bullhorn plus integrations?**

## Why it shows up in light-industrial and clerical stacks

- **Payroll is in the same database as the placement.** A temp's assignment, pay rate, bill rate, timecard, check, and invoice sit on one record. Avionté's 2026 features are back-office work: workers' compensation code management with an audit history, client credit limits on the company profile, and salary enforcement on job posts for pay-transparency rules.
- **High-volume shift work needs mobile.** Avionté Mobile pushes open shifts to workers, and the Summer 2026 release added reporting on how those offers are distributed and answered.
- **AI vendors write back to it.** [HeyMilo](/en/tools/heymilo/), [Tenzo](/en/tools/tenzo-ai/), [ConverzAI](/en/tools/converzai/), and [Ribbon](/en/tools/ribbon-ai/) all list Avionté as an ATS target. So do the programmatic job-ad platforms [Talroo](/en/tools/talroo/) and [Joveo](/en/tools/joveo/). Avionté says its partner ecosystem is called Avionté+. Its DataLink feed exposes more than 5,500 data elements to BI tools such as Power BI and Tableau.

## Pricing reality

Avionté publishes no prices, and we found no buyer-side benchmark for it. Directory listings are the only public numbers, and they disagree. ITQlick and SaaSrat put the entry point around **$750 per month**. SaaSrat estimates **$2,250+ per month** once implementation, add-ons, and renewal increases are included. Capterra shows a $10-per-user headline without saying what it covers. Treat all of these as rough guides. The quote is built from modules (ATS, CRM, payroll, billing, mobile, VMS), users, branches, and implementation scope.

For comparison, [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) publishes $99 and $165 per user per month for its lower tiers, but its back office and AI are quote-only. That makes the honest comparison a three-year total: Avionté's single quote against Bullhorn's seats, its Middle Office, and the cost of a separate payroll service.

## Best for

Owners and operations leads at US staffing firms of roughly 10–150 internal staff placing light-industrial, clerical, or warehouse temps on weekly payroll, who want to stop reconciling an ATS against a separate payroll system. Concretely: a regional industrial agency running 1,500 weekly timecards across six branches that today exports hours from its ATS into a payroll bureau every Monday.

**Not for you if** you run a perm or executive-search desk with no payroll. The back office is dead weight there, and [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) or [Recruit CRM](/en/tools/recruit-crm/) fit better. It is also a poor fit if most of your billing goes through client VMS portals at IT-contracting volume, where [JobDiva](/en/tools/jobdiva/) or [Ceipal](/en/tools/ceipal/) are built for that work.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/)** is the installed-base leader in staffing, with a 350+ partner marketplace and the deepest agentic AI in the segment through its Amplify skills. Pick Bullhorn when integration breadth and AI matter more than one database, or when you place across several verticals and countries. Pick Avionté when payroll is the operational center of the business and you would otherwise pay Bullhorn's Middle Office plus a payroll provider.

**[JobDiva](/en/tools/jobdiva/)** is the IT-contracting pick, with VMS sync and DivaFinancials for timesheets and billing. **[Ceipal](/en/tools/ceipal/)** is cheaper per seat, with a younger, separately priced workforce module. **TempWorks** is the closest like-for-like rival for light-industrial payroll, and the one to put next to Avionté on a shortlist. **[Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/)** is the fastest-growing platform in the segment, but it has no back office. Pick it only for perm placement where sourcing is the bottleneck.

## Watch-outs

- **The back-office rebuild is not finished.** At CONNECT 2026 Avionté said its most-used screens were modernized, but that the deeper payroll and time-entry layer is still ahead. Guard: run one full pay period, with taxes, garnishments, and multi-state workers, in parallel in a sandbox before signing. Write the payroll user interface you are buying, not the roadmap, into the order.
- **The AI data story has shifted.** The 2025 chatbot was announced as built on Anthropic and AWS. In 2026 Avionté says its AI works on customer data that "never leaves" the platform. Both can be true, since a model can be hosted inside a vendor's cloud account, but you need it in writing. Guard: ask for the AI subprocessor list and data-retention terms for the Matching Agent and PIXEL. Run an adverse-impact check by job category before you let match rankings drive who gets called, since NYC Local Law 144 and Colorado's AI Act (SB 24-205) apply to automated hiring decisions.
- **Reviews flag account management and long implementations.** Capterra averages 3.9 across 168 reviews. Review aggregators summarize complaints about ignored escalations and implementations longer than modern SaaS. Guard: name an implementation lead and go-live date in the contract, attach a support SLA with escalation contacts, and cap renewal increases. Third-party reviews also cite mid-contract price rises.
