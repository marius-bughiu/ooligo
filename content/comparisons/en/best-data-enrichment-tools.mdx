Three data enrichment tools for different jobs: custom research, company and contact data, and combined prospecting and outreach. The order below is a reading order, not a measured accuracy ranking. Choose against your own records and acceptance rules.

## 1. Clay — custom research and review steps

Clay's [Use AI](https://university.clay.com/docs/use-ai-integration-overview) supports web research, structured output fields and conditional runs. Consider it when your process needs additional research or custom review gates around enrichment.

**What it replaces:** identify the research and routing steps you would move into Clay. Do not assume buying it cancels every data subscription or removes integration maintenance.

**Where to start:** set a spending cap and use the [enrichment evaluation guide](/en/learn/data-enrichment-strategies/) to define accepted records. Try the [Clay and Claude sample](/en/workflows/lead-enrichment-clay-claude/) first: its 20 invented records run offline, without paid accounts. It demonstrates evidence checks and scoring, not live Clay setup or data accuracy. Review a real pilot's output before exporting any outreach list.

**[Full Clay review →](/en/tools/clay/)**

## 2. ZoomInfo — company and contact data

ZoomInfo provides [company and contact search and enrichment](https://docs.gtm.ai/docs/overview). Evaluate it when you need a data supplier and can test the required fields in your target segment. This page does not establish that its coverage beats other providers.

**What it replaces:** a data contract only if the replacement meets your field, access and export requirements. Check the actual package before assuming other products are included.

**Where to start:** if your current ZoomInfo process works, use it as the baseline. Add another tool only for a documented gap, then compare accepted records and review time on the same sample.

**[Full ZoomInfo review →](/en/tools/zoominfo/)**

## 3. Apollo — prospecting and outreach together

Apollo combines prospecting data with [sequences and calling](https://www.apollo.io/product/sales-engagement). Its [waterfall enrichment](https://www.apollo.io/product/waterfall) can consult partner sources after Apollo's data. Consider it when you want these jobs in one system.

**What it replaces:** compare combined subscription, usage and review costs against the separate tools you actually need. We have not established a universal cost advantage over ZoomInfo or a separate sequencer.

**Where to start:** test the combined process before adding custom orchestration. Read [Clay vs Apollo](/en/vs/clay-vs-apollo/) when research or review requirements exceed that process. Rep count alone is not a reason to switch.

**[Full Apollo review →](/en/tools/apollo/)**

## What is outside this shortlist

Cognism, Lusha, Hunter and RocketReach are outside this three-tool comparison; their omission is not a coverage verdict. If you already use one, include it as the baseline. Compare direct access with access through another platform using your required fields and contract terms.

## The minimum viable choice

- Custom research and review steps: evaluate **Clay**.
- A company and contact data supplier: evaluate **ZoomInfo**.
- Prospecting and outreach in one system: evaluate **Apollo**.

If none of those jobs is missing, keep the current process. Measure missing fields, rejected records, review minutes and total cost per accepted record before buying another layer. This is a source-reviewed shortlist, not a live vendor benchmark; the linked offline sample cannot establish coverage, deliverability or sales results.
