## What it is

Replit is a cloud development environment that became an AI app builder. You describe an app to Replit Agent, and it writes the code, provisions a database, runs it in the browser and deploys it on Replit's hosting. Agent 4, launched in March 2026, runs several agents in parallel on one project and builds web apps, mobile apps, data apps and slide decks from the same chat. Unlike prompt-to-app tools that hide the code, Replit keeps a full editor and shell one click away, so an engineer can take over inside the same workspace.

The company is independent, and its CEO Amjad Masad has said publicly that it intends to stay that way. On 11 March 2026 it closed a $400M Series D led by Georgian at a $9B valuation, triple the $3B it was valued at in its September 2025 Series C. Replit has said it is on track for $1B in run-rate revenue by the end of 2026; Sacra estimated about $525M annualised in April 2026. That revenue estimate is third-party, not a company disclosure.

## Why ops teams end up here

The question ops leaders are actually asking is whether a team should build its own internal tools in Replit, in Lovable, or in a governed builder such as Retool or Superblocks. Replit's answer is data apps. Its Enterprise plan ships first-party connectors to Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and Hex, and a Microsoft Fabric connector that publishes apps into Fabric against the builder's own Microsoft Entra tenant. That makes Replit the prompt-to-app builder most likely to be pointed at the warehouse rather than at a fresh, empty database.

Governance arrived in 2026. Enterprise adds SSO with SCIM provisioning (Okta, Azure AD, Google Workspace, OneLogin), role-based access mapped to identity-provider groups, audit logs streamed to a SIEM, and admin controls to require private deployments, ban public apps, mandate security scans and pin deployment geographies. Admins can also scope which teams and apps may use each connector, and bring their own OAuth app for connectors.

## A use case worth buying it for

A RevOps or finance-ops analyst needs an internal data app over the warehouse: a pipeline-coverage explorer on Snowflake, a renewal-risk dashboard joining product usage in Databricks with CRM fields, or a recruiting-funnel view for TA leadership. The app reads governed tables, is visible only to employees signed in through SSO, and changes weekly as the questions change. Build it in Replit on Enterprise with private deployments enforced, keep the connector scoped to read-only credentials, and let the analyst iterate without filing an engineering ticket.

## Pricing

Replit prices in credits that the Agent spends according to the effort each task takes, so there is no fixed price per feature. Core is $20/month, or $18/month billed annually, and includes $20 of monthly credits. Pro is $100/month, or $90/month billed annually, with $100 of credits, up to 15 collaborators, premium support and database rollback for up to 28 days; larger Pro tiers run at $215, $425, $825 and $2,000 a month (annual billing, replit.com/pricing, checked 30 October 2026). Pro replaced the old Teams plan in February 2026. Usage beyond the included credits is billed pay-as-you-go.

Enterprise has been self-serve since 21 May 2026: you commit to an annual credit amount, every committed dollar becomes a dollar of credits pooled across the organisation, and seats are unlimited. Replit sends contracts above $200K in spend to its sales team. The real-world band for an ops team running two or three data apps is $90-215/month on Pro while piloting; once SSO and connector controls become mandatory, budget for an annual Enterprise commitment, and Replit's upgrade wizard shows commitments in the $10K-200K range.

## Best for

RevOps, finance-ops and analytics leads at 100-2,000-person companies who want analysts to build and maintain internal data apps over Snowflake, Databricks or Fabric, with IT controlling access through SSO and private deployments.

## Alternatives, and when to pick them instead

- **[Retool](/en/tools/retool/)** — the segment leader for engineer-built internal tools. Pick it when the builders write SQL, the app writes back to production systems, and you need per-query permissions.
- **Microsoft Power Apps** — the other market-share leader, bundled into Microsoft 365 licensing. Pick it when the app is a form over SharePoint or Dataverse and IT owns the build.
- **[Lovable](/en/tools/lovable/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in the segment by revenue. Pick it when the app is a polished, external-facing portal or form and the warehouse is not the data source.
- **[Superblocks](/en/tools/superblocks/)** — pick it when governance is the reason for the evaluation and every app must run on centrally managed connections with row-level permissions.
- **[Claude Code](/en/tools/claude-code/)** or **[Cursor](/en/tools/cursor/)** — pick them when an engineer will own the app and it belongs in your own repository and hosting.

## Watch-outs

- **The Agent can act on production data.** In July 2025, during SaaStr founder Jason Lemkin's 12-day trial, Replit's Agent deleted a live production database during a declared code freeze and then misreported what it had done. Masad called it unacceptable, and Replit responded by separating development and production databases so the development environment cannot reach production. Guard: give connectors read-only warehouse credentials, keep any database the app writes to on Pro or Enterprise with rollback enabled, and review the Agent's proposed schema changes before deploying.
- **Effort-based credits make spend hard to forecast.** One complex request can cost far more than a simple one, and overage bills automatically. Guard: pilot on a single Pro seat for a full billing cycle, record credits spent per shipped feature, and set a usage budget in billing settings before you add collaborators.
- **Governance is Enterprise-only.** Core and Pro have no SSO, SCIM, audit-log streaming or admin deployment controls, so an app built there is protected only by whatever the generated code enforces. Guard: allow no app that touches customer or employee data outside Enterprise, and turn on "require private deployments" and "ban public apps" on day one.
- **The Fabric connector is still a private preview.** It requires a Fabric tenant with Power BI semantic models and acceptance into Replit's tester programme. Guard: do not plan a Microsoft-shop rollout around it until Replit's docs mark it generally available; start with Snowflake, Databricks or BigQuery instead.
