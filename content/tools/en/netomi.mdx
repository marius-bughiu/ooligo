## What it is

Netomi is an enterprise agentic customer-service platform: one AI layer that answers and resolves customer requests across chat, email, voice, SMS, social messaging and in-app surfaces, sitting on top of the help desk and contact-center systems you already run rather than replacing them. It started life as msg.ai and is led by founder and CEO Puneet Mehta. In April 2026 it raised a $110M Series C led by Accenture Ventures, with Adobe Ventures, WndrCo, Silver Lake Waterman, NAVER Ventures, Metis Strategy and Fin Capital participating, taking total funding past $160M. Public customers include Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, MetLife, Paramount, DraftKings, the NBA and Ingram Micro.

The product splits into three parts. **Autopilot** handles conversations end to end, including multi-step transactions. **Co-Pilot** works inside the human agent's workspace (Salesforce, Genesys, NICE, ServiceNow, Amazon Connect) and drafts or assists. **Agentic Studio** is the no-code control layer where a CX team configures, tests and deploys agent behavior. Under it, Netomi runs third-party models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google rather than its own model, wrapped in what it calls a governed orchestration pipeline: deterministic rules decide what the agent is allowed to do, and the LLM decides how to say it.

## Why it shows up in enterprise CX stacks

- **It goes in through the systems integrator, not around it.** The Accenture deal is more than a cap-table line: Accenture formed a global alliance to embed Netomi into client CX programs and build a deployment playbook for it. If your contact-center modernization already runs through Accenture, Netomi is the agent layer that arrives with the program instead of a separate vendor your SI has to learn.
- **It overlays your stack instead of replacing it.** Netomi ships apps in the Zendesk Marketplace (Virtual Agent and Agent Assist) and integrates with Salesforce, Freshdesk, Kustomer, Khoros, ServiceNow and Genesys. You keep the help desk, routing and reporting you have; Netomi takes the front of the conversation and hands off inside the tools your agents already use.
- **It is built for peak load in regulated businesses.** Netomi reports DraftKings traffic at 40,000+ concurrent requests per second with sub-3-second responses, and it lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, HIPAA and PCI DSS on its security page. That combination is why airlines, insurers and sportsbooks show up on the customer list.

## Pricing reality

There is no price list, no calculator and no self-serve trial; every deal is sales-led. Quotes are built from deployment scope, channels activated and automated-interaction volume, not seats. Vendr's procurement data puts the **median annual contract at about $85K, with a typical range of $77.5K to $103K**. That is roughly half of the $150K–$350K+ band Sierra contracts tend to land in, which makes Netomi the cheaper entry point into enterprise-grade agentic CX. Budget separately for implementation: deployments are multi-quarter projects with scoping, knowledge ingestion, guardrail setup and weekly tuning, and if Accenture runs the rollout, its services fees sit on top of the Netomi license.

## Best for

CX and contact-center leaders at large, regulated or high-peak businesses (airlines, insurance, financial services, betting, media) who want one agent layer across chat, email and voice, deployed inside an existing Zendesk, Salesforce or Genesys estate, often as part of an SI-led transformation program. It is the right pick when "can this survive our security review and our Super Bowl Sunday traffic" matters more than "can we launch it this month."

Skip it if you handle a few thousand tickets a month (a contract around the $85K median will not pay back), if you want to self-serve and launch without a sales cycle, or if your need stops at answering FAQs from a help center.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) Agentforce** and **[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) AI agents** are the share leaders, because they live inside the help desk or CRM you already pay for. Pick them when one-vendor consolidation matters more than the strongest standalone agent and your channels are mostly inside that platform.
- **[Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/)** and **[Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/)** are the fastest-growing pure-play AI-agent vendors. Pick Sierra when you want outcome-based pricing and agents that execute payments and retention saves end to end; pick Decagon when you are an internet-native business with a deflection-led support model. Pick Netomi over both when your rollout goes through Accenture or another global SI, or when the annual contract has to stay under $100K.
- **[Kore.ai](/en/tools/kore-ai/)** competes for the same regulated enterprise buyer with a broader platform that also covers employee-facing agents. Pick it when IT wants one conversational-AI platform for customers and staff.
- **[Ada](/en/tools/ada/)** is the mid-market option with faster time to launch. Pick it when you need agents live in weeks and your volume does not justify a multi-quarter project.

## Watch-outs

- **Implementation is long and services-heavy.** Netomi's value depends on integrations, knowledge ingestion and guardrail tuning, and its customer-success team typically meets clients weekly. Guard: contract a fixed-scope first journey (one channel, one high-volume intent set) with a named go-live date and a containment target, and tie the second phase of spend to hitting it.
- **Opaque pricing makes the billing unit easy to misread.** Netomi does not publish whether you pay per interaction, per resolution or per channel, and the answer moves your total cost more than the headline number. Guard: get the billable unit, overage rate and the definition of an "automated" interaction in writing, then model cost at 2x your forecast volume before signing.
- **Model dependence is third-party.** Netomi orchestrates OpenAI, Anthropic and Google models, so model deprecations and behavior changes upstream flow into your agent. Guard: require change notification for model swaps, keep a regression test set of your top 50 intents, and re-run it before any model change goes live.
- **The SI route can blur accountability.** When Accenture deploys and Netomi supplies the platform, a failed journey can bounce between them. Guard: write a single owner for containment and accuracy SLAs into the statement of work, with Netomi's platform SLAs referenced explicitly.
