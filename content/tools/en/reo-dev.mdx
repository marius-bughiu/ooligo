## What it is

Reo.Dev turns developer activity into account-level sales signals for companies that sell to engineers. It watches the places developers evaluate a tool before anyone fills a form: GitHub stars, forks, issues and pull requests; npm, pip, Docker, Maven and 40+ other package managers; documentation page views and copied code snippets; open-source telemetry from self-hosted deployments; anonymous GitHub or Gmail sign-ups; and mentions on Reddit, Stack Exchange, Hacker News and community Slack or Discord. It resolves that activity to a developer and their company, scores the account, and pushes it to the CRM, Slack or an MCP client.

The newest input is **Agent Intent**. When a coding agent reads your docs or calls your MCP server, Reo.Dev logs that interaction as a buying signal and shows which accounts are behind it. For a devtool, a growing share of evaluation now happens through an agent rather than a human in a browser, so this is the signal most competitors cannot see yet.

The company is venture-backed and growing. It raised an $11.3M Series A led by Elevation Capital in July 2026, with Heavybit, India Quotient and Foster Ventures returning, for $15.3M raised in total. The site claims 200+ devtool customers, with logos including NVIDIA, LangChain, n8n, ElevenLabs, Temporal and ClickHouse.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **It covers the signal the CRM never sees.** Developers install, star and self-host long before they talk to sales. Reo.Dev attaches those events to an account so an AE can call the company whose platform team pulled your Docker image 400 times last week.
- **Package and telemetry depth.** Generic intent vendors read website visits and third-party content consumption. Reo.Dev reads package installs and OSS telemetry, which is where developer evaluation actually happens.
- **MCP and API.** The Reo MCP connects account data to Claude Desktop (with five bundled skills), ChatGPT developer mode and Cursor. Data-out and product-usage APIs are documented, so signals can feed [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) tables or a warehouse without the UI.
- **Routing into the sales stack.** Docs cover [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/), [Attio](/en/tools/attio/), [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/), [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/), [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/), Gong flows, [Slack](/en/tools/slack/) alerts and webhooks, plus [RB2B](/en/tools/rb2b/) for website visitor identification.

## Pricing reality

Reo.Dev publishes no pricing page; its /pricing URL returns a 404. The best public anchor is its AWS Marketplace listing, checked on 2026-11-23, which shows three 12-month contracts:

- **Startup** — $12,000/year; 2 seats and 5,000 monitored contacts/accounts
- **Pro** — $30,000/year; 10 seats and 25,000 contacts/accounts
- **Business** — $50,000/year; 20 seats and 50,000 contacts/accounts

A "contact" is an individual developer profile and an "account" is the company behind it, and seats scale with volume: you cannot buy more monitored accounts without moving up a tier. No refunds unless the vendor breaches the agreement or SLA. The homepage offers a "Try for Free" sign-up, but no free plan is published. G2 reviewers rate it 4.9 from 73 reviews; the recurring complaints are price and the lack of a month-to-month or startup plan.

Read the tiers as roughly $1,000, $2,500 and $4,200 a month. Against [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/), whose Essential tier is $30,000 a year, Reo.Dev's Startup tier costs 40% as much, and its Pro tier matches Common Room on price with twice the seats.

## Best for

RevOps and growth leaders at open-source or developer-tool companies, from seed to roughly $50M ARR, whose product is installed through a package manager, a container or a CLI, and who need AEs to know which companies are running the free version. The scoped use case: turning "a platform team at a 2,000-person bank pulled our Helm chart" into a routed account with a named owner within a day.

**Not for you if** your buyers are not developers. A sales tool, a finance product or an HR platform produces almost no package or repo signal, and you pay for coverage you never use. Also not for you if anonymous self-hosting is a selling point you promised your community; see the second watch-out.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/)** — the incumbent signal platform, from $30,000/year. Pick it when community, social and job-change signals matter as much as developer activity, and you sell beyond devtools.
- **[Clay](/en/tools/clay/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in signal and enrichment tooling, from $185/month. Pick it when you have a GTM engineer to build your own signal tables; it has no package-manager or telemetry source of its own, so it usually sits downstream of a feed like Reo.Dev.
- **[RB2B](/en/tools/rb2b/)** or **[Warmly](/en/tools/warmly/)** — website visitor identification from $0–$159/month. Pick one when your evaluation happens on the marketing site, not in a terminal.
- **[Sumble](/en/tools/sumble/)** — technographic and hiring signals from $99/user/month. Pick it when the question is which companies run the stack you integrate with, rather than who is using your product.

If none fits, start with [intent data](/en/learn/intent-data/) and [signal-based selling](/en/learn/signal-based-selling/) to decide which signal your reps will act on first.

## Watch-outs

- **The integrations page and the docs disagree.** On 2026-11-23 every entry on reo.dev/integrations, including Salesforce and HubSpot, carried a "Coming soon" label, while the docs describe the same connectors as live. **Guard:** during the trial, push ten real accounts into your CRM and confirm the field mapping before you sign; write the CRM sync into the order form.
- **Identifying developers can collide with open-source trust.** Reo.Dev enriches telemetry and usage data with login IDs and IP addresses and de-anonymizes free sign-ups. A community that learns its installs feed a sales tool can react badly. **Guard:** update your telemetry and privacy disclosure before turning on the OSS and sign-up sources, and keep EU data subject to your own GDPR review rather than relying on the vendor's badge.
- **Account volume is capped per tier.** Startup monitors 5,000 contacts/accounts; a popular open-source project can exceed that in a quarter. **Guard:** count unique companies in your last 90 days of package and docs traffic before choosing a tier, and negotiate the overage or step-up rate in writing.
- **Signal without routing becomes noise.** Hundreds of "account is active" alerts a week train reps to ignore Slack. **Guard:** launch with two or three signal-to-action rules, each with an owner and an SLA, and track accepted-account rate per signal before adding more.
