## What it is

DiliTrust is a Paris-headquartered legal and governance suite that sells five modules on one platform: **Board Portal** (agendas, board packs, votes, minutes), **Entity Management**, **Contract Lifecycle Management**, **Matter Management**, and a **Dataroom**. The company says it has served legal departments for more than 30 years and now counts more than 2,500 customers in over 60 countries, with offices in Paris, Montreal, Madrid, Milan, Berlin, Munich, Dubai, Mexico City and Philadelphia. Named customers on its site include LVMH, BNP Paribas, Danone, Veolia, Renault and Sodexo.

The growth story is acquisitive. Eurazeo, Cathay Capital and Sagard NewGen invested in 2022 in a deal reported at €130M. DiliTrust then bought Hyperlex, the French AI contract-analysis startup (July 2022), doeLEGAL, a Delaware matter-management and e-billing vendor founded in 1971 (September 2024), and PACTA, a German CLM vendor (February 2025). Each deal filled a module or a market: doeLEGAL gave it a US base and US customers, PACTA gave it the DACH contract market. The company reports that 61% of 2025 revenue came from outside France and that the Americas grew from 18% to 36% of revenue between 2023 and 2025. Gartner listed it as a sample vendor for legal entity management in its May 2026 legal technology market map.

The buyer is a general counsel or legal ops lead in a European-headquartered or multinational group who wants entity records, the board calendar, contracts and disputes in one contract with one vendor, rather than four tools stitched together by a paralegal.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **One record set across governance and contracts.** The entity that signs a contract, the board that approved it, and the matter it ends up in sit on the same platform. Peers make you buy that join: Diligent sells board and entities but not CLM, Athennian sells entities but not a board portal, and a CLM specialist sells neither.
- **The board portal is a real board portal.** Agendas, board packs, votes and minutes generation run in the same suite as the entity register, and DiliTrust Sign handles signatures natively without a third-party e-signature account. For a corporate secretary, that means the resolution, the minutes and the updated officer list can come out of one workflow.
- **Lini is the AI layer across modules.** DiliTrust's proprietary engine handles automated data extraction, document summarization, a risk detector for contracts, audio transcription and minutes generation, plus an assistant called Ask Lini. A "Choose Your Own AI" option and a sovereign-hosting posture matter for European buyers whose works councils or data-protection officers block US-hosted models.
- **Security paperwork is ready.** ISO 27001 and ISO 27701 certification plus SOC 2 Type II compliance cover most procurement questionnaires without a custom audit.
- **Legora can now read it.** On November 11, 2026, DiliTrust and [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) announced an MCP-based integration in early-access beta. Legora reads Contract Management and Entity Management data under DiliTrust permissions, for contract research, due-diligence review, drafting from approved templates, and entity queries such as jurisdiction, shareholders and share capital. Writes, approvals and signatures stay in DiliTrust.

## Pricing reality

DiliTrust publishes no prices. Every deal is a quote, sized by module mix, user count and, for entities, entity volume, with a dedicated account manager and unlimited training included in every subscription. No third-party marketplace data (Vendr or similar) was available for DiliTrust when this page was checked.

Budget against the published comparables instead. Athennian lists entity management alone at **$25,000/year for 100 entities**, and Vendr's marketplace data puts the median Diligent contract around **$23,800/year**. Our estimate, not a DiliTrust figure: a single module for a mid-sized group should land in that low-to-mid five-figure band. Two or three modules is a different purchase, and the argument for DiliTrust only holds if that bundle undercuts buying a board portal, an entity tool and a CLM separately. Get a standalone quote for each of those three point tools, and price the DiliTrust bundle against their sum, not against any one of them.

Reviewers flag value for money as the weakest score: it is the lowest of Capterra's sub-ratings at 4.4, and a G2 reviewer cites high cost and non-trivial implementation.

## Best for

General counsel and legal ops leads at groups of roughly 30-500 entities, headquartered in Europe or Latin America or running a large footprint there, who need the board cycle, the entity register and the contract repository under one vendor and one security review. It is the strongest pick when the board portal is a hard requirement and data residency in Europe is a procurement condition.

It is a weaker pick for a US-only company that needs a specialist CLM: the suite's contract module competes with specialists that put more into negotiation and AI redlining.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Diligent Entities](/en/tools/diligent-entities/)**: the market-share leader in board and entity governance. Pick it when the board already runs on Diligent Boards and the board, not contracts, is the centre of gravity. You get board plus entities from one vendor, but you still buy CLM elsewhere.
- **[CSC Entity Management](/en/tools/csc-entity-management/)**: the other incumbent by share. Pick it when you want registered-agent service, filings and software from one provider. It is a service relationship more than a product choice.
- **[Athennian](/en/tools/athennian/)**: the fastest-growing entity-management specialist, with a published price and agentic entity workflows. Pick it when entities are the primary job and you would rather buy the best entity tool than a suite.
- **[MinuteBox](/en/tools/minutebox/)**: pick it for a lighter, minute-book-first entity tool when the entity count is modest and a board portal is not in scope.
- **[Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/)**: pick a CLM specialist when contracts are the volume problem and entities and board are handled adequately elsewhere.

If none of these fit, run the board on your existing collaboration suite, keep a maintained entity register with an owner and a review date on every row, and buy software when one person can no longer reconcile the register before a board meeting. ## Watch-outs

- **Suite pricing hides the per-module cost.** A competitor notes that DiliTrust's board portal comes inside the suite, and DiliTrust itself publishes nothing. Guard: ask for a line-item quote per module and a price for the board portal alone, then compare each line against the standalone comparables above before signing.
- **The suite is assembled from acquisitions.** Hyperlex, doeLEGAL and PACTA each came with their own codebase and customer base, and integration depth across modules is what you are paying for. Guard: in the trial, run one end-to-end case that crosses modules, such as a board approval that updates an officer record and triggers a contract amendment, and see whether data flows or gets re-keyed.
- **Form and template setup takes time.** Reviewers report that building forms is slow at first and that implementation is not always simple. Guard: get a named implementation lead and a fixed go-live scope in the order form, and migrate your highest-volume template first.
- **AI is per-module and the open AI hooks are new.** Lini runs inside DiliTrust, and the Legora connection is an early-access beta announced in November 2026. DiliTrust does not ship a general-purpose MCP server, so Claude or Copilot access means building against its API. Guard: confirm in writing which Lini features are in your tier and which data objects the API exposes before you plan an AI workspace around it. Background: [MCP server explained](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).
- **US depth is still being built.** The US presence came through the 2024 doeLEGAL deal and a Philadelphia office, and its reference customers skew European. Guard: if you are US-headquartered, ask for two US references at your entity count and confirm US-based support hours before signing a multi-year term.
