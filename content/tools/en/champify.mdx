## What it is

Champify watches the contacts already sitting in a firm's Salesforce org and fires an event when one of those relationships turns into pipeline somewhere else. The core event is the job change: a contact the firm sold to, lost to, or supported takes a role at a different company. Champify calls this Relationship Tracking, and layers four more products around it: Insider AI, which reads call transcripts, email, and CRM history to build nurture plays against target accounts; New Executive Hires at accounts already under coverage; Calendar Sync, which creates contacts from meeting invites; and CRM Clean, which repairs stale contact records.

The design decision that defines the product is delivery. Signals land as records and fields inside Salesforce through a managed package, not in a separate console with its own login. It sits in [signal-based selling](/en/learn/signal-based-selling/) next to [UserGems](/en/tools/usergems/) and [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/), and it is deliberately the narrowest of the three.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Salesforce-native by construction.** Champify is an AppExchange partner, and the managed package carries unlimited users on every tier. Signals write to the objects that routing, forecasting, and reporting already read, so a [LeanData](/en/tools/leandata/) assignment flow or a [Clari](/en/tools/clari/) forecast (Clari now trades as part of Salesloft) consumes them with no middle layer and no second seat count. The package ships 50+ pre-built reports and three dashboards inside Salesforce.
- **First-party relationships, not a purchased list.** The tracked population is the firm's own CRM contacts. That keeps the signal defensible in a way a bought intent feed is not, and it makes the [champion-builder](/en/learn/champion-builder/) motion measurable instead of anecdotal.
- **Renewal-risk detection on the same feed.** A champion leaving a customer account is an NRR event, not a prospecting event. Because both flow through Salesforce, the same subscription serves the AE and the CSM.

## Pricing reality

Champify restructured its price book in the second half of 2026. Published tiers, checked 2026-11-13, all quoted as "starting at" per month and sold through a demo rather than a checkout:

- **Lite from $500/month** — 5,000 tracked contacts, extendable to 40,000 with add-ons. Insider AI, New Executive Hires, and CRM Clean are add-ons. The plan card lists 10 synced calendars while the comparison table lists Calendar Sync as an add-on; get the order form to settle which applies.
- **Pro from $2,500/month** — 40,000 contacts (add-ons to 200,000), Insider AI and New Executive Hires on 2,000 accounts each, Calendar Sync for 50 users, CRM Clean on 50,000 contacts, a dedicated GTM engineer and CSM.
- **Enterprise from $5,000/month** — 150,000 contacts, Insider AI on 5,000 accounts, New Executive Hires on 10,000 accounts, Calendar Sync for 200 users, and Salesforce governance controls. The card says CRM Clean covers 200,000 contacts; the table says 150,000.

Annualized at the floor that is $6,000, $30,000, and $60,000. The old $2,000 Core tier is gone; Pro dropped from $3,000 and Enterprise from $6,000, both by about 17%. Add-on prices are not published — the page shows only the ceilings. The bill tracks contact and account volume plus synced calendars, not headcount, and Champify states there are no overage charges. The pricing page advertises a $500K ROI guarantee and the Lite card a 2x ROI guarantee; read the terms on the ROI page before counting on either.

Lite changes the viability math. The FAQ still says Relationship Tracking pays back best from 10,000 tracked contacts, with an exception for very high-ACV sellers, but a $6,000 floor means a firm with 5,000 clean contacts can now run a cheap pilot instead of guessing.

Against the category leader the gap widened. UserGems publishes $40,000/year for Core plus a $3,000 implementation fee — $43,000 in year one against $30,000 for Champify Pro, about 1.4x, and against $6,000 for Lite, though Lite tracks far fewer contacts than UserGems Core covers. At the top of both price books, Champify Enterprise starts at $60,000 while UserGems Elite is $150,000 plus $10,000 implementation, about 2.7x.

## Best for

RevOps and sales leaders at Salesforce-standardized B2B SaaS companies, running mid-market or enterprise deals where named champions decide outcomes. Lite fits a team with 5,000 to 40,000 contacts that wants to test whether job-change pipeline is real before committing; Pro fits the $20-200M ARR band once Insider AI and calendar-sourced contacts matter. The sharpest scoped use case: a customer-success team that needs the champion-departure alert to reach the owning CSM within days of the move, wired to a defined renewal-risk play rather than to an inbox.

## Versus the alternatives

- **vs [UserGems](/en/tools/usergems/).** UserGems is the segment leader, supports Salesforce and HubSpot on every tier, and now ships an MCP server on its Advanced and Elite plans. Champify's FAQ is explicit that there is no native HubSpot integration; Champify data reaches HubSpot only through the Salesforce-to-HubSpot sync. Pick UserGems if the firm runs HubSpot, or wants agent access and the wider AI-action surface the higher price buys. Pick Champify if the firm is Salesforce-only and wants the same core event at a fraction of the year-one cost.
- **vs [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/).** The largest data vendor in the segment bundles a job-change feed with the contact database the firm already buys. Pick ZoomInfo if a bundled feed covers the need and no separate line item can be defended. Pick Champify when the job-change signal has to arrive as a routed, owned Salesforce record with a play attached rather than as a list to export.
- **vs [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/).** The multi-signal entrant treats job change as one input among community, product, website, and social signal, with an MCP server on every plan, at a $2,500/month floor billed annually. Pick Common Room for product-led motions with developer or community signal. Pick Champify when relationship movement is the only signal that matters and the stack is contact-and-account-centric.
- **vs [Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/) and the status quo.** Sales Navigator surfaces job changes to the individual rep and stops there. Under about 5,000 tracked contacts that is workable. The predictable failure above it: nobody notices the champion left, and the renewal call is where the firm finds out.

## Watch-outs

- **Signal without an execution layer.** The most repeated critique in third-party reviews is that Champify reports the move and leaves the follow-up to the team — no sequencer, no dialer, no ranking of which of this week's movers to work first. **Guard:** treat the Salesforce field as a trigger, not a deliverable. Wire it into an [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/) sequence and a named play before launch, and measure adoption on plays fired, not alerts delivered.
- **No agent or API surface.** Champify publishes no MCP server and no public API; data leaves through Salesforce and the listed integrations. UserGems and Common Room both now expose MCP servers. **Guard:** if an AI agent needs to read champion moves, plan to read them from Salesforce, and confirm the Salesforce MCP or connector the firm already runs can reach Champify's objects.
- **Unpublished add-on prices on a cheap floor.** Lite's $500 covers 5,000 contacts and little else; Insider AI, executive hires, and CRM Clean are priced only on request. **Guard:** get the full add-on quote in writing before choosing Lite over Pro — a Lite plan with three add-ons can land near Pro's $2,500.
- **Salesforce is a hard dependency.** A CRM migration or a HubSpot-first business unit strands the subscription. **Guard:** confirm the Salesforce commitment outlasts the Champify term, and if a HubSpot move is on the two-year roadmap, price UserGems now instead of paying a migration penalty later.
- **Yield is a function of CRM hygiene.** Contacts with dead emails and missing titles produce no matches, and contact-volume tiers mean the firm pays to track them anyway. **Guard:** run a suppression and dedupe pass before the contract is sized, so the tier is bought against live contacts.
- **Tracking people carries a privacy posture.** Job-change monitoring on named individuals has GDPR implications for EU-resident contacts. **Guard:** clear the scope with the DPO before rollout; the common resolution is restricting tracked populations by region.
