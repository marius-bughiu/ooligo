The old way to frame [11x](/en/tools/11x/) vs [Artisan](/en/tools/artisan/) was multi-agent workforce against single email-first agent, with 11x as the enterprise pick and Artisan as the cheaper, faster one. That frame no longer holds. Both vendors spent 2026 repositioning, and they moved toward each other on the outbound side while separating everywhere else.

11x now sells by function rather than by persona. "11x Outbound" (formerly Alice) researches and sends across email and social and hands calls to your reps through a built-in power dialer. "11x Inbound" (formerly Julian) is an AI voice and chat agent that calls back form-fills in under a minute, qualifies, and routes. A third use case, expansion, has voice agents call existing customers for upsell. The site's line is "Reps own every deal." Artisan's Ava 2.0 (launched May 2026) is now pitched as "the autonomous AI BDR built for enterprise": it sources leads from a 250M+ contact database, writes and sends email and social sequences, handles replies, books meetings, and queues warm leads into a native dialer where human reps make the call. Both now claim SOC 2 Type II. 11x publishes its entry-tier prices; Artisan quotes every deal.

So the real split is this. **Artisan is an outbound-only AI BDR. 11x is an outbound agent plus an AI phone agent for inbound and for existing customers.** If inbound speed-to-lead or AI voice isn't on your list, most of the gap between them closes.

## Where 11x wins

- **AI voice that actually talks.** 11x Inbound answers and calls back inbound leads around the clock, qualifies against your rubric, warm-transfers to a live rep, and follows up by phone, SMS, email, and WhatsApp. It also places consented calls to existing customers for expansion. Artisan has no AI calling: its dialer is operated by humans, and Ava only queues the calls. If your pipeline leaks between form-fill and first touch, this is the deciding feature, and Artisan doesn't compete on it.
- **Inbound and outbound under one vendor.** Artisan is outbound only. 11x covers both sides, plus routing that plugs into [Chili Piper](/en/tools/chili-piper/). A team weighing 11x Inbound against [Default](/en/tools/default/) or [Qualified](/en/tools/qualified/) for speed-to-lead can fold outbound into the same contract.
- **Broader CRM and an API.** 11x syncs both ways with Salesforce, HubSpot, and Pipedrive and documents a single API with webhooks across its agents. Artisan supports Salesforce and HubSpot sync plus custom webhooks, with no documented public API.
- **Messaging channels beyond email.** SMS and WhatsApp are native in 11x. Artisan's sequences are email and social.

## Where Artisan wins

- **Lower contract value for an outbound-only job.** Artisan's median contract lands at roughly two-thirds of 11x's published outbound entry price, and far below it once 11x's voice agent is in the deal (see Pricing reality). If outbound is the whole job, you're not paying for a voice platform you won't use.
- **Data, sending, and deliverability on one bill.** Every Artisan plan includes the 250M+ contact database, mailboxes sized to your motion, warmup, and sending from your own domains in your reps' names. 11x also bundles a contact database (it claims 400M+ profiles plus live web search), so the data gap that used to separate them has narrowed. What still favors Artisan is that plans are scoped to a visible lead number: about 2,500 leads contacted a month on Team and about 6,000 on Scale.
- **A rollout team at every tier.** Artisan includes CSM support in a shared Slack channel and guided onboarding on every plan, a dedicated CSM from Scale up, and a forward-deployed strategist on Enterprise. For a team without an outbound operator, that hand-holding is part of what you're buying.
- **Tighter controls over what gets sent.** Ava supports approve-before-send per campaign, locked tone and CTAs, banned phrases, and escalation rules. That matters because generic copy is the most common complaint about both products.

## Pricing reality

11x publishes its entry tiers. Artisan doesn't: its pricing page lists Team, Scale, and Enterprise plans with "Pricing scoped on your plan." Both bill annually.

- **11x Outbound:** the Growth plan starts at $36,000/year, billed annually, for up to 5 end users and 2,000 new prospects a month. Contact data, managed mailboxes, warmup, inbox rotation, CRM sync, and onboarding are included. Pro and Enterprise are custom-quoted, with discounts for two- and three-year commitments. Vendr's median of about $40,000/year sits just above the published floor.
- **11x Inbound:** the Growth plan starts at $5,333/month for voice and $2,417/month for chat, billed annually. Pro and Enterprise scale with call volume, channels, and concurrency.
- **Artisan:** the widely cited Vendr median is about $26,000/year, but it rests on a small number of tracked deals. Third-party ranges run from under $10,000 to about $57,000/year, depending on lead volume, mailboxes, and dialer seats. Team is scoped at about 2,500 leads contacted a month. The AI dialer is a per-seat add-on.

For outbound alone, 11x's entry price is **about 1.4× Artisan's median** for a similar monthly prospect volume. Add 11x Inbound voice at its Growth price and the 11x bill reaches about $100,000/year, **roughly 4× Artisan**. That gap is the cost of the AI phone agent, not of better outbound. Some reviews from mid-2026 describe self-serve Artisan tiers in the hundreds of dollars a month around the Ava 2.0 launch. The current pricing page shows only sales-led plans, so don't budget on that.

## Implementation effort

11x quotes about **two weeks** to go live with white-glove onboarding, much of it spent waiting for new sending domains to warm. Inbound voice adds routing design, call scripts, and CRM field mapping. Budget another week or two of RevOps time if you deploy both agents at once.

Artisan's rollout is also CSM-led. The outbound-only scope means fewer moving parts: ICP definition, domain and mailbox setup, CRM sync, and approval rules. Plan on a similar two weeks, mostly for domain warmup.

On either product, the slow part isn't setup. It's the first 60-90 days of tuning ICP and copy. Both carry a documented record of generic, low-reply output when that tuning is skipped.

## Track record

11x's 2025 history still belongs in the decision. TechCrunch reported customer logos used without consent and annual contract value counted as ARR for accounts that had already churned. The founder stepped down as CEO in May 2025, and Prabhav Jain, previously CTO, has led the company since. Artisan's reputational baggage is its "Stop Hiring Humans" campaign. Its 2026 messaging has moved to "Ava emails, your reps dial," but independent reviews still flag low reply rates on broad ICPs. For both vendors: ignore the logo wall, ask for two or three live references at your ACV, and negotiate a written pilot exit before any annual commit.

## Verdict

- **Pick 11x when** inbound speed-to-lead is a real leak, you want AI voice on inbound or on existing customers for expansion, you run Pipedrive or need a documented API, or you want inbound and outbound qualification from one vendor.
- **Pick Artisan when** the job is outbound only: email and social sequences with your reps on the phone, one bill for data, sending, and deliverability, and a CSM to run the rollout at a lower annual commit.
- **Pick neither when** you contact fewer than about 2,000 prospects a month, or you don't yet have a proven ICP. At that scale an AI BDR contract costs more than the pipeline it can book. [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) plus one human SDR, or [Amplemarket](/en/tools/amplemarket/), covers the same ground for a fraction of the spend. GTM engineers who want control over signals and copy should assemble [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) plus [Regie.ai](/en/tools/regie-ai/) instead. If outbound is only failing on copy, an AI layer on [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/) or [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/) fixes it without renting an agent.

**The pick:** if you're choosing without a clear inbound or voice requirement, pick Artisan. It's the cheaper commitment for the job most buyers actually have, and if inbound speed-to-lead later becomes the bottleneck, add 11x Inbound or a dedicated speed-to-lead tool alongside it rather than swapping out your outbound.
