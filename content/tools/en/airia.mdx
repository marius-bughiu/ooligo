## What it is

Airia is an enterprise AI control platform: one product that builds agents, routes their model traffic, gates their tool calls and keeps the compliance record on all of it. The vendor sorts the platform into four modules. **Discover** covers AI discovery, security posture, inventory and risk classification. **Secure** covers red teaming, an MCP gateway, agent constraints and guardrails. **Govern** covers the governance registry, compliance reporting and system controls. **Optimize** covers an agent builder, model routing, AI FinOps, a prototyping studio, model lifecycle and data integrations.

The company is young and unusually well funded for its age. The Business Wire release of 16 September 2025 dates the founding to 2024 and announced **$100M in total: $50M already invested plus a further $50M commitment**, all from co-founder and chairman John Marshall's own capital rather than venture funds. Marshall co-founded AirWatch, which VMware bought for $1.54B, and was co-chairman of OneTrust until 2023. CEO Kevin Kiley and much of the leadership also came from OneTrust. At that release Airia reported more than 300 enterprise customers and 150 staff, with offices in Atlanta, London, Singapore, Dubai, Melbourne and Bangalore.

The 2026 product cadence is what put it on ops shortlists. AI Governance shipped on **14 January 2026**: an agent and model registry with ownership, risk classification and compliance status, aligned to the EU AI Act, the NIST AI framework and ISO 42001. Support for MCP Apps, the interactive-UI extension to MCP, followed on **12 February**. Model Risk Management, which runs Microsoft Foundry's evaluators against production traffic on a schedule or on events, arrived on **26 May**. Model Change Management shipped on **14 July**: it raises escalating alerts up to 90 days before a model is retired, lists every agent that depends on it, and moves them to an approved replacement in one bulk step with a version history for audit. Forrester named Airia a notable vendor in *The Agentic Control Plane Solutions Landscape, Q2 2026*.

## The question it answers

"Do we need one control plane across Copilot, Claude and our in-house agents, or does a point governance tool cover it?" Airia's answer is that governance is easier to enforce where the agents are built and their traffic is routed. Most of its peers watch agents that live somewhere else. Airia wants to be the place the agent lives. That is its advantage and its cost: you get one policy layer for model routing (sensitive data goes only to approved models, failover between providers), tool access (the MCP gateway, which the vendor says ships with 2,000+ pre-configured integrations) and the audit file. But the full value arrives only when teams move agent building into Airia.

Deployment is the second differentiator. Airia sells multi-tenant SaaS, private cloud inside your own AWS account including GovCloud, on-premises on VMware, Kubernetes or bare metal, and hybrid. It reports SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001. For legal-ops and regulated customer-success teams where data residency rules out a vendor's cloud, that list shortens the evaluation more than any feature.

## Pricing reality

Airia no longer shows a public price page: airia.com/pricing now redirects to login-gated documentation. Third-party trackers that recorded the page in July 2026 agree on four plans: **Free**, **Individual at $50/month**, **Team at $250/month** and **Enterprise on a quote**. They disagree on the execution and user caps for each tier, so treat any published limit as unconfirmed until you see it in your account. Enterprise is also sold through AWS Marketplace, with private offers that can count toward an existing AWS spend commitment. Annual and multi-year Marketplace subscriptions are non-refundable.

The real price band for an ops team sits in Enterprise, because the governance registry, private deployment and organisation-wide MCP gateway are what you are buying, and none of that is meaningful on a $250 team plan. Budget it the way you would budget [Zenity](/en/tools/zenity/): as a five- or six-figure annual platform contract, negotiated against the number of agents and the deployment model. The Team plan is a pilot vehicle for a single department that wants to build and route a few agents before procurement gets involved.

## Best for

The CIO's staff or the RevOps / legal-ops platform owner at a mid-size or larger company that is **about to standardise where agents get built**, and wants model routing, tool governance and the compliance record in the same product. The scoped case where it wins outright: a regulated firm that must run agents on-premises or in its own AWS account, maps them to the EU AI Act and ISO 42001, and has more than one model provider in production.

## Not for

A Microsoft-first tenant whose agents already live in [Copilot Studio](/en/tools/microsoft-copilot-studio/) and [Foundry](/en/tools/microsoft-foundry/). There, [Microsoft Agent 365](/en/tools/microsoft-agent-365/) at $15 per user per month governs inside the tenant with Entra and Purview you already run. It is also the wrong buy for a team under about 200 people with a handful of agents: the [AI agent ops stack](/en/stacks/ai-agent-ops-stack/) and a named owner per agent cover that for far less.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Microsoft Agent 365](/en/tools/microsoft-agent-365/)**: the market-share default for governance inside a Microsoft tenant. Pick it when Copilot Studio, Foundry and Teams hold the agents that matter. Pick Airia when agents span Microsoft, Claude, ChatGPT and your own code, or must run outside a Microsoft cloud.
- **[Zenity](/en/tools/zenity/)**: the leading independent for policing agents that business users built on Copilot Studio and Agentforce, with runtime blocking at the tool-call step. Pick Zenity when the agents already exist on those platforms and you only need governance. Pick Airia when you also want to build and route.
- **[Runlayer](/en/tools/runlayer/)**: the fast-growing entrant for MCP specifically, a managed MCP gateway with SSO-synced access and session recording. Pick it when the problem is staff wiring Claude and Cursor to MCP servers. Pick Airia when the MCP gateway is one part of a wider model and agent policy.
- **[LiteLLM](/en/tools/litellm/)** or **[Portkey](/en/tools/portkey/)**: pick one of these when all you need is model routing and spend control, without a governance registry.

If none fit, because you run one platform with no auditor asking, the correct spend is zero beyond the platform's native audit log and a human approval on writes.

## Watch-outs

- **The platform is broad, so the evaluation drifts toward demos of the agent builder.** The governance case is what justifies the contract. Guard: write the proof-of-value around your own audit question (produce the registry entry, risk class, owner and model history for three live agents), and score the builder separately.
- **Value assumes agents move onto Airia.** Agents that stay in Copilot Studio or Agentforce get discovery and posture coverage, not the routing and gateway enforcement. Guard: before signing, list your agents by where they will be built in 12 months, and price Airia only against the share that will actually live on it.
- **Funding comes from one source.** $100M of founder capital removes venture pressure, but it also concentrates the company's runway in one backer's commitment. Guard: hold the first term to 12 months, require data export of the registry and audit logs in an open format, and keep a written exit plan for the MCP gateway.
- **Public limits are not reliable.** Third-party listings disagree on executions and seats per plan. Guard: get execution caps, overage rates and the definition of an "execution" in the order form, not from a pricing tracker.
