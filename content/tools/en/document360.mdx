## What it is

Document360 is a standalone knowledge base platform from Kovai.co: an authoring workspace for writers and reviewers, plus public, private or mixed help sites that readers consume. Each knowledge base is a "project" with its own domain, branding, versions, languages and users. On top sits Eddy AI, Document360's AI layer for drafting, search answers, a support chatbot, and a remote MCP server.

The question it answers is narrow and recurring: **do you need a dedicated knowledge base to feed your AI support agent, or is the help centre bundled with your helpdesk enough?** Document360 is the answer when documentation has become its own product, with versioned releases, several languages, an editorial review workflow, and more than one consumer reading it.

## What changed in 2026

- **January (release 12.1.1):** the Eddy AI chatbot became a standalone module. You can build several chatbots, each trained on the knowledge base plus website pages, FAQs, text entries, uploaded files (PDF, DOC, DOCX, MD) and ticketing platforms such as Zendesk and Freshdesk, test them in a playground, and publish them to your website. Readers can raise a ticket from the chat.
- **March (12.3.1):** the MCP server shipped, letting ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or custom clients search and read articles over secure tokens, and, where permissions allow, create and update them. Document360's MCP page now lists 29 tools, including publish, workflow status and glossary actions, and states the server is included on **Business and Enterprise plans with API access enabled**.
- **April 20:** Zendesk made Document360 a generally available external content source for help centre search, generative search, AI agents Essentials and Agent Workspace, on any Zendesk Suite or Knowledge plan. That is the integration this page exists for: Zendesk's AI can answer from Document360 without you migrating content into Zendesk Guide.
- **September (12.9.1 and 12.9.3):** an advanced Eddy AI analytics page lists unanswered reader queries with a reason (content gap, out of scope, or misread query), and chatbot tickets can now route to email as well as to a connected helpdesk.

## Pricing

Document360 has published no list prices since late 2024, and it discontinued its free plan in November 2024. [document360.com/pricing](https://document360.com/pricing/) is a quote form. Its FAQ names six price drivers: team accounts (editors and reviewers), workspaces, languages, SSO and security, the privacy model (public, private or mixed), and AI Premium Suite usage. Public readers are free; reader accounts apply only to private knowledge bases. The AI chatbot and the AI Premium Suite (screen capture, step-by-step guides, interactive demos, video) are add-ons.

For a band, the last publicly listed prices (SoftwareAdvice, pre-2025) were **$299 per project per month for Professional and $399 for Business**, billed annually: roughly **$3,600 to $4,800 a year** for one knowledge base before add-ons. Treat that as a floor, not a quote. Because MCP and API access sit on Business and above, and the chatbot is extra, an AI-support rollout with SSO, two or three languages and a chatbot will land well above it. Get the chatbot priced in the first quote, not at expansion.

## Best for

A **support-operations or documentation lead at a B2B software company** whose help content ships with product releases. The scoped case where Document360 is the clear pick: **one versioned, multi-language knowledge base feeding Zendesk's AI agent, an embedded website chatbot, and internal AI assistants over MCP at the same time**, with a review workflow so nothing unapproved reaches any of them.

## Not for

- **Teams under about 50 articles in one language.** Zendesk's built-in help centre, Help Scout Docs or Intercom's Help Center already feed their own AI agents. A second system adds a sync job and a bill.
- **Internal wikis.** Document360 can run private knowledge bases, but for team knowledge that changes daily, Notion or Confluence is cheaper and more natural to write in.
- **Buyers who need a price this week.** Quote-only means a sales cycle.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) help centre**, the market-share leader for support knowledge, included with every Suite plan. Pick it when Zendesk is your only consumer and you have one product version. Pick Document360 when the same content must also feed a website chatbot, other tools or MCP clients, or when versioning and review workflow matter.
- **[Confluence](/en/tools/confluence/)**, the largest installed base for company documentation. Pick it when the corpus is internal and Atlassian is already paid for. Pick Document360 for customer-facing docs with branded sites, SEO and reader analytics.
- **[Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/)**, the fastest-growing AI-first challenger in support, whose Fin agent reads Intercom's own help centre and external sources. Pick Intercom when you are choosing the helpdesk and the AI agent together; its help centre is enough for most of its customers.
- **[Guru](/en/tools/guru/)** for short, owner-verified internal answers rather than public documentation.

If none fit, [best knowledge bases for teams](/en/vs/best-knowledge-bases-for-teams/) covers the wider field.

## Watch-outs

- **A daily-sync connector means stale AI answers for up to a day.** Zendesk reads external sources through a scheduled crawl, so a policy you fix at 10:00 can still be quoted wrong by the AI agent that afternoon. **Guard:** for urgent corrections, trigger a manual re-sync in Zendesk after publishing, and keep a short list of high-risk articles (refunds, security, pricing) that owners re-check after every change.
- **Eddy AI runs on a monthly credit allowance.** When the credits run out, the AI features stop until renewal or a top-up through support, and trial accounts cannot buy credits. **Guard:** watch credit use weekly in the first month, size the allowance against expected chatbot volume in the contract, and agree the top-up price in writing before launch.
- **MCP write access lets an AI assistant publish to your public help site.** The server inherits the connected user's role, so a token issued to an editor can create, update and publish articles. **Guard:** issue MCP tokens from a role that can draft but not publish, keep publishing behind the review workflow, and revoke tokens by disabling MCP when someone leaves.
- **Every editor and reviewer is a priced team account.** Support agents who suggest edits add to the bill. **Guard:** route agent suggestions through article feedback or tickets, and keep team accounts for the people who actually write.
