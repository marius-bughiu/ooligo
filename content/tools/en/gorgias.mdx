## What it is

Gorgias is a helpdesk that assumes the customer already bought something. Every ticket opens beside the order, the refund history, the subscription, and the shipment, read live from Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento, WooCommerce, or PrestaShop. The agent — human or AI — refunds the order or edits the subscription inside the conversation instead of tabbing into the store admin and pasting the result back.

That is the whole thesis, and it is why the category exists separately from general support software. A [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) or [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) install reaches commerce data through an app; Gorgias treats it as the ticket's spine. The platform around it is a single inbox for email, chat, SMS, WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, plus Voice, plus an AI Agent that splits into a Support Agent for post-purchase tickets and a Shopping Assistant for pre-purchase questions. Gorgias lists 300-plus integrations, with Recharge, Klaviyo, Yotpo, Loop Returns, Bloomreach, and Attentive named on the homepage.

The company is Shopify-backed. It raised a $30M Series C in August 2022 at a $710M valuation, then raised again in May 2024 at a $530M valuation with $100M-plus total raised. Sacra puts ARR at $25M in 2022, $51M in 2023, and $69M in 2024, growing 34% year over year.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Commerce context is the product, not an integration.** For a DTC brand, the majority of tickets are order status, returns, exchanges, address changes, and subscription edits. Those are all lookups-plus-an-action against the store. Gorgias is built so the lookup and the action happen in one place, which is what makes autonomous resolution tractable rather than aspirational.
- **The meter is volume, not headcount.** Gorgias' pricing page states it plainly: "Helpdesk scales from 50 to 5,000 tickets a month, never priced per agent." Seasonal DTC teams that triple agent count for Q4 do not triple the license.
- **The AI Agent bills on outcome.** Same page: "AI Agent on every plan, pay only when it resolves a conversation." A deflection bot billed per conversation charges you for its failures; this one charges on resolution.
- **First-party MCP.** Gorgias ships its own MCP server, documented at `docs.gorgias.com`, exposing ticket create/update/delete, customer records, and tagging to [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) or [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/). Support data becomes queryable outside the helpdesk without building an API client.

## Pricing

List prices are published: Starter $10/mo for 50 tickets, Basic $60 for 300, Pro $360 for 2,000, Advanced $900 for 5,000, and a custom Enterprise tier. Annual billing takes roughly 16% off — Basic $50, Pro $300, Advanced $750. Extra tickets beyond the allowance run $0.40 each on Starter and Basic, $0.36 on Pro and Advanced, and $0.32 on Enterprise. The AI Agent is metered separately at $1.00 per automated resolution on monthly billing and $0.90 on annual.

Model the real bill, not the tier. A brand handling 3,000 tickets a month on Pro annual pays $300 plus 1,000 overage tickets at $0.36, so $660 before any automation. Send 40% of that volume through the AI Agent and add 1,200 resolutions at $0.90 — another $1,080, for roughly $1,740 a month. The automation line is larger than the helpdesk line, and it grows as the agent gets better. That inversion is the single most important thing to model before signing.

## Best for

Ecommerce and DTC customer experience leads running 2 to 40 agents on Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento, or WooCommerce, where order-status, returns, and subscription tickets are most of the queue and the current cost is agents copying data between the helpdesk and the store admin. It is the wrong shape for B2B software support, where tickets become engineering work and accounts matter more than orders — that is [DevRev](/en/tools/devrev/) or [Pylon](/en/tools/pylon/) territory.

## Watch-outs

- **Two meters both accelerate with success.** Ticket overage and per-resolution AI charges move in the same direction as volume, and automation does not retire the ticket meter by itself. The guard: before signing, get Gorgias to state in writing whether a conversation the AI Agent resolves also consumes a plan ticket, and model 12 months at 2x your current volume across three automation rates — 25%, 50%, and 70% — so you see the slope rather than a single point.
- **The headline adoption stat is narrower than it reads.** The homepage says "We power customer conversations for 40% of Shopify brands." Sacra's figure is 40% of Shopify's *top 1,500* merchants, which is a different and much smaller denominator. The guard: ignore the aggregate and ask for three reference customers matched to your order volume, average order value, and category, then ask those teams what their measured automation rate settled at after month three.
- **Vendor outcome numbers are ceilings, not baselines.** Gorgias publishes "60% of inquiries resolved instantly," Orthofeet at a 56% automation rate in under 2 months, and Pepper at 19.2x ROI on AI-driven sales interactions. Those are the best cases from brands with clean policy pages and tidy catalogs. The guard: run a paid pilot with a contractual floor defined as resolved-without-human-reply within 24 hours, measured on your own tickets, and make the renewal price contingent on hitting it.
- **The valuation reset is public, and it belongs in your contract terms.** Gorgias went from a $710M valuation in 2022 to $530M in 2024. Growth continued at 34%, so this is not a distress signal, but it does change who has the negotiating power at renewal. The guard: negotiate a capped renewal uplift and an explicit data-export clause covering tickets, macros, and AI Agent training content before you migrate a queue you cannot rebuild.

## Alternatives, and when to pick them instead

[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) and [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) are the two incumbents you are choosing against, and both now meter their AI agents per resolution too, so price alone will not decide it. Pick Zendesk when support spans B2C and B2B, when you need mature admin and reporting, or when the store is not the center of the conversation. Pick Intercom when in-product messaging is your primary channel and the buyer is a software user rather than a shopper. Pick Gorgias when a majority of your tickets cannot be answered without reading an order.

[Siena AI](https://www.siena.cx) is the fastest-growing entrant in the ecommerce CX segment and takes the opposite architectural bet: an autonomous agent layer that sits on top of the helpdesk you already run instead of replacing it. Pick Siena when your helpdesk is fine and only the automation is missing, or when you refuse to re-platform mid-year. Pick Gorgias when you are replacing both at once and want one vendor owning the resolution.

[Gladly](/en/tools/gladly/) and [Kustomer](/en/tools/kustomer/) make the customer, not the ticket, the primary object, which pays off above roughly 50 agents with high repeat-purchase rates and heavy voice volume. Below that, their custom pricing and rollout length cost more than they return. And if you handle under 200 tickets a month, none of these is the answer yet — a shared inbox plus a Shopify app is cheaper than any per-resolution meter you can negotiate.
