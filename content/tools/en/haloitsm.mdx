## What it is

HaloITSM is the IT service management product of Halo Service Solutions, a privately owned, bootstrapped UK vendor that also builds HaloPSA for managed service providers and HaloCRM. In 2026 Halo folded the three into one platform sold simply as **Halo** at usehalo.com, with ESM, ITSM, CSM, CRM, managed-services and AI modules on one codebase. Many partners, reviewers and buyers still call the internal-IT product HaloITSM, which is why this page uses that name.

The product is a full ITIL desk: incidents, problems, changes with CAB approvals, a service catalog, SLAs, a CMDB with asset discovery integrations, and a self-service portal with a Virtual Agent chatbot. Its reputation on r/sysadmin and review sites is consistent: almost everything is configurable, it is priced well below ServiceNow, and the configuration burden is yours.

Two 2026 facts change the buying picture. On 13 August Halo announced **FedRAMP 20x authorization at the Moderate level** for its multi-tenant cloud, which puts it on the shortlist for US federal agencies and contractors. And around the end of June it replaced quote-only pricing with a public per-agent rate, according to PricingSaaS page captures. The company is also moving its global headquarters from Stowmarket to the Willis Building in Ipswich during 2026.

## Where it fits

- **ITIL-heavy internal IT desks that have outgrown a simple help desk.** Teams that need real change management, problem records and a CMDB tied to tickets, and want them without ServiceNow's licence and partner bill.
- **One platform for IT, HR, facilities and customer support.** Because ESM and CSM run on the same codebase, a single admin team can stand up HR onboarding, facilities requests and external support queues with shared workflows and reporting.
- **US public sector and contractors.** FedRAMP 20x Moderate is rare in this price band, and most mid-market rivals do not carry it.
- **AI agents reaching into the desk.** Halo describes native MCP servers built into the platform alongside its REST API, so Claude, Microsoft Copilot or Codex can read and act on tickets with per-agent tool scoping. The built-in Virtual Agent can search the knowledge base, create incidents, look up ticket status and hand off to a human, on Halo's AI or your own OpenAI or Azure OpenAI connection.

## Pricing reality

Halo's pricing page lists **£66 per agent per month, billed annually in the UK**, with local currencies in other regions. There are no tiers or add-ons: every module, integration and the AI features are in the single licence, and end users and requesters are free. Named and concurrent licences cost the same, and concurrent licences, shared across a pool of agents who log in at different times, are the main lever for shift-based desks. Non-profits get 15% off.

Two costs sit outside the per-agent number. **Onboarding is mandatory** and priced per project; PricingSaaS recorded the change that added it to every purchase in early July 2026. And if you bring your own model provider for the AI features, those token costs are billed by that provider, not Halo.

Real numbers: a 25-agent IT desk pays about **£19,800 a year** in licences before onboarding. Converted to dollars, that lands between Freshservice Growth and Pro for the same team, but with change management, CMDB and AI already included rather than tiered. At the other end, Halo offers an Enterprise Package of unlimited licences for a fixed $1 million a year, which only pays off at roughly a thousand agents or more. Halo also publishes "ARR Milestones": each time company ARR crosses a set threshold, customers on standard pricing get a further 5% off at their next renewal.

Third-party trackers disagree on the US price (PricingSaaS shows $80 per agent for the platform), so get a dollar quote in writing before you budget.

## Best for

IT and service-desk leaders at 500 to 10,000-employee organisations with 15 to 300 agents, who want ITIL depth (changes, problems, CMDB) and ESM on one licence, and who have an internal admin willing to own configuration for the first quarter.

## Versus the alternatives

The closest head-to-head is [Freshservice](/en/tools/freshservice/). Pick Freshservice when speed to go-live and a cleaner agent UI matter more than depth: it is easier to stand up in weeks, but its AI is tiered and priced on top. Pick Halo when you expect to outgrow a basic desk and want one flat price that will not climb as you add change management and assets.

The two largest players are [ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/) and [Jira Service Management](/en/tools/jira-service-management/). Pick ServiceNow above roughly 300 fulfillers or when IT, HR, security and customer service must share one data model and you can fund a partner-led rollout. Pick Jira Service Management when engineering already lives in Jira and service requests must link to engineering work items without a sync tool.

The fastest-growing entrants are Slack-native desks such as [Unthread](/en/tools/unthread/). Pick Unthread when most requests start as Slack threads, the team is under about 20 agents, and you do not need change management or a CMDB.

## Watch-outs

- **Setup takes longer than the demo suggests.** G2 and Capterra reviewers describe a steep learning curve driven by the number of settings, and some report delayed implementations. Guard: scope the onboarding package around your five highest-volume request types and your change process, and write go-live acceptance criteria into the statement of work before you sign.
- **Documentation trails the product.** The most repeated reviewer complaint is that help articles and training videos lag behind new features. Guard: budget admin training for at least two people before implementation, and keep an internal runbook of every configuration decision so knowledge does not sit with one admin.
- **The agent UI and CMDB speed get mixed reviews.** r/sysadmin comparisons with Freshservice cite a dated interface and a slow CMDB on large estates. Guard: in the trial, load a realistic asset count and time your agents on the ten most common ticket actions before committing.
- **The pricing page is new and still settling.** The page shows £66 while its FAQ still tells buyers to ask for a figure, and the onboarding fee was added a week after the calculator launched. Guard: get the per-agent rate, onboarding fee, renewal terms and currency in one written quote, and cap renewal increases in the contract.
