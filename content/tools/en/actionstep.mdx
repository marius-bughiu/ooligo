## What it is

Actionstep is cloud practice management for midsize law firms: matters, contacts, documents, workflow, time, billing, trust and, in North America, a full legal general ledger. It was founded in Auckland in 2004, has been majority-owned by Serent Capital since October 2020, and says it serves nearly 5,000 firms and around 50,000 users across New Zealand, Australia, the UK, the US and Canada. What sets it apart from [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) or [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/) is the workflow engine: each matter type runs on a configurable sequence of steps, data fields and automated tasks, which is why firms with 20 to 150 fee earners and several practice areas pick it, and why setup takes weeks rather than an afternoon.

On 2026-08-11 Actionstep released **Actionstep Intelligence**, an AI layer inside the platform rather than a separate app. Two capabilities shipped first: **Trace**, AI time capture, and **Acumen**, law-firm dashboards with an AI assistant that answers questions about the firm's own reports. Search, workflow execution and an MCP server are promised for later in 2026; when we checked on 2026-10-16 the vendor had not announced a general-availability MCP server, so we list it as not available.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Workflow per matter type.** A conveyancing matter, a family-law file and a commercial dispute can each carry their own steps, fields and document templates. Firms that outgrow Clio's lighter matter model usually cite this.
- **Time capture is now native.** Trace grew out of Traced, a passive time-capture startup Actionstep bought in January 2026. It watches activity in Outlook, Word, PDFs and the browser, maps it to matters and billing codes, and drafts entries that a fee earner approves; nothing posts to a bill on its own. Actionstep reports 25-50 extra billable hours recovered per lawyer per year (vendor-reported).
- **Accounting goes deeper than in most peers.** Soluno, bought from AffiniPay in 2023 and renamed Actionstep Legal Accounting in 2026, adds general ledger, payables and trust accounting for North American firms that would otherwise run a separate legal accounting system.
- **An API you can actually build on.** A documented REST API with a sandbox (docs.actionstep.com) and OAuth credentials issued after an API review. Integrations include Microsoft 365 with an Outlook add-in, Xero, DocuSign, InfoTrack, Legl, First AML and Zapier.

## Pricing reality

Actionstep publishes no prices. Every product on actionstep.com/pricing is "per user plus implementation fees", and Trace, Acumen, Builder (document automation) and Capture (intake) are separately priced add-ons. Third-party estimates put the core subscription from about $115 per user per month (ITQlick), and implementation from about $3,000 for a simple rollout to $10,000+ for a multi-practice firm, delivered by certified partners. Budget a 25-person firm at roughly $35,000-45,000 for the first year before AI add-ons (estimate), against $30,000 a year for the same firm on MyCase Pro at its $100 published annual rate. The premium buys the workflow engine and the accounting, not cheaper seats.

## Best for

Managing partners and operations directors at midsize firms (roughly 20-150 fee earners) in Australia, New Zealand, the UK or North America that run several practice areas with distinct processes, want time capture and firm analytics in the same system, and have someone who will own workflow configuration.

**Not for:** solo lawyers and firms under about 10 people, who will pay for configuration they never use; high-volume plaintiff personal-injury shops; and AmLaw-scale firms on Aderant or Elite 3E.

## Alternatives

- **[Clio](/en/tools/clio/)** — the market-share leader. Pick it when you want published pricing, a same-week rollout and the largest integration marketplace, and your matters do not need per-type workflow.
- **[MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/)** — the other large incumbent, now 8am MyCase. Pick it for a small firm that wants list-price billing ($50-130 per user per month) and built-in AI without a partner implementation.
- **[Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/)** — the fastest-growing platform in the category after a $400M raise in September 2025. Pick it for high-volume litigation and plaintiff work where templated case pipelines matter more than general-ledger accounting.
- **[Laurel](/en/tools/laurel/)** or **[PointOne](/en/tools/pointone/)** — pick a standalone AI timekeeper when you are staying on your current practice management system and only need time capture.
- **LEAP** — the main rival in Australia and the UK; pick it when you want bundled legal content and forms with your practice management.

## Watch-outs

- **Implementation is the product.** Reviewers on Capterra and GetApp repeatedly describe long setups and a steep learning curve, and one review aggregator cites G2's average implementation estimate at about five months. *Guard:* scope two or three matter types for phase one, write go-live criteria into the partner's statement of work, and keep the rest for phase two.
- **AI is priced separately and early.** Trace and Acumen are add-ons with no public price, and search, workflow execution and the MCP server are still announcements. *Guard:* buy the core platform on its own merits, pilot Trace with five fee earners for 60 days, and compare recovered hours against Laurel or PointOne before committing firm-wide.
- **Feature depth varies by region.** Legal Accounting is North America only; other markets use Xero or other accounting integrations. *Guard:* demo against your own jurisdiction's trust-accounting rules and ask which features are live in your region today.
- **No published price, so quotes vary.** *Guard:* get the per-user rate, add-on prices and implementation fee in one written quote, and ask for a renewal cap of 5-7% in the contract.
