This is the largest enterprise ATS pairing in the category, and it is not an ATS bake-off. [Workday](/en/tools/workday/) Recruiting is a module of the system that already holds your worker record, org chart and position budgets. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) is a standalone ATS that has spent a decade making hiring managers evaluate candidates the same way twice. The routing question is not which product is better at recruiting — Greenhouse wins that on the merits and no honest Workday reference call will argue otherwise. It is whether your hiring problem lives in systems architecture or in hiring-manager behavior, and whether Workday HCM is already signed.

## Where Workday Recruiting wins

- **Adjacency to the record.** The requisition, worker record, org chart, comp band and position budget already sit in one system, so a screening agent reads job architecture without an integration and [internal mobility](/en/learn/internal-mobility/) is a query rather than a project. This is the whole argument, and it only pays if you already run Workday HCM.
- **The agent bundle is bought, not built.** The Recruiting Agent runs sourcing, screening and matching on HiredScore, acquired in 2024. The Candidate Experience Agent runs conversational apply and self-scheduling on Paradox, whose acquisition closed October 1, 2025 — candidates apply by SMS, WhatsApp or Facebook Messenger with no login. Workday's published outcomes are over 30 million interviews scheduled with AI, [time to hire](/en/learn/time-to-fill-vs-time-to-hire/) as low as 3.5 days for frontline roles, and 95% candidate satisfaction. Those are vendor-selected results from finished deployments, not a forecast for yours.
- **Agent consumption is pooled, not per-SKU.** Flex Credits ship inside every subscription, refresh annually, and spend across any agent rather than being locked to a per-agent line. The Agent System of Record registers and governs agents, including third-party ones — the piece an audit asks for and most ATS vendors have no answer to.
- **Frontline volume.** Retail, health systems and logistics extract the most, because conversational apply and self-scheduling across thousands of requisitions is where the agent math turns positive.

## Where Greenhouse wins

- **Structured hiring is the product, not a feature.** Scorecards, interview kits and job-level interview plans are the core surface. If inconsistent hiring-manager evaluation is the thing costing you offers, [structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) discipline is what you are actually buying, and Workday Recruiting does not enforce it.
- **Fraud and identity controls that are shipped and dated.** Real Talent was announced June 3, 2025 and phased in from Q3 2025. Its Fraud Detection screens applications on phone number, email, IP address and location, and Greenhouse's own support documentation states it does not use AI or automated scoring — it applies rules to objective signals. CLEAR identity verification runs a selfie check inside MyGreenhouse against impersonation and deepfakes. Greenhouse cites Gartner's projection that a quarter of applicants will be fake by 2028 as the reason it built this. Workday's answer on the same axis is a partner integration — Persona syncs verification status back to the candidate profile — not a native control.
- **Voice interviewing moved in-product.** Greenhouse signed a definitive agreement to acquire Ezra AI Labs on May 5, 2026 and closed it May 27, 2026, bringing a voice AI interviewer into the platform. The old criticism that Greenhouse ships AI late is out of date.
- **Integration direction of travel.** Assessment, scheduling and sourcing vendors certify against Greenhouse first and test it hardest — fewer partners than an HCM suite carries, cleaner connections.
- **Time to live.** Greenhouse goes live in 6–12 weeks. Workday's average deployment is 8.2 months, and multi-module rollouts with custom HCM integrations run 6–9 months.

## Pricing: the gap is a decision, not a discount

Neither vendor publishes a price. Greenhouse bills per employee, so cost scales with headcount rather than recruiter count; buyer-reported Vendr data puts the median annual contract at $26,646 across 866 purchases, in a range of $10,215–$75,125.

Workday's full suite with financials and recruiting is quoted at $80–150 per employee per month. Annual software lands at $150–300K under 500 employees, $300–500K at 500–2,500, and $500K–1.5M at 2,500–10,000. Implementation adds $300–800K in year one, which is 100–200% of the annual subscription. The initial term is three years with a 60–90 day opt-out, escalators of 3–5% a year with documented cases at 9%, and full-time-equivalent billing that counts part-timers at 25% and contingent workers at 15–65%.

At 2,500 employees, Workday's software line alone is roughly 11–19x Greenhouse's median contract. That ratio is only a fair comparison if you are buying the suite in order to get an ATS — a $300K implementation aimed at a $60K problem. The real comparison runs the other way: if Workday HCM is already signed, the incremental cost of Recruiting is a module plus agent credits, and the alternative is a $26,646 median contract for a second system **plus** the integration that keeps the worker record in sync. Per-call API metering is what turns that integration into a variable line nobody costed at signature.

## Price the tier, not the logo

Greenhouse's ladder is Core, Plus and Pro. Real Talent Matching sits in Core. Fraud and spam detection, CLEAR ID verification, Sourcing Automation, texting and the Business Intelligence Connector start at Plus. Enterprise Real Talent features, unlimited CRM events, audit log and the developer sandbox are Pro. If fraud control is the reason you are choosing Greenhouse over Workday, then Plus is your floor — get the Plus quote, because a Core quote prices a product that does not include the thing you came for.

## Two API deadlines for the same budget line

Greenhouse's Harvest API v1 and v2 stop working after **August 31, 2026**; every integration built on Basic Auth against those versions has to move to Harvest v3, which uses OAuth 2.0 with bearer JWTs and cursor-based pagination. *Guard:* inventory every consumer of your Harvest key — assessment vendors, BI jobs, the offer-letter script someone wrote in 2022 — and confirm a v3 build for each before the cutoff.

Workday's mirror-image problem is that APIs external platforms call are now metered per call, so scheduled syncs carry a price they did not carry before. *Guard:* pull 30 days of call volume by consuming system before your next true-up, move high-frequency polling to event-driven pulls, and get the metering rate and included allowance onto the order form.

## Watch-outs

- **Workday is the named defendant in the AI hiring case the rest of the category is watching.** Mobley v. Workday is an ADEA collective action in the Northern District of California over algorithmic screening. The court authorized notice with a March 7, 2026 opt-in deadline, denied dismissal on March 6, 2026, and allowed core claims to proceed on June 22, 2026. Discovery is open, no trial date is set, and your exposure as the employer is separate from Workday's. *Guard:* hold a human decision step on every rejection an agent scores, keep the current [bias audit](/en/learn/ai-screening-bias/) artifact on file, and put a contractual duty on the vendor to supply audit evidence in the format [NYC Local Law 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/) requires rather than a compliance attestation.
- **A fraud flag is a signal, not a finding.** Greenhouse's rule-based detection fires on phone, email, IP and location patterns, which means a legitimate candidate behind a corporate VPN can trip it. *Guard:* route every flag to a named reviewer, log the disposition, and never let a flag auto-reject — the rules are deliberately not a score, so treating them as one is your error, not the vendor's.

## Verdict

- **Pick Workday Recruiting** when Workday HCM is already your system of record at 5,000+ employees, hiring is high-volume across many job families, and the question is what to add rather than what to replace. Frontline-heavy employers get the most, because that is where the Paradox agent's economics come from.
- **Pick Greenhouse** when hiring-manager behavior is the actual problem, when Workday is not your HCM, when you need candidate-fraud controls that shipped on a date you can cite, or when you need to be live this quarter instead of next year.
- **Pick neither** when you need enterprise TA breadth — career sites, CRM, high volume — without committing to an HCM, which is [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/); or when you are under roughly 2,000 employees and want recruiter speed with native analytics, which is [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/). If frontline conversational apply is the only thing you want, buy [Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/) standalone and keep your ATS.

If you cannot decide, default to **Greenhouse**. The suite argument is real but conditional — it depends on an HCM decision made for other reasons — while the structured-hiring argument holds on its own. Teams that buy Workday Recruiting because the HCM was already there stay happy; teams that buy the suite in order to get its ATS learn otherwise at the year-one implementation invoice.
