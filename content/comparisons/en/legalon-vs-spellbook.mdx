[LegalOn](/en/tools/legalon/) and [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) both put AI contract work inside Microsoft Word, and both get bought by teams that will not sit through a six-month CLM rollout. They approach the contract from opposite ends. LegalOn starts from a standard: 50+ attorney-written playbooks ship with the product, and Review grades a counterparty draft against one of them before a lawyer opens it. Spellbook starts from the document: Draft produces first-pass language, Review redlines it, Compare benchmarks the terms, and Associate runs multi-document transactional work from a single instruction. That split was clean until 30 June 2026, when Spellbook launched Autonomous Contract Management — intake from email and Slack, first-pass review against your standards, routing, a negotiation workspace, and a post-signature repository. That is the same job LegalOn sells as its Productivity Suite. The routing question is now sharper: does your paper cross borders, and whose lifecycle layer can you actually buy today.

## Where LegalOn wins

**Jurisdiction coverage shipped as content.** The April 2026 International Playbooks release added NDA and MSA review standards for 23 countries outside the US, each reviewed by attorneys admitted in that jurisdiction, plus EU and Singapore data-residency options. Spellbook reviews non-US paper too, but from general legal training rather than a jurisdiction-specific standard with an attorney's name behind it. When governing law changes from deal to deal, that difference is the entire purchase.

**A standard that exists before you write one.** LegalOn's 50+ playbooks are live on day one, and the Playbook Agent released in February 2026 generates more from templates or review guidelines you already have. Spellbook has playbooks as well, but they are the ones your team authors — its default review is anchored to its training. For a three-person legal ops team, that is the difference between buying a review engine and buying a review engine plus a quarter of playbook authoring.

**The queue, not just the document.** Intake Agent chases business stakeholders for the facts missing from a request. Triage Agent, in early access since February 2026, auto-approves low-risk agreements or escalates them. Matter Management tracks work from intake to close, and the October 2025 acquisition of Fides added entity and board management. That is the part of a legal-ops week that has nothing to do with redlining, and it sits on LegalOn's price list now. The company reported passing ¥10 billion in ARR (roughly $67M) in October 2025 and 8,000+ customer organizations as of April 2026, on $200M raised through a $50M Series E in July 2025.

## Where Spellbook wins

**Drafting from nothing.** Point Spellbook at a contract type and it produces a draft, proposes fallback language, and benchmarks the terms against comparable agreements. LegalOn drafts too, but its center of gravity is judging a document that already exists against a standard. On a novel agreement with no precedent anywhere in your library, Spellbook is the one that produces paper.

**Multi-document agent work.** Spellbook Associate takes one instruction and runs a matter-sized job across many documents — producing financing documents from a term sheet, checking hundreds of agreements for inconsistencies. LegalOn's agents are scoped to the intake and triage of incoming requests, not to executing a transaction's document set.

**The law-firm side of the table.** Spellbook sells to firms and in-house departments alike — 4,500+ legal teams across 80+ countries, 10 million contracts run through the product — and signed a two-year exclusive AI partnership with the Canadian Bar Association in March 2026 covering its 40,000 members. LegalOn is built for the in-house side and says so; a firm billing transactional work is not its buyer. Spellbook also tripled revenue in 2025 and raised $40M in debt from RBCx in March 2026 explicitly to acquire smaller legal-AI companies, which makes it the consolidator rather than a consolidation target.

## Pricing reality

The comparison changed in 2026, and not in Spellbook's favor. Spellbook used to publish working numbers, and a roughly $99/user/month entry tier was why it won every bake-off where the buyer needed a price before a procurement cycle. Its pricing page now carries no figures at all — a 7-day free trial, pricing described as "structured around the number of team members on your license," and a demo booking. LegalOn's page is the same shape: Core Review, Contracting Suite, Productivity Suite, a free trial on Core Review, no number against any tier. Spellbook also moved its front door to spellbook.com, so any quote or order form written before that points at a domain that now redirects.

So you are triangulating from third-party trackers on both sides. Treat what follows as estimates, not quotes. Spellbook's reported bands: ~$99/user/month entry, ~$149/user/month team on an annual commitment, ~$199–350/user/month enterprise with a 10-seat minimum and a six-month minimum term, which lands near $24K–42K/year at 10 seats. LegalOn's last self-published figure was a single-seat Individual plan at $550/month billed annually ($6,600/year), since pulled; competitor-published breakdowns put an all-modules seat near $8,000/user/year and a five-seat deployment near $40,000/year. Set against each other, an all-modules LegalOn seat estimates out at roughly 2x the top of Spellbook's enterprise band and 5–6x its entry band.

The shape of the bill matters more than that multiplier. LegalOn's price is modular — Review is the base, and Matter Management, Vault, Translate, and the international playbook pack each move the line. Spellbook's is closer to flat per seat, with ACM a new line whose price is not public. Guard both the same way: get the fully loaded per-seat number, with every module you intend to switch on in year one, in writing, before you compare anything. A LegalOn base tier set against a Spellbook all-in quote flatters LegalOn by a wide margin, and the reverse flatters Spellbook.

## Implementation effort

Both are Word add-ins, and both skip the rollout a CLM like [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) demands. What each needs from you differs. Spellbook is productive the same day on general drafting and review; its playbook step is optional, and skipping it costs you output quality rather than setup time. LegalOn is also productive the same day, because the standard ships with it — but its value scales with the modules you enable, and Matter Management and Vault are configuration projects, not toggles.

The availability gap is the fact to price in. LegalOn's lifecycle pieces — Intake Agent, Vault, Matter Management — are on the price list today, with Triage Agent still gated as early access. Spellbook's ACM is rolling out to selected teams from a waitlist, not generally available. If lifecycle orchestration is the reason you are buying, that gap decides the deal for this budget cycle, and it will close in a later one. Neither vendor publishes an MCP server, and neither names an iManage or NetDocuments connector, so if your executed contracts live in a DMS, prove the ingest and export path during the pilot rather than after signature.

## Verdict

**Pick LegalOn** when your paper crosses borders and you want a review standard you did not have to author — attorney-reviewed NDA and MSA playbooks across 23 non-US jurisdictions is coverage bought as content, not as a prompt you write yourself — or when the bottleneck is the request queue rather than the document, and intake, triage, and matter tracking are what eat the week. It is the in-house, multi-jurisdiction, standard-first pick.

**Pick Spellbook** when the constraint is producing paper rather than judging it: drafting from a blank page, market benchmarking, and Associate running a transaction's document set from one instruction. Pick it too when you are a law firm rather than a legal department, where LegalOn is simply not aimed at you.

**Pick neither** when the need runs past contracts into legal research, litigation, or broad advisory work — [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) and [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) cover that at materially higher cost, and [GC AI](/en/tools/gc-ai/) publishes a per-seat floor if you want contracts plus employment and compliance answers in one surface. And if what you actually need is a system of record with approval routing and renewal alerts, both of these are the wrong category: buy a CLM and put contract AI on top of it.

**If you are choosing in a vacuum, pick Spellbook.** It is cheaper at every band third parties report, it produces value without you authoring a playbook first, and the switching cost away from a Word add-in is a month rather than a migration. Move to LegalOn when a specific trigger fires — the first deal under non-US governing law that nobody on the team can benchmark, or the quarter when triage and intake, not redlining, become the constraint.
