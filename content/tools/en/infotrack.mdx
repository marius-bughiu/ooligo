## What it is

InfoTrack US is court e-filing and service of process that runs inside the practice-management system a firm already uses. From a matter in Clio, Smokeball, Filevine, LEAP or MyCase, a paralegal uploads a pleading, InfoTrack reads it and pre-fills the filing, submits it to the state court, and orders service from a local process server. The stamped copy, proof of service, status and cost come back to the matter. Around that core it sells DocketSync (other parties' filings as they post), address research, eSignature, federal and bankruptcy case search, and in California physical filing and courtesy-copy delivery.

It is the US arm of an Australian group (ATI Global) that built InfoTrack in Australia more than 20 years ago before expanding to New Zealand, the UK, Canada and the US. It owns the California filer One Legal (acquired 2020) and Green Filing (2021), and holds a majority stake in [LawToolBox](/en/tools/lawtoolbox/). [Smokeball](/en/tools/smokeball/) is a sister company.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Filing stops being a re-keying job.** InfoTrack Intelligence, launched in April 2025, uses OCR plus jurisdiction rules to fill the filing form from the document. InfoTrack says 95% of fields are completed by AI and accuracy is 99%. Those are vendor figures, not audited ones. The practical effect is that case numbers, party names and document types stop being typed twice.
- **Costs land on the matter automatically.** In Clio, filing fees post to the matter's Activities tab and documents sync to the matter folder. The same pattern holds for Filevine (integration announced September 24, 2025), Smokeball and LEAP. For a firm that bills disbursements back to clients, that removes a monthly reconciliation chore.
- **One vendor for filing and serving.** Proof of service can be filed in one click in e-filing states, and every serve attempt is logged with GPS, a timestamp and a photo.
- **Agents can file.** InfoTrack publishes a US MCP server with OAuth per user and separate test and production environments. A [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) agent can start a filing, order service and check status. See [what an MCP server is](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).

## Pricing reality

There is no subscription, no per-user fee and no contract. You pay per transaction, InfoTrack fronts the court fees, and everything arrives on one monthly invoice.

- E-filing: $9 service fee per filing in Texas (eService included), and the pricing page shows an $8 average across states, on top of statutory court fees. Rejected filings are not charged.
- Process serving: set by the local agency plus an InfoTrack technology fee. The pricing page shows a $110 average; a Chicago ZIP shows about $100 standard and $150 rush.
- Address research $25 (charged only when an address is found), eSignature $5 per document, DocketSync $10, California physical filing from $130.

A litigation practice filing 40 documents a month in Texas and serving 15 parties pays about $360 in InfoTrack filing fees plus roughly $1,500 in service, before court fees. That is about $4,300 a year for filing alone, which is the number to weigh against filing through the free state portal.

## Best for

Litigation paralegals and operations managers at firms of 2 to 100 lawyers in InfoTrack's ten e-filing states (Texas, California, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Maryland, Nevada, Utah, and select Virginia courts) that run Clio, Smokeball, Filevine, LEAP or MyCase and want filing, service and the expense entry to happen from the matter.

## Versus the alternatives

**The free state portal** (eFileTexas and its equivalents) is the default most firms start with. Pick it when you file a few documents a month and have no practice-management integration to feed. Pick InfoTrack once filing volume or disbursement reconciliation costs more staff time than the per-filing fee.

**ABC Legal** has the largest serving network of the three: 12,000+ servers by its count, flat per-party serving from $76 to $350 by state, and e-filing in 20+ states. Its e-filing only covers filings tied to service of process. Pick it when service is the bulk of your spend, which is typical of collections and high-volume plaintiff work. Pick InfoTrack when you need general e-filing in your state and matter sync.

**Proof** is the fastest-growing entrant. It publishes state-by-state serving prices (from $89 standard, $50 posting), raised a $30.4M Series B, and integrates with Clio, Filevine, Litify, MyCase and SmartAdvocate. Pick Proof when serving across many states at a known price matters more than e-filing coverage. Pick InfoTrack when your filing state is one of its ten and you run Smokeball or LEAP, which Proof does not list.

If none fit, because your courts are outside these coverage maps, file through the state's own EFM and order service from a local agency directly, logging costs to the matter by hand.

## Watch-outs

- **Coverage is ten states, and InfoTrack's own pages disagree on where AI pre-fill is live.** The court-filing page lists ten states; the Intelligence page lists eight, with California and Utah "next up". *Guard:* send InfoTrack your list of courts and get written confirmation of e-filing and AI pre-fill for each one before you move workflows.
- **Per-filing fees add up against a free portal.** *Guard:* count last quarter's filings, multiply by $8-$9, and compare that to the paralegal time the integration saves. If the integration saves less than about 15 minutes per filing, the portal is cheaper.
- **Serve prices are set locally, not by InfoTrack.** *Guard:* price your three busiest ZIP codes on InfoTrack, Proof and ABC Legal before committing service volume.
- **Fronted fees create a reconciliation dependency.** InfoTrack pays the court and invoices you monthly. *Guard:* map expense codes in the practice-management system before go-live, and reconcile the first two invoices against matter ledgers line by line.
- **An agent that can file needs limits.** *Guard:* build against the MCP test environment, require a human approval step before any submission, and keep agent access scoped to the user's own InfoTrack login.
- **Security documentation is not public.** None of the pages we checked mention SOC 2. *Guard:* ask for the current SOC 2 Type 2 report or equivalent before connecting it to your document system.
