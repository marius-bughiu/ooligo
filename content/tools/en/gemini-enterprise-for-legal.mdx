## What it is

Gemini Enterprise for Legal is Google Cloud's legal packaging of [Gemini Enterprise](/en/tools/gemini/), announced on 25 August 2026 and still in preview. It is not a separate application. It is a legal layer inside the Gemini Enterprise app: legal skills, MCP connectors into the systems firms already run, a handful of pre-built partner agents, and a governance control plane for IT and risk teams. Cleary Gottlieb, Freshfields, Weil, and Williams & Connolly are the named launch firms.

The question buyers are actually asking is whether this is a real rival to [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) and [Claude for Legal](/en/tools/claude-for-legal/), or Gemini with a legal label. The honest answer is between the two. The model and the governance are real. The legal depth comes mostly from the connectors, and some of them lead to the vendors Google is supposedly competing with.

## What you actually get

**Skills.** Google names brief drafting, citation verification, contract review and redlining, playbook creation from legacy agreements, regulatory horizon scanning, DSAR fulfilment, redaction for motions to seal, and NDA drafting. They work like Claude's plugins: packaged instructions and tools that encode a firm's playbook, not a separately trained legal model.

**Connectors.** MCP integrations into [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/) and [NetDocuments](/en/tools/netdocuments/) (permission-bound, so ethical walls carry through instead of being flattened into one index), Docusign for contract metadata and workflows, [Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/) and RelativityOne for litigation data, Thomson Reuters HighQ, Free Law Project's CourtListener and Courtroom5 for case law and procedure, and [Solve Intelligence](/en/tools/solve-intelligence/) for patent work. Workspace and Microsoft 365 (Word, Outlook, SharePoint) come with the base product.

**Agents.** The launch catalogue is thin: Deloitte's Contract Summarize Pro and Clause Guard, plus the [Eudia](/en/tools/eudia/) Knowledge Agent. Implementation partners include Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG, Devoteam, and Factor Law.

**The control plane.** VPC Service Controls, customer-managed encryption keys, data residency, access controls inherited from source systems, and audit logging. Google states that customer data is not used to train its foundation models. These controls are tied to the Standard and Plus editions of Gemini Enterprise, which is the practical reason this is a big-firm product.

## The scoped use case that justifies it

A 400-lawyer firm already on Google Workspace and iManage, with a privacy practice drowning in DSARs. Today an associate exports email, Drive, and DMS hits by hand, then reviews them in a spreadsheet. With the DSAR skill and the iManage connector, the agent assembles the responsive set across Gmail, Drive, and the DMS under the requesting lawyer's own permissions, and drafts the response for attorney review. Nothing is bulk-exported, so matter walls hold. That is the job where Google's estate-wide reach beats a standalone legal AI tool, which would need the data copied into it first.

## Pricing reality

Google publishes no price for the legal layer. No SKU, no seat minimum, no trial terms: access to the preview goes through Google Cloud sales. What you can anchor on is the base app, from Google's Gemini Enterprise pricing:

- **Business**: from $21/seat/mo, up to 300 seats, **but without CMEK, VPC Service Controls, or data residency**, so it is not the edition a firm handling privileged material should run.
- **Standard**: from $30/seat/mo, quote-only past the trial, with the compliance controls.
- **Plus**: quote-only; reseller guides put it near $50/seat on an annual commit.

A 100-lawyer firm on Standard pays at least about $36,000/year for the base seats before any legal premium. That is an order of magnitude below Harvey, which third-party reports put near $1,200/seat/month on a 20-seat minimum, and close to [Legora](/en/tools/legora/)'s reported $3,000/user/year. The catch is that the connectors run on licences you already pay for: iManage, Relativity, Everlaw, and Thomson Reuters are prerequisites, not inclusions. And if you want the Harvey or Legora connector to do anything, you are paying for Harvey or Legora as well.

## Best for

Large firms and corporate legal departments, meaning the CIO, the head of legal ops, or the innovation partner, that already run Google Workspace or Google Cloud, already license a DMS and an eDiscovery platform, and want one governed AI layer across all of it instead of another point tool. The fit is strongest for privacy, regulatory, and contract-heavy practices, where the work is moving data across systems rather than legal reasoning from scratch.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/)** is the segment leader by revenue and firm count. Pick it when you want a vendor accountable for legal output quality, with lawyer-facing onboarding, Shepard's citation validation through its LexisNexis alliance, and workflows built by people who sell only to lawyers. Google even connects to it, which tells you which layer each one is claiming.

**[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/)** is the incumbent for primary law. Pick it when Westlaw research is the core job. Note that Google's Thomson Reuters connector at launch reaches HighQ, not Westlaw, so Gemini does not replace a research licence.

**[Legora](/en/tools/legora/)** is the fastest-growing entrant. Pick it when you want a collaborative review and drafting surface your associates will open on their own, at a reported floor near $30,000/year.

**[Claude for Legal](/en/tools/claude-for-legal/)** is the closest analogue: a frontier lab's plugins and connectors on top of a general seat. Pick it over Gemini when you want to fork and own the prompts (it is Apache 2.0 on GitHub), when your team can self-serve without a sales cycle, or when you are a Microsoft shop with no Google contract to bundle into. OpenAI's Astra for Law, announced in mid-September 2026, is the third lab offering, sold initially to selected firms through a trusted-access programme.

Take Gemini Enterprise for Legal over all of these when your firm's estate is Google, your risk committee cares about CMEK and residency more than legal-specific UX, and you want one AI contract that also covers the business side of the firm.

## Watch-outs

- **It is a preview with no published price.** Capabilities, connectors, and terms can change before general availability. Guard: sign a pilot, not a multi-year commitment, and get the GA pricing and the edition requirement in writing before you scope a firm-wide rollout.
- **The "legal" depth is mostly partners.** Strip out the Harvey, Legora, Thomson Reuters, and Solve connectors and what remains is a strong general model with skills. Guard: list the five workflows you would buy it for, and check for each one whether the work is done by Google's skill or by a third-party licence you would still need to hold.
- **Citations still need a lawyer.** Citation verification is a named skill because generated briefs contain bad citations. Guard: attach CourtListener or your research platform before anyone drafts a filing, and make attorney verification of every cite a required step in the matter workflow.
- **The Business edition looks cheap and lacks the controls.** $21/seat is the number that gets quoted internally. Guard: budget on Standard or Plus from day one, and have your general counsel sign off on the retention, residency, and connector scopes before the first client matter goes through it.
