## What it is

Zammad is an open-source, ticket-first help desk. Email is the centre of gravity: every message becomes a ticket with a full history, and agents work it with text modules, macros, triggers, schedulers, SLAs, business hours and a knowledge base. Web forms, SMS, chat, Telegram, Facebook and WhatsApp feed the same ticket queue. The code is AGPLv3 and owned by the Zammad Foundation, which is separate from the commercial company, Zammad GmbH in Berlin. The founder, Martin Edenhofer, previously created the OTRS help desk.

You can run it two ways. Zammad GmbH hosts it in German data centres on per-agent plans, or you install it yourself from Docker Compose, a Helm chart for Kubernetes, or DEB/RPM packages. The stack is Ruby on Rails with a Vue and TypeScript front end, PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch. The GitHub repository has about 6,100 stars; the company reports more than 3.2 million downloads and 31 employees.

AI arrived late but arrived in one block. **Zammad 7.0** (4 March 2026) added AI agents that run inside triggers, macros and scheduler jobs to categorise tickets, set priority or rewrite titles, with every action written to the ticket history. It also added a ticket summary (customer intent, open questions, upcoming events, sentiment; off by default) and a writing assistant that drafts, rephrases, fixes grammar and translates. **7.1** (17 June 2026) added a Text Extractor agent that writes names, products and invoice numbers into ticket fields, plus a Ticket Tagger. **7.2** (23 September 2026) added knowledge-base answer drafts generated from solved tickets, semantic "Suggested Knowledge" search, a Summary Selector to control AI usage, and a tamper-proof admin audit log. You choose the model: Zammad's managed AI, OpenAI, Anthropic, Azure AI, Mistral AI, a local Ollama install, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

## Why it shows up on the shortlist

- **Self-hosting with AI that never leaves your network.** Point Zammad at a local Ollama model and ticket text stays on your servers end to end. That is the answer to a regulator or works council that rules out US SaaS, and it is a step past Chatwoot, whose AI calls OpenAI even on a self-hosted install.
- **The cloud price is a fraction of the incumbents'.** A 10-agent team on Professional pays €160 a month billed annually. The same team on Zendesk Suite Team pays $550.
- **Ticketing depth for email-heavy teams.** SLAs, custom ticket objects, AND/OR trigger conditions and Core Workflows (dynamic forms) cover what IT service desks, public-sector teams and B2B support queues need, without a marketplace of paid apps.

## Pricing reality

Hosted, per agent per month, in euros excluding VAT (monthly billing in brackets):

- **Starter**: €7 (€9). Max 5 agents. Email, web form and SMS, 20 GB storage. No AI.
- **Professional**: €16 (€18). Max 35 agents. Adds chat, Telegram, SLAs, custom roles, a single-language knowledge base and optional AI.
- **Plus**: €25 (€27). Unlimited agents. Adds WhatsApp, Facebook, Core Workflows, a multilingual knowledge base and Grafana reporting on Elasticsearch.

AI on the hosted service costs **€0.03 per AI call**, where one call is one interaction such as generating a summary. No plan includes a monthly allowance; the trial gives you 500 calls. Self-hosted, the software and AI features are free and you pay your LLM provider, or nothing beyond hardware with Ollama. Optional annual support contracts for self-hosters cost €2,999 (Business, email, 6-hour response), €5,999 (Enterprise, adds phone) or €9,999 (Corporation, 2-hour response and Zammad applies your updates).

Model the AI line before you sign. A 10-agent Professional team handling 3,000 tickets a month costs €1,920 a year in seats. If a trigger-based AI agent categorises every ticket and agents request a summary and one draft per ticket, that is 9,000 calls a month, or €3,240 a year: more than the seats. Annual contracts must be cancelled two months before renewal.

## Best for

Support and IT service-desk leads running 5 to 100 agents on email-first queues, especially in the EU, public sector, healthcare or any organisation that needs the help desk, and ideally the LLM, on infrastructure it controls. Skip it if your volume is mostly WhatsApp and live chat, or if you want an AI agent that resolves customer conversations on its own: Zammad's AI assists agents and routes tickets; it does not answer customers.

## Versus the alternatives

By installed base, [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) and [Freshdesk](/en/tools/freshdesk/) own this budget. Pick Zendesk when you need a deep app marketplace, workforce management and AI agents that resolve tickets; pick Freshdesk when you want a SaaS help desk with a free tier and session-priced AI deflection. [Zoho Desk](/en/tools/zoho-desk/) matches Zammad's seat price in the cloud with more AI included, but cannot be self-hosted. [Chatwoot](/en/tools/chatwoot/) is the other open-source pick; choose it over Zammad when messaging channels, not email, carry most of your volume. The fastest-growing entrants, [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) and [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/), sell autonomous customer-facing agents that sit on top of a help desk; they fill the gap Zammad leaves rather than replace it. Zammad wins when the constraint is data control and the queue is email.

## Watch-outs

- **Per-call AI billing scales with your automation, not your headcount.** A trigger that runs an AI agent on every inbound ticket bills on every inbound ticket. Guard: scope AI agents to specific groups with trigger conditions, use the 7.2 Summary Selector to limit automatic summaries, and read the AI usage for 30 days before rolling out to every queue. If volume is high, bring your own LLM key.
- **Self-hosting means you own the security patching.** In October 2026 Zammad shipped 7.2.1 to fix critical vulnerabilities, a package fix in 7.2.2, and an emergency 6.5.5 for the unsupported 6.5 series. Since April 2026 its security advisories are published only on GitHub. Guard: name an owner, watch the repository's security advisories, stay on a supported release line, and apply security releases within a fixed window such as seven days, or buy the Corporation contract and let Zammad patch.
- **Major upgrades remove things.** 7.0 dropped MySQL and removed the Slack and Twitter/X integrations. Guard: read the release notes before every major upgrade, move to PostgreSQL first, and test in a staging copy.
- **Channel and size limits sit in the plan tiers.** WhatsApp and Facebook need Plus; Professional stops at 35 agents. Guard: map every channel you run to a tier before you compare prices. The pricing table also does not list single sign-on; OpenID Connect support arrived in 6.5, so confirm in writing which hosted plan includes it.
- **The vendor is small.** Zammad GmbH has 31 employees. Guard: the AGPL code belongs to an independent foundation, so you can keep running it; export tickets on a schedule through the REST API either way.
