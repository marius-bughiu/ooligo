## What it is

DataGrail is a privacy-operations platform built for companies whose privacy program is run by one or two people, usually a privacy lawyer or a compliance lead without a dedicated engineering team. Founded in San Francisco in 2018, it last raised a $45 million Series C in October 2022, led by Third Point Ventures, which brought total funding to $84.2 million. DataGrail's press page lists no larger round since.

The modules are Request Manager (DSR intake and fulfillment for access, deletion and opt-out requests), Live Data Map (a system inventory built from your SSO and integrations rather than from questionnaires), Consent Management, Privacy Assessments (PIAs, DPIAs, TIAs and AI risk assessments), Responsible Data Discovery, and Risk Register. DataGrail claims 2,500+ integrations on its homepage (its own navigation says 2,000+), and risk flagging across about 22,000 applications.

The 2026 release that defines the product is **Vera**, launched March 17-18, 2026. Vera is an AI agent inside the platform that answers questions about your data map, builds reports, suggests consent rules for newly detected cookies, and drafts assessment answers with a cited source for each one. It acts under the user's own permissions and does not change anything without approval. The same launch shipped a DataGrail **MCP server**, so admins can query DataGrail from Claude Desktop, Codex or Cursor. Write access through MCP has to be provisioned by an administrator. **Assessment AutoFill** was still labeled beta in DataGrail's April 1, 2026 product update, and DataGrail's vendor figure of "90% faster" assessments is unverified.

Customer logos on DataGrail's site include HubSpot, MLS, FanDuel, Netgear, Okta and Dexcom.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **The privacy team is one lawyer.** DataGrail's pitch is that DSRs and a data map go live without an engineering project. G2's reviewer base matches that: 68% of DataGrail reviews come from mid-market companies, against an enterprise-heavy base for [OneTrust](/en/tools/onetrust/).
- **The data map stays current on its own.** Live Data Map finds systems through SSO and connected apps, so a new SaaS tool shows up without a survey round. That is the inventory a [GDPR](/en/learn/gdpr-for-legal-teams/) Article 30 record and a DPIA both depend on.
- **Support quality is the differentiator.** DataGrail scores 4.7/5 on G2 from about 205 reviews, against 4.3 for OneTrust Privacy Automation, and reviewers single out support and onboarding speed.

## Pricing reality

DataGrail publishes no prices; the site offers only a demo request (checked November 5, 2026). Pricing is quote-based and scales with modules, connected systems and request volume.

Vendr's February 2026 data puts the median contract at about $50,000 a year, with a range from $20,000 to $365,500. Vendr describes typical mid-market deployments at $40,000-$90,000 a year and implementation fees of $10,000-$40,000 on top. A two-module deal (Request Manager plus Live Data Map) at a mid-market company is the low end of that band; adding Consent and Assessments pushes it toward the middle. Vendr reports buyers saving about 16% on average off the first quote, and multi-year terms lowering the annual price.

## Best for

A privacy counsel or compliance lead at a 200-2,000 employee company, running privacy with one or two people, who needs DSRs automated and a defensible data map live within a quarter. Strongest fit: B2C or B2B SaaS companies with a large SaaS footprint and modest in-house infrastructure.

Do not buy it if deletion has to reach your own product databases and warehouse pipelines with engineering owning the work ([Transcend](/en/tools/transcend/) goes deeper there), or if you need third-party risk, AI governance programs and privacy under one enterprise contract ([OneTrust](/en/tools/onetrust/)).

## Versus the alternatives

**[OneTrust](/en/tools/onetrust/)** is the market leader by a wide margin. Pick OneTrust when the program spans privacy, third-party risk, ethics and AI governance and you have staff to configure it. Pick DataGrail when a small team needs results in weeks, not a multi-quarter rollout.

**Securiti**, now owned by Veeam, is the other large platform. Pick it when privacy and data security posture management must share one discovery engine across large unstructured data estates.

**[Transcend](/en/tools/transcend/)** is the engineering-led option. Pick it when a platform team will own the integrations and you need enforcement such as Do Not Train inside your own systems.

**[Ketch](/en/tools/ketch/)** wins when consumer consent and Do Not Sell signals across ad tech are the main job.

**[TrustArc](/en/tools/trustarc/)** wins when assessments, regulatory research and certifications matter more than DSR volume.

## Watch-outs

- **Integration count is not integration depth.** Many of the 2,000+ connectors only detect systems; fewer fulfill access and deletion requests automatically. *Guard:* list the 15-20 systems that hold most of your personal data and make DataGrail mark each one as automated deletion, automated access, or manual task before you sign.
- **Implementation fees can add a fifth to the first-year bill.** *Guard:* get implementation priced as a fixed fee with a go-live date, and tie part of it to the first live DSR workflow.
- **Vera's output is a draft, not an assessment.** AutoFill launched as a beta and suggestions require human acceptance. *Guard:* name a reviewer for every AI-filled DPIA, check the cited sources, and keep a human-run [DSAR intake flow](/en/workflows/dsar-intake-triage-n8n/) as a fallback until audits accept the output.
- **The last disclosed round was 2022.** Privacy vendors have been changing hands (Securiti to Veeam, TrustArc to Main Capital). *Guard:* negotiate a change-of-control termination right, a full data-map export in a neutral format, and a renewal cap on a term of 24 months or less.
