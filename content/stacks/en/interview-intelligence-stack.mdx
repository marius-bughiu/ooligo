This is the stack for the interview itself: the hour that produces the hiring decision and, at most companies, the worst record of it. It assumes you already source candidates and already run an ATS. The constraint it addresses is evidence. Five interviewers, one candidate, and a debrief that runs on memory and tone of voice.

The question changed in 2026. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) now lists its AI Notetaker in all three plans, Core, Plus and Pro. It joins the Zoom, Teams or Meet call, transcribes it, and maps AI-drafted notes to the scorecard questions the interviewer is accountable for, who then edits and submits. That is the baseline job the dedicated recorders in this category were built to do, and it ships inside the ATS most readers of this page already pay for. **On Greenhouse, the default answer is the bundled notetaker and no second contract.** Everything below is what you buy when you can name the reason to spend past it.

The category also stopped being independent, which shortens the list further. Zoom announced its acquisition of [BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/) on 13 November 2025 and closed it that December. The pricing page now reads "BrightHire by Zoom" and splits the product into three separately sold lines. [Pillar](/en/tools/pillar/) went first: Employ acquired it on 5 March 2025, and it now ships as Employ's AI Interview Companion inside Lever and Jobvite, with no dedicated release since June 2025. [Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/) is the last independent of the three, and the only one that publishes a price for the notes job alone. The practical consequence: **your ATS and your reason for buying pick the recorder**, not a feature bake-off.

If you want the whole hiring loop rather than the interview stage, that is the [AI-augmented recruiting stack](/en/stacks/ai-augmented-recruiting-stack/). If your problem is measuring technical skill rather than capturing judgment, that is the [technical hiring stack](/en/stacks/technical-hiring-stack/). If your problem is proving the interviewee is the applicant, that is the [hiring integrity stack](/en/stacks/hiring-integrity-stack/). This one goes deep on one hour.

## How the pieces fit

- **[Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) is the rubric, the record, and the recorder of first resort.** Interview kits and scorecards are first-class objects, which is what makes the rest of this stack worth evaluating at all. A kit defines the competencies each interviewer is accountable for, and the scorecard is where the answer lands. The AI Notetaker attaches the recording and the drafted notes to those competencies rather than to a video library nobody opens, and it is ticked on Core, Plus and Pro, so no upgrade buys it. Greenhouse is also the trigger surface: stage transitions tell any recorder which loop it is sitting in. Greenhouse MCP, which opened in beta on 6 July 2026, makes the interview record readable by an approved agent under scopes a Site Admin sets in Dev Center → MCP Access, permission-aware and audited per call.
- **[BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/) is the recorder when interviewer coaching is the reason you are buying.** Its Interview Intelligence product, priced by company size, bundles AI notes with 1-click ATS scorecards, interviewer quality trends and coaching, SSO with role-based access and opt-out controls, implementation support, and fraud signals in live interviews collected with candidate consent. Two other products now sit beside it and are bought separately: **BrightHire Screen**, an AI interviewer that runs structured screening interviews priced by interview volume, and **BrightHire Inbound**, AI application review priced by applicant volume. The old Recruiters / Teams / Enterprises package ladder is gone from the pricing page, so if an older quote names those packages, ask for a re-quote against the current three products.
- **[Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/) is the recorder when you want the notes job at a published price.** Same core mechanics: it joins the call, transcribes, and syncs notes or a scorecard draft into Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and other ATSs. Its Notetaker is priced on its own: free for 25 calls a month, Pro at $60 per user per month with unlimited calls, Enterprise quoted. Sourcing ($0 / $100 / $300 per user per month) and Application Review are separate products with separate meters. Buying Metaview to record interviews does not commit you to either.
- **[Pillar](/en/tools/pillar/), now Employ's AI Interview Companion, is the recorder if your ATS is Lever or Jobvite.** It records and transcribes, serves the interviewer skills-based questions while the interview runs, collects feedback in the moment, writes a summary back to the ATS, and flags coachable moments afterward. Employ's page is explicit that it "does not provide candidate scoring or hiring recommendations". It belongs here as an alternative to the Greenhouse pairing, not a companion to it: on Greenhouse it would compete with the ATS's own bundled notetaker, and nothing Employ publishes says it is sold apart from an Employ ATS.
- **Zoom, Microsoft Teams, or Google Meet is the capture surface and the consent surface.** It is where the recording notice appears and where the recorder joins as a visible participant, which is the fact your candidates and your counsel will both ask about. BrightHire's fraud signals in live interviews depend on candidate consent, so the consent step in the invite is part of the fraud workflow, not just the legal one.

## The handoffs that make it a stack

Each arrow is an event, not a habit.

A req opens and the recruiter builds the loop in Greenhouse: kits per stage, competencies per interviewer. That kit is the object the recorder reads. When Greenhouse advances a candidate to an interview stage, the scheduled event carries the kit's competencies into the recorder, so the notes come back shaped like the scorecard instead of like a transcript. The calendar invite carries the recording notice and, where AI analysis is involved, the consent request. That goes out before the call, not in its first 30 seconds. The interview runs and the recorder joins the Zoom, Teams, or Meet call as a participant. Within minutes of the call ending a drafted scorecard lands on the Greenhouse candidate record, and **the interviewer edits and submits it**. The draft is not the submission. The moment you allow it to be, you have automated the appearance of structure rather than the practice. The debrief then runs against submitted scorecards, with timestamped clips behind the contested ones. That is the difference between "I didn't get a strong signal" and a 90-second replay. Afterward two readers work the same corpus: interviewer analytics that name who talks most of the hour and who never asks their assigned competency, and, where you have enabled MCP, an agent answering a hiring manager's question against the scorecards and notes under audited scopes, instead of a recruiter exporting a CSV.

## Why this combination

Because interview intelligence is worth nothing without a rubric, and a rubric decays without evidence. [Structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) is the precondition here, not the output. Where no competency is assigned per interviewer, recording produces a larger pile of the same unstructured judgment, at a per-seat price.

Two load-bearing rules. First: **one recorder, and the ATS owns both the rubric and the trigger.** Two recorders on one funnel produce two scorecard drafts and no canonical record, and interviewers will submit whichever one loaded faster. Letting the recorder own the rubric, by defining competencies in the recording tool instead of in Greenhouse, puts your hiring criteria inside the layer you are most likely to replace. Two of the three vendors in this category changed owners inside nine months.

Second: **do not buy fraud detection twice.** Greenhouse's fraud detection and CLEAR ID verification sit in Plus and Pro, not Core, and screen the application. BrightHire now sells fraud signals at three points: applications (Inbound), AI screens (Screen), and live interviews (Interview Intelligence). They catch different stages of the same problem, so name where your leak is, inbound application volume or the interview itself, and buy the one that covers it before adding the other.

## What it costs

Budget roughly **$25K-$75K a year for a 50-250 hire loop**, ATS included, with the ATS as the dominant line. That band is our estimate built from the Vendr medians below plus a recorder line, not a vendor figure.

- **[Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/)** publishes no prices for Core, Plus or Pro; its pricing page says pricing is "customized based on your company's hiring needs." [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/greenhouse) puts the median contract at **$26,553 a year** across 887 purchases, against a range of $10,218 to $73,846, with buyers saving 16% on average. SSO sits in every plan, so security review does not push you up a tier. Fraud detection does: it starts at Plus.
- **[BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/)** publishes no numbers. Interview Intelligence is priced by company size, Screen by interview volume, Inbound by applicant volume, with a discount for bundling. [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/brighthire) reports data from 60 purchases, last updated February 2026: a median of **$18,350 a year**, against a range of $5,500 to $46,083. The same page also prints size bands that run well above its own reported high, so negotiate from the median and the range, not the bands.
- **[Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/)** Notetaker is $0 for 25 calls a month per user, then $60 per user per month. Twenty interviewers on Pro is **$14,400 a year** at list. Sourcing and Application Review are separate subscriptions; do not let them into a notes-only budget.
- **Employ's AI Interview Companion** has no published price. Jobvite lists a separate "Request Pricing" action for it, so treat it as an add-on line in your Employ renewal, and negotiate it there.
- **Conferencing** is a cost you already carry. Treat it as sunk.

The unit that matters is the **marginal** cost per recorded interview, not the gross one. With the Notetaker included in every Greenhouse plan, BrightHire's $18,350 median does not buy recording. It buys coaching analytics, fraud signals in the live interview, and cross-ATS portability on top of recording you already have. At 800 interviews a year that delta is about $23 an interview. Ask whether the named owner of interviewer coaching would pay $23 an interview for those alone. Metaview's Pro list price sets the other reference point: if a dedicated notes tool at $60 a seat is not clearly better than what Greenhouse already produces, a $18K contract will not be either.

## Common variations

- **Notetaker only, nothing else.** On Greenhouse Core or above, run the bundled notetaker for two full quarters, then look at what you still cannot answer. If the gap is "which interviewers are miscalibrated," you have a coaching purchase. If there is no gap, you have saved a five-figure contract and one vendor relationship.
- **BrightHire on the coaching trigger.** Move when you have a named owner for interviewer analytics and a standing monthly slot to review them. The premium over the bundled notetaker is largely that coaching layer, and it bills the same whether or not anyone reads it.
- **Metaview when you are not on Greenhouse, or the bundled notes fall short.** On an ATS with no bundled recorder, Metaview's published $60 seat is the fastest way to a priced pilot without a sales call. Its MCP server can write, including ATS updates, so require confirmation before an assistant acts through it.
- **Employ's companion instead, when you are on Lever or Jobvite.** Native beats integrated on those platforms, and the live in-interview prompt is the feature the others lead with less. Get the next 12 months of companion features and a price hold in writing at renewal, because Employ's 2026 announcements have gone to screening and fraud checks, not to the companion. On Greenhouse it is the wrong pick.
- **Swap Greenhouse for [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/).** Ashby's native analytics answer the interviewer-calibration questions without a reporting upgrade, and both BrightHire and Metaview integrate with it. Choose it when you are selecting an ATS now rather than replacing a working one, and price the recorder as a real line item.
- **Notes only, no AI screener.** BrightHire Screen and Greenhouse's Voice AI both run first-round interviews with no human present. That is a different purchase carrying different disclosure obligations, and buying the notes layer alone is the defensible starting point. Add the screener when your first-round volume, not your curiosity, forces it.

If live in-interview guidance is the specific fix you want and you do not want it tied to your ATS vendor, [Textio's Lavalier](/en/tools/textio/), launched in March 2026, is the newest entrant on that model. It is a shortlist candidate, not yet a stack default.

## When this stack is the right pick, and when it isn't

Pick it when you run 50-250 hires a year through multi-interviewer loops, your debriefs are the weak link, and you already have kits and scorecards defined. It is also the right pick when someone has asked you to show *why* a specific hire was made. A recording tied to a defined competency and a submitted scorecard is the only artifact that answers that question a year later.

Skip the paid layer in four cases. Below roughly 30 hires a year, the same recruiter usually sits in most of the interviews and the memory problem this stack solves is not yet real. If you are on Greenhouse and cannot name which analytic the bundled notetaker fails to produce, the second contract is buying a feeling. If your hiring is high-volume hourly, the interview is a screening question set rather than a judgment loop; that is the [high-volume recruiting stack](/en/stacks/high-volume-recruiting-stack/). And if your loop has no rubric, fix that first with the [interview loop builder](/en/workflows/interview-loop-builder-skill/). Recording an unstructured interview yields an unstructured recording with a transcript attached.

## What this stack does NOT replace

- **Sourcing and scheduling.** Nothing here fills the calendar. Candidates have to arrive from somewhere, and the panel still has to be booked.
- **The hiring decision.** The recorder produces notes, clips, and a drafted score. Weighing them is the panel's job, and the debrief stays human. See [interview loop design](/en/learn/interview-loop-design/) and the [debrief summary workflow](/en/workflows/interview-debrief-summary-skill/) for the shape that survives contact with a disagreement.
- **Skills assessment.** Capturing judgment well is not the same as measuring whether someone can do the work. That is a separate layer with separate vendors: the [technical hiring stack](/en/stacks/technical-hiring-stack/).
- **Identity assurance end to end.** Fraud signals at the interview flag a suspicious call. They do not prove that the person who applied, the person who interviewed, and the person who receives the laptop are one human. That chain is the [hiring integrity stack](/en/stacks/hiring-integrity-stack/).
- **Your consent and disclosure obligations.** Recording consent is jurisdictional, and AI analysis raises the bar again. Illinois' Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42) requires advance notice that AI will be used to analyze the interview, an explanation of how it works and the general characteristics it evaluates, and consent before the interview, and it bars using AI to evaluate applicants who have not consented. On request you must delete the video within 30 days, including copies held by anyone you shared it with, which is a retention requirement your recorder's default settings will not satisfy for you. Where AI ranks or scores candidates, NYC Local Law 144 and its bias-audit and notice duties attach to you, not to the vendor. Consult counsel on how either applies to your jurisdictions, run the [AI interview compliance audit](/en/workflows/ai-interview-compliance-audit-skill/) before the first recorded loop, and read [AI screening bias](/en/learn/ai-screening-bias/) before you turn on scoring.
- **[Quality-of-hire](/en/learn/quality-of-hire/) measurement.** This stack tells you how the interview went and how the interviewer performed. Whether the people you hired turned out to be good at the job is measured after they start, against performance data no recorder holds.
