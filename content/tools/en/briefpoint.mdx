## What it is

Briefpoint is a point tool for one job in litigation: written discovery. You upload a set of interrogatories, requests for admission (RFAs) or requests for production (RFPs), and it returns a captioned Word draft with objections already applied ("Auto Object"), formatted for the court, including California pleading paper. It also works in the other direction, propounding interrogatories, RFAs and RFPs from the allegations in a complaint, and it drafts supplemental responses and interrogatory answers from the case file. The vendor covers all 50 states and the 98 federal district courts.

Two additions moved it past a template engine. Autodoc (September 2025) reads an uploaded production, finds the responsive documents, Bates-stamps them and drafts the RFP response citing each document by Bates number; the vendor aims it at productions of roughly 20,000 documents or fewer. Discovery Playbooks (July 2026, included in every plan) saves a finished response to a firm-wide or client-specific library in one click; when a matching request shows up later, Briefpoint reapplies the firm's objections and response strategy and logs who added the entry and how often it has been used. A Client Bridge link sends plain-language questions to the client, in English or Spanish, with no login, and turns uploads into Bates-stamped PDFs.

The company (The Simple Associate, Inc.) launched in June 2022, founded by Nathan Walter, a former litigator, and Chris Maffin, formerly an engineer at Relativity. It reports 1,000+ firms drafting discovery on the platform.

## Why it shows up in legal-ops stacks

Discovery responses are high-volume, low-margin associate and paralegal hours, and most of the work repeats: the same objections, the same definitions fights, the same production logistics. Briefpoint attacks exactly that slice and nothing else. That narrowness is the reason to buy it. A general legal assistant can draft a response if you prompt it well; Briefpoint ships the caption, the jurisdiction's format, the objection set and the Bates citations without the prompting, and Playbooks makes a senior partner's preferred objection language the default for every associate.

It sits next to practice management rather than replacing it: it works with Word, [Clio](/en/tools/clio/), [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/) and [Smokeball](/en/tools/smokeball/). It is not an [eDiscovery](/en/learn/ediscovery/) review platform; large ESI reviews still belong in a tool like [Syllo](/en/tools/syllo/) or Relativity.

## Pricing reality

One published plan: **$150 per attorney per month, billed annually** ($1,800 per attorney per year), with unlimited document generation and no per-case fees. There is no monthly billing and no published free trial; access starts with a demo. Enterprise adds governance setup (playbooks built in about 14 days), usage reporting by attorney, managed instances and SSO at an unpublished price, and legal-aid organizations get discounted custom pricing.

What a firm actually pays is the list price times the number of attorneys who touch discovery, not total headcount: a 10-lawyer litigation shop where 6 lawyers draft discovery lands near $10,800 a year. That is below the $200-500 per attorney per month that bundled general legal-AI seats typically run, which is why Briefpoint often gets bought alongside one rather than instead of one.

## Best for

Litigation associates and paralegals at small and midsize defense, insurance-defense, employment and lemon-law firms that answer the same kinds of discovery dozens of times a quarter, roughly 3 to 50 litigators. The ROI is best where the firm already has strong house objection language that Playbooks can enforce.

**Not for:** transactional practices, firms whose discovery burden is mostly ESI review rather than written responses, or firms that send fewer than a handful of discovery sets a month; at that volume the annual seat commitment buys little over a general assistant you already own.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/)** — the incumbent with the widest install base in legal AI, usually bundled with Westlaw. Pick it if you already pay for it and discovery is one task among research, review and deposition prep; pick Briefpoint when written discovery is a weekly volume problem and you want jurisdiction formatting and objection libraries out of the box.
- **[Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/)** — the other large-share platform, sold to BigLaw and in-house teams. Pick it when the buyer needs one platform for every practice group; it is overkill and over budget for a 10-lawyer litigation firm.
- **[Eve](/en/tools/eve-legal/)** — the fastest-growing entrant on the plaintiff side ($103M Series B at a $1B+ valuation in 2025). Pick Eve if you are a plaintiff PI or employment firm that also wants demand letters, medical chronologies and intake; pick Briefpoint if discovery is the only bottleneck or you are on the defense side.
- **LegalMation** — the older discovery-automation vendor focused on enterprise and high-volume defense (insurers, automakers). Pick it for corporate-scale programs; pick Briefpoint for firm-level self-serve pricing.

## Watch-outs

- **Objections by default can mean boilerplate objections.** Courts sanction general, unsupported objections, and an auto-applied set can look exactly like that. *Guard:* build the Playbook from responses a partner has already approved, strip objections you would not defend at a meet-and-confer, and keep attorney review on every set before service.
- **Annual-only, per-attorney billing.** There is no monthly plan to trial the tool through a single case. *Guard:* run the demo on a real (redacted) request set from a closed matter, count the attorneys who actually draft discovery, and license only them.
- **Thin integration surface.** Beyond Word and three practice-management systems, there is no published API or MCP server, so Filevine or custom-matter-system shops move files by hand. *Guard:* confirm your system's integration in the demo, and if it is missing, budget the upload and download step into the time-saving math.
- **Client data and PHI in the pipeline.** Medical records flow through discovery responses. Briefpoint says it is SOC 2 Type II audited, HIPAA compliant with BAAs available, and uses zero-retention model providers. *Guard:* request the SOC 2 report and sign the BAA before uploading any medical records.
