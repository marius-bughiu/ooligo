## What it is

Centerbase is a cloud practice and financial management platform for midsize US law firms. Matters, time, billing, general-ledger business accounting and trust accounting run in one system of record, so a firm doesn't bolt QuickBooks onto a small-firm suite. The company is based in Dallas and backed by growth investor Mainsail Partners, which still lists it as a current portfolio company. Michael Dunn is CEO. Centerbase doesn't publish a firm count; third-party listings put its sweet spot at roughly 30 to 200 attorneys.

The core modules:

- **Billing.** Time and expense billing with LEDES 2002 output and UTBMS task codes. An August 2026 partnership with Scan Logic adds direct submission to clients' e-billing portals.
- **Accounting.** Built-in general ledger plus trust and retainer accounting, IOLTA handling and three-way reconciliation, with a vendor-payments (AP) product alongside it.
- **Payments.** Centerbase Payments, launched 19 August 2025, is embedded and runs on [Stripe](/en/tools/stripe/): card, debit and ACH straight from the invoice, with the PCI scope carried by Stripe.
- **Time capture.** Native passive capture from Outlook and Word. [Billables AI](/en/tools/billables-ai/) went live as an API integration in April 2026, and [PointOne](/en/tools/pointone/) also lists a Centerbase connector.
- **Workflow and documents.** A workflow engine with a [NetDocuments](/en/tools/netdocuments/) connector. In March 2026 Centerbase announced a deeper ndMAX integration: phase one, in its April release, pushes matter data into NetDocuments to set up workspaces and templates, and phase two, announced for May or June, writes extracted parties, dates and obligations back. [LawToolBox](/en/tools/lawtoolbox/) also lists Centerbase among its integrations.

## Centerbase IQ: what it does and what it doesn't replace

Centerbase IQ was announced at the ALA annual conference in April 2026 as a limited release. It began with a pilot of about half a dozen customer-advisory-board firms and a waiting list for other existing customers, and it is included in new customer deals. It answers plain-English questions, such as which matters are over budget or what realization looks like by attorney this quarter, over more than 20 Centerbase data entities, and returns narrative, tables and charts with citations to the source records. Firms can load their own benchmarks, such as associate monthly hour goals. LawSites reports it defaults to Anthropic's Claude as the underlying model.

Does it replace a BI tool? For questions about data that lives inside Centerbase, mostly yes. A managing partner who asked the billing team for a weekly realization report can ask IQ directly and click through to the records. It does not replace [Power BI](/en/tools/microsoft-power-bi/) or [Tableau](/en/tools/tableau/) when you need to join Centerbase data with HR, CRM, marketing or document-system data, build dashboards for the whole partnership, or keep a governed metrics layer. Pulling in partner data such as NetDocuments and Billables is on Centerbase's roadmap, not in the product today.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price. Centerbase's pricing page describes flexible, user-based pricing without dollar amounts, and G2 and Software Advice say "contact vendor". One directory shows "from $59 per month", which Centerbase does not confirm; don't budget from it. Expect a per-user subscription quote plus a separately scoped data migration and implementation. Moving ledger and trust history is the bigger unknown, so get both numbers in one proposal. Ask whether IQ is in your quote, and what the Stripe payment-processing rates are.

## Best for

Firm administrators, controllers and billing managers at US firms of roughly 20 to 150 lawyers who want billing, trust and full general-ledger accounting in one cloud system, plan to add AI time capture, and want partners to ask the financial system questions without a report request. It fits best on NetDocuments, where the ndMAX workflow blocks pay off.

## Versus the alternatives

[SurePoint](/en/tools/surepoint/) is the head-to-head rival for the same band. Pick it when the firm wants lateral-hiring and competitor intelligence (Legal Insights) or already runs a legacy SurePoint/LMS install; pick Centerbase when partner self-service analytics and NetDocuments workflow matter more. [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) is the market-share leader and the fastest-moving entrant into midsize firms, with Clio Operate launched in North America in March 2026. Pick Clio when client experience, app marketplace breadth and published pricing (from $49 per user per month) matter more than built-in general-ledger depth. [Aderant](/en/tools/aderant/) and [Elite 3E](/en/tools/elite-3e/) are the large-firm finance platforms; pick one above roughly 150 to 200 lawyers or with multi-entity, multi-currency books. If none fit, a firm under 20 lawyers is usually better served by Clio or [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/) plus [QuickBooks](/en/tools/quickbooks/).

## Watch-outs

- **IQ is still a limited release.** The April 2026 launch was a pilot with a waiting list, and the agentic, action-taking features are roadmap. Guard: make IQ access a dated line item in the order form and test it in the demo on your own sample data, not the vendor's.
- **The ndMAX integration ships in phases.** Phase two (write-back from documents into Centerbase) was announced for May or June 2026, and we found no confirmation it shipped. Guard: ask for a live demo of the write-back on a real contract before you count it in the business case.
- **No published price and a separate migration bill.** Guard: get a per-user rate, migration scope, implementation fee and a cap on year-two and year-three increases in the same written proposal.
- **Reviewers flag depth gaps at the edges.** Capterra rates customer service 3.8 of 5, below the 4.4 overall, and comparison reviewers say expense accounting and some billing functions are less granular than heavy users want. Guard: run a month-end close, a trust reconciliation and a LEDES batch to your two largest e-billing clients in a sandbox before signing.
- **AI sends firm data to a third-party model.** IQ defaults to Claude. Guard: request the current SOC 2 Type II report, the AI subprocessor list and the data-retention terms for IQ before client financial data is indexed.
