[Canny](/en/tools/canny/) and [Productboard](/en/tools/productboard/) both promise to turn customer requests into a roadmap, and buyers shortlist them together for that reason. They are metered on opposite sides of the same problem. Canny charges for the people who *give* feedback: 25 tracked users free, then a published ladder that reaches $1,079/month at 5,000, while your PMs sit on free seats. Productboard charges for the people who *act* on feedback: makers, with a 5-maker minimum and AI credits on top, and since its autumn 2026 repricing no public price at all. Both of its remaining plans, Business and Enterprise, sit behind a demo. Their AI points in opposite directions too. Canny's Autopilot works the inbox, and Productboard's Spark works the PM's desk. Decide which of those two jobs is actually your bottleneck, and the pricing question mostly answers itself.

## Where Canny wins

- **You can buy it today, at a price you can read.** Canny publishes its whole Pro ladder and lets you start free. Productboard's pricing page lists Business ("Request demo") and Enterprise ("Contact sales") with no dollar figure on either. The Free and Plus plans and the self-serve trial are gone. A two-PM team can be live on Canny this afternoon, but it starts a sales cycle with Productboard.
- **The intake AI is unmetered on every plan, including free.** Autopilot captures, deduplicates and groups feedback with no credit counter behind it. Its 2026 additions widened the capture surface: [Fathom](/en/tools/fathom/) and Grain call capture on 30 July, Fireflies.ai on 11 August, and `@Canny` mentions inside any [Slack](/en/tools/slack/) thread on 13 August. Productboard's equivalent analysis draws down credits every time it runs.
- **A public voting board that scales to your whole user base at no extra seat cost.** Canny gives you unlimited boards, unlimited feedback, unlimited contributor seats, and a native changelog with subscriber emails. Productboard's Business plan caps you at 2 Product Portals and 25 contributors, and white-labeling the portal is Enterprise only.
- **Feedback that carries opportunity value.** Autopilot links open [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) and [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) opportunities to the feedback it captures, so a request arrives weighted by the deal attached to it rather than by vote count alone. Both vendors gate CRM behind their top tier. Canny's is a Business quote above an already-published ladder. Productboard's Salesforce integration is Enterprise only, Business gets a CRM CSV importer, and HubSpot does not appear on its pricing page at all.
- **Agent access with no seat floor.** Canny's MCP server exposes more than 55 tools, including `move_idea`, `create_insight`, `link_opportunity` and portal comment management, and it ships on every paid plan from Pro at $79/month. Productboard's MCP server and connectors are included on both of its plans, but the cheapest way in is a five-maker Business contract. At the last published list price that is about $295/month.
- **PM headcount is free.** Pro includes 10 manager seats and unlimited contributors, so adding three PMs to Canny costs nothing. On Productboard, a two-PM team still pays for five makers.

## Where Productboard wins

- **Spark does product work, not just triage.** The Specification tab (19 May) drafts specs collaboratively on initiatives and features. System skills cover spec generation and competitive analysis, and since 12 August the MCP server creates and edits objectives, key results, initiatives, components and features programmatically. Skills are reusable instructions you write once, upload as `.skill` packages, or generate with `/create-skill`. Canny triages the inbox and stops there, and it has nothing on this axis.
- **An actual prioritization model.** Productboard gives you custom prioritization formulas, feature hierarchies, dependency tracking, release planning, and timeline or column roadmaps. The objective hierarchy itself is an Enterprise feature. Canny gives you statuses on a board and a vote count. If your argument for a quarter has to survive a steering committee, Canny hands you raw demand and Productboard hands you a scored, structured plan.
- **Usage data sits next to the request.** [Amplitude](/en/tools/amplitude/) and [Mixpanel](/en/tools/mixpanel/) integrations are available, and Spark's MCP connectors reach Amplitude, [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/), Hex, [Linear](/en/tools/linear/) and [Notion](/en/tools/notion/), with a GitHub connector added 20 July. Spark also reads Confluence, Google Drive and Notion documents on both plans. You can weigh a loud request against whether anyone uses the adjacent feature. Canny weighs by votes and, on Business, by pipeline.
- **The handoff to engineering agents is native.** The MCP server pulls a spec and its comments straight into [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) Code, Codex or [Cursor](/en/tools/cursor/) with the surrounding context intact. Canny's MCP surface is built around feedback objects, not specs.

## Pricing reality

Canny publishes its whole ladder. Free is $0 for 25 tracked users and 5 managers. Pro billed annually runs $79/month at 100 tracked users, $279 at 500, $529 at 1,000, $829 at 2,500 and $1,079 at 5,000. Month-to-month is roughly 25% higher: $99, $349 and $661 at the first three rungs. Above 5,000 you are on a Business quote. A tracked user is anyone who posts, votes or comments, including users that Autopilot discovers on your behalf. Canny's own planning number is that 1-5% of a B2B SaaS user base becomes tracked.

Productboard prices by maker and no longer shows the price. Both plans carry a 5-maker minimum, and the page offers 21% off for annual billing without saying 21% of what. The last list price Productboard published for Business was $59 per maker per month annually ($75 monthly), in its own September 2026 billing FAQ. Treat that as a reference point, not a quote. At the minimum it puts the floor at $3,540/year on annual billing, or $4,500/year monthly. Enterprise has no public rate. AI credits ration the part that matters. Business includes 500 per maker per month, and Enterprise includes 1,500 base plus 800 per additional maker. The vendor's own conversion rates are the number to model: analyzing 100 feedback items costs 30-50 credits, and a full specification costs 50-200. A team processing 5,000 feedback items a month burns 1,500-2,500 credits on triage alone, which is 60-100% of a five-maker Business workspace's 2,500 before anyone writes a spec. Top-ups cost $5 per 50 credits monthly or $60 per 600 annually, which is $0.10 a credit either way. When credits run out, Spark stops until the monthly reset or a top-up.

The minimum moves the crossover. At five PMs and 500 feedback-givers, Canny Pro costs $3,348/year against roughly $3,540/year on Productboard Business. That is within 6%, so price routes nothing there. Below that scope Canny is 3-4× cheaper: five PMs at 100 tracked users pay $79/month against roughly $295. The minimum also means a two- or three-PM team pays that same $295. Under the old $19 Plus seat it could have paid about $38. Above that scope the curves invert. At 5,000 tracked users Canny is $12,948/year, while five Productboard makers stay near $3,540 no matter how much feedback arrives. At procurement scale both converge anyway. Vendr's data puts Canny's median contract at $20,000/year and Productboard's average at $24,280 across 191 deals, ranging from $7,728 to $78,666 with an average 21% saved off list.

## Implementation effort

Canny takes days to set up: a board, the widget and one capture integration. The trap is billing, not configuration. Users that Autopilot discovers count toward your tracked-user limit, and crossing the limit auto-upgrades you to the next rung with a prorated charge unless you have set a monthly spend limit. With a cap set, Canny stops tracking feedback from new users once it is reached. Turning on Fireflies and Fathom capture across a sales team can move you up a rung in a week. Set the spend limit before you enable call capture, not after the invoice.

Productboard takes weeks, and now it starts with a sales call. You define teamspaces, a prioritization formula and segments, then teach Spark enough context to be worth its credits. With no self-serve trial listed, the old trick of measuring your credit burn on a free trial is gone. Ask for a pilot or trial credits in the demo, pipe one honest month of feedback through it, and count. Get three numbers written into the order form: the per-maker rate, the maker count, and whether overage hard-stops or bills at month end. Legacy self-serve accounts moved to the new pricing at renewal from October 2026, so an existing Plus customer should read the renewal notice closely. The April 2026 AI-only pivot cut more than 30% of the company, weighted toward sales and customer success, and live onboarding is now an Enterprise-only line item. Ask for support response commitments in the order form, not in the deck.

If you end up running both, note that neither vendor's integration directory lists the other. The bridge is [Linear](/en/tools/linear/) or Jira, which both sync to natively. Jira and Azure DevOps are capped at 5 integrations each on Productboard Business. You have to name one of the two tools as the owner of the request object, or you will maintain two backlogs by hand.

## Bottom line

- **Pick Canny** when collection is the bottleneck. That means feedback arrives from customers across support, calls and Slack, you want it deduplicated and shown back publicly on a voting board and changelog, and your PM team is small. It is also the right pick when the feedback program is community-facing, since the public surface costs nothing per participant, and whenever you need to buy without a sales cycle.
- **Pick Productboard** when deciding and writing are the bottleneck and you have at least five people who will use maker seats. You need a prioritization formula that survives review, features tied to objectives, usage data next to requests, and specs that flow into Cursor or Claude Code. Pay for makers, not for the crowd.
- **Pick neither** if you have two or three PMs and feedback arrives in one channel. Linear plus a shared doc covers it, and Productboard's five-maker floor alone runs about $3,540/year against that. If you already own [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/), its in-app feedback covers the intake job you would be buying Canny for. If your engineering org lives in Jira, evaluate Jira Product Discovery before Productboard. It prices per creator on the same logic, publishes its rates and inherits your existing permissions.

If no condition clearly points either way, pick Canny. The repricing widened the gap: Canny is self-serve and published, and Productboard is a demo with a five-seat floor. Canny also fails in the recoverable direction. A year of deduplicated, revenue-linked feedback exports cleanly into a Productboard workspace later, whereas a prioritization model built on thin intake has nothing underneath it. Switch when you can name the prioritization formula you want to run, when your PMs are short on time for writing specs rather than collecting requests, or when tracked users pass roughly 1,000 and the meter starts working against you.
