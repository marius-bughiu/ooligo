## What it is

Appian is a low-code platform for building process and case-management applications: you model the process, design the forms and record views, and the platform runs every case with its tasks, timers, escalations and audit trail. Three pieces set it apart from a general app builder. The data fabric reads and writes data where it already lives — [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), an ERP, a SQL database — without copying it into Appian first. Case Management Studio ships a prebuilt case workspace, task records, configurable workflows and reporting you customise instead of building from zero. And Agent Studio, generally available since version 25.4 in late 2025, puts AI agents inside those processes as steps with defined inputs, permissions and a human review point.

Appian is a public company (NASDAQ: APPN) and not a startup bet. Q2 2026 revenue was $203.3M, up 19% year on year, with cloud subscriptions up 23% to $131.7M and full-year guidance of $845-853M. Gartner named it a Leader in the Magic Quadrant for Business Orchestration and Automation Technologies published in September 2026, and the companion Critical Capabilities report ranked it first for deterministic workflow automation and platform consolidation.

## What changed in 2026

Since version 26.1, cloud customers can upgrade every month. The March 2026 release let agents invoke other agents as tools and let third-party agents pull context from the data fabric. At Appian World on 28 April 2026 the company announced MCP in both directions — Appian agents calling external systems, and outside agents built in Vertex AI or LangGraph reaching Appian's data and process logic — plus developer MCP servers so [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) Code and AWS Kiro can build Appian apps, AI-assisted spec-driven development that reverse-engineers legacy applications into specs, and a Snowflake partnership so agents can call Snowflake Cortex AI. Appian's own press release labels the MCP, Snowflake and spec-driven items "coming in future releases". Only the expanded data-fabric metadata model shipped at the event.

## The fork it resolves

The question this page answers: *we need governed case management for legal intake and CS escalations — Appian or [Camunda](/en/tools/camunda/)?*

Pick by who builds and maintains the process. Camunda executes BPMN models that developers own, and it wants Java or platform engineers. Appian gives a business analyst the forms, record views, task lists and reports in the same designer as the process, so a legal-ops or CS-ops team with one technical analyst can own the application after a partner builds the first release. If your process is long-running, has human approvals and needs a user interface the business will live in all day, Appian is the stronger default. If engineers own the process and the interface lives in another system, pick Camunda.

## A use case worth buying it for

Legal intake for a 3,000-person company. Anyone in the business submits a request through a portal form: NDA, vendor contract, marketing review, employment question. An agent in Agent Studio classifies the request, extracts the counterparty and deal value from attached documents through DocCenter, and proposes a matter type. Low-risk NDAs route to a paralegal queue; anything above a value threshold or with non-standard terms goes to counsel with a 48-hour SLA timer that escalates to the deputy GC. The requester sees status without calling anyone, and every classification, override and handoff lands in one audit trail. The same pattern fits a CS escalation desk pulling account data from Salesforce and ticket history from [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) or [ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/).

The licensing detail that makes this pencil out: people who only submit a form and check its status can be licensed as Minimal Users, which Appian's UK government price list sets at £3.90 per user per month at up to 250 users and under £1 above 5,000 users.

## Pricing reality

Appian publishes three tiers — Standard, Advanced and Premium — with no prices, licensed per user, per month, per application. The tiers differ in data limits (4M, 20M and 50M rows), RPA bots (5, 25, unlimited) and AI Actions (200,000, 500,000 and 1,000,000 a month). Case Management Studio and Process HQ start at Advanced.

The most detailed public anchor is Appian's own price list for UK public-sector buyers (G-Cloud 14, May 2024), annual term, excluding VAT, at up to 200 users:

- Single-application user: £59 Standard, £74 Advanced, £90 Premium per month.
- Platform user (all applications): £156, £195 or £234 per month.
- Starter Kit Small, one application for up to 50 users: £3,120 per month.
- Minimal User (form submitters, external portal users): £3.90 per month.

Unit prices fall steeply with volume: a Standard single-app user is £11.80 at 1,001-2,500 users. US buyer guides in 2026 put the Standard user at around $75 a month and report that enterprise contracts typically start near $100,000 a year. Budget services on top: professional services were $45.6M of Appian's $203.3M Q2 2026 revenue, about 22%, which tells you how most first apps get built. For a first legal-intake app with 60 legal and ops users on Advanced, three platform-user builders and a few hundred Minimal requesters, plan on £60,000-100,000 a year in licences at those 2024 list prices before a partner's build fee.

## Best for

The legal-ops, CS-ops or operations leader at a company of 1,000+ employees who has to run a regulated or SLA-bound case process — intake, escalations, onboarding, claims — with a business-facing interface and an audit trail, and who wants analysts rather than engineers to own the application after launch.

## Not for

A team of 20 automating SaaS-to-SaaS handoffs, or anyone who wants to try before talking to sales. The free Community Edition is a personal developer site for learning, not a production tier, and the 14-day trial is the only full-feature test.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Microsoft Power Apps** — the default low-code option inside Microsoft 365 estates, $20 per user per month for Premium, rising $2 on 1 January 2027. Pick it when you are an M365 shop, the apps are departmental, and no regulator will ask for a case audit trail.
- **[ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/)** — App Engine on the platform IT already runs. Pick it when the case process is a request to an internal service desk and ServiceNow is already licensed across the company.
- **Pega** — Appian's oldest case-management rival, built around decisioning and next-best-action. Pick it for high-volume customer-facing case work in banking or insurance where the vendor's consultants build and run it.
- **[Camunda](/en/tools/camunda/)** — pick it when developers own BPMN models and the user interface lives elsewhere.
- **Temporal** — the fastest-growing entrant in orchestration, with a $550M Series E in September 2026. Pick it when engineers want durable workflows as code and nobody in the business needs to read the model.

If none fit because the process is small, [n8n](/en/tools/n8n/) plus a [Retool](/en/tools/retool/) front end covers a single-team intake queue for a fraction of the cost, without the case audit model.

## Watch-outs

- **Single-app licences re-bill users for the second app.** A user licensed to one application pays again to use another. Guard: at G-Cloud list prices a platform user costs about 2.6 times a single-app user, so move anyone who will touch three or more apps onto platform licences at signing, while the discount is still open for negotiation.
- **The 2026 AI story is partly roadmap.** MCP both ways, the Snowflake integration and spec-driven development were announced in April as "coming in future releases". Guard: make every AI capability in the business case demonstrable in your trial environment before signature, and price only generally available features.
- **AI Actions are capped by tier.** Standard allows 200,000 a month. An agent making five calls per case on 20,000 cases a month uses half of that. Guard: estimate calls per case times monthly volume during the pilot and pick the tier on that number, not on seat count.
- **Appian skills are a specialist market.** Interfaces and rules are written in SAIL, Appian's own expression language, and the first app is usually partner-built. Guard: put two of your own analysts through Appian developer training during the build, and write knowledge transfer and source handover into the partner's statement of work.
