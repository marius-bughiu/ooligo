## What it is

Gryphon AI sells Gryphon ONE, a contact-governance platform that decides, before each outbound touch, whether you are allowed to make it. The checks cover the federal and state do-not-call lists, the FCC Reassigned Numbers Database, wireless and ported-number status, calling-hour curfews and holidays, a database of serial TCPA plaintiffs, consent and preference records, and established-business-relationship exemptions. It applies them to voice, pre-recorded messages, SMS, email, mail, and human or AI agents. The company is based in Boston, sells as Gryphon AI after years as Gryphon Networks, and claims more than 200 enterprise customers.

The part that separates it from a scrubbing service is where enforcement happens. For calls routed through Gryphon's own carrier connectivity, a non-compliant call is blocked in the network path in real time — the rep cannot place it, whatever the dialer or the list says. For channels that do not ride that network (SMS, email, AI agents, third-party dialers), Gryphon returns an allow-or-block decision through an integration and your system has to honor it. Gryphon's own comparison page draws exactly this line in a footnote; read it before you assume every channel is hard-blocked.

## What changed in 2026

- **Salesforce (January 2026).** Gryphon ONE for Salesforce went onto AppExchange, certifying each outbound voice, mobile, email, and text interaction for TCPA, DNC, and wireless status before it starts, with automatic call logging plus live call guidance and post-call notes.
- **1H 2026 launch (April 28, 2026).** Five additions: the Compliance Hub dashboard across phone, text, email, and mail; automated rule updates for federal and state changes; a native Genesys Cloud Outbound integration; Compliance Registration Services, a managed service for state telemarketing registrations and DNC list subscriptions; and an omnichannel decision engine that covers autonomous agents.
- **Genesys AppFoundry (September 29, 2026).** The Genesys integration became a marketplace listing, checking automated outbound campaigns against TCPA, TSR, DNC, and state rules before dialing.
- **2H 2026 launch (November 10, 2026).** An Agentic Governance API and MCP integration, so an AI agent asks for a compliance decision before it places a call or sends a message.

## Pricing reality

Gryphon's own site says "contact for pricing." The concrete numbers are on its Salesforce AppExchange listing: Advanced Edition from **$12,000 per company per year**, Premium from **$20,000**, Enterprise from **$30,000**. The same listing states that professional services and Gryphon's external app must also be purchased, so the edition price is a floor, not a total. A realistic first-year budget is the edition price plus an implementation services line plus, if you route calls through Gryphon, carrier usage. Multi-year discounts are offered. Treat $12,000 as the entry point for a single business unit, not as the price for a contact center with hundreds of seats.

## Best for

- A RevOps or compliance lead at a consumer-facing outbound operation — lending, insurance, home services, healthcare, education — where reps or AI agents contact individuals on mobile numbers and one class action costs more than a decade of the license. TCPA statutory damages are $500 per call or text, up to $1,500 if willful.
- Teams deploying AI voice or SMS agents that need a pre-contact permission check an agent can call, which the 2H 2026 API and MCP release targets directly.

Don't buy it for a pure B2B SDR team calling business landlines a few hundred times a day: a list-scrub subscription and your dialer's internal DNC list cover that risk at a fraction of the cost. But "we're B2B" does not settle the question if your data provider sells personal mobile numbers — check that with counsel before you decide you don't need suppression at all.

## Versus the alternatives

[Sonera](https://dnc.com/dncscrub), formed in July 2026 when DNC.com (Contact Center Compliance) merged with Pure CallerID, is the incumbent for pre-dial scrubbing: federal and state DNC, wireless ID, litigator lists, and caller-ID reputation, priced on tiers by records scrubbed with monthly or one-time options. Pick Sonera when your dialer enforces suppression reliably and you want list hygiene plus caller-ID reputation, not a network in the call path.

PossibleNOW is the consent and preference-management pick: it holds the same core data and is stronger on enterprise preference centers. Pick PossibleNOW when the problem is consent records across marketing systems rather than blocking dials at the moment of contact.

At the low end, TCPA Black List publishes $80, $95, and $140 monthly tiers for 30,000, 50,000, and 100,000 checks, or $0.05 per check. Pick it when you need litigator suppression on a small list and have no budget for a platform. Pick Gryphon over all three when you want enforcement that does not depend on every rep, dialer, and agent obeying a list — that is what the network-path block buys. ## Watch-outs

- **Hard blocking applies only to calls on Gryphon's network.** SMS, email, AI agents, and calls on another carrier receive a decision, not an enforced block. Guard: list every outbound channel in the evaluation and get written confirmation, per channel, of whether Gryphon blocks or advises.
- **The edition price excludes mandatory services.** AppExchange requires professional services and the external app on top of the $12,000 to $30,000 editions. Guard: require the services fee, carrier usage rates, and renewal uplift in the same quote as the license.
- **Reach-recovery claims are vendor math.** Gryphon markets recovering 25-45% of a "suppressed" market; its own footnote says the figure is based on customers and prospects and results vary. Guard: run a two-week pilot on one list and compare contactable records against your current suppression logic before you put that number in a business case.
- **Thin independent review base.** The AppExchange listing shows a single review. Guard: ask for two reference customers in your industry and channel mix, and have your TCPA counsel review the rule configuration before go-live.
