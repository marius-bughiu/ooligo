## What it is

Influ2 is a contact-level ABM platform. It targets display and social ads at *named individuals* on a CRM-synced or uploaded contact list, reports which of those named people saw and clicked, and — since a 2026-05-06 release — routes what it learns into sales cadences and CRM campaigns. The vendor's own line is "ABM that starts with people. Because people make decisions, not accounts."

That is a wider scope than where the product started. Through 2024 Influ2 sold person-based advertising: an ad-delivery layer bolted onto someone else's account-based program. The 2026 platform adds contact-level intent (Audienscope, a paid add-on shipped 2026-03-27), a Signals Center that uses generative AI to summarize why a given contact is worth calling, cohort and journey builders, and orchestration that pushes next-best actions into Salesloft, Outreach, HubSpot and Salesforce. The ads are still the delivery mechanism, but no longer the whole product.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Person-level targeting that account-level platforms don't do.** [6sense](/en/tools/6sense/), [Demandbase](/en/tools/demandbase/) and [AdRoll ABM](/en/tools/rollworks/) (RollWorks until 2025-08-27) score and serve at the account. Influ2 serves at the named contact and reports engagement by name, which is what a rep working a seven-person buying committee needs.
- **The signal lands where reps already work.** StarTree's vendor-published case study fires a [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) notification when a targeted contact clicks once or crosses 15 impressions. That threshold, not the impression count, is what makes ad data usable by an AE.
- **An MCP server, gated but real.** Influ2 documents an MCP integration that lets Claude search the audience, pull contact and account briefings, and read program performance, plus create and edit programs behind a preview-before-commit step. Destructive operations — replacing a journey, removing audiences, archiving — stay UI-only. The access key comes from your CSM, so it is not self-serve.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price. `influ2.com/pricing` returns a 404 and no pricing page appears in the sitemap; the buying path is the demo form at `/contact-us`. Vendr's February 2026 benchmark puts the median Influ2 buyer at **$60,000/year**, with observed contracts spanning **$34,800 to $113,520**. Contact volume is the primary driver, and per-contact rates fall as volume rises.

Two costs sit outside that figure. Media spend is billed separately from the platform fee — budget both. And Audienscope, the contact-level intent layer, is a paid add-on governed by its own contract addendum, not something bundled into the base agreement. Mid-market programs land around $40,000-$80,000 platform plus media; enterprise deployments start above $100,000.

## Best for

Enterprise B2B teams with $100K+ ACV deals, named buying committees of five or more, and a marketing org that already knows which individuals need to be warm before the AE dials. The band where the economics hold is roughly $40K-$100K of annual platform spend against a pipeline where one influenced deal covers the year.

**Not for** transactional or low-ACV motions, self-serve products, or teams that cannot name the individuals they want to reach. If the target list is "VPs of Engineering at 2,000 accounts," that is an account-level job and the budget belongs elsewhere.

## Versus the alternative

- **vs 6sense / Demandbase / AdRoll ABM.** All three run at the account level with intent scoring and display. Pick them when the job is finding which accounts are in-market. Pick Influ2 when the accounts are already chosen and the job is warming named people inside them. Running both is the normal configuration, not a redundancy.
- **vs ContactLevel.** The direct contact-level competitor, and the one that publishes prices: $1,000/month for 10,000 contacts, $2,500/month for 30,000, syncing to LinkedIn, Meta, Google, Reddit and X. It claims 90%+ match rates against roughly 30% for native list uploads. Pick ContactLevel when you want activation infrastructure and will orchestrate inside your own CRM; pick Influ2 when you want the journey builder, intent layer and revenue reporting on one contract and can defend a five-figure floor.
- **vs LinkedIn contact-list targeting alone.** Cheapest path, one channel, no cross-channel reporting by named individual. Pick it when the committee lives on LinkedIn and the team will maintain the lists by hand.

## Watch-outs

- **Match rate caps the reach you actually buy.** Influ2's own Cato Networks case study reports a 60-70% match rate against the target list — a third of the people you paid to reach never see an ad. **Guard:** price the contract against matched contacts rather than uploaded contacts, and ask for match rate broken out by region before signing.
- **Audienscope moves privacy work onto the buyer.** Its addendum states that Influ2 acts as processor and that "the Client remains responsible for lawful collection/use of data, notices/consents, compliance with marketing/advertising/privacy laws." Signals include job changes, search-campaign results and public activity tied to named individuals. **Guard:** have privacy counsel review the addendum before enabling Audienscope against EU-resident contacts; default the intent layer to US lists.
- **Attribution numbers are vendor-published multiples.** Quantexa's case study claims 4.53x conversion and 837% ROI versus cold outreach; StarTree's claims 3.17x. Both are vendor case studies, and neither describes a holdout. **Guard:** run a matched holdout on 20% of the target list for one quarter and measure lift against that, not against the attributed-contact report.
- **List hygiene is the running cost nobody budgets.** The platform's value depends on the contact list being current, and Audienscope's job-change signal exists because lists decay. **Guard:** wire the audience sync to CRM contact updates instead of periodic CSV upload, and put a named owner on quarterly list review.
