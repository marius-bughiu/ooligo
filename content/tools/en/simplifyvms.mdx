## What it is

SimplifyVMS is a vendor management system (VMS): the software an enterprise uses to request, approve, onboard, track time for, invoice and offboard people who are not on payroll. It is built by Simplify Workforce, a privately held company that dates its releases from Jersey City, New Jersey, and has offices in India, Europe, Australia and Dubai. Manoj Agarwal is CEO. Do not confuse it with **SimpleVMS**, a different product owned by staffing software vendor Avionté. Most of the review-site listings and price figures you will find for "SimpleVMS" belong to that other product.

The product is a set of modules on one platform. The **VMS** core runs requisitions, approvals, onboarding, time and expense, and invoicing, and SimplifyVMS says it covers employees, contractors, freelancers and temporary staff on a single platform across multiple countries, currencies and languages. **Services Procurement** handles statement-of-work (SOW) contracts; the vendor announced SOW enhancements on 28 October 2026. **Direct Sourcing & Talent Pools**, sold alongside a sister brand called hireHQ, lets a program build its own pool of pre-vetted contingent talent instead of routing every request through an agency. A healthcare line, launched on 23 July 2026, adds healthcare workforce management and credentialing. Employer-of-record (EOR) and agent-of-record (AOR) services come through a partnership with People2.0, announced in November 2025, which covers 130+ countries. The question it answers: **can we run a contingent workforce program on a VMS that is not bundled with our HR suite, our ERP or a managed service provider?**

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks

Three of the best-known VMS vendors each come attached to something. Workday VNDLY is tied to Workday HCM, SAP Fieldglass to SAP's procurement suite, and Magnit sells its VMS together with its own managed service. Beeline is independent, but it has grown into a broad extended-workforce platform. SimplifyVMS is the independent option for a program that wants a VMS and nothing else.

Analysts rate it highly. SimplifyVMS reports that Everest Group named it a Leader and Star Performer in its 2026 Global VMS PEAK Matrix, which assessed 29 providers, and a Leader and Star Performer in 2025 and 2024 as well. Those placements come from the vendor's own releases. We could not read the licensed Everest report, so ask for the report excerpt during evaluation.

The AI layer is an **AI Suite** of assistants rather than autonomous agents. It parses résumés, gives candidates match scores against the job, drafts job descriptions, and benchmarks compensation. Two "copilot recruiters", Genie and EMMA, run first-round screening over text or voice. The platform also has an AI-assisted SOW builder and an AI compliance configuration engine. SimplifyVMS calls itself "AI-first", but we found no launch dates, no agent framework and no MCP server, and the features are the matching and drafting aids that most VMS vendors now ship. That is why we score it as AI-assisted, not AI-native.

Integrations are a real strength for an independent vendor. SimplifyVMS lists connectors for [Workday](/en/tools/workday/), SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, [UKG](/en/tools/ukg/), SAP, NetSuite, Coupa, [Okta](/en/tools/okta/), SailPoint, [ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/), [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), Taleo, [Snowflake](/en/tools/snowflake/), Power BI, [Slack](/en/tools/slack/), Microsoft Teams and [Workato](/en/tools/workato/), plus a documented API. ADP is not on the list. Logos on its site include PwC, Marriott, easyJet, CEVA Logistics, Penn State and the Commonwealth of Massachusetts.

## Pricing reality

SimplifyVMS publishes no price list and does not say whether it prices on a supplier-funded or client-funded model. G2 shows no pricing either. Category economics apply. A VMS is either **supplier-funded**, where a fee comes off each staffing supplier's invoice, or **client-funded**, where you pay a subscription. The working benchmark for the technology fee is about 0.3% to 1% of managed spend, falling with scale, based on Beeline's published pricing examples. That works out to roughly $60K to $200K a year on a $20M program. A managed service provider (MSP), if you use one, adds 2% to 3.5% of spend on top.

An independent mid-market vendor usually has more room to move on price than a suite vendor negotiating a VNDLY or Fieldglass line inside a much larger deal. Use that. Ask for both funding models in writing and compare them against your program's total spend, not per-seat.

## Best for

Contingent workforce program leads and TA or procurement operations teams at mid-market and lower-enterprise companies, roughly a few hundred to a few thousand external workers, that run a mix of HR and finance systems and want a VMS with no suite or MSP attached. It also fits healthcare staffing programs that need credentialing in the same platform, and programs starting direct sourcing for the first time.

Skip it if you already run Workday HCM and want contractors in the same worker record as employees; [Workday VNDLY](/en/tools/workday-vndly/) is built for that. Skip it if you run a large global program and need a long public reference base; the incumbents have far more of one. Employee hiring stays in an ATS such as Greenhouse or [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/).

## Versus the alternatives

**[Beeline](/en/tools/beeline/)** is the main independent incumbent and reports 450+ customers. It bought MBO Partners in June 2025 for independent-contractor engagement and launched Beeline MCP in September 2026. Pick Beeline when freelancers, shift workers and agency temps must live in one platform, or when you want agents connected to the VMS through MCP today.

**[SAP Fieldglass](/en/tools/sap-fieldglass/)** is the other long-standing incumbent. Pick it when procurement owns the program and you run SAP S/4HANA and Ariba.

**[Workday VNDLY](/en/tools/workday-vndly/)** is the suite option, sold by Workday since it bought VNDLY in 2021. Pick it when Workday HCM is your system of record.

**[Magnit](/en/tools/magnit/)** sells its VMS together with its own MSP. Pick it when you want one firm to run both the software and the program.

Pick SimplifyVMS when none of those conditions apply and you want an Everest-rated independent VMS that sells the software on its own.

## Watch-outs

- **Thin public evidence.** G2 shows around five reviews and Gartner Peer Insights a handful more, against hundreds for the incumbents, and the vendor publishes no customer count or spend under management. Guard: ask for three reference customers with a program the size of yours, in your countries, and ask each how long a rate-card or approval-chain change takes.
- **Analyst claims are vendor-reported.** The Everest placements come from SimplifyVMS releases, not from a report you can read. Guard: request the Everest provider profile under NDA before you weight the placement in your scoring.
- **Name confusion.** SimpleVMS (Avionté) has different pricing, reviews and coverage. Guard: check that every price signal, review and reference in your RFP file is for simplifyvms.com.
- **AI is assistive, and its pricing is unpublished.** There is no published agent roadmap, MCP server or AI price list. Guard: get any AI module fees in writing and run the match-score and screening features against a sample of past requisitions before you rely on them.
- **Freelancers go through a partner.** EOR and AOR coverage comes from People2.0, not SimplifyVMS itself. Guard: put the partner's service levels and data-handling terms into your contract, or contract an EOR such as [Deel](/en/tools/deel/) directly.
- **Vendor size.** A privately held, smaller vendor carries more continuity risk than SAP or Workday. Guard: put a full data-export right (worker, SOW and invoice history), a change-of-control clause and a renewal price cap in a multi-year contract.
