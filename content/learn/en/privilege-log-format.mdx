A privilege log explains why a party has withheld otherwise discoverable material. Someone who cannot see the document should be able to assess the claim without learning what counsel advised. In U.S. federal civil discovery, [Rule 26(b)(5)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf#page=63) requires the party to assert the privilege or trial-preparation protection expressly and describe what it withheld. The rule does not prescribe spreadsheet columns.

[Download the editable privilege log template (Excel)](/downloads/privilege-log-template.xlsx). The **Log** sheet contains two fictional draft entries; **Field guide** explains each column. Use the examples to test the format with counsel, then remove them before using the workbook for a matter.

## Choose the unit and timing before review

Under the [amendments effective December 1, 2025](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/frcv25_limp.pdf), Rule 26(f)(3)(D) calls for the discovery plan to address the parties’ views and proposals on how and when to comply. Rule 16(b)(3)(B)(iv) lets the court set that timing and method in the scheduling order. Check local rules, the judge’s orders and the discovery protocol before choosing a format.

- **Document by document:** use a stable item ID to match each entry to the review set. A fully withheld document need not have a production Bates number. For an email chain, establish how to treat earlier messages and attachments; a new recipient can change the analysis.
- **By category:** if the matter permits it, group items with the same basis and enough shared facts for the other party to assess the claim. For example, identify the date range, participants’ roles, purpose and number of items. If a group mixes legal requests with ordinary purchasing emails, split it or review the items individually.

## Work through two example entries

| Item | Facts to describe | Decision for the matter team |
|---|---|---|
| PL-001 · Sept. 14, 2026 | Email from Maya Ortiz, operations lead, to Devon Park, company counsel, requesting legal advice about a supplier notice obligation. | Proposed: withhold in full on attorney-client privilege grounds. Counsel still needs to verify the purpose, recipients and confidentiality. |
| PL-002 · Sept. 16, 2026 | Memorandum from Park to Ortiz concerning preparation for an anticipated supplier proceeding. | Hold for review. Confirm which proceeding was anticipated and why the memo was prepared. Leave the disposition blank until counsel resolves the possible work-product claim. |

“Privileged email” would tell the recipient of the log almost nothing about PL-001. The example identifies who asked whom for what kind of help; it leaves out both the advice and the company’s intended response. PL-002 shows why the author’s job title alone cannot decide protection. Neither row is approved language to serve.

## Prepare the copy to serve

1. On **Log**, enter the item ID, date, document type, author, recipients and their roles. Specify the disposition (withheld or redacted), the particular basis claimed and a neutral description that supports it. These are example fields; adjust them to the governing protocol.
2. Use the two internal columns for review status and reviewer. Record unresolved questions in the separate internal matter file, outside the log. Check recipients against the source, inspect attachments separately and reconcile entries with the items withheld and any [redactions](/en/learn/redaction-workflows/) the protocol requires you to log.
3. Have counsel resolve pending decisions. Build a clean export containing only the approved rows and columns. Remove the samples, reviewer names, internal status and field guide. Open the export itself; hiding rows or tabs in the working file does not remove their contents.

For the decisions that precede the log, see [privilege review](/en/learn/privilege-review/). The [batch review workflow](/en/workflows/privilege-review-batch-skill/) can draft entries for counsel to check against the underlying documents.
