## What it is

LinkedIn Ads is the self-serve advertising platform behind LinkedIn's feed, inbox and, since 2026, connected TV. You buy it in Campaign Manager: pick an objective (awareness, website visits, lead generation, conversions), target by the job title, seniority, function, company and industry fields members keep current on their own profiles, and bid in an auction. No other paid channel lets you say "VPs of Finance at these 800 companies" and have the targeting hold.

The parts that matter to a RevOps or ABM team:

- **Matched Audiences.** Upload a company list, a contact list, or retarget site visitors and video viewers. Company lists are the native ABM lever; LinkedIn's own guidance suggests 1,000+ companies for best results, and every ad set needs at least 300 matched members to serve.
- **Conversions API and CRM connections.** Server-side conversion events from HubSpot, Salesforce or Dynamics 365, plus a **Revenue Attribution Report** that joins ad engagement to CRM opportunities. A Company Attribution view added account-level rollups.
- **Thought Leader Ads.** Since 2024 you can promote posts from employees and, with permission, customers, partners or creators. Agency-reported benchmarks put their CPC well under brand-page ads; treat the exact ratio as vendor data.
- **AI in Campaign Manager.** The Accelerate campaign type automates targeting and creative; on 2026-07-01 LinkedIn added Draft with AI (a first-draft ad from a landing-page URL), Brand Kit, AI ad variants and Ads Personalization by title, company and industry.
- **CTV and video.** LinkedIn CTV Ads became buyable through Amazon DSP in May 2026, with The Trade Desk rolling out as a second programmatic route; BrandLink (formerly the Wire Program) runs your video before publisher and creator content.
- **BrandWorks.** Announced 2026-06-10, a LinkedIn in-house team for brand strategy, creative and events, available only to select managed customers (SAP and Webflow named as early users). It is a services layer, not a self-serve feature.

Lookalike Audiences were retired on 2024-02-29; Predictive Audiences and Audience Expansion replaced them.

## Pricing reality

There is no platform fee. You pay for clicks (CPC), impressions (CPM) or sends (Message Ads), and the platform floors are a $10 daily budget, a $100 lifetime budget on new campaigns, and a $2 minimum bid.

The floor tells you nothing about the real cost. Agency benchmarks published in 2026 disagree, but they cluster here:

- **CPC:** roughly $5-15 for B2B audiences filtered by seniority; senior-buyer and narrow-ABM audiences sit at the top of that band.
- **CPM:** roughly $27-35 on broad professional targeting, $50-65+ when you target executives.
- **Workable test budget:** agency guidance converges on $2,000-5,000 a month for a first B2B test, with two weeks minimum before judging. LinkedIn publishes no official figure; below that range, a campaign rarely collects enough clicks to judge the creative.

That makes LinkedIn the most expensive click in most B2B paid mixes, often several times a Google search click. The case for it is audience precision, not cost efficiency.

## Best for

**Demand-gen and ABM marketers at B2B companies with deal sizes big enough to absorb $50+ CPMs**, who need to reach a defined buying committee by title and account, and who want the channel the rest of the ABM stack (6sense, Demandbase, AdRoll ABM) pushes audiences into anyway.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[6sense](/en/tools/6sense/) and [Demandbase](/en/tools/demandbase/)**, the two ABM platforms with the largest installed base, both sync intent-scored account audiences into LinkedIn and report account-level engagement back to the CRM. **Pick one instead of running LinkedIn natively** when you need intent data to decide *which* accounts to target this week, when you also buy programmatic display, or when the board wants account-level influence reporting across channels. Contracts typically start around $40,000-60,000 a year before media.
- **[Vector](/en/tools/vector/)**, the fastest-moving entrant here, sits on top of LinkedIn Ads itself: contact-level retargeting of identified site visitors, bid agents that adjust LinkedIn bids every 15 minutes, and an MCP server for querying performance. **Pick it** when LinkedIn is already your main paid channel and the gap is identification and bidding, not intent.
- **[AdRoll ABM](/en/tools/rollworks/)** and **[Influ2](/en/tools/influ2/)** are the cheaper ABM-advertising paths: AdRoll ABM for SMB budgets, Influ2 for person-level ads to named contacts.
- **Run LinkedIn natively** when your target account list is fixed for the quarter, your budget is under roughly $20,000 a month, and the Revenue Attribution Report plus a CRM connection answers your reporting question.

## Watch-outs

- **Audience Expansion and the LinkedIn Audience Network are often switched on at campaign creation.** Both quietly widen a tight ABM audience to lookalike members and off-LinkedIn placements. **Guard:** switch both off on every new ABM campaign (a duplicated or newly created campaign can bring the defaults back), and check placement reports in week one.
- **Small ABM lists fail the 300-member floor.** A 100-person buying-committee list rarely matches enough members to serve; agencies report contact-list match rates of 30-60%. **Guard:** upload at least 3x the members you need, prefer company lists with name plus Company Page URL, and layer job-function targeting on top of the company list.
- **Replacing a CSV list pauses the campaign.** Per LinkedIn's Marketing API docs, a campaign on a CSV audience pauses for 24-48 hours while the replacement is evaluated. **Guard:** feed lists through the HubSpot or Salesforce connector, or through your ABM platform's sync, so updates refresh in place.
- **Native attribution is lead-centric and generous.** The Revenue Attribution Report credits engagement inside LinkedIn's own windows, and view-through conversions flatter awareness spend. **Guard:** reconcile against an account-level attribution tool such as [Dreamdata](/en/tools/dreamdata/), [HockeyStack](/en/tools/hockeystack/) or [Factors.ai](/en/tools/factors-ai/) before you defend the budget.
- **New AI features lag in the API.** The July 2026 creative tools did not appear in the Marketing API changelog at launch. **Guard:** if a third-party tool manages your campaigns, confirm it supports a feature before you plan around it.

## The pick

If you sell to a definable buying committee and can afford the clicks, LinkedIn Ads belongs in the plan; the real question is whether you drive it yourself or through an ABM platform. Start native with company lists, Audience Expansion off and the CRM connection live. Add 6sense or Demandbase once you need intent to choose the accounts, or Vector once LinkedIn spend is large enough that bidding and visitor identification are the bottleneck. Pair it with [Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/) so reps follow up with the same accounts the ads are warming.
