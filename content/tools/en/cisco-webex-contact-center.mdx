## What it is

Webex Contact Center is Cisco's cloud contact center (CCaaS): voice, chat, email, SMS and social routing, Flow Designer for call flows, Analyzer for reporting, and supervisor and agent desktops. It sits beside Cisco's on-premises lines, UCCE (its on-premises Contact Center Enterprise), Packaged CCE and Contact Center Express, and the hosted Webex Contact Center Enterprise. That is the real buyer base: organizations already running Cisco calling or a Cisco call center, who want to move to cloud without leaving the vendor.

The AI layer is **Webex AI Agent**, built in AI Agent Studio in two modes: scripted (intent-and-flow bots) and autonomous (LLM agents that take actions). At Cisco Live in June 2026, Cisco made Webex AI Agent generally available for on-premises UCCE and PCCE estates, and Cisco's release notes add MCP fulfillment actions for autonomous agents in October 2026, so the agent can call third-party tools during a live conversation. Cisco is still a large vendor shipping steadily; nothing here is at risk of being retired. What has been retired is admin plumbing: the old tenant management portal (moved into Control Hub by mid-2026) and the legacy Flex contact center SKUs (last order date 1 August 2026).

## Why it shows up in support stacks

- **It stays inside the Cisco estate.** One vendor for calling, meetings, devices and the contact center, with Control Hub as a single admin console. For a 500-seat center running UCCE, a move to Webex Contact Center is the lowest-friction cloud path.
- **The hosted MCP server is real and documented.** The Webex Contact Center MCP Server on developer.webex.com exposes admin configuration (entry points, business hours, dial plans), real-time telemetry and a read-only Search API query tool to outside AI agents. An org admin enables it in Control Hub under Agentic Apps. At WebexOne 2026 (October, Austin) Cisco framed this as opening the platform to outside agents rather than forcing everything through Webex AI Agent.
- **Native ServiceNow and Salesforce.** Webex Contact Center for ServiceNow was in beta in March 2026 with GA targeted for Q3 2026, alongside existing Salesforce and Microsoft Teams integrations.

Much of the AI story is still roadmap. As of this check: **AI Concierge** (a pre-built front-door agent with memory and multi-agent orchestration) is targeted for GA in Q4 2026; Splunk-based agent observability is due in December 2026; **Dialog**, the agentic harness that tracks one customer across channels until an issue is resolved, is due in beta in Q1 2027; **AI Quality Management**, which scores 100% of human and AI interactions, is targeted for Q3 2027, after an earlier Q1 2026 target; and AI WEM forecasting and scheduling enter controlled availability in Q3 2027. Cisco has published no AI Agent containment figure. Its one public customer number at WebexOne, GetixHealth's 86% cut in wrap-up time, is a Cisco-supplied figure and measures after-call work, not automation.

## Pricing reality

Cisco publishes no rate card; seats are sold through partners on the Collaboration Flex Plan. The firmest public number is Cisco's 2024 UK G-Cloud filing: £94.44 Standard and £139.61 Premium per concurrent agent per month. Third-party trackers put US named-agent seats at roughly $70-110 for Standard and $110-180 for Premium, with concurrent licences priced higher. Webex AI Agent is metered on usage, not bundled into the seat; Cisco has not published that rate, and third-party estimates of $0.06-0.12 per AI-handled minute are unverified. Budget separately for WFM and QM, carrier minutes, and partner professional services for Flow Designer migration.

## Best for

Heads of contact-center operations at Cisco-standardized organizations (roughly 100-2,000 seats, often regulated or global) that run UCCE, PCCE or Webex Calling today, want one vendor and one admin console, and can wait 12-18 months for the AI roadmap to land.

Skip it if you are not already a Cisco shop, need proven AI containment this year, or want WFM and QM from the platform vendor now rather than in 2027.

## Versus the alternatives

**[NICE CXone](/en/tools/nice-cxone/)** and **[Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/)** are the two largest enterprise CCaaS platforms and both were Leaders in Gartner's 2025 CCaaS Magic Quadrant, where Cisco slipped from Challenger to Niche Player. Pick NICE when WFM and QM drive the decision; pick Genesys when you want one orchestration layer over several AI agents. **[Talkdesk](/en/tools/talkdesk/)** is the fastest climber (Visionary to Leader in that report); pick it for faster mid-market rollout. **[Five9](/en/tools/five9/)** wins on outbound, **[Amazon Connect](/en/tools/amazon-connect/)** for AWS-native teams, and **[Zoom Contact Center](/en/tools/zoom-contact-center/)** is the like-for-like choice if you are standardized on Zoom instead of Webex.

The rule for a Cisco shop: keep Webex Contact Center as the routing and telephony layer if you are a Cisco shop, but do not wait for Dialog or AI Concierge to automate your top call reasons. Add a specialist AI agent, such as [Parloa](/en/tools/parloa/) or [PolyAI](/en/tools/polyai/) for voice or [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) or [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) for chat and email, and hand off to Webex queues. Re-test native Webex AI Agent when AI QM ships and you can measure both side by side. The [voice support agent stack](/en/stacks/voice-support-agent-stack/) shows the handoff pattern.

## Watch-outs

- **Roadmap dates slip.** AI QM moved from Q1 2026 to Q3 2027. Guard: price and sign only what is GA on the day you sign; put roadmap items in the contract as named deliverables with a credit or exit if they miss by two quarters.
- **AI usage is metered and unpublished.** Guard: get the AI Agent per-minute or per-session rate in writing, with a monthly cap and alert, before any pilot leaves the lab.
- **The MCP server acts as a signed-in user.** Tools such as `wxcc-admin-config` can change entry points and dial plans. Guard: connect outside agents with a dedicated, least-privilege service user, keep admin-config tools out of production agents, and log every MCP call.
- **Partner-dependent delivery.** Most deployments run through a Cisco partner, and Flow Designer flows do not port from UCCE scripts automatically. Guard: require a fixed-scope migration statement of work with a flow inventory and a parallel-run period before cutover.
