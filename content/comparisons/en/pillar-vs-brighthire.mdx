For two years this comparison turned on one question: which ATS do you run? [Pillar](/en/tools/pillar/) was the bundled option for Employ shops and unavailable to everyone else; [BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/) was the independent layer you bought on top of whatever you already had. Both halves of that framing are now wrong. Employ finished absorbing Pillar into a rebranded **AI Interview Companion** and sells it as a modular add-on to *your* ATS — its own buying guide puts "supercharge your ATS with AI Companions" as step one and picking an Employ ATS as an optional step two. Zoom closed its acquisition of BrightHire in December 2025, so the independent challenger is now a business unit of a conferencing company. Neither is a standalone vendor and both will sell to you regardless of your ATS. What separates them is what each product is allowed to do with a candidate: BrightHire scores, screens, and judges; the AI Interview Companion states in writing that it does not.

## Where BrightHire wins

- **It reaches a verdict, and shows the receipts.** Five modules ship under the BrightHire brand inside Zoom — Interview Planning, BrightHire Screen, AI Interview Notes, Interview Insights, and Decision Intelligence. When a hiring committee splits, BrightHire pulls the timestamped moment where the contested signal appeared, which is the same artifact you want when a rejected candidate challenges the call. The Companion produces a summary; BrightHire produces evidence attached to a decision.
- **Interview fraud detection, if you are on Zoom.** Since 25 June 2026 BrightHire has run candidate-fraud detection in early access for Zoom Workplace customers, layering deepfake rendering artifacts, IP location, answer timing, tab switching, and LLM answer matching. Nothing in the Companion touches this problem. If remote engineering hiring is where your fraud losses actually land, this capability decides the comparison by itself.
- **Coaching aimed at a named interviewer.** Interview Insights grades who skipped a required question, who talked over the candidate, and who led the witness — per person, per loop, with the recording attached. Employ also coaches, but from summaries; BrightHire's analytics operate on interviewer behavior at the pattern level.
- **The screen and the loop share one rubric.** BrightHire Screen runs an asynchronous first round against a recruiter-defined rubric and hands the same scale forward into the human panel. Employ sells screening as a separate AI Screening Companion, so the two scores are not born on one scale.

## Where Pillar's AI Interview Companion wins

- **A written ceiling on what the AI decides.** Employ states on the product page that the Companion "assists with transcription and summarization" and "does not provide candidate scoring or hiring recommendations." That sentence is a procurement asset. A tool that ranks or scores candidates is an automated employment decision tool under NYC Local Law 144 and lands squarely inside Illinois HB 3773, in force since January 2026 — bias audits, notice obligations, disclosure. A tool that only transcribes and summarizes, with the human scoring, sits outside that definition. If your counsel has been the blocker on interview AI, this is the shorter path.
- **Intervention during the interview, not after it.** The Companion serves skills-based questions live and collects feedback in the moment rather than chasing a scorecard the next day. Employ reports over 90% scorecard completion, 12 minutes saved per interview, and a 32% drop in first-year attrition. For a hiring-manager population that interviews quarterly rather than daily, real-time prompting beats a post-hoc report they will not open.
- **Prep and remediation in one place.** Paste a job description and the Companion generates the interview guide in under five minutes; a Training Center hosts the coaching content, closing a gap this product had a year ago.
- **Cheapest incremental line on Lever, Jobvite, or JazzHR.** On an Employ ATS the Companion is native rather than integrated — no second login, no separate security review. Still a real advantage; no longer a precondition.

## Pricing reality

Neither publishes a number, and both route to a demo. BrightHire's pricing page names three packages — Recruiters, Teams, Enterprises — plus BrightHire Screen sold standalone or bundled. The quote moves on platform seats and recorded interview volume. [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/brighthire) publishes anonymized data from 57 transactions, last updated February 2026: a **median of $18,000 a year**, with $15,000–$35,000 at 5–15 users and under 100 interviews a month, $35,000–$75,000 at 15–50 users and 100–300 interviews, and $75,000–$150,000 and above past 50 users and 300 interviews.

Employ publishes no line-item price for the Companion, bundled or standalone, so a like-for-like ratio cannot be stated honestly. The structural read holds anyway: BrightHire is priced as a platform with an analytics premium, the Companion as a module attached to an ATS conversation — bundled on Employ, negotiated as an add-on elsewhere. Budget it against the low end of BrightHire's smallest band and expect to argue about interview volume in both negotiations. Zoom committed in writing to "no immediate changes to pricing or existing contracts"; that is a statement dated December 2025, not a renewal term.

## Implementation effort

BrightHire is a 30–60 day standalone deploy: ATS wiring across Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday, Lever, Rippling, BambooHR, or Gem, recording-consent setup per jurisdiction, and template configuration. Zoom's written FAQ says BrightHire "remains platform agnostic" and that no Zoom license is required, with Microsoft Teams and Google Meet still supported — but the fraud-detection module is Zoom Workplace only, so the frontier of the product is gated even though the base is not. The Companion is faster on an Employ ATS because there is no platform to stand up; off Employ it is a normal integration project against your existing ATS, video, and calendar tools, and Employ's rollout sequence went Lever first.

## Verdict

- **Pick BrightHire** when interview integrity and decision defensibility are the purchase: senior or high-judgment hiring where a bad call outruns the contract, remote roles exposed to candidate fraud, or an interviewer panel that needs named, behavioral coaching. On Zoom Workplace it is the only one of the two that addresses deepfakes at all.
- **Pick Pillar's AI Interview Companion** when the blocker is governance rather than analytics — you need interview AI that provably does not score candidates — or when you are on Lever, Jobvite, or JazzHR and the failure mode is hiring managers who never submit feedback. It is the lower-friction, lower-cost line in both cases.
- **Pick neither** if your bottleneck is interview *scheduling*, where a scheduling tool serves better, or if you hire under 50 roles a year — at that volume a disciplined interview template and a shared rubric in your ATS beat both on cost.

If you cannot decide, default to **BrightHire**: it is the deeper product on coaching, evidence, and fraud, and it now carries an acquirer with the resources to keep shipping. Overrule that default in exactly one case — your legal team has drawn a line at AI that scores candidates, in which case the Companion's written non-scoring boundary is worth more than every analytic BrightHire sells. For the documentation-first third option, see [BrightHire vs Metaview](/en/vs/brighthire-vs-metaview/).
