## What it is

Nuix is an Australian, ASX-listed (NXL) platform for processing and reviewing large, messy unstructured data: email stores, forensic images, encrypted and legacy files, chat exports. It sells two things that matter to a legal ops buyer. **Nuix Neo** is the processing and investigations platform, built on the engine that made Nuix Workstation a default for forensic and service-provider processing teams. **Nuix Discover** is the [eDiscovery](/en/learn/ediscovery/) review application, sold as SaaS or on-premises. It competes with [Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/) and [Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/) for review, and with [Exterro](/en/tools/exterro/) FTK for forensics.

The company is healthier than it was. FY26 (year to 30 June 2026, reported 24 August) showed ACV of A$260.0M, up 13.9%, revenue of A$263.2M, and a statutory profit of A$16.4M against a A$9.2M loss the year before. Neo ACV rose 179% to A$78.5M across 135 customers. Nuix closed its up-to-€20M purchase of Linkurious, a Paris graph-analytics company, on 20 April 2026. John Ruthven became permanent CEO on 4 May 2026 after six months as interim. On 23 April 2026 the Federal Court dismissed ASIC's disclosure case against Nuix and its directors; ASIC has appealed against the company only, and a shareholder class action began an eight-week hearing on 3 August 2026.

The question this page answers: **is Nuix Discover a real alternative to RelativityOne or Everlaw, and is its AI usable?** For processing-heavy investigations and on-premises review, yes. As a US litigation review platform picked on AI, not yet.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Processing is the moat.** Nuix reads 1,000+ file types, including corrupted, encrypted and legacy formats, at up to 1.5 TB an hour on its own figures, with deterministic output: the same inputs give the same results. Many service providers run Nuix processing and review in Relativity; Nuix is a Relativity ISV partner. If your problem is getting a forensic image or a 20-year-old mail archive into reviewable shape, Nuix is often already in the chain.
- **You choose where it runs.** Discover ships as SaaS and on-premises, and Neo is built for local deployment. Government agencies, regulators and banks that cannot send case data to a US cloud pick Nuix for that reason, and Nuix called out strong government-sector performance in FY26.
- **The AI now covers review.** On 4 September 2026 Nuix made Document Summaries (generated at ingestion), Semantic Search, Similar Documents, and AI Clustering and Visualizations generally available in Discover SaaS. On-premises follows next quarter. AI Chat, which answers plain-language questions about a matter with document citations and an audit trail, is in an early adopter program with GA set for December 2026. Nuix's BYO AI lets you pick the model, including a local LLM.

## Pricing reality

Nuix publishes no official price list (checked 2026-09-14). The public reference points:

- **Discover SaaS, single matter:** $10 per GB per month with a $250 monthly minimum, per third-party listings.
- **Discover private cloud:** no more than $4 per GB per month, falling toward $1 per GB at volume, per the same listings.
- **UK public sector:** Nuix Discover SaaS is listed on G-Cloud 14 at £4.64 per GB per month.
- **Nuix Neo:** custom enterprise licensing, usually multi-year. Multi-year deals were 35% of FY26 revenue.

The per-matter math: a 200 GB matter hosted for 12 months costs about $24,000 at the single-matter rate and about £11,000 at the G-Cloud rate. Processing speed matters as much as the hosting rate, because every day saved on processing comes off the matter timeline.

## Best for

Investigations and eDiscovery leads at government agencies, regulators, financial institutions and service providers who process terabytes of mixed-format data and need it on-premises or in-country. Also for corporate legal teams that already run Nuix processing and want review on the same stack.

**Not for you** if you are a US litigation team choosing a review platform on AI maturity, if your matters depend on the large pool of Relativity-certified reviewers and service providers, or if you run a handful of small matters a year. Pick a self-service tool for those.

## Versus the alternatives

- **RelativityOne:** the market-share leader. Since early 2026, aiR for Review, aiR for Privilege and aiR for Case Strategy come inside standard RelativityOne per-GB pricing. Pick it for large US litigation and when outside counsel and service providers already work in Relativity.
- **Everlaw:** the cloud-native growth challenger. Single-document AI Assistant actions have carried no extra charge since October 2025; batch AI actions still use credits. Pick it when review speed and usability decide the deal.
- **DISCO:** since February 2026, one per-GB price on processed data with no ingest or user-license fees and unlimited Cecilia AI. Pick it when price predictability matters most.
- **Exterro FTK:** pick it when forensics sits inside a wider legal hold and privacy program.

The direct answer: shortlist Nuix when processing volume or data sovereignty is the constraint, and run a live matter through both Nuix Discover and RelativityOne before you sign.

## Watch-outs

- **Discover is the weaker half of the business.** Discover is roughly an A$50M business, and SaaS revenue fell in FY26 after Nuix lost a large SaaS contract. Nuix has since set up a separate Discover team under its own EVP. *Guard:* ask for the Discover SaaS roadmap in writing, and cap the first term at one year with a renewal price ceiling.
- **The new AI is weeks old.** Summaries and semantic search went GA on 4 September 2026; AI Chat is not GA until December. *Guard:* test the AI features on a closed matter where you already know the key documents, and write a defensibility check into the pilot.
- **Steep learning curve.** G2 and Capterra reviewers of Discover and Workstation say the advanced tools take time to learn. *Guard:* budget vendor training for the first two matters and name one internal power user.
- **Legal overhang.** ASIC's appeal and the shareholder class action are still open. *Guard:* include data-return and escrow terms in the contract, and check the next ASX update before signing a multi-year deal.
