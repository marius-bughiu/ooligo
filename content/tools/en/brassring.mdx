## What it is

BrassRing is an enterprise applicant tracking system built for large, regulated, high-volume employers. It has three products: the BrassRing ATS, BrassRing Onboard, and BrassRing Lead Manager, its candidate CRM. Around them sit the Rules Automation Manager, which runs workflow rules around the clock, the eLink dashboard for hiring managers, Interview Manager with Microsoft Teams and Outlook calendar integration, and the Data Insight reporting suite. The vendor says the platform runs on Microsoft Azure, holds 350 million candidate records, works in 42 languages, and is used in 172 countries.

The ownership history is the first thing a buyer should know. Kenexa bought BrassRing in 2006 for about $115 million, and IBM bought Kenexa in 2012. In October 2021 Infinite Computer Solutions completed its purchase of IBM's talent acquisition suite: BrassRing, Onboard and Lead Manager. On July 16, 2026 Signal Labs, a Chicago startup backed by Lightspeed Venture Partners and led by founder and CEO Rajeev Ronanki, announced that it had acquired BrassRing, with no terms disclosed. That is the fourth owner in twenty years.

What BrassRing is not: an AI-native ATS. The homepage claims AI matching and scoring, with vendor-reported figures of 45% shorter time to hire and 50% less time on resume review. Signal Labs' own pitch is SignalOS, a layer it calls a "system of attention" that would read 25 years of hiring and post-hire data to flag stalling requisitions and predict which candidates will accept and stay. Signal Labs' customer FAQ calls SignalOS an optional enhancement, not a forced migration, and lists it as roadmap, so judge BrassRing on what ships today.

## Why it still shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Installed base in the Fortune 500.** BrassRing is the ATS behind a long tail of large employers that standardized on it during the IBM years. Dollar Tree, for example, has run it for about a decade. Many of these teams have tuned rules, requisition templates and integrations they do not want to rebuild.
- **Rules-heavy, high-volume processing.** The Rules Automation Manager and requisition templates handle the multi-step, multi-country flows that regulated employers need, and the vendor claims more than 90% of hiring managers use eLink without extensive training.
- **Continuity commitments after the sale.** Signal Labs' FAQ says existing contracts stay in force without re-papering, current pricing and terms continue at renewal, the support team stays the same, and no migration or extra downtime is planned. BrassRing also stays open to new customers.

## Pricing

Checked 2026-11-15. BrassRing publishes no prices; the homepage offers a demo form with a pricing option. Every figure below is a third-party estimate.

- **Typical subscription.** Third-party pricing guides put annual contracts at roughly $20,000 to $60,000 for a standard deployment, and above $150,000 a year for large global implementations.
- **Implementation.** Estimates run $5,000 to $30,000 for process configuration plus $5,000 to $25,000 for moving candidate records, and above $50,000 for large enterprises. G2's pricing page lists an average implementation time of 7 months.
- **How it compares.** The HCM suites bill per employee whether recruiters use the module or not: [Oracle Recruiting](/en/tools/oracle-recruiting/) lists at $8.00 per employee per month with a 500-employee minimum, which is $48,000 a year at that floor before the required HCM Base license. A standalone ATS such as BrassRing is priced on hiring volume and users, so at high headcount and modest hiring it can cost less than a suite module.
- **Use the renewal.** The ownership change is the moment to negotiate. Get Signal Labs' "pricing and terms continue unchanged" promise written into the renewal, along with a price-increase cap.

## Best for

- TA leaders at large employers already on BrassRing, with tuned rules and integrations, who want to stay put through a renewal cycle while they watch what Signal Labs ships
- High-volume, multi-country hiring programs (retail, healthcare, financial services) that need a standalone ATS separate from their HR system of record
- Not for a new enterprise buyer whose HR system is Workday, SAP or Oracle: the suite module removes an integration and carries the vendor's AI investment. Not for anyone who needs AI screening or agents today rather than on a roadmap.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Workday Recruiting](/en/tools/workday/)** — the Fortune 500 share leader; 2026 listing studies put it at about 38% of the Fortune 500. Pick Workday when Workday HCM is already your system of record and you want HiredScore matching and Paradox scheduling in the same contract.
- **[iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/)** — the largest standalone enterprise ATS. Pick iCIMS when you are leaving BrassRing but want to stay independent of your HCM vendor, with a deeper partner marketplace and CRM.
- **[SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting](/en/tools/successfactors-recruiting/)** and **[Oracle Recruiting](/en/tools/oracle-recruiting/)** — pick the one that matches your HCM. Note that SAP has put its legacy module into a "Core Stability Phase" in favor of [SmartRecruiters](/en/tools/smartrecruiters/).
- **[Avature](/en/tools/avature/)** — pick Avature when you need BrassRing-level configurability plus a stronger CRM and contingent-workforce handling in one platform.
- **[Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/)** — the newest challenger in this list, with an Enterprise tier for 1,000+ employees. Pick Ashby when a business unit or subsidiary under a few thousand employees wants modern analytics and scheduling now, rather than an enterprise-wide re-platform.

## Watch-outs

- **Four owners in twenty years.** Each sale has reset roadmap priorities, and BrassRing's own pages still carry Infinite Computer Solutions branding in places. Guard: before renewing, ask Signal Labs for the dated 12-month roadmap for the ATS and Lead Manager in writing, and keep the renewal to 1 or 2 years until releases match it.
- **The AI is mostly a promise.** SignalOS predictions and faster talent matching are described as roadmap, and the outcome figures on the homepage are vendor-reported. Guard: run a pilot on one requisition family and measure time to hire against your own baseline before you pay for any SignalOS add-on.
- **Weak user ratings.** G2 rates BrassRing 3.5 out of 5 across 74 reviews, with slow performance and limited customization the recurring complaints; TrustRadius has it at 7.2 out of 10. Guard: include recruiter and hiring-manager usability tests in any renewal review, not only an admin demo.
- **Data access under the new owner.** Signal Labs says it will not access customer data until agreements are in place, and that FedRAMP is on the roadmap rather than certified. Guard: get the data-processing terms and any SignalOS data use signed off by your privacy team before enabling new features, and do not plan a federal program around FedRAMP that has not shipped.
- **Migration takes a year if you leave late.** Implementation runs about 7 months by G2's count, and assessment vendors such as [Talview](/en/tools/talview/) and onboarding links have to be re-integrated. Guard: start any move 12 months before contract end, and negotiate a short extension clause now in case it slips.
