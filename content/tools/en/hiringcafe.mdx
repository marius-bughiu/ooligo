## What it is

HiringCafe is a free job search engine for candidates. Employers do not post to it; it crawls company career pages directly and indexes the jobs it finds there. The vendor claims more than 5.7 million active listings. An AI layer reads each job description and pulls out structured fields: years of experience, requirements, tools mentioned, salary where the posting states one, work mode, and a one-line summary of the company. Candidates filter on those fields, and when they click Apply they leave HiringCafe for the employer's own application page. HiringCafe never takes the application itself.

The company was founded in 2022 by Ali Mir and Hamed Nilforoshan. It started as Mir's spreadsheet of startup jobs. On 28 September 2026 it announced a $6.8M pre-seed round led by Spark Capital, with Nonfiction Capital, Silicon Gardens and angels from ZipRecruiter and Indeed. On the same day it launched the free AI Talent Agent: a candidate uploads a resume, and an agent keeps searching the index for matching roles and flags new ones. The company claims more than 2 million monthly active users, grown without paid marketing. Its homepage states the aim as building a better job search engine than Indeed and LinkedIn. Trade coverage put the team at about six people when it raised.

What it is not: an ATS, a sourcing database you can search, or a job board where you buy slots. It most resembles Google for Jobs: an index built on top of your career site, not a channel you manage.

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks

TA teams usually notice HiringCafe from the outside. Applicants say they found the role there, or the brand shows up in the free-text source field. Nobody bought it, and it is already sending you applicants, because your career page is its source.

That shifts the work from buying to hygiene. HiringCafe shows what your ATS publishes, run through an AI parser. On 2 November 2026 we checked one employer's profile at `hiringcafe.com/org/<domain>`. It listed the same role several times, once per country req, next to an AI-written company summary, headcount and funding stage that the employer never supplied. Candidates can also open "See how many viewed or applied" on a listing. The page rewards clean postings: one req per role with a multi-location field, a stated pay range, and closed reqs taken down from the career site the day they close.

## Pricing reality

HiringCafe's employer form lists four services. "Job Postings" and "Manage listings" are marked "Always free, forever". "Job Advertisement" and "Talent Network" (contact with job seekers who opt in to share their profiles, set up on a Zoom call) have no published price. No independent price data existed as of 2 November 2026. Plan on $0. Treat paid advertising as an unpriced experiment you can run once you can measure what it delivers.

Compare that with what the incumbents charge. On [Indeed](/en/tools/indeed/), third-party 2026 guides put sponsored applications at about $15-50 each. On [ZipRecruiter](/en/tools/ziprecruiter/), one always-on Standard slot costs about $3,600-4,800 a year.

## Best for

TA operations and recruiting ops leads at companies that publish jobs on a public, crawlable career site, whichever ATS hosts it, and hire for salaried professional, technical or remote roles, the inventory its audience searches. The scoped use case: make sure your free listings on HiringCafe are accurate and attributed, and measure whether they produce hires before you spend on job-board slots.

Do not expect it to fill a req on demand. You cannot boost a posting at a known price, target a market, or search candidates. If you need guaranteed volume for hourly or frontline roles, or a req has to fill this month, pay for Indeed sponsorship.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Indeed](/en/tools/indeed/)** is the largest job board by traffic and the default paid channel. Pick it when you need controllable volume per req and can pay per application. **[LinkedIn](/en/tools/linkedin-recruiter/)** wins when you also need outbound to passive candidates. HiringCafe only reaches active seekers. **[ZipRecruiter](/en/tools/ziprecruiter/)** suits SMB employers who want distribution and matching sold as a monthly slot.

Rule: keep HiringCafe as a free layer on top of whichever paid channel you run, not instead of it. Replace paid spend only if a quarter of source data shows HiringCafe hires at a level that justifies it. For startup roles, [Wellfound](/en/tools/wellfound/) still adds candidate profiles HiringCafe does not have. For early-career hiring, [Handshake](/en/tools/handshake/) still owns the campus channel.

## Watch-outs

- **Applicants arrive as "career site" in your ATS.** The Apply link goes to your own apply page, so HiringCafe hires get counted as direct traffic. *Guard:* add HiringCafe to the "How did you hear about us?" list in Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby this week, and review the counts every quarter before any spend decision.
- **Stale and duplicate reqs on your career site get indexed with them.** HiringCafe says it filters ghost and stale listings, but your career site is the source. *Guard:* close reqs in the ATS the day they fill, merge per-country clones into one multi-location req, and check your `/org/<domain>` page every month.
- **The AI parser can misstate your posting.** Years of experience, salary and company facts are extracted, not supplied by you. *Guard:* put the pay range, minimum experience and work mode as plain text near the top of every job description, and report any wrong field through the employer form.
- **Coverage depends on crawling.** A career site behind bot protection, or one rendered only on the client side, may drop out of the index without telling you. *Guard:* search your own company on HiringCafe after any career-site redesign or ATS migration. If roles are missing, submit the career page URL through the "Add Career Page" form.
