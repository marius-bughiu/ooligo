## What it is

Avvoka is document automation for law firms and in-house teams that draft the same agreement types over and over. You turn a precedent into a template with placeholders, conditional clauses, loops and formulas, either in Avvoka or through its Word add-in. A lawyer answers a questionnaire and gets a Word document, or a whole bundle of related documents, in the firm's house style. Negotiation, redlining and e-signature then run on the same platform. Former Magic Circle lawyers Eliot Benzecrit and David Howorth founded the London company in 2016. It ran on £600,000 of angel money until March 2026, when it raised £14 million led by Valhalla Ventures to expand in the US. Avvoka says 25% of the Am Law 100 draft on it. Named users include A&O Shearman, Fenwick, Fried Frank, Ropes & Gray and the in-house team at Warner Bros. Discovery.

Its closest category peers are HotDocs and Thomson Reuters Contract Express: rules-based assembly engines, not chat-based drafting assistants.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It is the deterministic layer under the AI assistants.** [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) announced a partnership on August 20, 2026 that lets lawyers draft from the firm's Avvoka templates and clause library without leaving Harvey. [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) followed on September 8 with an integration due to reach beta customers by Q4 2026. When a request matches an Avvoka template, Legora runs the questionnaire; when nothing matches, Legora's own AI writes the draft. The model handles the open-ended work, and the template handles the documents where the wording is not up for debate.
- **It shipped an MCP connector before most legal vendors.** Since September 3, 2026, a connector in the official [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) directory lets Claude search your template library, draft from a template with the details filled in, pull clause variants, check negotiation status and export to Word or PDF. It needs a Claude Team or Enterprise plan. Admins switch it on and choose read-only or read-write, and signed or locked documents cannot be changed through it.
- **Curate goes after the step that kills automation projects: building the template.** Curate is a standalone product. It reads a firm's past deal documents, surfaces the variants of each clause, and lets a lawyer pick the preferred wording and fallbacks. The output is a bracketed, footnoted Word template. It has come out of private beta, a wider waitlist opened in September 2026, and there is no general-availability date yet.

## Pricing reality

Nothing is published. Directory listings describe quote-only, per-feature pricing with a free trial, and we found no credible third-party figure for what firms actually pay. Don't budget from blog guesses; build your own ceiling. Avvoka's headline claim is a full drafting day cut to 90 minutes, about 6.5 hours saved per document. Take one document type you draft at least 200 times a year, halve the vendor's saving to be safe, and price the hours at your internal cost. At 200 documents, 3.25 hours each and $150 an hour, that is about $97,500 a year, and that is the most the first use case is worth. The platform is sold as modules (Curate, template building, drafting, the Execute negotiation and signing layer, MASSDRAFT for high volume). Ask for the quote itemized by module and by integration so you pay only for what the first use case needs.

## Best for

Knowledge-management and [legal operations](/en/learn/what-is-legal-ops/) leads at law firms and in-house teams that produce high volumes of structured documents: fund documents, facility agreements, NDAs, employment packs, financing bundles where one set of answers produces a dozen related documents. It fits best when the firm already runs Harvey or Legora and wants their drafts to stay on approved wording.

Do not buy it for a small firm that needs intake answers turned into letters and pleadings. Clio Draft (part of [Clio](/en/tools/clio/)) or Gavel cost less and are self-serve. Do not buy it either if your real problem is approval routing, a contract repository and renewals on your own sales paper. Your [CLM](/en/learn/contract-lifecycle-management/)'s templating in [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) or [Juro](/en/tools/juro/) covers that, and Avvoka would put a second system in the path of every contract.

## Versus the alternatives

**HotDocs**, now owned by [Mitratech](/en/tools/mitratech/), is the longest-running legal assembly engine. Pick it when you already run Mitratech or have years of HotDocs templates, because rebuilding them is the real cost of switching. Pick Avvoka when templates are being built from scratch and lawyers, not developers, have to maintain them.

**Contract Express** from Thomson Reuters ships Practical Law templates pre-automated with a subscription and connects to [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/), NetDocuments, DocuSign and HighQ. Pick it when you are a Practical Law shop and want market-standard forms automated on day one. Pick Avvoka when the value is in your own precedents.

**Harvey and Legora** are the fast-growing entrants. Legora passed $100 million ARR in 2026 and Harvey reported about $190 million, and both draft from precedent directly. Use either alone when drafts are bespoke and low-volume. Add Avvoka underneath when the same document type goes out hundreds of times and a partner has to approve every variant of every clause.

If none fit, the constraint is usually clean precedents, not software. Run a [clause library](/en/learn/clause-library-design/) exercise before buying any engine.

## Watch-outs

- **Three of the four 2026 headline moves are not generally available.** The Legora integration is beta by Q4 2026, Curate is on a waitlist, and the Harvey partnership was announced without a GA date. Only the Claude connector is live today. *Guard:* buy on what runs in your tenant on signing day. Put any partner integration in the order form as a dated deliverable with an exit clause, or leave it out of the business case.
- **The public review base is thin.** Capterra UK shows four reviews (4.3 out of 5). One G2 reviewer found the initial integration slower than expected, and Capterra reviewers flagged template quality and an unfinished clause library. *Guard:* ask for two references at firms your size in your practice area, and run a paid pilot on one document type before any multi-module commitment.
- **Templates decay.** Automation fails when the lawyer who built a template leaves or the market standard moves. *Guard:* name an owner and a review date for every template, and track how much lawyers edit output after generation. Rising edits mean the template has drifted. See [legal knowledge management](/en/learn/legal-knowledge-management/).
- **The Claude connector can write, not just read.** *Guard:* enable it read-only first, give read-write to one pilot group, and audit its drafts for a month before a firm-wide rollout.
