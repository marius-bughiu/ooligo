[Catalyst](/en/tools/catalyst/) and [Vitally](/en/tools/vitally/) are both positioned as the modern alternative to [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) for mid-market and growth-stage CS teams, but they've diverged meaningfully since Catalyst merged with Totango in February 2024. That merger matters for this comparison: Catalyst now operates as the "Catalyst Customer Growth Platform" within Totango's broader product suite, backed by Great Hill Partners, with co-CEOs Edward Chiu and Alistair Rennie at the helm. Catalyst brings workflow-customization depth; Vitally brings a collaboration surface and a single automation model that doesn't require two separate configuration systems. This is a comparison between a platform mid-transition and a platform that has been shipping consistently against a single roadmap.

## Where Catalyst wins

- **Salesforce-native workflow depth.** Catalyst was built for teams that run their entire CS motion out of [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/). Its account planning, goal tracking, and playbook structure map to Salesforce objects more granularly than Vitally's Salesforce sync. If your CSMs live in Salesforce and treat the CS tool as a Salesforce overlay, Catalyst's data model fits that pattern.
- **Totango's enterprise governance layer.** Post-merger, Catalyst customers gain access to Totango's enterprise security and governance: SSO, team management at scale, advanced user hierarchy, and audit controls that smaller standalone CS tools don't surface. For customers of Catalyst's parent entity who need those controls, the merger added capability rather than removed it.
- **Expansion signal detection.** Catalyst's expansion signals engine — now augmented by Totango's Unison Customer Intelligence layer — surfaces upsell and cross-sell signals from product usage patterns. For CS teams with revenue expansion quotas (not just retention mandates), that proactive signal layer is a genuine differentiator against Vitally's more manual expansion identification.
- **Proven track record on workflow customization.** Before the merger, Catalyst built a strong reputation among mid-market SaaS CS teams for the flexibility of its task and playbook structure. CSMs could configure their own workflows without going to an admin. That capability remains in the current product.

## Where Vitally wins

- **Single automation system.** Vitally's playbook engine uses one interface for all automation — 7 trigger types, 13 actions, branching logic — covering the full range from data-triggered alerts to CSM-assigned tasks. Catalyst's workflow tooling is strong but narrower on the automation side; complex multi-step lifecycle programs require more manual orchestration. The Totango merger adds Journey Orchestrator-style capabilities to the roadmap but those aren't fully baked into the Catalyst product layer yet.
- **Collaborative docs embedded in the CS surface.** Vitally's native Docs feature puts QBR prep, success plans, and meeting notes inside the same platform as the account data — with real-time customer metrics embedded. Catalyst doesn't have a native docs layer. CS teams running Catalyst typically maintain their account plans in Salesforce, Notion, or Google Docs, which means manually reconciling written plans against live account data every week.
- **No merger uncertainty.** Platform mergers take 18-36 months to reach product-level integration. Catalyst and Totango announced their merger in February 2024; the products are still converging. If you're buying Catalyst today, you're partly buying a roadmap promise — that the best of Catalyst's workflow UX and Totango's enterprise infrastructure will be coherently merged. Vitally has no integration complexity on its roadmap; the platform ships from one codebase.
- **Predictable pricing model.** Vitally prices by engagement model (Tech-Touch, Hybrid-Touch, High-Touch) with all features included at each tier. Post-merger Catalyst/Totango pricing is more opaque — the combined entity has multiple product tiers (Catalyst Growth Platform, Totango CS platform, Unison intelligence engine) that can be purchased separately or bundled, making total cost of ownership harder to forecast before a sales conversation.
- **Time to value.** Vitally's average go-live is approximately 2 months with all features available on day one. Catalyst's implementation, while historically faster than Gainsight's, has added complexity post-merger as teams must now navigate which features live in the Catalyst layer versus the Totango layer.

## Pricing reality

Vitally's mid-market deployments run $18,000-50,000/year for a 10-15 person CS team, with all platform features included. Catalyst's pre-merger pricing history showed $15,000-50,000/year for similar team sizes. Post-merger, the combined Totango/Catalyst pricing structure is quote-only and harder to benchmark without a sales conversation: Catalyst's Growth Platform, Totango's core CS platform, and the Unison AI engine are priced and combined differently depending on which capabilities you need. Estimates from post-merger RFP disclosures suggest Totango/Catalyst bundle deals start around $20,000-40,000/year for smaller deployments. Neither platform publishes list pricing. At comparable scope and team size, Vitally and Catalyst are in the same rough band (roughly 1.0-1.5× of each other), which means the pricing decision comes down to the capability match, not a structural cost difference.

## Implementation effort

Vitally: ~2 months average go-live, single product, all features available on day one. Catalyst post-merger: implementation complexity has increased modestly as the combined product suite has more moving parts. Teams buying Catalyst now need to be explicit about which components they're deploying (Catalyst platform only vs. Totango full suite) to avoid scope creep during onboarding. Pre-merger Catalyst was known for fast implementation; that characteristic is still present in the Catalyst-only deployment path but less certain when the full suite is in scope.

## Verdict

- **Pick Catalyst** if you're already in the Salesforce-heavy CS motion, value account-plan flexibility, and your CS team is actively evaluating Totango's broader suite — meaning the merger is a feature, not a risk, for your evaluation.
- **Pick Vitally** if you want your entire CS team (not just admins) to use the platform daily, you need QBR docs and success plans to live inside your CS tool alongside live account data, and you want a platform that is shipping against a single coherent roadmap rather than converging two.
- **Pick neither** if you need Gainsight PX — in-app product engagement woven into CS health scores. Neither Catalyst nor Vitally offers that bundle. If PX is a genuine use case, evaluate Gainsight instead.

If you're choosing in a vacuum, pick Vitally. The Catalyst-Totango merger may ultimately produce a stronger platform, but mid-transition is the worst time to commit to it. Buy Catalyst when the product integration is complete and the combined platform reviews post-merger are consistent with what was sold. Until then, Vitally's single-codebase, no-asterisks delivery is the lower-risk choice at comparable price.
