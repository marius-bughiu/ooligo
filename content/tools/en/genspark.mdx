## What it is

Genspark is an AI workspace built around one idea: you describe the deliverable, and agents produce it. The center of the product is Super Agent, which plans a task, browses, calls tools and returns a finished file. Around it sit purpose-built agents for slides, sheets, docs and email (AI Slides, AI Sheets, AI Docs, GenMail), a coding agent, AI Meeting Notes, and Call For Me, which places phone calls on your behalf. Add-ins for Word, Excel and PowerPoint bring the same agents into an open Office file.

Two newer layers matter for business buyers. **Genspark Claw**, launched on 12 March 2026, is an always-on assistant that runs on its own dedicated cloud computer (or on your machine through a desktop app), takes instructions from Slack, Teams, WhatsApp and other chat apps, and runs scheduled jobs while you are away. **AI Workspace 6.0**, released on 21 July 2026, added SecondBrain, a persistent memory layer that pulls context from email, meetings, documents, calls and the CRM so agents do not start cold on every task.

The company, MainFunc, started as an AI search engine and shut that product down despite more than five million users to concentrate on agents. It extended its Series B to $485 million at a $2.6 billion post-money valuation on 17 June 2026 and named a chief revenue officer the same day. It reports a $250 million annualized run rate and more than 7,000 business customers; both figures are company-reported and unaudited. On 29 April 2026 it signed a global partnership with Microsoft: the Slides, Sheets and Docs agents ship as Microsoft 365 plugins, and Super Agent can be added through [Microsoft Agent 365](/en/tools/microsoft-agent-365/) with its own Entra Agent ID.

## Why it lands on shortlists

- **It ships the file, not the chat.** A 12-slide QBR deck from a pipeline export, a vendor comparison sheet with sources, a first-draft board memo. For ops teams whose real output is decks and spreadsheets, that is the job.
- **It reaches into the CRM.** One OAuth connection to Salesforce is shared by Claw, Super Agent, the workflow builder and AgentBase, so an agent can pull records, run reports and update fields. HubSpot, Slack, Notion, the Google and Microsoft 365 suites, and custom MCP servers are also on the connector list.
- **Claw handles the standing jobs.** A Monday digest of stalled opportunities, a daily scan of a competitor's pricing page, or a weekly candidate-pipeline summary posted to a Teams channel runs on a schedule without anyone opening the app.
- **It fits inside a Microsoft shop.** If your company is standardizing on Agent 365 for agent governance, Genspark is a pre-integrated partner rather than another unmanaged tool.

## What it costs

From Genspark's help center, checked 22 November 2026, in USD. **Free** gives 100 credits a day up to a lifetime cap, with no downloads or exports. **Plus** is $24.99 a month ($19.99 billed annually) for 10,000 credits. **Pro** is $249.99 a month ($199.99 annually) for 125,000 credits. **Team** covers 2-150 seats at $35 per seat per month on the Team plan page; the membership page lists $40 month to month and $35 billed annually. Each Team seat gets 12,000 credits that do not roll over, plus zero-credit use of the top chat and image models in AI Chat and AI Image. Extra credits are $45 per 10,000-credit pack. **Enterprise** is quoted by sales, starts at 20,000 credits per seat, and typically runs on a 36-month initial term.

Claw's always-on mode is billed separately: a flat monthly Cloud Computer subscription in three hardware tiers, from Lite upward, on top of the credits Claw consumes.

Credits are the real price. Every agent turn, regeneration and failed retry costs credits, and long chats cost more per reply as context grows. A realistic band for an ops user who builds a few decks and research sheets a week is $35-$80 per seat per month: the seat, plus one top-up pack for the heavy weeks, plus the Cloud Computer fee for anyone running Claw.

## Best for

- RevOps and sales-ops leads who turn CRM exports into QBR decks, territory plans and forecast summaries every week, and want the slides done rather than outlined
- Legal-ops and procurement analysts producing vendor comparisons and research memos from public sources
- Recruiting ops teams building market maps and hiring-plan decks for leadership

It is a weak fit for work that has to stay inside an existing Microsoft or Google tenant with no new vendor, and for privileged or regulated data on the Team plan (see watch-outs).

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/)** and **[Microsoft 365 Copilot](/en/tools/microsoft-365-copilot/)** — the market-share leaders, and often already licensed. Pick them when procurement will not add a vendor, when the work is mostly chat and drafting, or when Copilot already sits inside the Office files your team uses. Genspark's edge is producing a finished deck or sheet from one prompt.
- **[Claude Cowork](/en/tools/claude-cowork/)** — pick it when the inputs are files on your own machine and the output must stay there.
- **[Manus](/en/tools/manus/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in autonomous agents. Pick it for long, parallel, browser-heavy research runs such as building a 200-account list; pick Genspark when the output is a polished deck or a workspace your team shares.
- **[Perplexity](/en/tools/perplexity/)** — pick it when you need a cited answer, not a built file.
- **[Lindy](/en/tools/lindy/)** — pick it when the job is a standing CRM or inbox workflow with strict triggers, rather than a document.

## Watch-outs

- **Credits burn on failures.** Genspark's own guide says each failed agent retry and each regeneration costs credits, and public reviews through mid-2026 repeatedly cite tasks that failed and still drained the balance. Guard: run a two-week pilot on Plus, log credits per task type from the Credit Usage page, and set organization-wide usage caps before rolling seats out.
- **Refunds are narrow.** Team subscriptions are non-refundable for partly used billing periods, and Enterprise terms typically include a 36-month initial term with 60 days' notice to terminate. Guard: start on monthly Team billing, and negotiate a 12-month term and an exit clause before signing an Enterprise order form.
- **Team-plan data is US-only, on shared infrastructure.** EU or APAC residency, a dedicated VPC, a custom DPA and the SOC 2 Type II report (under NDA) are Enterprise items. Guard: keep EU personal data, candidate records and privileged legal material out of Team workspaces until an Enterprise agreement covers them.
- **Claw acts unattended and holds state.** It can send, schedule and update records while no one is watching, and deleting or cancelling a Cloud Computer permanently removes its files, memory and stored credentials. Guard: connect CRM and email with read-only scopes first, review scheduled outputs for two weeks before granting write access, and export anything you need to keep before changing the Claw plan.
