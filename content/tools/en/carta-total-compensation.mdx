## What it is

Carta Total Compensation (CTC) is Carta's salary and equity benchmarking and pay-band product for venture-backed private companies. Its edge is where the equity data comes from: Carta runs the cap tables of a large share of US startups (its own pages cite 45,000-50,000+), so equity benchmarks are built from grants actually recorded on cap tables, not from survey answers. Salary and cash benchmarks come from customers' connected HRIS and payroll systems.

CTC has been sold to companies that do not use Carta for their cap table since August 2024, and in 2026 it is priced as its own subscription rather than bundled into a cap-table plan. Demand is rising for a second reason: Pulley shuts down on 8 December 2026 and has arranged a Carta-only assisted migration, so a wave of seed and Series A companies is landing on Carta and being pitched CTC with it.

The question it answers for a recruiting team: **how much equity should this offer carry for a company at our stage, valuation and headcount, and does the cash side line up with our bands?**

## What you get

**Benchmarks by peer group.** Salary, total cash and equity benchmarks by job area, level and geography, filterable by post-money valuation, capital raised and headcount. Carta refreshes them quarterly. In the Q2 2026 refresh (Carta release notes, published around July 2026), about 91% of cells met Carta's medium or high confidence threshold for equity and 80% for salary, with median salary and total cash up about 3% and median equity up 3-8%. Recent refreshes added business operations, information security and engineering specialisms, CEO benchmarks, and board-member and advisor equity grants issued since January 2024.

**Pay bands and plans.** A compensation plan with pay bands that recalculate minimums and maximums when you move a target, employee and company scorecards against the bands, and a hiring plan tied to an equity-pool forecast. That last piece is CTC's practical advantage for a startup: it shows whether the offers in next year's hiring plan fit the option pool before the board is asked to top it up.

**HRIS data in.** Benchmarks are available as soon as you buy; reports and scorecards need an HRIS connection. Target bonus and commission sync automatically from [Rippling](/en/tools/rippling/), and arrive by CSV or SFTP from other systems.

**What it lacks.** No AI agent or MCP server for comp questions as of November 2026, and no merit-cycle workflow comparable to [Pave](/en/tools/pave/)'s. If you want recruiters to price offers from inside [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), Pave's MCP access or our [offer prep skill](/en/workflows/offer-prep-claude-skill/) does that today.

## Pricing reality

Carta does not publish CTC prices; it is sold by quote. Vendr's Carta listing estimates roughly $21,000 a year for small teams. Treat that as directional: Vendr sells negotiation services, and it reports that Carta prices move with negotiation, timing and competing quotes. Expect the price to scale with headcount, and expect a bundle offer if you also move your cap table to Carta.

## Best for

Founders, People leads and TA leads at US venture-backed companies from seed to roughly Series C (about 20-500 employees) who already run their cap table on Carta, or are moving to it from Pulley, and whose hardest offer question is the equity grant. Ask for the benchmark preview of your ten most-hired roles before signing.

Skip it if you are a public company (the data covers private companies only), if most of your headcount is outside the US, or if your board or auditor expects a named survey.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Pave](/en/tools/pave/)** is the market-share leader in startup benchmarking and has the broader product: live salary data from 9,000+ connected companies, a free Lite tier, merit cycles, and an agent with MCP access. Pick Pave when salary benchmarks and comp planning matter as much as equity, or when you want to start free. Pick CTC when equity is the main question and your cap table already lives on Carta.

**[Aon Radford](/en/tools/aon-radford/)** is the default survey house for tech. Pick it once you are pre-IPO or public, or when a compensation committee needs a named survey.

**[Ravio](/en/tools/ravio/)** is the pick for European companies, with EU Pay Transparency Directive reporting built in. **[Compa](/en/tools/compa/)**, the fastest-growing entrant, prices against peer offers rather than employee pay and fits enterprises of 2,000+ employees.

## Watch-outs

- **Salary data trails equity data.** Independent reviews, including Ravio's benchmarking roundup, describe CTC's equity benchmarks as more mature than its salary benchmarks, and the Q2 2026 confidence figures (80% salary versus 91% equity) agree. *Guard:* for cash pay, check every role with a thin or low-confidence cell against a second source, such as Pave's free tier, before setting the band.
- **Equity values follow your last valuation.** Benchmarks are cut by post-money valuation, so a company that raised at a peak valuation is compared with peers that may now be worth less. *Guard:* compare grants as a percentage of fully diluted shares as well as dollar value, and have finance sign off on the valuation used in offers.
- **Pulley migration pressure.** Carta's Pulley offer keeps first-year contract economics, which makes CTC easy to add during the move without a real evaluation. *Guard:* treat the cap-table migration and the CTC purchase as separate decisions, and get a CTC quote with and without the bundle.
- **Thin international coverage.** Outside the US and Canada, sample sizes drop quickly. *Guard:* if more than a quarter of your hiring is international, run [Ravio](/en/tools/ravio/) or Pave for those markets and keep CTC for US equity.
