## What it is

Five9 is a cloud contact-center platform (CCaaS): inbound and outbound voice, digital channels, a predictive dialer, workforce engagement, and an AI layer branded Genius AI, sold per concurrent seat with AI usage metered on top. It is publicly traded (NASDAQ: FIVN), serves more than 3,000 organizations, and sits in the Leaders quadrant of Gartner's 2025 CCaaS Magic Quadrant alongside NICE, Genesys, AWS, and Talkdesk.

The reason to look at it now is the AI line. In Q2 2026 (reported 6 August 2026) Five9 posted $312.4M in revenue, up 10%, with subscription revenue up 14%. AI revenue was about $39M, up 78% year over year and roughly 15% of subscription revenue against 9% a year earlier, and management raised its full-year AI growth outlook to at least 60%. The quarter also included a $100M, five-year renewal with a Fortune 100 financial-services customer, transacted through Google Marketplace. Amit Mathradas, previously CEO of Nintex, took over as CEO on 2 February 2026.

## Why it shows up in support stacks

- **Voice AI Agents are the native self-service layer.** The release launched on 23 June 2026 at Customer Contact Week runs on what Five9 calls an Agentic Voice Switch, handling speech, reasoning, and voice generation in one path, with multi-agent orchestration, tool calling for authentication and record updates, and post-call evaluation. AI Agent Studio covers build, call testing, versioning, environment promotion, and rollback. The one named production reference is PODS, which said it beat its containment targets; Five9 published no independent containment figure.
- **Blended inbound and outbound is the heritage.** Five9 grew up as a dialer company and bought Acqueon in 2024 for outbound engagement, so collections, sales, and proactive-notification teams get a deeper outbound toolset than most help-desk-first platforms offer.
- **It lets you keep your CRM and WEM vendor.** Every bundle includes a CRM adapter choice (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Dynamics, Zendesk, Oracle) and a UC adapter (Microsoft Teams, Zoom, RingCentral), and workforce engagement can be Five9's own, Verint, or Calabrio. Five9 does not publish a customer-facing MCP server; integration runs through its REST APIs and adapters.

## Pricing reality

Five9 lists two prices publicly: Digital at $119 per concurrent seat per month (chat, email, SMS, social, no voice) and Core at $159 (all channels including voice). Plus, Pro, and Enterprise, which add advanced AI and workforce engagement, are quote-only. Every bundle has a 50-seat minimum and includes 3,000 AI minutes per bundled seat, with usage billed beyond that.

The floor is therefore about $95K a year for voice (50 Core seats × $159 × 12), before telecom and usage. A 200-seat voice center at Core list is about $382K a year. On AI, Five9 told investors at Goldman Sachs Communacopia in September 2026 that pricing is a committed volume of minutes or units, with overage charged at a higher rate, and that it is moving customers to a revenue-commit model where spend can shift between human seats and AI agents within agreed bands. Independent analyst write-ups report 36-month minimum terms with about 5% annual escalators; treat that as a negotiating baseline, not list.

## Best for

Heads of contact center and CX operations running 50 to roughly 1,500 seats with a real outbound motion — collections, renewals, appointment setting, sales follow-up — alongside inbound support, who want dialer, routing, and AI agents on one contract and need to keep an existing CRM or WEM vendor.

Skip it if you run under 50 agents (the seat minimum makes it overhead next to a help desk's own AI), or if your bar is a published containment benchmark today: Five9's voice-agent evidence is still one named customer.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/)** and **NICE CXone** hold the largest share of enterprise CCaaS. Pick Genesys when you plan to run several vendors' AI agents behind one front door at 500+ seats; pick NICE when workforce engagement and quality management drive the decision. Pick Five9 over both when outbound volume matters and you want a smaller vendor that will negotiate harder. **Talkdesk** moved from Visionary to Leader in Gartner's 2025 Quadrant, the fastest climber in the segment; pick it for faster mid-market deployment and its industry editions. **Amazon Connect** wins for AWS-native teams willing to build on per-minute primitives.

Most Five9 buyers face a second question: native Genius AI or a specialist on top. [Parloa](/en/tools/parloa/), [PolyAI](/en/tools/polyai/), and [Cresta](/en/tools/cresta/) all integrate with Five9. Rule: pilot native Voice AI Agents on your top five call reasons first, using your own containment and transfer data; add a specialist only where its resolution rate beats native by enough to pay for a second contract.

## Watch-outs

- **Business-model churn.** Revenue growth slowed from 40% in 2021 to 10% in Q2 2026, the share price is down more than 80% from its 2021 peak, a new CEO arrived in February 2026, and adjusted gross margin dropped to 61% in Q2 2026 from 63%. Guard: negotiate a change-of-control clause that lets you exit or freeze price if Five9 is acquired, and cap renewal escalators in writing.
- **AI overage is priced above committed volume.** Five9 says overage runs at a higher rate than the committed band. Guard: size the commit from three months of real call-reason volume, not the sales forecast, and get the overage rate and an 80% usage alert written into the order form.
- **Reliability and support show up in reviews.** Five9 averages 4.1 on G2, and recurring complaints cite outages, call quality, and uneven support. Guard: put a 99.99% uptime SLA with service credits in the contract, and test failover routing to a backup number before go-live.
- **Voice AI proof is thin.** One named customer is not a benchmark. Guard: make any AI commit contingent on a 60-day pilot against containment and transfer-rate targets you set, measured on your own calls.
