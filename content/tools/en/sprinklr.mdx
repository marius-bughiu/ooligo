## What it is

Sprinklr is an enterprise customer-experience suite sold as four products on one platform: Sprinklr Social (publishing, engagement, governance), Sprinklr Insights (social listening and voice of the customer), Sprinklr Service (contact centre, digital and voice care, AI agents), and Sprinklr Marketing. It started in social media management in 2009, and of the three remaining Leaders in Gartner's Voice of the Customer Magic Quadrant it is the only one that also sells a full contact centre. Its nearest like-for-like on the listening side is [Medallia](/en/tools/medallia/); on the service side it competes with [Salesforce Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/), [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/), and [Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/).

It is listed on the NYSE (CXM) and is not an acquisition target in any reporting we found. The business is large and flat: fiscal 2026 revenue was $857.2M, and the updated fiscal 2027 guidance of $866.5M–$868.5M implies about 1% growth. Q2 FY27 (quarter to 31 July 2026) came in at $213.7M, up 1% year on year, with remaining performance obligations up 11% to $1.03B.

## Why it shows up in CX stacks

- **One data model across social, listening, and care.** A complaint that starts as a public post, becomes a DM, and ends as a voice call stays one case with one customer record. That is the whole argument for Sprinklr, and no listening-only or service-only vendor makes it.
- **It is still a VoC Leader.** Gartner's March 2026 Magic Quadrant for Voice of the Customer Platforms named four Leaders: Qualtrics, Medallia, Sprinklr, and Press Ganey Forsta. Qualtrics closed its acquisition of Press Ganey Forsta on 18 May 2026, so Sprinklr is one of three independent Leaders left.
- **Its AI work is aimed at service.** The Spring '26 (26.4) release on 7 April 2026 added Autonomous Evaluation — large-scale simulations with pass/fail criteria and logs for AI agents before go-live — and AI+ Studio, a no-code workspace to build and monitor generative-AI agents. Summer '26 on 15 July 2026 added rebuilt voice agents with better turn-taking and noise handling, video analysis from the May 2026 ViralMoment acquisition, an Adobe Customer Journey Analytics integration, and a first-party MCP connector, still labelled beta, that surfaces Sprinklr data inside Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude. The company reports ARR from its generative-AI Service SKUs up 50% year on year.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price. Self-serve plans were retired on 30 April 2026, and the old pricing page now returns a 404; every suite is sold through a demo and an annual or multi-year quote. The last listed self-serve prices were $249 and $359 per user per month for Social — a useful reminder of the per-seat order of magnitude, but not what enterprise contracts look like.

Third-party pricing guides put the practical floor at roughly $50,000 per year before implementation, with multi-suite programs reaching several hundred thousand. Treat that as an estimate: none of them publishes a sample size. Vendr's February 2026 buyer guide gives no median but documents the negotiating mechanics — 20–35% off initial quotes is common, 24- or 36-month terms price 15–30% below a 12-month term, and auto-renewal notice periods of 60–90 days. The price is driven by named user licenses (admin, publisher, analyst, agent), channel and message volume, the number of suites, and professional services, which are usually a separate line.

## Best for

Enterprise CX or social-care leaders at consumer brands — telecoms, airlines, consumer electronics, retail banks — where public social channels carry a large share of service demand and the job is moving a conversation from listening to resolution without switching tools. The fit is strongest above roughly 50 care agents plus a social team that already lives in Sprinklr.

Do not buy it if social is a marketing channel and service happens in email and chat: a helpdesk plus a lighter social tool costs a fraction of this. Do not buy it for a single NPS or survey program — that is a Qualtrics or Medallia decision. And do not buy it under roughly 1,000 employees; since early 2025 the company has explicitly concentrated on its largest accounts, and smaller customers are not the priority.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Qualtrics](/en/tools/qualtrics/)** and **[Medallia](/en/tools/medallia/)** are the two largest VoC platforms by install base. Pick either when the signal is surveys, frontline feedback, or employee experience. Pick Sprinklr when the signal is public social and the same team has to respond to it.

**[Salesforce Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/)** and **[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/)** are the default service platforms. Pick them when service is ticket-led and Salesforce or Zendesk already holds the customer record; neither does enterprise social listening.

**[Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/)** and **[Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/)** are the fastest-growing entrants taking AI-agent budget off the suites. Pick one when the job is a resolution agent on top of your current helpdesk and you do not want to re-platform.

**[Enterpret](/en/tools/enterpret/)** is the pick when feedback already exists in tickets and reviews and the job is classifying it for product and support teams.

If none fit — you need social care but not the suite — pair a mid-market social tool with your existing helpdesk and revisit Sprinklr when social volume justifies a single platform.

## Watch-outs

- **Growth has stalled, and the company is triaging accounts.** CEO Rory Read's 2025 "Project Bear Hug" focused the company on its top 700–800 customers, management called fiscal 2026 a transitional year with elevated churn, and fiscal 2027 guidance is about 1% growth. **Guard:** ask whether your account is inside the named strategic-customer program and which customer-success resources come with it; if you are outside it, write named support SLAs and a success-manager allocation into the order form.
- **Complexity is the most common complaint.** G2 review tags for Sprinklr Social are led by "complex usage" and "learning curve", and setup typically needs paid professional services. **Guard:** scope a phased rollout, one suite and two channels first, with a fixed-fee implementation statement of work and acceptance criteria before signing the subscription.
- **Bundle pricing hides the expensive suite.** Buying Social, Insights, and Service together makes it hard to see what each costs at renewal. **Guard:** require line-item pricing per suite and per user type, and a renewal-uplift cap; third-party pricing trackers report 5–15% annual uplifts as common.
- **The MCP connector is beta.** Summer '26 shipped it labelled beta, with no published tool list or data-access model. **Guard:** do not build an agent workflow on it until it is GA; ask for its scopes and audit logging in writing, and use the REST API with a read-only service account in the meantime.
- **Announced voice-agent performance is a target, not a measurement.** "Sub-second" response is Sprinklr's stated goal. **Guard:** run the agent through Autonomous Evaluation on your own call recordings and set containment and latency thresholds before routing live traffic.
