"Legaltech" is the umbrella category for any technology used in the practice or operation of law — [CLM](/en/learn/contract-lifecycle-management/), [eDiscovery](/en/learn/ediscovery/), [matter management](/en/learn/matter-management/), legal research platforms, e-billing, [practice management](/en/tools/clio/), and more. "Legal AI" is the subset of legaltech specifically focused on machine learning and (increasingly) generative AI applied to legal tasks. Most legaltech is now adding AI features; many legal-AI vendors are evolving into broader legaltech platforms. The distinction is blurring.

## The legaltech landscape

Legaltech historically organized into seven main categories:

| Category | Examples | Primary user |
|---|---|---|
| Contract management | [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/), [Agiloft](/en/tools/agiloft/), [SirionLabs](/en/tools/sirionlabs/), [Concord](/en/tools/concord/) | In-house Legal Ops |
| eDiscovery | [Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/), [Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/), [DISCO](/en/tools/disco/), [Logikcull](/en/tools/logikcull/) | Litigation teams, in-house and firm |
| Practice management | [Clio](/en/tools/clio/), [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/), [Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/) | Solo and small firms |
| Legal research | Westlaw, LEXIS, Bloomberg Law, [Casetext](/en/tools/casetext/) | All practicing attorneys |
| Matter and spend management | Onit, Mitratech, BusyLamp, Brightflag | In-house Legal Ops |
| Document production | [Litera](/en/tools/litera/), iManage, NetDocuments | Mid-to-large firms |
| Court filing | One Legal, File & ServeXpress, ECF systems | Litigation teams |

Each category has its own vendor landscape, sales cycle, and integration patterns.

## Legal AI as a layer

Legal AI organizes by capability rather than category:

- **Drafting AI.** [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/), [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/), Casetext Cocounsel — AI for drafting contracts, briefs, memos.
- **Review AI.** [BlackBoiler](/en/tools/blackboiler/), [Luminance](/en/tools/luminance/), [LawGeex](/en/tools/lawgeex/) — AI for reviewing inbound contracts against playbooks.
- **Research AI.** [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/), Lexis+ AI, [Casetext](/en/tools/casetext/) — AI for legal research.
- **eDiscovery AI.** Relativity AI, Everlaw AI, DISCO Cecilia, Reveal Brainspace — AI for [privilege review](/en/learn/privilege-review/), document classification, case analysis.
- **Knowledge management AI.** [Litera](/en/tools/litera/) Foundation, increasingly direct [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) Skills — AI for [knowledge retrieval](/en/learn/legal-knowledge-management/).
- **General-purpose AI.** [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) (with appropriate enterprise terms) — used across categories.

## How the categories converge

Three patterns:

1. **Legaltech adds AI.** [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) ships Ironclad AI; [Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/) ships Relativity AI; [Litera](/en/tools/litera/) ships AI features across products. The legaltech vendor becomes a legal-AI vendor by extension.
2. **Legal AI broadens scope.** [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) started as drafting AI; now spans research, contract review, document analysis. The legal-AI vendor builds toward a broader platform.
3. **General-purpose AI enters legal.** [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) with custom Skills replace specialized legal-AI tools for some use cases. The general-purpose platform competes with specialized legal-AI vendors directly.

## When to choose legal-AI specialists vs general-purpose AI

The strategic question for Legal Ops:

| Use case | Legal-AI specialist | General-purpose AI + Skills |
|---|---|---|
| Highest-bar drafting (M&A, complex commercial) | [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/), [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) | Borderline; fine-tuned playbook needed |
| Routine NDA review | [LawGeex](/en/tools/lawgeex/), [BlackBoiler](/en/tools/blackboiler/) | [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) + [contract redline Skill](/en/workflows/contract-redline-claude-skill/) |
| Legal research with citations | [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/), Lexis+ AI | Not viable — need verified sources |
| Knowledge retrieval from firm corpus | [Litera](/en/tools/litera/) Foundation | [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) + custom Skills against firm DMS |
| First-pass eDiscovery review | [Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/) AI, [Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/) AI | Not viable — production-grade scale needed |
| Generic summarization, drafting, analysis | Specialist overkill | [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) is the right answer |

Specialists win when the data, the workflow, or the integration is legal-specific. General-purpose wins when the task is generalizable and the data flows in normally.

## How to think about the budget

Most in-house legal AI budgets in 2026 have three line items:

- **Enterprise general-purpose AI.** [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) Enterprise or equivalent — covers the broad use cases at predictable per-seat pricing.
- **One or two legal-AI specialists.** Typically Harvey or Spellbook for drafting, plus one CLM-integrated AI ([Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) AI or similar) for contract workflow.
- **Specialty AI for high-leverage use cases.** [Casetext](/en/tools/casetext/) for research-heavy practices, AI eDiscovery features in the matter platform when discovery is recurring.

The over-buying pattern is purchasing every legal-AI specialist; the under-buying pattern is trying to do everything with general-purpose AI alone.
