Both names on this page have changed since the comparison was first written. [Clari](/en/tools/clari/) merged with [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/) on 3 December 2025, and on 2 September 2026 the combined company moved to a single Salesloft brand. What you buy today is **Clari Forecast**, one module on Salesloft's menu next to Cadence, Conversation Intelligence, Deals, Inspect and Rhythm. [BoostUp](/en/tools/boostup/) renamed itself **Terret** on 9 September 2025 and repositioned from a forecasting point tool into an agent platform. It is still independent. `boostup.ai` now redirects to `terret.ai`, and the app still logs in at `app.boostup.ai` (both checked 26 November 2026). This site keeps the `boostup` slug; the product is Terret.

The old version of this choice was "enterprise depth versus mid-market price". In late 2026 it is a different question: should your forecast come from your engagement vendor, or from an independent vendor whose main product is the forecast?

## Where Clari Forecast wins

**An audit trail the board already trusts.** Clari's roll-up hierarchy did not change in the rebrand. Commits roll up from rep to manager to VP to CRO, every override is recorded at each layer, and every submitted call is snapshotted. When the quarter closes below Commit, you can trace the gap to named overrides at named layers. Terret produces a defensible machine forecast and a board-ready narrative, but it has less public evidence of override-level documentation at that depth. If forecast-miss analysis is a standing board item, or an IPO or acquisition process will put your forecast under diligence, this is what you are paying Clari for.

**One contract with your engagement layer.** If Salesloft already runs your cadences and calls, Clari Forecast is an add-on to a contract you have, not a second vendor. Cadence activity and conversation signals reach deal inspection through Salesloft's own pipes, and Salesloft markets the AI Forecast Agent as triggering next-best actions directly in the seller's workflow. For a team on [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/), the same fact cuts the other way: your forecast roadmap would be set by your sequencer's direct competitor.

**Forecast data reachable from an LLM through MCP.** The Salesloft MCP server launched on 14 April 2026 and now exposes forecast and deal-inspection data alongside Cadence and call intelligence. It is listed in [Claude](/en/tools/claude/)'s connector directory, with ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini connectors after it. Terret's demo page says you can ask questions "via Slack, your LLM, Salesforce, or the Terret app", but we found no published MCP server from Terret (checked 26 November 2026).

**Bigger deployments.** Vendr's data covers 291 Clari purchases, with contracts up to about $415,000 a year. For multi-region teams with several business units and non-standard deal structures, Clari has more documented deployments at that size than Terret does.

## Where Terret (BoostUp) wins

**A smaller contract.** Vendr's median for Terret is about $30,420 a year, in a band of $23,327 to $80,868. Clari's Vendr median is about $76,000. At the median, Clari costs about 2.5× more. Add Clari's $15,000-$75,000 of professional services and the first-year gap widens further.

**Forecasting is the product, not a module.** Terret sells three surfaces, Nexus, Forecast and Conversation Intelligence, and Forecast is the direct descendant of what BoostUp sold for years. Nobody will try to price an engagement platform against it. Post-rebrand, Salesloft account teams price Cadence and Conversations alongside Clari Forecast, so a forecast-only quote is now the exception.

**Neutral on engagement tools.** Terret reads [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) or [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) for the CRM spine, [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) and [Chorus](/en/tools/chorus/) for calls, and Outreach and [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) for engagement. If you do not run Salesloft, that neutrality matters more than any single feature.

**A daily manager workflow.** Terret's deal inspection is built around manager review: stalled activity, odd stage jumps, rep-versus-machine commit gaps, with call briefs pushed to Slack 30 minutes before each call. Clari's interface is deep but aimed at RevOps and the CRO. Mid-market sales managers are more likely to run their own reviews in Terret without asking RevOps to build the report first.

## Pricing reality

Neither vendor publishes a price. `salesloft.com/pricing` is a "Talk to Sales" form and `terret.ai/pricing` is a demo-booking page (both checked 26 November 2026). Every number below is third-party procurement data, not list price.

- **Clari Forecast**: Vendr median near $76,000 per year across 291 purchases, with a range of $19,065 to $415,001. Docket estimated Forecast alone at about $100-$120 per user per month on annual billing in April 2026. Implementation services run about $15,000-$75,000.
- **Terret**: Vendr median about $30,420 per year, with a band of $23,327 to $80,868. Software directories list about $80 per user per month for forecasting, but they contradict each other and Terret confirms none of it.

On those numbers Clari's per-seat price is roughly 1.25× to 1.5× Terret's. The contract-level gap is wider, about 2.5×, because Clari deals carry bundled modules and services. An earlier version of this page cited a $160,000 Clari median and $200+ per-user all-in costs. Current Vendr data does not support those figures, so they are gone. Compare total contract value over the full term, including services and any bundled modules you would not otherwise buy.

## Implementation effort

**Clari Forecast**: 8 to 16 weeks to a forecast you can trust for a 25-100 rep team, longer on heavily customized Salesforce. Mapping the hierarchy, activity capture and snapshot cadence is the gating work, and someone in RevOps operates it after go-live.

**Terret**: it advertises a free 48-hour proof of concept on your own data. That tests whether the signal is real, not how long rollout takes, and Terret publishes no implementation timeline. The real dependency is [CRM hygiene](/en/learn/crm-hygiene/). Activity-grounded scoring thins out where reps work outside connected email and calendar, so check capture coverage per team before you sign, not after.

## Verdict

**Pick Clari Forecast when** Salesloft is already your engagement platform or will be, the board expects an auditable Commit / Best Case / Pipeline roll-up with an override trail, and you want forecast data in Claude or ChatGPT through a vendor-supported MCP server today.

**Pick Terret when** you have roughly 25 to 300 reps, the forecast is the reason for the purchase, and you run Outreach, Apollo or no sequencer you plan to change. Do not buy it for the agent fleet. Those agents launched in September 2025 with vendor-published evidence only. Buy the forecast and treat Nexus as upside to prove in the pilot.

**Pick neither when** you have fewer than about 20 reps or under $10M ARR. A $30,000 contract is still a large share of GTM budget at that size. Native Salesforce or HubSpot forecasting plus a [pipeline-review prompt pack](/en/workflows/pipeline-review-prompt-pack/) gets most of the discipline. If you want an independent forecasting vendor with more enterprise depth than Terret, see [Clari vs Aviso](/en/vs/clari-vs-aviso/) and [Aviso vs BoostUp](/en/vs/aviso-vs-boostup/).

**The pick if you cannot decide:** default to Terret. This keeps the earlier default, for a different reason. The old reason was Clari's price. The new one is that Clari Forecast is now sold and roadmapped by an engagement vendor, and the forecast-only buyer is no longer its customer. The exception is a team already on Salesloft. There, take Clari Forecast; the integration advantage is real. Getting this wrong costs about $45,000 a year in contract value at the medians, plus a forecast migration that resets your override history.
