## What it is

Libra is a legal AI workspace — research, drafting, contract review and bulk document analysis in one browser app, with Word and Outlook add-ins — that Wolters Kluwer now sells as Libra by Wolters Kluwer. It started as Libra Technology GmbH, a Berlin startup that launched its assistant in late 2024. Wolters Kluwer Legal & Regulatory agreed to buy it on 14 November 2025 and closed on 19 November for up to €90 million: €30 million upfront, the rest contingent on performance targets, against an expected ~€5 million ARR by the end of 2025.

The deal was about content. Libra answers over Wolters Kluwer's own commentary, statutes and case law country by country, which puts it in the same category as [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) on Westlaw and [Lexis+ AI with Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/) — a publisher's corpus with AI on top — except that its home ground is continental Europe, not the US. The surface is Chat, reusable Assistants, multi-step Workflows, playbook-based Review, and Discovery for pulling structured data out of large document sets. Underneath, Libra routes work to Anthropic and OpenAI models through its own layer; you pick a mode (Standard, Deep Thinking, Fast), not a model. Libra's own site claims more than 1,200 legal teams.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It is already live across nine European markets.** Wolters Kluwer launched it in the Netherlands on 7 January 2026, then Belgium and Italy in February, Poland, and Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary and Romania in March — each with local Wolters Kluwer content. Swiss material from Stämpfli, including the Berner Kommentar plus federal and cantonal law, was added on 10 June 2026. Beck-Noxtua's cross-border Europe License, by comparison, spans six countries and was still in beta this year.
- **Data stays in the EEA.** Chats and documents are stored encrypted on servers in Germany, OpenAI calls run through Azure's European service, and the FAQ states that no data goes to the United States and none is used for model training. The certification is ISO 27001:2022. Note what this is not: the models are Anthropic's and OpenAI's, served from European regions. If the requirement is European-built models on European-operated infrastructure, that is Noxtua's argument, not Libra's.
- **It connects to Wolters Kluwer's practice software.** The Kleos integration announced on 19 May 2026 carries Libra output into Kleos matter and case structures without re-keying, rolling out first in the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and Italy; Kleos serves more than 30,000 lawyers. In Belgium and Italy, Libra workflows also run inside InView Legal and One through an add-in.
- **Contract review works from your playbook.** The 5 May 2026 release lets a team structure review templates by topic, set acceptable and fallback positions, and attach rule-based criteria with cited reasoning; an Auto Mode handles one-off clause questions against selected research sources.

## Pricing reality

Libra publishes its prices, which is rare in this segment. Three tiers, per user, billed annually, excluding VAT: **Starter €900/year, Professional €2,000/year, Team €2,400/year.** Monthly billing on Professional runs €200 per user per month, according to an independent March 2026 review — 20% above the annual rate.

What the tier table does not make obvious:

- **Starter is trial-sized.** 100 chat messages and 100 uploads a month, 3 projects, and no Word or Outlook add-in, SharePoint, automated review or workflows. For a practising lawyer the real entry price is Professional, about €167 per user per month on annual terms.
- **Content is a separate line.** The pricing page states that legal-content subscriptions are required separately. The trial includes Wolters Kluwer and Otto Schmidt sources capped at three queries a day, and both must be bought afterwards. Existing Wolters Kluwer subscribers already pay this line; a firm without one should quote both before comparing against Beck-Noxtua, which bundles beck-online PREMIUM into €350 per user per month.
- **SSO is an add-on even on Team**, and premium models — every Claude Opus model and GPT-5.6 Sol — are metered daily: 10 messages on Starter, 20 or more on paid plans.

A 10-lawyer firm on Professional pays €20,000 a year before content. Against Legora at roughly $3,000 per user per year with a 10-seat minimum, and Harvey at about $1,200 per seat per month with a 20-seat minimum, Libra is the cheapest way into a publisher-backed legal workspace in Europe, and the pricing page states no seat minimum.

## Best for

Lawyers and in-house teams of roughly 1 to 50 seats in the nine launch markets — sharpest for Dutch, Belgian, German and Italian firms already on Kleos or a Wolters Kluwer content subscription — who want research, drafting and first-pass contract review over national law in one workspace at a price they can see before a demo. It also fits the legal ops lead at a mid-size corporate legal department who needs playbook-based contract review without buying a CLM.

Don't buy it for US or UK work: every launch market is continental European, and the value sits in the local content. Don't buy it if your documents live in iManage or NetDocuments today — iManage is listed as "soon," and the FAQ says other document management systems are not supported out of the box. And don't buy it as infrastructure: there is no documented API or MCP server.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Noxtua](/en/tools/noxtua/)** (sold in Germany as Beck-Noxtua) — the main German rival. Pick it when beck-online is your research standard and the requirement is European models on European infrastructure, with BSI C5 and TISAX in the certification stack. It costs €350 per user per month behind a 3-licence floor; Libra Professional is about half that per seat before content.
- **[Legora](/en/tools/legora/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in the segment. Pick it for cross-border or common-law work, Word-native drafting and Tabular Review across a data room. See [Noxtua vs Legora](/en/comparisons/noxtua-vs-legora/).
- **[Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/)** — pick it when the buyer is a multi-market large firm with the budget for a ~$288,000 annual floor and needs its agent depth.
- **[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/)** and **[Lexis+ AI with Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/)** — the top two by share in the US. Pick one when the matter turns on US or UK law and KeyCite or Shepard's is the citator you cannot give up.
- **[vLex Vincent AI](/en/tools/vlex-vincent-ai/)** — pick it for breadth, a corpus spanning 100+ countries, over the depth of any single European commentary.

The status quo alternative is a Wolters Kluwer content subscription plus a general assistant. Choose Libra over that when answers need to cite commentary you can open and check.

## Watch-outs

- **The knowledge graph has not shipped.** Wolters Kluwer announced on 10 September 2026 that laws, rulings and commentary will be linked into a graph the AI reasons over, with early access for selected customers "in the coming months" and a phased rollout by market through Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. Guard: evaluate the product as it exists today, and if graph-backed research is a buying reason, write the availability date for your market into the order form.
- **The quality claims are the vendor's own.** Wolters Kluwer says internal testing shows better-sourced answers than general-purpose models; we found no independent benchmark that includes Libra. Guard: run the trial on live matters, score the output against an associate's work, and open every cited source.
- **The trial understates the product.** Content queries are capped at three a day and the trial never converts to a paid plan. Guard: ask for a quote that includes the content line for your jurisdiction and practice areas, and for trial access with uncapped content before you judge research quality.
- **The integration ceiling is low.** No API or MCP server, and iManage is still pending. Guard: if your DMS is iManage, get the connector date in writing before signing, and keep a separate path for any agent work you plan to build.
