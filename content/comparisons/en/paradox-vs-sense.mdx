The first fork in this decision is no longer conversational AI quality. It is ownership. [Workday](/en/tools/workday/) closed its roughly $1B all-cash acquisition of [Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/) on October 1, 2025, and on January 8, 2026 Paradox Conversational ATS became available through Workday, sold on Workday's own price book. Olivia still exists, but on Workday's site the capability is named Candidate Experience Agent from Workday Paradox. [Sense](/en/tools/sense/) is the last independent vendor of comparable scope in this category, still on $90M raised across four rounds including a SoftBank Vision Fund 2 Series D, and it started publishing per-package starting prices in 2026 while Paradox continues to quote everything. Decide the ownership question first; the feature comparison below only matters after you have.

## Where Paradox wins

- **Frontline apply-and-schedule at volume.** Candidates apply through a two-minute chat or text. Paradox reports a 72% average application completion rate, 30M+ interviews scheduled annually, and an average time-to-hire of 3.5 days across its customer base. These are vendor-reported, so treat them as a ceiling — but the reference list behind them is real: 7-Eleven, Ace Hardware, Valvoline, Chipotle, Marriott.
- **It replaces the ATS, not just the chat layer.** Paradox Conversational ATS runs req-to-hire for hourly roles as the system of record. Sense sits on top of your ATS and engages candidates in it. For a retailer whose legacy ATS is itself the drop-off problem, that architectural difference is the whole business case.
- **First-party path for Workday Recruiting shops.** If you already run Workday Recruiting, the Candidate Experience Agent arrives as a first-party agent: one vendor, one contract, no integration project to scope. No other conversational hiring product has that position, and it will not be available to anyone else.
- **Scheduling depth.** Multi-shift, multi-location manager-calendar scheduling with reschedules, reminders, and no-show chasing. Sense schedules competently; Paradox schedules 30M interviews a year.

## Where Sense wins

- **Redeployment is a business case Paradox does not have.** Auto-nurture against past placements and silver medalists converts an existing database into fillable supply. For a staffing desk, the gross margin on a handful of redeployed contractors covers the annual contract — a payback story no frontline screening tool tells.
- **[Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/)-native, with bidirectional sync.** For staffing firms on Bullhorn, this integration depth — not the AI — is why Sense makes the shortlist.
- **Published floors you can plan and negotiate against.** Six packages with printed starting prices. You walk into the first call knowing the number.
- **Agents sold as their own SKU.** Sense AI Recruiter — Recruiting Orchestration, Messaging, Scheduling, Screening, Candidate Matching, Candidate Evaluation, and Onboarding agents — is buyable from $3,000/month without first buying the engagement platform. Paradox has no equivalent unbundling.
- **HCM neutrality.** Sense has no parent steering it toward one suite. If your HCM strategy is unsettled, or deliberately multi-vendor, that is worth real money at renewal.

## Pricing reality

Sense publishes, Paradox does not — and that asymmetry is itself the finding.

Sense's per-month starting prices, verified on its pricing page: Platform for Talent Acquisition from $7,000; Platform for Staffing Teams and Platform for Enterprise Staffing from $2,000; AI Recruiter from $3,000; standalone SMS/WhatsApp Messaging and standalone Interview Scheduling from $500 each. Annualized, that is $84,000 for corporate TA, $24,000 for a staffing desk, and $6,000 for a single module. Every package includes a dedicated CSM, an implementation manager, three months of hypercare, and 24/7 support.

Paradox quotes by deployment size, module mix, and integration depth. Third-party buyer reports put small deployments around $1,000–2,000/month, mid-market at $25K–60K/year, and large enterprise at $75K–150K+/year, with implementation billed separately at roughly $15K–35K.

Run the first-year math and the gap is structural, not marginal. A mid-market Paradox deployment lands at $40K–95K in year one once implementation is added. A Sense staffing desk lands at $24K all-in, because the implementation manager is in the package. Sense's corporate TA floor of $84K/year is 1.4–3.4× Paradox's mid-market band, so at the corporate end Paradox is usually the cheaper software — but only after you have won a quote you cannot benchmark. And nothing in Paradox's line card competes with a $6,000/year single module: if your budget is under five figures, this comparison has exactly one participant.

## Implementation effort

Paradox is an 8–16 week project plus change management, because the value depends on hiring managers actually accepting self-scheduling. Under Workday ownership that number now forks. On Workday Recruiting, the integration work largely collapses. On iCIMS, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle, or [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), Paradox still deploys standalone — but you are carrying an integration its new owner has no commercial reason to deepen, and that is the risk to price into a multi-year term.

Sense is modular by construction. Buy one $500/month module, prove the scheduling or messaging lift on one desk, then expand. The staged path is why Sense loses fewer pilots: there is less to be wrong about at the start.

## Verdict

- **Pick Paradox** if hiring is hourly and frontline at 5,000+ hires a year, funnel drop-off is the main cost, and either you run Workday Recruiting today or you accept a roadmap steered by Workday. The volume references and the ATS-replacement architecture are worth the quote-only pricing at that scale.
- **Pick Sense** if you are a staffing or RPO firm on Bullhorn where redeployment carries the case, if you are corporate TA that wants to stay HCM-neutral, if you want agents without the platform, or if you need a published floor to negotiate against instead of a blind first quote.
- **Pick neither** if you hire fewer than 1,000 people a year, or if you hire knowledge workers. Below that volume this is a native-ATS scheduling question, not a platform purchase. Bullhorn shipped Amplify Digital Workers in May 2026 — Prospect, Verify, Audit, and Transcribe skills plus Amplify Chat — so if you are on Bullhorn, price Sense against what Amplify already includes before signing anything. For knowledge-worker hiring, interview intelligence ([Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/), [BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/)) or a sourcing CRM ([Gem](/en/tools/gem/)) fits the problem better than either of these.
- **If you cannot decide between those conditions, pick Sense.** Its floors are published, its entry point is $6,000/year rather than a six-figure first year, and it has no owner pulling its roadmap toward a suite. Switch to Paradox when frontline volume clears roughly 5,000 hires a year, or the day you standardize on Workday Recruiting — both are legible triggers you will see coming.

The mistake to avoid is still choosing on chatbot quality. Both handle a screening conversation. In 2026 the differentiation is ownership and packaging: Paradox is now a Workday product with a Workday roadmap, and Sense is an independent vendor that will tell you its price before you take the call.
