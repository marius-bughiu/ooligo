## What it is

Casefleet is cloud fact-management software for litigators: the case lives as a structured record of facts, issues, witnesses and source documents, and every fact carries a date and a citation to the exact page it came from. Out of that record come the chronology, the case outline, the witness lists and the reports a trial team works from. Its closest analogue is LexisNexis CaseMap, the desktop tool that defined the category, which LexisNexis has replaced with the cloud product CaseMap+ AI. Casefleet also handles document review, OCR, transcription of audio and video, a deposition transcript viewer that turns highlights into pin cites, and, added in 2026, document production. It is not an [eDiscovery](/en/learn/ediscovery/) platform for terabyte-scale productions, and founder Jeff Kerr has said he is not chasing those cases.

Kerr, a former employment litigator, taught himself to code and left practice in 2015 to build Casefleet. The company is based in Atlanta, has been profitable since 2018 and is largely bootstrapped (LawNext, September 2026).

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **The chronology builds itself, with cites.** Since June 22, 2026, Advanced AI accounts get agentic fact extraction: from the attorney's first instruction, the agent reads the case documents and proposes facts, each with a date, a description, a page-level citation, and links to witnesses, parties and issues. The attorney accepts, edits or discards. Kerr's pitch is that the lawyer starts at the review stage instead of data entry.
- **Casey proposes, you approve.** The assistant, branded Casey since July 2026, plans a multi-step task as a checklist, then proposes facts, issues with supporting and contrary evidence, tasks, source tags and renames, and drafts of letters, motions and discovery. Every change to case data waits for approval, its citation engine rejects any cite that does not match the source text, and it will not send email or file anything.
- **Retrieval stays inside the case.** Documents are indexed per firm and per case, and Casey does not pull from outside the matter. For a litigator worried about one client's record leaking into another's answer, that is the right default.
- **The price is printed.** CaseMap+ AI and Opus 2 are quote-only; Casefleet publishes a per-user price, a 14-day trial with no card, and 30,000 AI credits for trial accounts.

## Pricing reality

The pricing page, checked 2026-11-24, lists two plans:

- **Advanced AI:** $140 per user per month billed annually, $180 month to month. It includes OCR, transcription and Document Intelligence (summaries, entity recognition, semantic indexing) with no separate processing charge since September 29, 2026, plus 100,000 AI credits per user per month, then $0.001 per credit.
- **Enterprise:** custom quote for firms of 20 or more users, adding SSO, data migration, custom privacy terms and priority support.

What teams actually pay depends on two things the headline hides. Storage is 5 GB per account, not per user, then $10 per GB per month, so a single 30 GB matter adds $250 a month. And PricingSaaS logged the Advanced AI price rising from $75 to $140 per user in June 2026, so anyone quoted from an older review is working from stale numbers. A five-lawyer, two-paralegal team pays $11,760 a year on annual billing before storage. Casefleet's own FAQ still mentions a $30 Starter plan; the pricing page does not sell one, so ask before you budget for it.

## Best for

Litigation partners and paralegals in firms of roughly 3 to 20 users on document-heavy employment, commercial or personal-injury cases, where someone now builds the chronology by hand in a spreadsheet or Word table and wants an agent to draft it with page cites.

**Not for:** firms that want practice management with billing and trust accounting in the same tool, or teams running six-figure-document productions.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **LexisNexis CaseMap+ AI** — the category incumbent. Pick it when your firm already licenses Lexis and has years of CaseMap files to import; existing cases move over with facts, issues and document links.
- **[Opus 2](/en/tools/opus-2/)** — the large-firm and arbitration choice. Pick it when the case ends in a hearing that needs electronic bundles and real-time transcription.
- **[Mary Technology](/en/tools/mary-technology/)** — the fastest-growing AI-first entrant. Pick it when volume is uneven and you would rather pay per page ($0.13 to $0.30) than per seat.
- **[Syllo](/en/tools/syllo/)** or **[Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/)** — pick one when review and production volume, not the chronology, is the bottleneck.
- **[Clearbrief](/en/tools/clearbrief/)** — pick it when the need is checking that each sentence of a brief in Word is supported by the record.

If none fit, a shared spreadsheet with one row per fact, a date column and a mandatory Bates cite still beats an unstructured memo. Move to software when two people can no longer keep it consistent.

## Watch-outs

- **No published SOC 2 report or model provider.** The security page covers AWS hosting, encryption and optional 2FA, but names no audit and no AI subprocessor. Guard: send your security questionnaire, and get the subprocessor list and a no-training clause in writing before uploading client documents. A HIPAA BAA is available on annual plans for an extra fee.
- **Few integrations.** Lawyerist reports no Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or Zapier connection, and there is no public API or MCP server. Guard: keep Casefleet as the fact record beside your [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) or [Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/) system, and test a full case export during the trial.
- **Usage costs sit outside the seat.** Storage, credit overages and bulk uploads are billed separately, and daily upload limits apply. Guard: load your largest live matter during the trial, then set an overage cap in account settings.
- **A small, bootstrapped vendor.** Guard: negotiate a renewal price cap in the order form, given the June 2026 increase, and export each case at milestones.
