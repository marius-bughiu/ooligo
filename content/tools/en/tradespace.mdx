## What it is

Tradespace is an IP management platform built around one argument: most of the patent work a company pays outside counsel for by the hour can be done in-house with AI, and the rest can be bought at a flat fee. It covers the whole lifecycle in four modules — **Create** (invention harvesting and disclosure), **Protect** (drafting and prosecution), **Manage** (portfolio, docketing, and annuities), and **Commercialize** (licensing) — so the same record follows an idea from an engineer's Slack thread to a licensing deal.

CEO Alec Sorensen founded it in San Francisco after running the IP practice at the consultancy Avascent, with Kapil Israni as CTO. Its first market was the licensing end: university tech-transfer offices and government labs trying to sell underused patents. The drafting half arrived by acquisition. On 10 November 2025 Tradespace bought Paragon, an AI patent-drafting startup built by three Princeton computer science students, and made its CEO AbdurRahman Bhatti head of product for drafting. On 26 January 2026 it announced a $15M Series A led by AVP, with Eniac Ventures, Amplo VC, and Scrum Ventures following on from the $4.2M seed Eniac led in November 2023 — roughly $20M raised in total.

The layer that separates it from a pure software vendor is the **Tradespace Counsel Network**: 235+ vetted patent attorneys and agents across 30+ US metro areas (the Q2 2026 roster; marketing rounds it to 250+), who sign and file what the platform drafts at fixed prices.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It attacks the billable hour, not just drafting time.** Sorensen's line from the Series A release: the billable hour "acts as a tax on innovation." The product is shaped around that. A GC whose patent line item is 80% outside-counsel fees gets a platform and a price list, not a productivity tool that still routes every filing to the same firm.
- **Disclosure capture starts where engineers already talk.** Create surfaces candidate inventions from Slack, Gmail, and Notion and drafts disclosures from engineering artifacts. Tradespace claims 40% more disclosures from R&D teams. The effect you can check in a pilot is whether inventors fill in the form at all — most IP departments lose ideas at that step, not at drafting.
- **One record from disclosure to licence.** Manage pulls patent-office and law-firm correspondence into matters, proposes deadlines, and models annuity costs per asset. Commercialize runs licensee identification and outreach on the same portfolio data. Point tools like [Solve Intelligence](/en/tools/solve-intelligence/) and [DeepIP](/en/tools/deepip/) stop at the application.
- **Public-sector references.** Named customers include BAE Systems, Raytheon, MITRE, the Department of Defense, the Department of Energy, the FDA, Georgia Tech, Northeastern, Unity, and 8x8. The vendor reports 440K+ patents under management for 80+ organisations, including 75% of the top US research universities. Vendor figures, unaudited.

## Pricing reality

Tradespace is the rare IP vendor with prices on its website — for two of its three motions.

- **Self-serve drafting:** two applications free, then $149 per application, with no minimums or term commitment. That buys the drafting tool, not an attorney.
- **Managed filing through the Counsel Network:** $5,000 flat per non-provisional filing, $1,000 per provisional, $1,000 per office action response (a PCT filing is also $5,000), with a dedicated USPTO-registered practitioner and a one-week disclosure-to-filed target. The first patent is free. The pages do not say whether USPTO government fees are included — assume they are not.
- **Enterprise platform (Create, Manage, Commercialize):** quote only. The only published tiers — $500/month Essentials, $2,500 and $5,000 per technology per month for scouting and monetisation — sit in a LawNext directory entry last updated November 2023, before the drafting pivot. Do not budget on them.

For scale, Tradespace's own 2026 fee guide puts a boutique firm's hourly bill for drafting and filing a US non-provisional at $8,000–15,000 and BigLaw at $15,000–30,000+, with USPTO large-entity fees of roughly $1,820 on top. The same guide puts other flat-fee filing services at $3,000–8,000 — so $5,000 is mid-market for the flat-fee model, not a floor. The savings claim is real only against an hourly baseline. Model it on your own last ten invoices, not the vendor's table.

## Best for

In-house IP leads and GCs at US technology, defence, and life-science companies — and university tech-transfer offices — who file a steady 10–100 applications a year, want to pull disclosure capture and first drafts in-house, and would rather buy the remaining attorney work at a flat price than renegotiate hourly rates.

**Not for you if** your data must stay in the EU: all Tradespace servers sit in Azure's US Northern Virginia region. Not for you if you run a 10,000-asset global docket on [Anaqua](/en/tools/anaqua/) and need its depth in foreign annuities and trademark docketing. And not for you if your attorneys want the AI inside Microsoft Word: drafting runs in the Tradespace web app, and no Word add-in is documented.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Anaqua](/en/tools/anaqua/)** — the enterprise IPMS incumbent by market share. Pick it when the job is a large multi-jurisdiction docket and IP spend management, and AI drafting can be a bolt-on.
- **Clarivate (IPfolio, CPA Global)** — the other half of the incumbent pair. Pick IPfolio when you want a cloud IPMS that deploys in weeks and already buy Derwent data.
- **[DeepIP](/en/tools/deepip/)** — the fastest-growing drafting entrant, $40M raised across two rounds in twelve months, working as a Word add-in. Pick it when the attorneys will not leave Word and you keep your existing docketing system.
- **[Solve Intelligence](/en/tools/solve-intelligence/) or [Patlytics](/en/tools/patlytics/)** — deeper drafting and claim-charting products. Pick one when prosecution quality at volume is the bottleneck, or when EU processing is required (Solve).
- **Wellspring or Inteum** — the established tech-transfer systems. Pick one when you are a university office whose need is compliance and inventor-agreement tracking rather than drafting.

## Watch-outs

- **Managed filing puts a third-party firm on your matter.** The flat-fee attorney is a Counsel Network member, not a Tradespace employee. **Guard:** get the engagement letter from the filing firm itself, confirm who is counsel of record and whose malpractice cover applies, and check conflicts against your existing panel before the first filing.
- **The drafting engine is eleven months old inside Tradespace.** It came with Paragon in November 2025, and the post-deal target was 10,000 patents drafted on the platform within 18 months. **Guard:** use the two free drafts on inventions you have already filed through outside counsel and compare the claims side by side before any contract.
- **Hosting is US-only.** Azure in Northern Virginia, SOC 2 Type 2 maintained through [Vanta](/en/tools/vanta/), AES-256 at rest, a private model per customer, and no training on customer data without written consent. No ISO 27001 is listed, and export-controlled work needs its own review. **Guard:** route EU-origin and ITAR-sensitive inventions through a separate path until your security team signs off in writing.
- **Integrations are thinner than the lifecycle story.** An FAQ says APIs connect to CRM, CLM, and ERP systems, but there are no public API docs and no MCP server. **Guard:** make bulk export of dockets, deadlines, and documents a contract term, and dual-run your existing docket for one full annuity cycle before switching it off.
- **The headline numbers are the vendor's.** The 50% cut in outside-counsel spend, 40% more disclosures, and 20 hours saved per disclosure are Tradespace figures. **Guard:** baseline disclosures per quarter and cost per filed application before rollout, and remeasure at six months.
