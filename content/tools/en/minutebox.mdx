## What it is

MinuteBox is cloud [entity management](/en/learn/entity-management/) and digital minute-book software, built first for the law firms that keep corporate records for their clients and second for in-house legal departments that keep their own. Each entity's directors, officers, share capital, registered addresses, filings, and minute book live in one structured record, and the same record drives org charts, document generation, data rooms, and registry searches and filings. It was founded in Toronto in 2017 by Daniel Levine (CEO), Steven Pulver, Sean Bernstein, and Brian Hunt, and raised a 2022 seed round that LawSites puts at $4 million and LegalTech.ca at $5 million. Customers range from accounts with dozens of entities to one managing more than 60,000; named users include Torkin Manes, Wildeboer Dellelce, Fogler Rubinoff, and adidas.

The news that puts it on a shortlist now is the **MinuteBox AI agent**, launched on November 16, 2026 and reported as a LawSites exclusive. Levine's framing is that MinuteBox has been a system of record and the agent turns it into a system of work. In practice, a paralegal types a request in plain language and the agent reads the entity record, orders reports from government registries, drafts documents from the firm's own precedents, and writes corporate changes back to the record. Every change and every paid order or filing waits for a human to approve it. It is available in the US, the UK, and Canada.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **The agent works on structured data, not a document pile.** Asking for director consents to add a director and issue 100 common shares gets checked against the entity's actual share classes and people. In the LawSites demo, the agent flagged an ambiguous director name and a missing "common" share class instead of drafting around them, and caught an existing duplicate entity.
- **Precedents stay the firm's.** The agent drafts from a Word precedent you attach or store, and firms can add plain-language drafting instructions to each precedent. That keeps the house style of a corporate group intact, which generic legal drafting tools do not.
- **Registry work happens inside the record.** MinuteBox lists the Ontario Business Registry, Corporations Canada, and BC Online among its integrations and markets registry searches and electronic filings across Canadian and US jurisdictions. In the demo, the agent paused for approval before placing a paid Ontario registry order.
- **Each conversation is also a data room.** An "AI room" carries watermarks, expiry dates, PIN access, download controls, and notifications, and clients can upload into it. For a closing, that replaces the separate virtual data room many firms spin up per deal.
- **It plugs into the systems a corporate group already runs.** Named integrations include [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/), [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) Manage, OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, DocuSign, Acrobat Sign, and Intapp Walls for ethical walls.

## Pricing reality

MinuteBox publishes tiers and entity caps but no prices; every plan routes to sales. The caps are what you model against.

- **Law firms:** Starter up to 300 entities, Starter Plus up to 700, Small Enterprise up to 4,000, Enterprise above 4,000. Every law-firm tier includes **unlimited users**, which is the main commercial difference from Athennian. Data import and personalised training start at Starter Plus; the Compliance Module and SSO are Enterprise.
- **Corporate counsel:** Starter up to 50 entities with 2 write users, Starter Plus up to 400 with 3, Small Enterprise up to 1,000 with 5, Enterprise from 1,250 with 10. Here the write-user count is the binding constraint, not the entity count.
- **Third-party listing:** G2 shows a Standard plan at $5 per entity. MinuteBox does not publish it, so treat it as a starting point for negotiation, not a quote.
- **The AI agent is a per-seat add-on with no published price.** Levine told LawSites the monthly cost per user should land around one billable hour of a paralegal. If your paralegals bill $150 an hour, model $150 per agent seat per month. Bring-your-own-key is supported, so a firm already licensing a model from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google can connect it instead of buying tokens from MinuteBox.

For a reference point, Athennian publishes $25,000/year for 100 entities. MinuteBox's unlimited users favour a law firm where a dozen clerks touch the records; a per-entity platform aimed at in-house teams does not charge for that headcount either way. Get both quotes on the same entity count before you assume either is cheaper.

## Best for

Corporate and commercial law firms, and the clerks and paralegals in them, that maintain minute books for a book of client entities and want the agent to draft resolutions, run registry searches, and update records from one window. It fits best when much of the entity book is Canadian or the firm already lives in iManage or Clio. In-house teams with fewer than 50 entities and two people writing to the record also fit the Corporate Counsel Starter tier.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Athennian](/en/tools/athennian/)** — the Calgary-founded platform aimed at in-house corporate secretaries running 50-500 entities across many jurisdictions, with published pricing and UBO tracking at the entry tier. Pick it when you are an in-house team that needs structure charts and beneficial-ownership tracking more than a drafting agent.
- **[Diligent Entities](/en/tools/diligent-entities/)** — the volume incumbent. Pick it when board and governance workflows already run on Diligent and you want entities on the same contract.
- **[CSC Entity Management](/en/tools/csc-entity-management/)** — the other enterprise incumbent. Pick it when you want the registered-agent service, filing coverage, and software from one provider.
- **DiliTrust** — the fastest-growing challenger, combining entity management with CLM, board portal, and matter management in one platform. Pick it when entities are one of several legal-department modules you want from a single vendor.
- **[Discern](/en/tools/discern/)** — pick it when the real problem is US annual reports, franchise tax, and registered-agent coverage being filed on time, not minute books.
- **MinuteKeep** — pick it for a single Canadian corporation that needs a digital minute book at about $99 CAD a year, per its Capterra listing.

If none of these fit, keep the minute books in your document management system with a named owner per entity and a filing calendar, and revisit software when one person can no longer keep the register current.

## Watch-outs

- **The AI price is not public.** The agent is priced per seat, depends on how many tokens you buy, and has a bring-your-own-key option with features that vary by model. Guard: get the agent seat price, the token allowance, and the overage rate in the same order form as the base tier, and run your own model through a BYOK trial before you sign for MinuteBox tokens.
- **End-to-end filing was described, not demonstrated.** In the launch demo, the agent stopped at the paid registry order; Levine said it can carry a change through to filing and record the result. Guard: during the trial, push one real corporate change all the way to a filed registry document in your main jurisdiction and check what lands back on the record.
- **Registry depth outside Canada needs proof.** The named registry integrations are Canadian; US and UK coverage is described more generally. Guard: list your top five filing jurisdictions, such as Delaware or Companies House, and ask for a live search and filing in each before you buy.
- **There is no MCP server and no documented public API today.** Querying entity and director data from Claude or Copilot is not possible out of the box. Guard: if entity data must reach a warehouse or your own AI workspace, get the export path in writing; MinuteBox supports CSV, PDF, and JSON export. Background: [MCP server explained](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).
- **The review base is thin.** G2 shows 5.0 from 15 reviews, and one Trustpilot reviewer rated it 1 out of 5. Guard: ask for two reference customers your size, one of them a law firm that migrated from another entity platform, and ask what the concierge migration missed.
- **Corporate Counsel tiers cap write users.** Two write users at Starter breaks quickly once tax and finance want to edit records. Guard: count everyone who needs to write to the record, not just legal, before picking a tier.
