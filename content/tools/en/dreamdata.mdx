## What it is

Dreamdata is a B2B revenue attribution platform that joins ad spend, website sessions, marketing automation, CRM records, and sales activity into one account-level customer journey, then attributes pipeline and closed revenue back across that journey. It is based in Copenhagen with an office in New York, was founded in 2018, and sells itself as an **agentic B2B attribution platform** under the line "no black boxes, verifiable numbers". The AI works inside the attribution rules you define rather than inventing its own.

It is the second attribution vendor in the ooligo catalog and the warehouse-grade pole of the category. Where [HockeyStack](/en/tools/hockeystack/) puts a fleet of GTM agents on top of the attribution graph, Dreamdata's bet is that the graph itself is the product: a governed account-based model you can query in-app, pull into your own LLM client, export to your warehouse, and defend line by line to a CFO.

Dreamdata raised a $55M Series B led by PeakSpan Capital on 14 October 2025, with InReach Ventures, Angel Invest, Curiosity Venture Capital, and Crowberry Capital participating, for about $67M raised since 2018. Named customers include Finastra, Clio, Cognism, Oyster, Turing, Sendoso, Gorgias, ElevenLabs, and BlackLine. On G2 it holds 4.7 out of 5 from 271 reviews as of November 2026.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **The attribution model is inspectable, and that is the whole pitch.** Rule-based models ship out of the box (first-touch, last-touch, linear, U-shaped, W-shaped) alongside a data-driven model that derives credit from your own journeys instead of a fixed rule. W-shaped is the documented default for reporting because it weights the first touch, the lead-creation touch, and the opportunity-creation touch rather than spreading credit evenly. Custom models let you exclude specific session types from receiving credit or set a hierarchy of attribution groups with a linear fallback. When finance asks why a channel got credit, the answer is a rule you wrote.
- **Funnel definitions are yours.** Dreamdata calls a funnel goal a **Stage Model**, and you define the stages against your own CRM object states. This stops the classic attribution failure where the vendor's idea of "opportunity" and yours differ by one stage and every number is quietly wrong.
- **Dreamdata AI is three pieces with three different trust profiles.** The **Analytics Agent** answers plain-English questions in-app and shows the filters, model, and date range it used; Dreamdata charges no per-question fee or credits for it. The **MCP Server** is hosted by Dreamdata at `https://mcp.dreamdata.io/mcp`, so there is nothing to install: you sign in with your Dreamdata account over OAuth 2.1 and the assistant sees only what that account can see. It runs on the same semantic layer as the Analytics Agent and exposes tools to run and explore reports, find companies for audiences, read company journeys, and build reports and dashboards from scratch. Documented clients are Claude, Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code, ChatGPT, Lovable, Gumloop, Dust, Writer, and Langdock. The **Data Warehouse** add-on exports the account-based model to BigQuery, Snowflake, Amazon S3, or Azure Storage for agents you build yourself. That path has no semantic layer, and Dreamdata says plainly that it needs data engineering resources to run.
- **The free tier is real, not a trial.** $0 with no credit card gets B2B web analytics, cookie and cookieless tracking, company identification, engagement scoring, the audience builder, an ad spend report, B2B benchmarks, and Slack or Teams notifications. It is capped at 5 seats, 2 months of user history, 3 stage models, 2 notifications, and 1 sync, plus a monthly tracked-user limit. Dreamdata claims it identifies up to 80% of visiting companies; the free tier is where you test that claim on your own traffic before a procurement conversation.
- **Integration coverage is broad on the sources that matter for attribution.** Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics, and Pipedrive on CRM; Marketo, Pardot, Eloqua, and Salesforce Marketing Cloud on automation; LinkedIn, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Reddit on paid, plus G2, Capterra, and TrustRadius on review traffic; [6sense](/en/tools/6sense/), [Demandbase](/en/tools/demandbase/), [RollWorks](/en/tools/rollworks/), [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/), and Dealfront on intent; [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/), [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/), [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/), and [Chili Piper](/en/tools/chili-piper/) on sales.

## Pricing

As of 19 November 2026 the pricing page has two plans, not three. The paid Starter plan is gone: the "Starter" section, labelled "plan for smaller teams", now contains only **Free** at $0/month. Everything else sits in a single **Attribution & Activation** plan at custom pricing, with custom limits and volumes, a guided free trial, full onboarding, a dedicated CSM and technical manager, solutions consulting, and data science support. The plan includes AI and custom attribution models, revenue and content analytics, ROI and ROAS reporting, the Analytics Agent, and the MCP Server.

Three things are priced as add-ons on top of that plan: **Activation** (AI audience targeting, audience syncs to the major ad platforms, and sales notifications on engaged accounts), the **Data Warehouse** export, and **SCIM** provisioning. SSO and SAML are included, but SCIM is not. The page has no price for any add-on, and the free plan's monthly tracked-user ceiling is not published either.

Vendr's marketplace data, last updated February 2026, puts the median Dreamdata buyer at **$35,440 per year** with a range of **$27,000 to $96,000**. Tracked-account volume moves the number more than seats do: under 5,000 accounts lands around $15,000-$28,000, 5,000-20,000 accounts around $25,000-$50,000, and 20,000+ accounts at $50,000-$75,000 and up. Those figures predate the September 2026 Dreamdata AI launch and the new add-on packaging, so treat them as a floor for negotiation rather than a quote.

Against the alternatives: [HockeyStack](/en/tools/hockeystack/) has no free tier and lands in a similar annual range, so Dreamdata's difference is the $0 entry point rather than the median. The catch with the new layout is that there is no longer a mid-priced step: you go from $0 straight to a custom annual quote.

## Best for

RevOps and demand-gen leaders at $20M-$200M ARR B2B companies running paid, events, and outbound into multi-stakeholder buying groups, where the binding requirement is an attribution number finance will accept. It fits especially well when the team wants to ask that model questions from Claude or ChatGPT without building a data pipeline first, since the hosted MCP server needs no warehouse. It is also the right first pick for anyone who wants to validate account-level identification and journey stitching on real traffic before spending budget.

Pick something else when the shape is wrong. Choose **HockeyStack** if you are buying a prebuilt agent layer that acts on the model (forecast challenging, win/loss analysis, closed-lost resurrection) rather than a model you query yourself; it is the closest structural substitute. Choose **Adobe Marketo Measure** (formerly Bizible) if you are standardized on Adobe and Marketo and attribution can ride an existing enterprise agreement. Choose **Factors.ai** if you are under $10M ARR with a LinkedIn-led ABM motion and need a self-serve paid tier between free and a five-figure annual contract, which Dreamdata no longer offers. Choose **RevSure** if the question is forward-looking (will this quarter's pipeline convert) rather than backward-looking credit assignment.

## Watch-outs

- **The jump from Free to paid is now a cliff, and three add-ons sit on top.** With Starter gone, the first paid step is a custom quote, and Activation, Data Warehouse, and SCIM are each priced separately. Teams that need audience syncs to LinkedIn and Google discover late that Activation is not in the base plan. **Guard:** ask for the base plan and each add-on as separate line items in the first pricing conversation, get the tracked-account tier boundaries in writing, and benchmark against the Vendr $27,000-$96,000 band before you counter.
- **MCP write scopes can change your workspace and your ad audiences.** On sign-in the MCP server asks for "Read Dreamdata Resources" plus optional permission to save reports, audiences, and dashboards. An assistant that saves an audience feeds whatever ad platforms that audience syncs to. Plan availability is also unclear: the developer docs say the MCP server is on all plans, while the pricing page lists it only under Attribution & Activation. **Guard:** grant read-only by default, let the account owner enable MCP per user rather than for everyone, give write scopes only to Admin or Data Manager users, and get MCP availability on your plan confirmed in writing. Token costs for MCP and Data Warehouse agents are billed to you by your LLM provider, not by Dreamdata.
- **The Data Warehouse path has no semantic layer.** Your own agents query exported tables, guided only by schema documentation, so they can define "pipeline" differently from the in-app reports. **Guard:** keep board-facing and finance-facing numbers on the Analytics Agent or the MCP server, both of which use Dreamdata's semantic layer. Build a fixed set of 10-15 reference queries with known answers against the warehouse, and re-run them after every schema version change. Dreamdata already ships versioned destinations such as BigQuery V2 and Snowflake Schema V3.
- **Attribution output is capped by CRM hygiene, and no vendor fixes that for you.** Inconsistent opportunity stages, missing closed-won dates, and undisciplined UTMs produce a confident model of the wrong funnel. **Guard:** define your Stage Models against audited CRM stage definitions before the trial starts, and run 30 days in parallel with your existing reporting. If the two disagree, resolve the source data before either number reaches finance.
- **The free tier cannot judge attribution on a long sales cycle.** Two months of history is shorter than most B2B cycles, and the tracked-user cap is unpublished, so a traffic spike can end the free period without warning. **Guard:** use free to validate company identification rates and journey stitching against a list of accounts you already closed, watch your monthly tracked users from week one, and judge the attribution model itself only in the guided paid trial with a backfill.

For the deal-inspection and forecasting layer downstream of this, see [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) and [Clari](/en/tools/clari/), whose forecasting product now sits inside Salesloft after the two companies merged. For the intent sources upstream, see [6sense](/en/tools/6sense/), [Demandbase](/en/tools/demandbase/), and [Common Room](/en/tools/common-room/).
