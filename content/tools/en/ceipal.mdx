## What it is

Ceipal is a staffing platform sold per recruiter seat by Ceipal Corp., founded in 2015, headquartered in Rochester, New York, with R&D in Hyderabad and its main sales office in New York City — over 300 employees globally per the company's own About page. Four products sit on one data model: the ATS and recruiting CRM, Ceipal AI, Procurewise VMS, and Workforce Management, plus a healthcare line covering credentialing, compliance, and invoicing. The vendor claims 2,500+ clients, 200,000+ recruiters and hiring managers on the platform, and 200+ integrated job boards and portals. On Capterra it carries 4.6 stars across 1,264 reviews as of 16 September 2026.

The buyer this entry is written for is the IT or contract staffing firm asking whether Ceipal's VMS story justifies leaving [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/). That question turns on a distinction the marketing blurs, so settle it before you take the demo. Ceipal ships two different things with "VMS" in the name:

1. **VMS integration — sell side, inside the ATS.** Ceipal syncs bidirectionally with client VMS portals including SAP Fieldglass, Workday VNDLY, Medefis, iLabor360, and Works Health: requisitions land in the ATS as they post, and submission and status changes flow back to the portal in real time. Recruiters stop re-keying reqs and stop working jobs that were filled yesterday. Ceipal bundles 50+ job board and VMS portal integrations at no extra charge.
2. **Procurewise — buy side, a separate product.** A vendor management system for whoever is *managing* a supplier network: pre-qualify and digitally enroll vendors, assign tiers, run contracting through DocuSign, enforce priority rules on job distribution and candidate submissions, approve timesheets, expenses, invoices and payments, and run SOW and direct-sourcing programs against supplier rate cards.

If you are a staffing supplier placing contractors into other companies' VMSes, the part that pays for itself is the first one. Procurewise earns its keep only if you also sit on the other side of the table — an MSP desk, a prime vendor pushing overflow reqs to subs, or a firm consolidating its own sub-vendor bench. Buying Ceipal *for* the native VMS and then discovering that what you actually needed was the integration layer is the standard way this evaluation goes wrong.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **VMS portal breadth at the entry price.** Portal coverage is table-stakes infrastructure for any firm working Fortune 500 contract accounts, and it is normally what forces agencies up into enterprise-tier pricing. Ceipal puts it in the base product.
- **AI agents are in the platform, not behind a quote.** Recruiter Assistant ships four named agents under Ceipal Engage: Job Optimizer (rewrites the posting), Search and Tag (ranks the database by match score and triggers screening workflows or voice interviews), Candidate Relevancy Check (scores a profile against the req), and a Voice Agent for routine outreach. [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) puts its first AI tier — Pro — behind a quote and publishes no price for AI at all.
- **One system for the contract desk and the client desk.** The ATS, the sub-vendor network, and timesheet-to-invoice workflow share a record. A firm running both a direct placement desk and an MSP program buys one platform instead of stitching two.
- **Healthcare is a real line, not a checkbox.** Credentialing, compliance tracking and invoicing are built for travel nursing and allied desks, alongside Medefis and Works Health portal sync — a segment [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) and [Recruiterflow](/en/tools/recruiterflow/) do not serve.

## Pricing reality

Ceipal publishes no rate card. The [pricing page](https://www.ceipal.com/pricing/) checked on 16 September 2026 carries a demo booking form and nothing else, so every number below is triangulated from software directories rather than the vendor, and should be treated as an estimate you confirm in writing before signing.

Directory listings converge on **$24-25 per user per month** as the ATS entry point with a **five-seat minimum**, putting the realistic floor near **$120-125 per month** for a small desk. Advanced automation bundles are listed around **$48 per user per month**, roughly doubling the seat. Workforce Management, which adds onboarding and payroll, is quoted separately from about **$100 per month**. Capterra lists a free trial and no free version.

Against the segment, that entry point is the argument. [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) publishes $99 per user per month for Starter and $165 for Core, with third-party TCO trackers putting a ten-seat first year at $34,000-$46,100 all-in once training and separately licensed modules land. [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) publishes $149 and $199. [Recruit CRM](/en/tools/recruit-crm/) starts at $95. A ten-recruiter desk on Ceipal's estimated ATS seat lands near $3,000 a year before add-ons — roughly a quarter of Bullhorn's published Starter line and an order of magnitude under Bullhorn's realistic all-in.

The catch is that the quote-only model means the discount is not guaranteed to be yours. Ceipal prices per module, and ATS, Procurewise, and Workforce are three separate purchases.

## Watch-outs

- **The suite is separate products, priced separately.** "ATS + VMS + Workforce" in one sentence on the website is four line items on an order form. **Guard:** make the vendor put every module you were shown in the demo on the quote by name, with its own seat count and price, before you compare anything to your Bullhorn renewal.
- **Performance degrades on large datasets.** Reviewers consistently report lag against big candidate databases, frequent auto-logouts, and inconsistent results from boolean searches. **Guard:** load a real export of your candidate database into the trial — not the vendor's demo tenant — and time a boolean search and a bulk submission against it before you commit.
- **AI sits on a 2015-era ATS.** Recruiter Assistant is a capable layer, but the underlying application is ten years old and the click paths show it against [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) or [Recruiterflow](/en/tools/recruiterflow/). **Guard:** run a two-week trial on a live req with the recruiter who will use it daily, and score clicks-per-submission rather than the feature grid.
- **VMS portal sync is only as good as your specific portals.** Coverage is broad, but "50+ portals" is not "your client's portal." **Guard:** hand the vendor the list of the VMS portals your top five accounts actually run, and get the supported ones confirmed in writing — an unsupported portal means your recruiters keep re-keying reqs, which was the whole reason for the switch.

## Best for

Owners and staffing ops leads at IT, general, and healthcare contract staffing firms of roughly 5-150 recruiters who bill through client VMS portals and want portal sync plus AI agents inside the base license instead of at enterprise tier. It is the strongest case for a firm that is paying Bullhorn enterprise money largely for VMS integration and wants the same capability at a fraction of the seat cost, and for a prime vendor that needs Procurewise to govern its sub-vendor network.

**Not for you if** your back office is the reason you are buying — Bullhorn's Middle Office and pay-bill depth are real and Ceipal's Workforce line is younger and priced apart. Also not for you if you run a retained executive search desk, where [Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/) and [Recruiterflow](/en/tools/recruiterflow/) fit the workflow better and the VMS machinery is dead weight; or if you are an in-house corporate talent team, where [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) and [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) do structured interviewing and funnel analytics that no staffing-first platform matches.
