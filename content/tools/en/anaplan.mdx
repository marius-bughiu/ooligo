## What it is

Anaplan is an enterprise planning platform. Finance, supply chain, workforce and sales teams all build their plans in the same multidimensional modeling engine. For RevOps, the sales performance management (SPM) side covers territory and quota planning, go-to-market capacity planning, account segmentation and scoring, sales forecasting and sales incentives. Anaplan was founded in the UK in 2006, listed on the NYSE in 2018, and was taken private by Thoma Bravo in June 2022 for about $10.4 billion. It still operates as an independent company under CEO Charles Gottdiener, and it bought the retail-planning AI startup Syrup Tech in September 2025. SPM customers named on its site include LinkedIn, Marsh, PagerDuty, Pure Storage and AWS.

The question this page answers: **Anaplan, Varicent or Xactly — which one should own territory and quota planning at enterprise scale?**

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Quota lives in the same model as the finance plan.** This is the real differentiator. When finance cuts the bookings target or the workforce plan delays hiring, the capacity model, territories and quotas recalculate from the same numbers. No export, no reconciliation. SPM specialists hold territory and comp in one system, but the finance plan still sits somewhere else.
- **Analyst positioning.** Gartner brought back its Magic Quadrant for SPM in 2026, after five years of covering the market only in Market Guides. Anaplan was named a Leader (announced 2026-07-09) with the highest Ability to Execute of the evaluated vendors. Varicent, Xactly and CaptivateIQ are Leaders in the same report.
- **Scenario modeling at scale.** Territory carve-ups, coverage ratios and quota what-ifs run live on the model instead of as overnight batch jobs. That is the reason enterprise sales-finance teams put up with the build effort.
- **The AI is mostly for model builders.** CoModeler shipped in March 2026. It drafts lists, modules, line items and formulas from a plain-English or CSV spec, alongside Agent Studio and Custom Analyst for building your own agents. The role-based analyst agents announced in December 2025 answer questions over your models. CoModeler speeds up the build. It does not remove the need for one.

## Pricing

Anaplan publishes no prices. Contracts combine a platform fee, the applications you deploy, and licenses priced by user type: model builders cost the most, then power users, then read-only users. Vendr's buyer data (67 purchases, February 2026) shows a median annual contract of $114,608, with a range of $30,590 to $301,931. That median is about four times Varicent's $28,350 on the same dataset. Vendr's average negotiated saving is 10.44%, and it notes that first quotes tend to be inflated.

Two costs sit outside the license. According to CFO Shortlist's 2026 review, implementation through a systems integrator typically runs 1.5 to 3 times the license fee. The same review says the default annual price escalator is 10%, negotiable to 3 to 5%. Cap the escalator in the first order form, because your negotiating position weakens once the models are live.

## Best for

Sales-finance and RevOps leaders at enterprises where the sales plan has to reconcile with the corporate financial plan every cycle. The strongest fit is a company that already runs, or is about to run, finance or workforce planning on Anaplan, so the sales models sit next to them. It is also a fit where territory, quota and capacity planning is a formal annual process with CFO sign-off.

**Do not buy it** if the actual problem is paying commissions accurately. It is also the wrong buy if you have no budget for a certified model builder, or if sales planning is the only use case and finance will never join the platform.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Varicent](/en/tools/varicent/)** puts territory, quota and comp on one SPM data model and ranked first in all three use cases of Gartner's 2026 Critical Capabilities report. Pick Varicent when territory planning and payouts are the purchase and the finance plan can stay in another system.

**[Xactly](/en/tools/xactly/)** is the comp-first enterprise incumbent, with compensation benchmarking data. Pick Xactly when commission accuracy and plan design are the pain, not planning.

**[Everstage](/en/tools/everstage/)** is the fastest-growing entrant among the SPM specialists. Pick it when you need commissions live in weeks, not planning depth.

**Pigment** is the fastest-growing challenger in connected planning, with a more modern interface. Put it on the shortlist next to Anaplan when finance and sales planning are both new purchases and neither system is entrenched yet.

**[Fullcast](/en/tools/fullcast/)** is the pick when territory design and routing inside [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) are the problem. It costs a fraction of an Anaplan build.

If none fit, fix the process first. Written [territory design](/en/learn/territory-design/) rules and a [capacity model](/en/learn/capacity-planning/) in a spreadsheet will teach you more about your requirements than a platform RFP will.

## Watch-outs

- **Nothing works without a model builder.** Every model is a custom build, and G2 and CFO Shortlist reviewers both cite the steep learning curve and teams that never become self-sufficient. *Guard:* name an internal Anaplan-certified model builder (or pay for a partner retainer) before you sign. In the proof of concept, have that person change a territory rule without help from the vendor.
- **Incentive comp is the thinnest SPM module.** The March 2026 launch of 12 prebuilt applications included Sales Forecasting and nothing for incentives. The incentives page makes no mention of payroll integration or dispute workflows. *Guard:* if payouts matter, have a partner demo statements, disputes and payroll export on your plans. Otherwise run comp in Varicent, Xactly or CaptivateIQ and feed quotas from Anaplan.
- **The first year costs far more than the license.** With implementation at 1.5 to 3 times the license and complex rollouts of four months or more, the first-year budget is the one that gets rejected. *Guard:* get the integrator's fixed-scope statement of work before the final software negotiation, and phase the rollout so territory and quota go live before comp.
