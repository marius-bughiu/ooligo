## What it is

Certinia Customer Success Cloud (CS Cloud) is a customer success platform that runs inside your [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) org rather than beside it. Health scores, success plans, playbooks and CSM tasks live as Salesforce records next to the account, opportunity, case and services-project data, so there is no sync job between the CS tool and the CRM. Certinia is the renamed FinancialForce, best known for its Salesforce-native ERP and professional services automation (PSA); CS Cloud is the post-sale piece of that suite. The AI layer is Veda, which on the CS side ships a Customer Success Agent plus account summaries, health summaries and predictive churn and expansion signals. Veda drafts account briefs, success plans and business-review prep.

Certinia is owned by Haveli Investments (acquired 2023), with TA Associates holding a minority stake, and it is very much alive. In October 2026 it was placed as a Visionary in its first appearance in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Customer Success Management Platforms, where [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) and [Planhat](/en/tools/planhat/) were again named Leaders.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **No second system of record.** Every other mainstream CS platform keeps its own data store and syncs with Salesforce. CS Cloud's data is Salesforce data, so health scores show up in standard reports, flows and dashboards, and sales, support and finance see the same account record the CSM sees.
- **Services and success on one record.** If you already run Certinia PSA for onboarding and implementation projects, CS Cloud reads project status, resource assignments and time-to-value from the same org. That handoff from implementation to CSM is the weak joint in most CS stacks.
- **Salesforce's own vote.** The product page quotes Salesforce's President of Customer Success, Jim Roth, endorsing it as the only CS platform natively built on Salesforce. That is a vendor-selected quote, but it tells you where the product's center of gravity is.

## Pricing reality

Certinia publishes no CS Cloud prices; every deal is a quote, priced by user count and modules. The best independent anchor is Vendr's Certinia-wide data (page updated February 2026): a median of $74,402 per year across 177 purchases, ranging from $4,424 to $304,949, with buyers saving about 8% on average. That figure blends ERP and PSA deals, which dominate Certinia's book, so a CS-only deal for a 10-30 CSM team should land below the median. Vendr reports mid-market buyers negotiating 15-30% off initial quotes and multi-year terms coming in 10-20% lower.

The cost most buyers miss: every CS Cloud user also needs a Salesforce license, which Vendr puts at $25-$75 per user per month. If your CSMs already hold Sales Cloud or Service Cloud seats, that cost is sunk; if not, add it to the comparison before you put CS Cloud next to a Gainsight or ChurnZero quote.

## Best for

**Best for:** a VP of Customer Success or CS Ops lead at a Salesforce-standardized B2B company with roughly 10-100 CSMs, whose CSMs already live in Salesforce and who wants success plans, renewals and services delivery on the account record instead of in a synced tool. The ROI is strongest when you already own Certinia PSA, or when finance and sales leadership refuse to look at a CS health score that lives outside Salesforce.

**Not for:** HubSpot shops, product-led companies whose health signal is mostly product telemetry at high volume, or teams under about 10 CSMs without a Salesforce admin. For those, the org overhead outweighs the no-sync benefit.

## Versus the alternative

- **vs [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/).** The share leader and the safe enterprise default, with the deepest feature set and the largest admin ecosystem. Pick Gainsight when CS is big enough (50+ CSMs) to justify a dedicated platform team and you want the broadest feature set; pick Certinia when keeping CS data inside Salesforce matters more than feature breadth.
- **vs [Planhat](/en/tools/planhat/).** A Gartner Leader with a flexible custom data model and CRM/PSA modules of its own. Pick Planhat when you want CS to own its data model outside the CRM; pick Certinia when the CRM must stay the single source of truth.
- **vs [ChurnZero](/en/tools/churnzero/) or [Vitally](/en/tools/vitally/).** Faster to stand up and usually cheaper for mid-market teams, both syncing with Salesforce rather than running in it. Pick them when time-to-value under two months matters more than eliminating the sync.
- **vs [Velaris](/en/tools/velaris/).** The newer AI-native entrant, built around a copilot that drafts QBRs and success plans. Pick Velaris if AI depth is the deciding factor and Salesforce-native architecture isn't.
- **vs building on [Salesforce Service Cloud](/en/tools/salesforce-service-cloud/) and [Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/).** Viable below about 10 CSMs with a strong admin; past that, you end up rebuilding success plans, playbooks and health scoring that CS Cloud ships ready-made.

## Watch-outs

- **Salesforce seats change the math.** A CS Cloud quote that looks cheaper than Gainsight can end up more expensive once each CSM, CS ops analyst and occasional viewer needs a Salesforce license. **Guard:** list every user who needs access, mark which already hold a Salesforce seat, and ask for a total-cost quote that includes the license gap.
- **Product telemetry eats Salesforce storage.** Pushing raw usage events into Salesforce objects consumes data storage, which Salesforce bills at a premium, and slows reports. **Guard:** keep event-level usage in your warehouse and sync only daily or weekly aggregates (active users, feature adoption scores) into the health score inputs.
- **Thin independent evidence.** Gartner Peer Insights shows only a handful of CS Cloud reviews, and Certinia's churn-reduction claims (3-8% less churn, 2-6% more renewals and expansion) are vendor numbers. **Guard:** ask for two references running CS Cloud at your CSM count, ideally without Certinia PSA, and set a pilot metric (time spent on QBR prep, renewal forecast accuracy) before you sign.
- **Suite gravity.** Certinia's sales motion leans toward ERP and PSA bundles. **Guard:** get CS Cloud priced as its own line item so the renewal can't be held hostage to a bundle you didn't need.
- **Your Salesforce admin becomes your CS admin.** Configuration changes go through the same org governance, release cycle and sandbox process as the rest of Salesforce. **Guard:** budget admin time explicitly in the implementation plan, and agree with the Salesforce owner who approves CS-driven changes before go-live.
