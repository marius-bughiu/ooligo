Choose conversation intelligence by the work that follows the meeting. A recruiter needs interview evidence in a scorecard; a sales manager needs coaching material or deal context; an individual contributor can need only a reliable recap. These four products cover those different tasks. Their order is for reading, not a measured ranking.

For a notes-only starting point, evaluate Fathom Free. For structured hiring interviews, start with Metaview Notetaker. Put Gong and Chorus on the shortlist when the purchase is tied to a defined revenue-management process. Keep an existing recorder if it already meets your requirements: another transcript store is not an outcome.

## 1. Gong — revenue reviews with an operating owner

[Gong](/en/tools/gong/) is a candidate when managers will use conversation evidence in coaching and pipeline reviews. Its [Forecast documentation](https://www.gong.io/platform/revenue-forecasting-software) distinguishes Forecast Essentials, with deal/account inspection, from the fuller Forecast product, which adds forecast submissions and analytics. Ask which product your quote includes rather than assuming a recording license buys the whole workflow.

[Gong's pricing page](https://www.gong.io/pricing) describes per-user licenses and a platform fee related to the supported user count. It does not publish a universal dollar rate. Request the same seat roles, modules, contract term and support scope from every vendor you compare.

**Choose it for:** a team with someone responsible for connecting call evidence to a recurring deal-review or forecast decision. **First check:** can a manager locate a claim's underlying conversation and act on it inside the agreed review process? If the job is simply sending meeting recaps, begin with a narrower purchase. The [revenue-forecasting stack](/en/stacks/revenue-forecasting-stack/) shows where this kind of tool sits in a wider process; it is not proof of your account's integration readiness.

## 2. Chorus — evaluate alongside your ZoomInfo contract

[Chorus](/en/tools/chorus/) covers [customer-conversation analysis and CRM-connected deal context](https://www.zoominfo.com/products/chorus). It belongs in a revenue-team evaluation, especially when your organization already buys [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/) and can compare the incremental contract scope.

Existing ZoomInfo access does not establish that Chorus is included, nor that a combined quote is cheaper than Gong. We have not verified a public numeric Chorus rate. Ask for a written module and user breakdown, including what remains available if you remove another ZoomInfo product.

**Choose it for:** a team that wants to evaluate call analysis within its existing ZoomInfo relationship. **First check:** trace a conversation to the correct contact, account and opportunity in your CRM, then test manager access and sharing. Compare that result with Gong on the same review task; do not substitute a bundle discount for fit.

## 3. Fathom — start with notes, then check the paid scope

[Fathom](/en/tools/fathom/) is the default trial here for meeting recording and recaps. Its [pricing page](https://www.fathom.ai/pricing) lists a free plan with recording, transcription and summaries. Paid tiers add capabilities; Business explicitly lists CRM field sync, Deal View, coaching metrics and AI scorecards. Calling the entire product “only a recorder” would miss that distinction.

**Choose it for:** someone trying to reduce manual note preparation before committing to a wider revenue platform. **First check:** inspect speaker attribution, agreed actions, owners and dates on your own meeting sample. For team use, verify the exact CRM sync and administration limits of the selected tier; an individual free plan does not establish organization-wide suitability.

Do not buy Fathom plus a second recorder by default. Identify which users or workflow need the additional product, including the work required to manage duplicate recordings, access and storage.

## 4. Metaview — interview notes within a recruiting platform

[Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/) remains relevant to interviews even though it also sells Sourcing and Application Review. Its [Notetaker product](https://www.metaview.ai/interview-notes) documents candidate summaries and ATS scorecard sync. That is a different buying task from sales forecasting.

The [Notetaker pricing tab](https://www.metaview.ai/pricing?product=notes) lists Free with 25 calls/month and Pro at $60/user/month with unlimited calls. Sourcing has separate user/profile allowances; Application Review uses selected review volume. The [profile](/en/tools/metaview/) details those units. Notetaker is not quote-only, and a Sourcing purchase is not a prerequisite for evaluating it.

**Choose it for:** a hiring team that needs interview evidence in its debrief and ATS. **First check:** use a role rubric to compare generated notes with the actual interview. Look for omissions, unsupported conclusions and wrong candidate or scorecard destinations. A fluent summary does not validate a hiring decision.

## Compare the full task, not an isolated price

Define one approved meeting set and one acceptance checklist before the trial. For each product, record missing or incorrect facts, correction minutes, successful destination updates and the number of outputs a reviewer accepts. Keep uncertain statements visible. Test sharing permissions and your organization's recording/retention process before expanding access.

Budget subscriptions, platform fees, add-ons, setup and human review over the same period. Fathom Free is $0; two Metaview Notetaker Pro seats are an illustrative $120/month at list before tax or additions. Those amounts buy different tasks and do not establish relative value. Without comparable Gong and Chorus quotes, a universal price ranking would be invented.

## What is outside this shortlist

Otter, Avoma and other meeting assistants are outside this four-product comparison, not judged inferior. Include your current recorder as the baseline. The [conversation-intelligence guide](/en/learn/conversation-intelligence/) explains the category if you have not yet separated recording, coaching and revenue analysis.

Revisit the choice when your required output, user access or integration changes. Upgrade only when a trial demonstrates the missing capability and someone will own it. **Evidence status:** official sources checked November 2, 2026; no hands-on comparative benchmark, adoption study or measured sales/hiring outcome.
