## What it is

ConverzAI sells a "Virtual Recruiter": an AI agent that works a staffing firm's open requisitions the way a junior recruiter would. It finds candidates in the ATS database and on new applications, calls, texts, and emails them, runs a voice screening conversation, scores fit against the job, schedules the next step, and writes notes, statuses, and submittals back to the ATS. Voice is the center of the product. The calls are full conversations, not IVR menus, and a competitor's teardown puts typical screens at 6 to 21 minutes.

The company is based in Redmond, Washington, founded and run by CEO Ashwarya Poddar. It raised a $5M seed from Foundation Capital and Afore Capital, then a $16M Series A in February 2025 led by Menlo Ventures with Left Lane Capital. In April 2026 it was named to The Agentic List 2026, curated by the AI Agent Conference. Named customers are almost all staffing firms: Apex Systems (250,000+ candidate conversations at the Series A announcement), TalentBridge, TalentBurst, Integrity Staffing, SSI People, Hoffmann, and Malone, which the vendor credits with 262 placements in three months.

ConverzAI is **not** an ATS, a job-ad distributor, or an assessment suite. It sits on top of the system of record you already run and does the outreach-and-screen labor between "req opened" and "candidate submitted."

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Built for the staffing desk, not corporate TA.** The integration list reads like a staffing-ATS census: [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/), Avionté, [Ceipal](/en/tools/ceipal/), [JobDiva](/en/tools/jobdiva/), Erecruit, LaborEdge, TargetRecruit, TempWorks, Tracker, and [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/)-based setups. Corporate-TA screeners usually start from Workday and Greenhouse and treat Bullhorn as an afterthought.
- **It works the database, not only the inbound queue.** Most AI interviewers wait for an applicant. ConverzAI also re-engages people already sitting in your ATS, which is where a staffing firm's cheapest placements come from.
- **Speed-to-candidate is the metric it moves.** A recruiter carrying 40 reqs cannot call every applicant inside an hour. An agent can, at 9 p.m. and on Sunday. The vendor's case studies are all speed claims: TalentBurst screening 5,000 applicants in three days, TalentBridge 60,000 in 90 days.

## Pricing

- **No public price.** There is no pricing page and no rate on G2; every path routes to a demo.
- **Model:** ConverzAI is described as outcome-linked, paid against placements rather than recruiter seats, by a competitor's 2026 teardown (HeroHunt), which estimates contracts at low five figures to low six figures a year. We could not confirm the fee mechanics from the vendor. Treat both as estimates.
- **What to compare it against:** [Sense](/en/tools/sense/) publishes floors of $2,000/month for its staffing platform and $3,000/month for Sense AI Recruiter, so a staffing desk running both starts near $60K a year. Use that as the anchor in a ConverzAI negotiation.
- **Contract terms to watch:** if the fee is per placement, get the definition of "placement" in writing (start date, a minimum days-worked threshold, or submittal), and how a candidate already in your ATS before go-live is attributed.

## Best for

Commercial and light-industrial staffing firms, IT staffing desks, and RPOs that run Bullhorn, Avionté, Ceipal, or JobDiva, carry hundreds to thousands of open reqs, and lose candidates to slow first contact. The fit starts around 10 or more recruiters with a real backlog of unworked applicants and a database of past candidates worth redeploying.

**Do not buy ConverzAI if** you are a corporate TA team on Workday or Greenhouse (the integration depth and references point elsewhere), you place senior or executive candidates who expect a human first call, or you fill fewer than about 20 roles a month, where a recruiter's own phone time is cheaper than any enterprise contract.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/)**: now part of Workday and the market-share leader for hourly hiring. Pick it for corporate frontline hiring (retail, hospitality, logistics) where conversational apply and self-scheduling at enterprise scale matter more than staffing-ATS depth. ConverzAI wins on voice-first screening and staffing integrations.
- **[Sense](/en/tools/sense/)**: the other incumbent in staffing engagement, with a 1,000+ brand base and published floors. Pick it when you want SMS, WhatsApp, and redeployment campaigns as one platform and value price transparency. ConverzAI wins when the job is autonomous phone screening rather than messaging.
- **[HeyMilo](/en/tools/heymilo/)**: per-interview pricing and explainable rubric scores, also strong on Bullhorn, Avionté, and JobDiva. Pick it when you want a scored first-round interview and will keep sourcing and outreach in human hands.
- **[Apriora (Alex)](/en/tools/apriora/)**: the conversational interviewer with adaptive follow-ups and calendar coordination. Pick it for professional and technical roles where interview depth matters more than outreach volume.
- **[Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/) Amplify**: the ATS vendor's own AI on its Pro and Max tiers. Pick it when you would rather upgrade the ATS than add a vendor and can live with lighter voice screening.

If none fit because your volume is low, a recruiter with a call script, a scorecard field in Bullhorn, and an hour blocked daily for new applicants beats every product on this list. ## Watch-outs

- **Unsolicited calls and texts carry legal risk.** An agent that dials and texts candidates from your database at scale runs straight into TCPA consent rules in the US, and candidates who never opted in to SMS complain. G2 reviewers already report candidates confused or put off by automated first contact. *Guard:* audit SMS and call consent per record before go-live, restrict outreach to set hours in the candidate's time zone, and have the agent identify itself as AI in the first sentence.
- **You own the automated screening decision.** Fit scores that decide who gets submitted are an automated employment decision tool under [NYC Local Law 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/), and the EU AI Act puts hiring AI in its high-risk category. ConverzAI says it is third-party bias audited; that audit covers its model, not your use of it. *Guard:* run your own adverse-impact check by segment (the [screening score variance audit](/en/workflows/screening-score-variance-audit-skill/) is built for this) and keep a human on every reject.
- **Fit scores are blunt.** Reviewers call the fit-score accuracy uneven, and the agent loses context in long, multi-topic calls. *Guard:* during the pilot, have recruiters blind-score 50 screened candidates and compare against the agent before you let scores route anything automatically.
- **Outcome pricing hides the real unit cost.** Per-placement fees look cheap until you count placements your recruiters would have made anyway. *Guard:* measure a 60-day pre-launch baseline of placements per recruiter, and negotiate attribution so only agent-sourced or agent-screened placements count.
- **Vendor claims are vendor numbers.** "3x more placements" and "90% faster submissions" come from ConverzAI's own site, and G2 holds only 8 reviews (4.1 average). *Guard:* ask for two references on your ATS at your volume and call them without the vendor on the line.
