## What it is

Firstwork is an AI-agent layer that sits on top of the ATS, HRIS, payroll and screening systems a frontline employer already runs. It does not replace any of them. It works the gap between "offer accepted" and "first shift": collecting and checking documents, launching background checks and I-9 collection, booking training, and chasing workers who go quiet. Vardhan Kapoor (ex-Remitly, ex-Deliveroo) and Shubham Choudhary (ex-Director of Engineering at Deel) founded it in 2024, and it went through Y Combinator in the Summer 2024 batch.

The question it answers: **after the offer, how do we stop frontline hires stalling in paperwork, I-9 and background checks without adding coordinators?**

The product is a set of agents:

- **Document Agent** rejects blurry, expired or mismatched files at upload and checks IDs and licences against issuing sources.
- **Workflow Agent** is a no-code builder with event triggers. A candidate moved to "Approved" can automatically get a background check, I-9 collection and training invites.
- **Browser Agent** drives third-party portals with no API: DMV and MVR lookups, background-check and insurance portals, and training sites, where it completes modules and stores certificates. It keeps screenshots as evidence.
- **Compliance Agent and Recollection Agent** recheck credentials daily, nudge workers before documents expire, and block profiles that fall out of compliance.
- **AI Caller** calls and texts applicants, screens them against fixed criteria and books interviews into Google Calendar or Outlook.

Firstwork also ships a Netherlands product covering the Dutch employer ID-check duty (Verificatieplicht), right-to-work and pre-employment screening in one flow. It is one of the few US-born tools in this category with a European compliance module.

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks now

On October 21, 2026 Firstwork announced a $15M Series A led by Earlybird Venture Capital, with Legion VC, Deel Ventures, Gradient and Y Combinator participating. Total funding is $20M, including a $5M seed reported by Axios in February 2025. The money goes to engineering and expansion in the US and Europe, and coverage says the company is setting up a London head office.

Named customers are Deel (also an investor), Job&Talent, Relay, Wonolo and BGSF, across staffing, logistics, delivery, care, retail and light industrial work. The company says more than 150,000 frontline workers start work through it each month, that customers cut time per new hire by up to 90%, and that Wonolo saves about 4.5 hours per hire. These are all vendor claims. Firstwork has no G2 or Capterra reviews yet, and its website testimonials carry badge numbers that do not match the quotes beside them.

## Pricing reality

Firstwork publishes no price. Every page routes to a demo, and directory listings say "contact for pricing". Expect a sales-led annual contract with forward-deployed engineers doing the setup. That model rarely fits a buyer with fewer than a few hundred hires a year.

You can bound what it is worth from the vendor's own figure. If an onboarding coordinator costs $25-35 an hour fully loaded and Firstwork really saves 4.5 hours per hire, the ceiling is roughly $110-160 per hire before screening fees. A staffing firm starting 2,000 workers a month would pay up to about $2.6M-3.8M a year for that saving at full value, so any quote well under that has room to pay back. Background-check fees still go to your screener on top: [Checkr](/en/tools/checkr/) Basic is $29.99 a report. Ask for per-active-worker or per-onboarding pricing in writing, plus the implementation fee.

## Best for

**Operations or onboarding leads at staffing agencies, gig and logistics platforms, and care providers that start hundreds to thousands of hourly workers a month**, already have an ATS and a screener, and lose hires between offer and first shift because documents, checks and training run through portals and spreadsheets.

It is the wrong pick for an employer hiring under about 50 frontline workers a month, for anyone who wants applicant tracking itself, and for a buyer who needs a published rate card.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Fountain](/en/tools/fountain/)** is the high-volume ATS with onboarding built in. Pick it when you have no frontline ATS yet and want SMS screening, scheduling and onboarding in one system rather than a layer on top.

**[Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/)**, now part of Workday, runs conversational screening and scheduling and has added onboarding nudges. Pick it when you are a Workday shop and the bottleneck is before the offer, not after.

**[Workstream](/en/tools/workstream/)** carries a restaurant or retail hire from application through I-9, E-Verify and payroll. Pick it when you run hourly W-2 staff at fixed locations and want one vendor for hiring to payroll.

If none fit, start with the tools you already pay for. An ATS workflow rule can launch a [Checkr](/en/tools/checkr/) or [GoodHire](/en/tools/goodhire/) check on stage change, and the screener chases the candidate for consent. See [AI agent vs RPA](/en/learn/ai-agent-vs-rpa/) for when a portal bot is enough.

## Watch-outs

- **Every outcome number is self-reported.** The 90%, the tripled first-shift conversion and the 150,000 workers a month have no third-party check, and there are no public reviews. *Guard:* pilot one branch or client for 60 days and measure offer-to-first-shift time, drop-off between offer and start, and coordinator hours against a control branch.
- **Browser agents break when portals change.** Automating DMV, insurer and training portals without an API means a page redesign can stall a step quietly. *Guard:* require alerting on failed runs in the contract, a named support SLA, and a fallback queue your team can work by hand.
- **The agent touches regulated decisions.** Auto-blocking a worker or launching a background check carries FCRA, I-9 and, in the Netherlands, Verificatieplicht duties that stay with you. *Guard:* keep a person approving every block and every adverse step, export the audit log monthly, and confirm the SOC 2 report type, because the site only says "SOC 2 & GDPR-ready". Watch [candidate experience](/en/learn/candidate-experience/) at each nudge as well: frontline workers ignore automated texts quickly.
