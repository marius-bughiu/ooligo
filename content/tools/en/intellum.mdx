## What it is

Intellum is an enterprise learning platform that runs customer, partner, and employee education out of one instance: course delivery, certification, ecommerce for paid catalogs, and click-level learner analytics, with its own HTML5 authoring tool — Evolve — built in rather than bought as a separate SCORM tool. The company was founded in Atlanta in 2000, took a $25M minority growth investment from Guidepost Growth Equity in August 2023, and is still independently held. Its published customer list is weighted toward platform companies teaching people outside their own walls: Meta, Google, Amazon, Stripe, Mailchimp, Reddit, Pinterest, and Yelp.

The reason Intellum matters in a 2026 Customer Success stack is structural rather than featural. Northpass went to [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) in July 2023. Skilljar went to Gainsight in April 2025, carrying 400-plus customers including LinkedIn, Verizon, and Shopify. The two best-known customer-education pure plays now report to the same CS-platform vendor. If your education purchase is not supposed to also be a Gainsight purchase, the independent shortlist is two names long: Intellum and Thought Industries.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **One instance covers customers, partners, and employees.** Audiences split into separate catalog, registration, and authentication experiences with their own permission controls. Skilljar is built for external learners, so running internal enablement there means standing up a second system. For a company that trains a partner channel, its own CSMs, and its end users, Intellum collapses three procurements into one.
- **Certification is treated as a legal artifact, not a badge.** Intellum sells what it calls legally defensible certification with verifiable credentialing — the part that matters when a partner tier, a compliance obligation, or a reseller agreement turns on who passed what and when. SCORM and AICC content import natively.
- **Ecommerce is native.** One-time course purchases or time-based catalog subscriptions, with regional pricing and taxation handled in-platform. If customer education is meant to be a revenue line rather than a retention cost, that removes a payments-and-glue project from the rollout.
- **Tracking goes below the SCORM floor.** Intellum records click-level learner behavior rather than the completion-and-score model the standard defines, and exposes the underlying data through a Data Connector for real-time access. Reporting is also reachable in natural language through the Manager agent.
- **The AI is three scoped agents, not one chat box.** Creator drafts course structure from source material — Intellum claims up to 70% less development time, and Accenture is its published reference for that figure. Learner runs an in-course tutor and assessment review. Manager turns plain-language requests into filters and answers platform questions. Read the availability line carefully: as of Intellum's 17 March 2026 announcement, the newest four — AI Course Tutor, AI Assessment Review, AI Custom Filter, AI Knowledge Base Assistant — ship as opt-in through Intellum Labs, not as general availability.

## Pricing reality

Intellum publishes no prices and quotes against active learners, feature tier, and add-ons. Discard any aggregator page quoting $61 or $81 per month; that is a scraped mismatch with a different product, not this one.

Vendr's anonymized contract data puts the median annual contract at **$40,997**, across a range from $6,792 to $215,029, with bands by deployment size:

- Up to 5,000 learners — $30,000 to $60,000 per year in platform fees
- 5,000 to 25,000 learners — $60,000 to $150,000 per year
- 25,000 learners and above — $150,000 to $400,000 per year and up

Implementation adds $10,000 to $25,000 depending on complexity. The honest first-year number for a mid-market customer-education program is therefore **$45,000 to $85,000**, not the platform fee you will be quoted first.

Two things move that quote. Learner count is the meter, so a product with a large free tier prices like an enterprise deployment even when a small fraction of those accounts ever open a course — negotiate on learners who launched something, not on registered ones. And buyers who run a competing quote report landing 15% to 30% below the opening number, a band wide enough to justify pricing Thought Industries in parallel even after you have decided.

## Best for

A Customer Education or Customer Success leader at a platform company with 5,000 or more external learners who has to train customers *and* a partner channel *and* internal staff, needs certification that survives a contractual or compliance audit, and does not want the education system of record owned by their CS-platform vendor.

**Not for you if** the program is under roughly 1,000 learners or you are still proving the first business case. Against a $30,000 platform floor plus implementation, cost per learner does not clear, and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) guide content paired with a [Whatfix](/en/tools/whatfix/) or [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/) in-app guide track will answer more of the same questions for a fraction of it. Intellum is also the wrong pick when what you actually need is in-product onboarding rather than courses — that is a product-adoption purchase, and buying an LMS for it produces a course catalog nobody visits.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Skilljar by Gainsight** holds the largest installed base in customer education. Pick it when you already run Gainsight for CS and want course completions and certifications feeding health scores without integration work; buying Intellum instead means building that link yourself over the API and Pub/Sub events. Skilljar is also the faster route to a first academy when your scope is external-only and will stay that way.
- **Docebo** is the broadest enterprise learning platform of the set and the pick when employee learning is the dominant workload and customer education is the secondary one. Route on where the majority of your learners sit: if most of them are on your payroll, Docebo; if most of them are your customers and partners, Intellum.
- **Thought Industries** is the closest structural match — the other independent customer-education pure play. Route on audience breadth. Thought Industries is built around external customer and partner learning; Intellum's argument is one platform spanning external and internal. If you will never run employee training in the same system, Thought Industries' narrower scope is a feature rather than a gap, and it is the sharper competitive quote to put next to Intellum's.
- **WorkRamp** is the fastest-growing modern entrant in this segment. Pick it when you want a lighter admin surface and a faster first launch, and you are willing to trade Intellum's certification depth and native ecommerce to get there.

## Watch-outs

- **The admin surface is the consistent reviewer complaint.** Reviewers name too many clicks for routine administration, documentation that lags the product, and fixed elements that should be configurable — down to the email sent to a waitlisted learner. Intellum has shipped workflow improvements against this in the past year, but it remains the most repeated criticism. *Guard:* run the trial as an administrator, not as a learner. Build one certification path, one audience segment, and one recurring report end to end, and time each. If any of the three takes more than an hour, price a dedicated LMS administrator into the business case before you sign.
- **Native report visualization is thin.** Reviewers ask for it repeatedly, and the current answer is the Data Connector feeding something else. *Guard:* confirm in writing that the Data Connector is included at your tier rather than an add-on, and plan for your existing BI tool to own the dashboards your executives will actually look at.
- **The 2026 AI is Labs opt-in, not GA.** Course Tutor, Assessment Review, Custom Filter, and Knowledge Base Assistant were all Intellum Labs opt-ins as of 17 March 2026, and Intellum has published nothing on whether they stay included once they graduate. *Guard:* get the AI feature list, each item's GA status, and its price named in the order form. Do not sign a three-year term on a business case whose savings depend on Creator agent output you have not measured against your own source material.
- **Learner-count pricing punishes free-tier growth.** A self-serve product that adds 100,000 signups in a year re-prices the LMS at renewal whether or not those accounts ever took a course. *Guard:* define "active learner" in the contract as someone who launched a course during the term, and cap the annual uplift with a fixed percentage rather than a re-count.
