## What it is

Forma.ai is enterprise sales performance management software for incentive compensation, territories and quotas. It was founded in Toronto in 2016 and is led by founder and CEO Nabeil Alazzam. ACME Capital led its $45 million Series B in May 2022, and it bought SeaMonster, a Toronto activity-based incentives startup, in May 2024. It has offices in Singapore and India. Named customers include Autodesk, Stryker, WEX, Hootsuite, B. Braun, Solventum and Trustpilot. We found no acquisition or wind-down news from the past 12 months.

Three products share one data model: **Operator** runs incentive compensation, **Architect** handles territory and quota planning, and **Prophet** is an advisory service in which Forma's data scientists and strategy consultants model plans with your team.

The question this page answers: **CaptivateIQ wants your comp analyst to build the plan, and Xactly wants a six-figure implementation. Is there a vendor that runs the plan for you?** Forma is the closest thing in the category, with conditions.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **The vendor owns the data plumbing.** Forma's integrations page claims more than 200 pre-built integrations across CRM, ERP and HRIS, and says its team works with you continuously to keep feeds accurate and matched to its data model. Most rivals hand the admin a connector and a mapping screen. At Forma, onboarding is a one-time fee that covers setup, and reviewers describe a standing weekly service cadence after launch.
- **Plan logic is written in plain language.** Forma's AI Configurator takes a plan component described in natural language and compiles it into calculation logic. It is priced as its own line item, scaled by how much configuration power you need. This is what lets Forma say plan changes take hours instead of months.
- **It has run at enterprise scale.** Forma's Autodesk case study reports 2,500+ payees, 1,900 channel partners and more than 100 million transaction lines, consolidated from two legacy platforms and a home-grown system. Forma says it has processed more than $25 billion in commissions. Both are vendor figures.
- **Analysts rank it one tier below the leaders.** Forrester's Wave for SPM incentive compensation, Q1 2025, named Forma a Strong Performer, behind Leaders Varicent and CaptivateIQ. Forma announced no placement in Gartner's 2026 Magic Quadrant for SPM.

## Pricing reality

No prices are published. forma.ai/pricing, checked 2026-09-22, lists three components: a one-time onboarding and implementation fee, the CompOps platform priced by features selected, and the AI Configurator priced by configuration level. There is no free trial or self-serve plan.

We found no independent transaction data. Vendr's listing named "Forma" is a different company, a benefits platform, so its figures do not apply here. CFO Shortlist estimates Forma at Xactly and Varicent economics, commonly six figures a year plus implementation. For scale, Vendr's Xactly median is about $80,000 and its CaptivateIQ median about $36,000. Budget for $80,000 or more a year at a few hundred payees, and treat that as an estimate, not a quote. Third-party guides put the practical floor at around 100 payees.

## Best for

Heads of sales compensation and sales-finance leaders at enterprises with several hundred to several thousand payees, frequent mid-year plan changes, channel or partner payees, and no appetite for building an in-house comp-ops team. It fits best when the real constraint is analyst headcount, not software features, and when you also want territory and quota planning on the same data.

**Not for you** if you have fewer than about 100 payees, if your plans are a flat rate plus an accelerator, or if your admins want full control of plan logic without a vendor in the loop. You pay for the service either way.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Xactly](/en/tools/xactly/)** is the enterprise incumbent and a 2026 Gartner Leader. Pick it when you need pay benchmarking or commission-expense accounting under ASC 606 in the same system and have an admin team to run it.
- **[Varicent](/en/tools/varicent/)** is the other incumbent, a Leader in both Forrester and Gartner. Pick it when territory, quota and capacity planning are a bigger purchase than compensation and you want deeper native AI.
- **[CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/)** is the mid-market leader. Pick it when you have a comp analyst who wants to own the logic in a spreadsheet-shaped model, at roughly half the likely cost.
- **[Everstage](/en/tools/everstage/)** is the fastest-growing entrant. Pick it when time to first payout matters more than a managed service.
- **[Performio](/en/tools/performio/)** is the middle option. Pick it at 70 to a few hundred payees when data comes from ERP or TMS systems.
- **[QuotaPath](/en/tools/quotapath/)**: stay there under 100 payees on quota-attainment plans.

If none of these fit, the problem is likely the plan, not the tool. Start with [compensation plans](/en/learn/compensation-plans/).

## Watch-outs

- **You depend on Forma for changes.** Capterra reviewers cite limits on what admins can do independently, and CFO Shortlist lists reduced self-service control. *Guard:* in the proof of concept, have your own admin build one new plan component with the AI Configurator unassisted, and write the service hours and turnaround time for plan changes into the contract.
- **Onboarding the data is heavy.** G2 reviewers name a learning curve on Forma's data model, and third-party reviews note CSV-only imports without native Excel. *Guard:* send a full pay period of real source files before signing, and require Forma to own the mapping to its data model in the statement of work.
- **ASC 606 coverage is unclear.** Forma's product pages mention 606 automation; CFO Shortlist reports no ready-made commission-expense accounting module. *Guard:* have your controller walk through capitalization and amortization in a demo on your own data, or budget a separate tool.
- **No path for AI clients.** We found no MCP server or public API documentation, while Xactly and Performio ship MCP servers. *Guard:* if you plan to query comp data from Claude or another agent, get a written API scope and roadmap commitment before signing.
- **Lock-in and opaque pricing.** A proprietary data model plus quote-only pricing leaves you little bargaining power at renewal. *Guard:* negotiate a three-year cap on renewal uplift, and require a full export of plans, calculations and history in a documented format on exit.
