## What it is

Agentforce Contact Center is Salesforce's own contact center as a service (CCaaS): native voice, digital channels, IVR, call recording, transcription and routing, running inside [Salesforce Service Cloud](/en/tools/salesforce-service-cloud/) (now sold as Agentforce Service). Salesforce unveiled it at Enterprise Connect on 10 March 2026, for customers in the US and Canada. On 22 June 2026 it added Agentforce Contact Center WEM, a workforce and quality management layer that schedules and scores human and AI agents together.

The difference from what Salesforce sold before is the phone line. Service Cloud Voice either resold [Amazon Connect](/en/tools/amazon-connect/) or bridged a partner CCaaS such as [Five9](/en/tools/five9/) into the agent console. The new product runs its own telephony layer on Salesforce's Hyperforce infrastructure, bought from telecom wholesalers, so a call, its transcript and any [Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/) voice agent all live on the same Case and Contact records with no CTI connector in between.

The question it answers: **we run Service Cloud, so do we replace Five9, Genesys or Amazon Connect with Salesforce's own contact center, or keep the integration?**

## Where it fits

- **Mid-market voice and chat teams already on Service Cloud.** IVR is built in Flow, routing runs on Omni-Channel, and the supervisor console handles native calls. A team of 20 to 150 agents with mostly inbound voice, chat and SMS gets one vendor, one admin skill set and one data model.
- **AI agents that answer the phone.** Agentforce voice agents sit in the IVR flow and hand off to a human with the transcript, intent and sentiment attached. Without the integration layer, the handoff context does not depend on a partner connector staying in sync.
- **Teams leaving Open CTI.** Salesforce has put Open CTI in maintenance mode, closed to new Agentforce Service orgs and scheduled for retirement on 28 February 2028. A native option is now one of the two supported routes off it; Partner Contact Center is the other.

## Pricing reality

Published on salesforce.com and checked 17 November 2026, per user per month, annual contract:

- **Contact Center Voice: $75.** Native voice with unlimited platform voice minutes, sold only to Max edition customers, who already have digital channels.
- **Contact Center: $125.** Native voice, unlimited platform minutes, digital channels and AI enhancements.
- **Contact Center Plus: $250.** Adds workforce and quality management.
- **Workforce Management** and **Quality Management** are also sold alone at $50 each.

That is the add-on, not the bill. It sits on a Service seat: Core at $195, Advanced at $395 or Max at $550 per user per month, and Salesforce's own pages are inconsistent about whether older Enterprise and Unlimited editions still qualify. A 50-agent team on Core plus Contact Center lists at $320 per agent, or $192,000 a year; move to Contact Center Plus and it is $267,000. Max at $550 includes digital channels, Partner Contact Center and WFM/QM, so a Max shop adds native voice for $75 and lands at $625.

AI is metered separately. Agentforce bills Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits; third-party breakdowns of the rate card put a voice action at 30 credits (about $0.15) against 20 for a standard action, with $2 per conversation as the alternative meter. Messaging packs, bot sessions and conversation-insight hours are bought as extra usage. For comparison, Five9 Core lists at $159 per seat with a 50-seat minimum and Genesys Cloud CX 3 at $155, both before telecom; neither needs a $195 Salesforce seat underneath. Native voice is not listed in AUD or JPY. Bandwidth announced bring-your-own-carrier support and rollout to 25+ countries across Europe, the UK, Australia and New Zealand for autumn 2026; confirm your country is live before you budget for it.

## Best for

Service operations leaders at mid-market companies that already run Agentforce Service, with 20 to 150 mostly inbound voice and chat agents, who want AI agents and humans on one record and are willing to trade contact-center depth for one fewer vendor.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/)**: the share leader in cloud contact center. Pick it for 300+ agents, multi-country routing, outbound and mature WFM, and keep it on the Salesforce integration.
- **[NiCE CXone](/en/tools/nice-cxone/)**: the other enterprise incumbent. Pick it when WFM, QM and compliance recording in regulated industries decide the deal.
- **[Five9](/en/tools/five9/)**: pick it when outbound campaigns and a proven Salesforce connector matter more than a native data model.
- **[Amazon Connect](/en/tools/amazon-connect/)**: pick it, directly or through Salesforce's Partner Contact Center, when call volume is spiky and per-minute pricing beats a seat.
- **[Zoom Contact Center](/en/tools/zoom-contact-center/)**: the fastest-growing entrant, from $69 per agent. Pick it when you are not on Service Cloud, or already run Zoom Phone.

## Watch-outs

- **The gaps are real at launch scale.** Analysts at No Jitter and CX Foundation flagged no outbound dialer, no real-time agent assist and no third-party CRM support, and the WEM release lacks an employee app and time-off management. Salesforce agreed on 2 November 2026 to buy Cresta, which may close the agent-assist gap once the deal completes in early 2027. The guard: list your outbound, WFM and compliance requirements, run them as scripted tests in a pilot, and keep [Cresta](/en/tools/cresta/), [Verint](/en/tools/verint/) or your current CCaaS where a test fails.
- **Seat math hides the real comparison.** A $125 add-on looks cheaper than Five9 until the Service seat under it is counted, and AI is billed on Flex Credits on top. The guard: compare total cost per agent including the Service edition, Flex Credits for your projected AI-handled calls, and messaging packs, and ask for the minute and overage terms in writing.
- **Vendor concentration.** Phone lines, CRM, AI agents and WFM on one contract give Salesforce all the pricing power at renewal, and partner neutrality on the AppExchange is no longer a given. The guard: negotiate a renewal cap, seat-swap rights between the contact-center tiers, and number-porting assistance in the order form before you port a single number.
