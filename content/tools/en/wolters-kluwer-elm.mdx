## What it is

Wolters Kluwer ELM Solutions is the enterprise legal management business inside Wolters Kluwer Legal & Regulatory, and the incumbent that Brightflag, Onit, and Mitratech usually have to displace in large corporate legal departments. It is not one product. You buy one of two platforms and then add services on top:

- **Passport** — the configurable platform for large corporate legal departments and insurance claims defense teams: matter management, e-billing, a rules engine for outside counsel guidelines, budgets and accruals, a law-firm collaboration portal, and toolkits for building your own modules. It runs on-premise or as a dedicated hosted deployment. Named customers include Walmart, Nissan, United Airlines, and Marsh McLennan.
- **TyMetrix 360°** — the multi-tenant SaaS alternative, built for faster implementation: e-billing, matter management, Power BI analytics, and a mobile app for approving invoices. Named customers include Dell, PNC Bank, and Applied Materials.
- **LegalVIEW BillAnalyzer** — AI bill review delivered as a managed service. The model flags likely guideline violations and Wolters Kluwer's own reviewers confirm them and run the appeals with your firms.
- **LegalVIEW Analytics and DynamicInsights** — rate and spend benchmarks built from more than $230 billion of invoice data.

The lineage explains the two-platform split. Passport came from Datacert, which Wolters Kluwer bought in 2014 and merged with its own TyMetrix business to form ELM Solutions. Wolters Kluwer says more than 35,000 law firms and other legal service providers in more than 190 countries work through the two platforms.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **The benchmark set is the largest in the category.** $230 billion of invoice data sits behind LegalVIEW. LexisNexis CounselLink Insight, the nearest public comparison, cites $75 billion. When you need to tell a firm that its partner rates are out of market for a practice area and region, the size of the reference set decides whether the argument holds.
- **The firm network already exists.** At enterprise scale the real cost of e-billing is getting hundreds of firms to submit through your system. With 35,000 firms already onboarded and a dedicated onboarding team for firms, most of your panel is probably already there.
- **AI shipped onto both platforms in 2026.** On 30 July 2026 Wolters Kluwer added AI matter summaries to Passport, covering status, financials, recent activity, and open items, with references back to the underlying records. The feature launched on TyMetrix 360° first. On 15 September 2026 TyMetrix 360° gained an Outside Counsel Management capability that compares firms on senior-heavy staffing, staffing continuity, budget variance, billing-guideline compliance, and rates against market.
- **It covers claims litigation as well as corporate legal.** Both platforms have insurance claims-defense configurations. That puts it on carriers' shortlists, where a corporate-first tool like [Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/) does not fit.

Both platforms carry SOC 1 Type 2 and SOC 2 Type 2 audits and ISO 27001:2022. We found no first-party MCP server. TyMetrix 360° has had a Data API since 2021, and Passport ships a connector library, including SharePoint Online, plus ELM Companion for Office, which brings matter work into Outlook, Word, Excel, and PowerPoint.

## Pricing reality

Wolters Kluwer publishes no prices for either platform. Gartner's listing describes a subscription priced per user or per matter, plus implementation fees and support. No buyer-reported band for Passport or TyMetrix 360° exists. [Vendr's Wolters Kluwer data](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/wolters-kluwer) covers 47 purchases at a $10,612 median, but those deals are mostly tax, audit, and clinical products, not ELM. Do not anchor on it.

The usable anchors are the peers. Buyer-reported [Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/) contracts run $20,980 to $168,400 a year, and SpendHound puts the average Mitratech contract at about $145K. Our estimate, triangulated from those two: TyMetrix 360° for a large department lands in the upper half of the Brightflag band, and a Passport deployment with BillAnalyzer is a six-figure annual commitment before implementation. Get a quote before you budget on either figure.

Vendr's Wolters Kluwer negotiation notes do apply to this line: 10-20% off for multi-year terms, escalators that drift to 3-7% unless you cap them at 3%, and December as the quarter with the most room. BillAnalyzer's selling claim is savings of "up to 10%" of legal spend when paired with Passport. Treat that as the vendor's ceiling, not your base case.

## Best for

Legal ops leaders at Fortune 1000-scale companies and insurance carriers with high invoice volume, roughly $50M or more in annual outside counsel spend across a panel of hundreds of firms. Pick Passport if the legal department expects to configure its own processes and has admin capacity in-house. Pick TyMetrix 360° if it wants a hosted SaaS product with less to configure.

Not for: departments under about $10M of outside counsel spend, where implementation and admin outweigh what the benchmarks recover.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Thomson Reuters Legal Tracker](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-legal-tracker/)** — the in-house e-billing incumbent by installed base. Pick it when your firms already submit through it and you want to avoid re-onboarding them.
- **[Mitratech](/en/tools/mitratech/)** — the other enterprise anchor, with 14,000+ firms on Collaborati. Pick it when legal, GRC, and HR compliance belong under one vendor.
- **[Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/)** — same owner since June 2025, positioned on the mid-market. Pick it when AI review of every invoice line and spend-based pricing matter more than configurability.
- **[LexisNexis CounselLink](/en/tools/lexisnexis-counsellink/)** — pick it when you want managed bill review from attorney reviewers and a published annual rate-trends report, and can live with a smaller benchmark set.
- **[LawVu](/en/tools/lawvu/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in the segment. Pick it when an in-house matter workspace is the job and e-billing comes second.
- **[PERSUIT](/en/tools/persuit/)** — not a replacement. Add it when the savings come from sourcing work before the invoice exists.

Under $2M of outside counsel spend, skip the category: a spreadsheet plus AP covers it.

## Watch-outs

- **One owner now sells three legal spend products.** Passport, TyMetrix 360°, and Brightflag overlap, and Wolters Kluwer has published no roadmap for where they converge. Guard: get product-specific roadmap and support commitments in writing, price protection across two renewals, and a full machine-readable export of invoice and matter history.
- **Passport's configurability is an implementation and admin cost.** Toolkits and custom modules are why enterprises pick it, and each one is scope someone has to design, build, and maintain. The one Gartner Peer Insights review we found is titled "be prepared to wait". Guard: fixed-fee implementation with named milestones, and budget for an internal Passport admin. If no one will own configuration, buy TyMetrix 360°.
- **BillAnalyzer savings are vendor-reported.** "Up to 10%" is a ceiling, and the managed-review fee comes out of whatever is saved. Guard: baseline three months of approved spend and appeal outcomes before go-live, and measure savings net of the service fee at renewal.
- **The AI layer is new and closed.** Matter summaries and Outside Counsel Management shipped in July and September 2026. There is no first-party MCP server, so your legal team's AI assistant cannot query the data directly. Guard: demo both features on your own matters before signing, and confirm the Data API scope covers what you would pipe into [Claude for Legal](/en/tools/claude-for-legal/) or another assistant.
- **The firm network is also the lock-in.** Leaving means re-onboarding your whole panel onto another system. Guard: write LEDES and rate-history export into the master agreement on day one, not at the exit.
