## What it is

Microsoft Power BI is the BI layer of Microsoft Fabric: Power BI Desktop (a free Windows authoring app) builds semantic models and reports, and the Power BI service shares them, refreshes them and answers questions about them. The semantic model — tables, relationships and DAX measures — is where "pipeline", "gross revenue retention" or "active account" gets defined once. It connects to almost anything, including Salesforce, Dynamics 365, Snowflake, Databricks and BigQuery. Gartner named Microsoft a Leader in its 2026 Magic Quadrant for Analytics and BI Platforms, its 19th consecutive year.

The 2026 story is that the semantic model now feeds agents outside Power BI. At FabCon Europe in Barcelona (28 September to 1 October 2026), Microsoft made Fabric IQ generally available in Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat and Cowork, so users get answers grounded in Power BI semantic models without opening a report. It also announced GA for the Fabric IQ MCP server, which serves the same governed answers to any MCP client, and for Fabric data agents in Copilot Studio. Agentic app creation in Power BI Desktop — describe an app, Copilot builds it from a trusted model — was announced as a preview rolling out after the event. Inside Power BI, the report Copilot pane is GA; the standalone, full-screen Power BI agent and app-scoped Copilot are still in preview. A Remote MCP server (query) and an open-source Modeling MCP server (build and edit models) are both in preview.

## Why it shows up in RevOps and CS stacks

- **You probably already pay for it.** Power BI Pro is included in Microsoft 365 E5 and Office 365 E5. For an E5 shop, the question is not "which BI tool" but "what do we add on top".
- **One semantic model, many consumers.** A CS leader asking Copilot Chat "which renewals over $50k slipped this quarter?" gets the answer computed from the same DAX measure the QBR report uses. Data agents built in Copilot Studio can sit on the same model.
- **Ops data lives in Microsoft anyway.** Teams, SharePoint and Excel distribution are native, and Dynamics 365 Sales has first-party connectors. Salesforce connects through a native connector, though most teams land it in a warehouse first.

## Pricing reality

List prices, checked 28 October 2026: **Free** (Desktop authoring, no sharing), **Pro** $14 per user per month and **Premium Per User** $24 per user per month, both billed annually. Prices rose from $10 and $20 in April 2025. Users who already hold Pro or E5 can add PPU for $14.

Copilot is the catch. A Pro or PPU license alone does not unlock it — Microsoft's documentation requires a paid Fabric capacity (F2 or higher) or Premium P1. Copilot usage burns that capacity's compute units (CUs), billed on tokens. Microsoft lists F2 at $262.80 a month pay-as-you-go ($0.36 an hour) and F64 at $8,409.60; a one-year reservation cuts about 40.5%, to roughly $156 and $5,003 a month.

Real-world bands: an E5 company with 200 report users pays $0 more for reporting, plus about $1,900 a year for an F2 reservation to pilot Copilot. A non-E5 company pays 200 × $14 × 12 = $33,600 a year in Pro. Below F64, every viewer needs Pro; at F64 and above, free users can view shared content, so an F64 reservation (about $60,000 a year) beats per-seat Pro at roughly 360 viewers.

## Best for

RevOps and CS ops leaders at Microsoft 365 companies with 50 to 5,000 report consumers, where the data team already writes DAX and the goal is getting governed numbers into Copilot Chat and Teams rather than into another BI login.

Skip it if your company runs on Google Workspace and BigQuery, or if business users need to edit and write back to warehouse tables.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Tableau](/en/tools/tableau/)**, the other long-standing Gartner Leader and Power BI's closest rival by installed base, lists Creator at $75 per user per month. Pick it when trained analysts do heavy visual exploration and the company is on Salesforce. Pick Power BI on cost and Microsoft 365 reach.

**[Looker](/en/tools/looker/)** wins when you want a code-reviewed LookML semantic layer and you are on Google Cloud. Pick Power BI when the reports end up in Teams and Copilot.

**Omni** is the fastest-growing entrant — founded by former Looker executives, it raised a $120 million Series C at a $1.5 billion valuation in April 2026. Pick it for a Looker-style semantic layer with spreadsheet-speed self-serve, outside the Microsoft stack.

**[Sigma](/en/tools/sigma/)** wins when business users must write back to [Snowflake](/en/tools/snowflake/) or Databricks. **[Hex](/en/tools/hex/)** wins for exploratory SQL and Python notebooks. If none fit because your CRM data is not modeled anywhere yet, start with [data warehouse vs CDP](/en/learn/data-warehouse-vs-cdp/).

## Watch-outs

- **Pro does not include Copilot.** Teams buy E5, assume Copilot comes with it, and find the button missing. *Guard:* reserve one F2 capacity for a pilot workspace, watch Copilot CUs in the Fabric Capacity Metrics app for 30 days, then size up — or move Copilot billing to a dedicated Fabric Copilot Capacity so it cannot throttle refreshes.
- **Q&A retires at the end of December 2026.** Existing Q&A visuals stop working, and Microsoft points users to Copilot. *Guard:* inventory every report using Q&A now, rebuild those questions as Copilot verified answers, and follow Microsoft's "prepare your data for AI" steps before the switch.
- **AI access to semantic models is on by default.** Community write-ups of the FabCon controls report that models are exposed to Copilot Chat, data agents and remote MCP servers unless an admin turns access off. *Guard:* before enabling Fabric IQ in Copilot, use the per-model setting to opt in only the certified models that hold your official metrics.
- **Unprepared models give confident, wrong answers.** Microsoft's own docs warn that Copilot misreads models with unclear names and missing descriptions. *Guard:* rename columns in business language, add measure descriptions (Copilot can draft them), and test 20 real questions against known answers before rollout.
