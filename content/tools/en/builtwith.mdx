## What it is

BuiltWith is a technographic data company from Australia. It crawls public websites, fingerprints the code they load, and records which technologies each site runs: CMS, ecommerce platform, analytics, tag managers, chat widgets, ad pixels, payment providers, CDNs and so on. Its plans page says it tracks 128,510 technologies across 491.9 million domains, with history going back decades. These are vendor figures.

You use that data in three ways:

- **Lead lists.** Pick a technology ("every site running Shopify Plus" or "every site that dropped Marketo this year") and export the list with company, traffic, spend estimates and, where available, contact names, emails and titles.
- **Lookups and alerts.** Check one domain's stack, or get Slack alerts for net-new sites adopting a technology.
- **API and MCP.** The Domain API returns a site's technology profile for one credit per domain. In February 2026 BuiltWith added a hosted MCP server at `api.builtwith.com/mcp`, an `llms.txt` file and a WHOAMI endpoint that reports your key's quota, so Claude or your own agents can query it directly. In July 2026 it added an "Acquired and Funded Websites" view that lists sites tied to acquisition and funding announcements, with amounts, rounds and investors where known.

Single-site lookups stay free.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

The question this page answers: do you buy technographics from BuiltWith directly, or get them bundled inside HG Insights, ZoomInfo or Clay?

**BuiltWith sees what a website loads, and almost nothing else.** That is its strength and its limit. If the technology you sell against leaves a trace in a site's code (Shopify, Klaviyo, HubSpot forms, Intercom, Google Tag Manager, a payment widget), BuiltWith detects it, keeps the first-seen and last-seen dates, and lets you list every adopter. If your competitor is back-office software (an ERP, a data warehouse, an HR suite), it usually leaves no web trace, and BuiltWith will miss it. HG Insights and Sumble infer those from job posts, documents and other signals.

**So the rule is simple.** Selling to ecommerce brands, agencies or marketing teams, where the target stack is visible on the site: buy BuiltWith direct. Selling infrastructure or enterprise software: buy a provider that sees behind the website, and treat BuiltWith as a secondary filter.

## Pricing reality

BuiltWith publishes its prices. Monthly list prices on its plans page on November 14, 2026:

- **Basic: $295 a month.** Two technologies and two keywords to report on, one login, CRM integration. BuiltWith's knowledge base adds 2,000 domain uploads a month.
- **Pro: $495 a month.** Unlimited technologies, keywords and retail reports, one login, 20,000 upload credits a month.
- **Team: $995 a month.** Everything in Pro with unlimited logins and 1.2 million API credits a year.
- **Team Ultra: unpublished.** Unlimited lookups on a dedicated API endpoint and datasets up to 100 million records. Ask for a quote.

Yearly billing is discounted. BuiltWith's knowledge base quotes Team at $9,950 a year, about two months free. Monthly plans have a one-month minimum and cancel any time. There is no free trial; BuiltWith's own advice for a one-off list is to buy a month of Basic and cancel.

**What teams pay:** a solo seller or agency typically pays **$295 to $495 a month**. A sales team that wants shared logins and API access pays **about $10,000 to $12,000 a year** on Team. Compare that with HG Insights, where Vendr data puts the median contract near $90,000 a year.

## Best for

A **RevOps lead or founder-seller at a company that sells to ecommerce or website-led businesses** (a Shopify app, a marketing agency, a payments or reviews vendor) who needs a list of every site running, adding or dropping a named technology, pushed into HubSpot or Salesforce. The scoped case where BuiltWith wins outright: a weekly list of net-new Shopify Plus stores that just installed a competitor's app, routed to an SDR.

## Not for

Teams whose buyers' stack is invisible from the outside. Also skip it as a contact database. Reviewers consistently rate its contact data below its technology detection, and a G2 comparison page shows a contact-accuracy score of 6.0 out of 10. Plan to enrich emails and phones elsewhere.

## Versus the alternatives

- **ZoomInfo**: the market-share leader in B2B data. Its technographics are a filter inside a contact database. Pick ZoomInfo when verified contacts come first. Pick BuiltWith when the technology list is the product and you want history and first-seen dates. See [ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/).
- **HG Insights**: the technographic leader for enterprise IT. Pick HG when you need install counts, IT spend and back-office software. It costs roughly ten times more. See [HG Insights](/en/tools/hg-insights/).
- **Sumble**: the fastest-growing technographic entrant we cover. It infers stacks from job posts and other public signals, with a free plan and Pro at $99 per user per month. Pick Sumble for back-office and data tools BuiltWith cannot see. See [Sumble](/en/tools/sumble/).
- **Clay**: offers BuiltWith as a native enrichment ("Find Technology Stack", 2 Clay credits per row on Clay's managed account, or your own BuiltWith key on paid plans). Pick Clay when technographics are one step in a longer enrichment flow and you only need lookups on accounts you already have. Buy BuiltWith direct when you need lists of all adopters. See [Clay](/en/tools/clay/).

## Watch-outs

- **Detection lags real changes.** A company that removed a tool last month can still show it. **Guard:** filter on BuiltWith's last-detected date and drop anything not seen in the last 30 to 60 days before a displacement campaign.
- **A detected tag is not a paying customer.** A leftover script or a free tier counts the same as an enterprise contract. **Guard:** pair the technology filter with a spend or traffic threshold, and have reps confirm on the first call before you score the account as a competitor customer.
- **Basic is narrower than it looks.** Two technologies and one login suit a fixed list, not exploration. **Guard:** if you will test more than two technologies, start on Pro for a month and downgrade once your target list is set.
- **API credits are counted per domain.** **Guard:** run a WHOAMI call to check your quota before wiring the MCP server into an agent that loops over large account lists.
