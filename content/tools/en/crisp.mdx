## What it is

Crisp is a customer messaging suite for small and mid-sized teams: website chat widget, shared inbox for chat and email, omnichannel messaging (WhatsApp Business, Instagram DMs, SMS, Line, Viber), a knowledge base, a lightweight CRM, a ticketing portal and an AI agent called Hugo. Hugo answers from your help center, crawled web pages, Q&A pairs and files, can run "tools" against connected systems, and escalates to a person when it cannot resolve the conversation.

The company, Crisp IM SAS, was founded in 2015 and is based in Nantes, France. It is 100% bootstrapped, with no venture investors, and reports 600,000+ users and about 10,000 paying companies. That ownership is the reason it is on many shortlists this year: Intercom renamed itself Fin in May 2026, and Salesforce completed its acquisition of Fin on 2026-09-10. Teams that want an independent, low-cost alternative land on Crisp.

The product ships monthly. The April 2026 update added per-conversation AI budget caps, a Hugo MCP server that exposes Hugo's knowledge search to outside agents, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 as a selectable Hugo model. The Summer 2026 update (2026-09-03) added Hugo Observability, a per-conversation timeline of models used, tools and MCP calls, reasoning steps and errors, plus EU-hosted Gemini models and widget bot protection. Separately, a Crisp MCP server at `api.crisp.chat/mcp/` lets Claude or Mistral read and write conversations, contacts and knowledge-base articles through the REST API.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Seats are nearly free.** Pricing is per workspace, not per agent. Plus is $295 a month with 20 seats included, about $15 per seat, where per-seat helpdesks charge that or more for one agent before AI.
- **The AI meter is cheap.** Crisp's own billing guide estimates a full Hugo-handled conversation at $0.05 to $0.10 in credits. Fin bills about $0.99 per resolution. Even at a 50% resolution rate, Hugo's cost per resolved conversation lands near $0.10 to $0.20.
- **It is independent and EU-based.** For European teams wary of a vendor now inside Salesforce, a French, self-funded company with EU-hosted model options is a real procurement argument, not a slogan.

## Pricing

As published on 2026-11-26, all prices per workspace per month, unlimited conversations on every plan:

- **Free**: $0, 2 seats, chat widget and shared inbox, no AI credits.
- **Mini**: $45, 4 seats, $5 of AI credits (Crisp estimates about 90 automated conversations), shared email inbox, triggers, unlimited data retention. Hugo is included from this plan up.
- **Essentials**: $95, 10 seats, $25 of AI credits (about 450 conversations), WhatsApp, Instagram and SMS, workflow builder, knowledge base, analytics, routing rules.
- **Plus**: $295, 20+ seats, $75 of AI credits (about 1,350 conversations), ticketing portal, unlimited task automations, white label, advanced analytics.
- **Enterprise**: quote only; the only tier with a personalized SLA and dedicated onboarding.

Additional agents are listed at $10 a month each. Yearly billing is available in-app, but no yearly discount is published. A 14-day trial needs no card; nonprofits and students get 50% off; Essentials and Plus get 20% off three additional workspaces.

Hugo billing is token-based, not per message or per resolution. Cost depends on conversation length, knowledge retrieved and the model you select, and Crisp publishes no per-model rate. When included credits run out, Hugo stops answering and conversations go to humans unless you turn on Pay-As-You-Go, which takes a spending limit but has no published rate. Workflows (the rule-based bot) do not consume credits.

Real-world band: a 5-agent team that wants WhatsApp and Hugo lands on Essentials at $95 a month plus whatever Hugo overage it allows. A 15-agent team that needs ticketing and branding removed pays $295. Both are a fraction of a per-seat helpdesk with an AI add-on at the same headcount.

## Best for

Support leads and founders at SaaS and ecommerce businesses with 3 to 20 agents, one brand, website chat as the main channel, and a monthly conversation volume where per-seat pricing hurts more than per-conversation AI fees. It also fits EU teams that want an independent vendor. It is the wrong pick for multi-brand operations (each brand is its own workspace and its own bill), for teams that need a contractual SLA without an Enterprise contract, and for B2B support where tickets become engineering escalations with heavy Jira workflows.

## Watch-outs

- **Hugo goes silent when credits run out.** The default behavior is to stop answering and dump conversations on agents, which surfaces as a queue spike on the busiest day of the month. The guard: turn on Pay-As-You-Go with a hard billing limit at onboarding, set per-conversation budget caps, and alert on credit balance weekly.
- **The AI meter has no published rate card.** Token billing plus a choice of models means two teams with identical volume can pay very different amounts, and switching Hugo to a frontier model raises cost by an amount Crisp does not list. The guard: run two weeks on the automatic model, divide credits spent by conversations Hugo actually resolved, and only then test a premium model against that baseline.
- **Pricing is per workspace, and so are channel limits.** Each workspace connects one WhatsApp, one Instagram and one of each other social channel, and a second brand means a second subscription. The guard: count brands and phone numbers before you pick a plan, and price the multi-workspace discount into the quote.
- **Key features are gated to Plus.** Ticketing, white label and unlimited task automations (Hugo actions against third-party systems) start at $295. The MCP server appears in the plan comparison, but its per-plan availability was not legible on the pricing page, and third-party guides place it on Plus. The guard: list the five features you cannot launch without and confirm each against the plan in writing during the trial.
- **It is a small company.** Crisp's team page shows about 30 people, reviewers on G2 and Capterra flag uneven support responsiveness, and no SLA exists below Enterprise. The guard: test a support request during the trial and schedule regular conversation exports through the API.

## Alternatives, and when to pick them instead

[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) and [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) are the category's two largest vendors. Pick Zendesk when support spans email, voice and many queues and you need mature routing, workforce management and QA. Pick Intercom, now Fin inside Salesforce, when in-app messaging for software users is the main channel and you want an AI agent priced per resolution with a longer enterprise track record; budget five to ten times Hugo's per-resolution cost for it.

[Tidio](/en/tools/tidio/) is the closest like-for-like rival. Pick it when you want a contractual resolution guarantee (Premium) or Shopify-native product recommendations in the AI agent; pick Crisp when human-handled volume is high, because Tidio meters billable conversations while every Crisp plan includes unlimited conversations. Pick [Chatwoot](/en/tools/chatwoot/) when you want an open-source inbox you can self-host. Pick [Help Scout](/en/tools/help-scout/) or [Front](/en/tools/front/) when email, not chat, is the primary channel. [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) and [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) are the fastest-growing AI-agent entrants, but they sell six-figure enterprise deployments; they are not a Crisp substitute for a team on a three-figure monthly budget. For the broader shortlist, see [alternatives to Intercom](/en/vs/alternatives-to-intercom/).
