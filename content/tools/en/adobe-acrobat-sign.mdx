## What it is

Adobe Acrobat Sign is Adobe's e-signature service, formerly Adobe Sign and before that EchoSign. It ships two ways. Self-serve, signing is bundled into the Acrobat for teams plans: you buy a PDF editor and get send-for-signature with it. Sales-led, Adobe sells **Acrobat Sign Solutions**, the transaction-priced tier with the enterprise integrations, the API at volume, and the compliance configurations (Adobe lists HIPAA, FERPA, GLBA, eIDAS and FDA 21 CFR Part 11). DocuSign's own FY2026 10-K names Adobe as its primary global eSignature competitor, which is the cleanest statement of where Acrobat Sign sits: the default second quote in every CLM signing step.

This entry covers Acrobat Sign as the signature layer under a contract process. It is not a CLM. There is no repository workflow, obligation tracking or intake engine here; for that, see [what CLM actually covers](/en/learn/contract-lifecycle-management/).

## What changed in 2026

Adobe ships Acrobat Sign on a numbered release train, and 2026 was a year of workflow plumbing rather than AI:

- **v17.0 (production 2026-02-03).** Dynamic Participation removes a recipient from an in-flight agreement without cancelling and resending; completed, cancelled or expired agreements can be copied into a new draft; checkbox groups get validation rules; and fields auto-fill from verified identity data after a signer authenticates through a supported digital identity provider.
- **v17.1 (production 2026-05-05).** In-Person Signing lets an internal host run a signing session in a browser, logged in the audit trail. Bulk digital signatures let a signer select several agreements in "Waiting for you" and apply certificate-based signatures under one signing authorization.
- **v17.2 (production 2026-09-08).** Agreements can pause, for 1 to 30 days, when a recipient fails authentication, instead of being cancelled. The sender switches the authentication method and resumes, and completed signers do not sign again.
- **v17.2.1 (production 2026-10-06).** Inline text editing while authoring library templates, without re-uploading the file.

New features ship disabled by default; an account or group admin has to switch each one on.

## Pricing reality

Self-serve list prices, annual commitment billed monthly, per license:

- **Acrobat Standard for teams — $16.99.** Send, sign and track.
- **Acrobat Pro for teams — $23.99.** Adds bulk send, web forms, reusable templates and agreement branding.
- **Acrobat Studio for teams — $29.99.** Everything in Pro plus the Acrobat AI features.

The number that matters is not the seat price. Adobe's transaction-limit page states that user-licensed plans include **150 transactions per user per year** unless the contract says otherwise, and a transaction is each send to one or more recipients. Ten Pro seats is $2,879 a year at list and 1,500 sends. High-volume buyers move to Acrobat Sign Solutions, priced by transaction band (reseller SKUs run from 1–999 to 5,000–14,999 transactions a year) and quote-only. Vendr does not publish an Acrobat Sign-only benchmark; its Adobe-wide median of $63,796 a year across 731 purchases is mostly Creative Cloud and Acrobat, so do not use it to anchor a signature budget.

Against [DocuSign](/en/tools/docusign-iam/), whose eSignature Business Pro is $45/user/month, Acrobat Pro for teams is roughly half the seat price and includes a PDF editor most legal teams already pay for separately.

## Best for

A **legal-ops or contracts manager at a 50–2,000 employee company that already licenses Acrobat**, signing standard paper — NDAs, MSAs, order forms, offer letters — out of Microsoft 365, Salesforce or Workday, under about 150 sends per user per year. The scoped win: signature added to a license you already own, with no new vendor review.

## Not for

Teams buying the signature layer for its AI. Acrobat Sign has no first-party agents and no official MCP server; agent access today runs through third-party bridges such as Zapier's Acrobat Sign MCP. Also not for a company whose signature volume sits with a few senders — 150 transactions per seat punishes exactly that pattern.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[DocuSign IAM](/en/tools/docusign-iam/)** — the share leader, and the pick when you want signature and post-signature repository from one vendor, a published path into agreement management, and an official MCP server. Pick Acrobat Sign when signature is the whole job and Acrobat is already on the desk.
- **Dropbox Sign** — the third incumbent. Pick it when the company's files live in Dropbox and the requirement is a clean API for embedded signing in a product flow.
- **[PandaDoc](/en/tools/pandadoc/)** — pick it when the document is a sales proposal or quote with pricing tables and payment collection; Acrobat Sign has no quoting layer.
- **Native CLM signing** — [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) and [Juro](/en/tools/juro/) both sign inside the contract platform. Pick native signing when the CLM is already the system of record and a separate signature vendor is one more integration to maintain.

## Watch-outs

- **The 150-transaction allowance is per user per year.** A sales-ops team where three people send every order form burns through it in weeks. **Guard:** count last year's envelopes by sender before buying seats, and if any sender exceeds 150, price Acrobat Sign Solutions on transaction volume instead.
- **The embedded Power Automate experience leaves the Acrobat Sign web app with v18.0 in January 2027.** Existing flows keep running and move to management in [Microsoft Power Automate](/en/tools/microsoft-power-automate/) itself, where flows with premium connectors need Power Automate Premium at $15/user/month. **Guard:** before January 2027, inventory every Acrobat Sign flow, confirm owners can open it in Power Automate, and check each for premium connectors.
- **Features ship switched off.** A release note is not a capability in your tenant. **Guard:** have the account admin enable and test pause-on-authentication-failure, In-Person Signing and bulk digital signatures in sandbox before you promise them to the business.
- **Legacy screens are being retired on a rolling schedule.** Adobe's notices put the legacy signature-request screen and legacy reports on a 2027 removal path. **Guard:** rebuild any SOP screenshots and report exports against the new experiences now, not in the release week.
