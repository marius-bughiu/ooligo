## What it is

LawToolBox is court-rules deadline calculation and docketing that runs inside Microsoft 365 instead of beside it. You enter a triggering event, such as a trial date or a service date, pick the court, and it generates the rules-based deadlines that follow (its site says 80+ from a single trial date) and syncs them to Outlook calendars. When a rule or a trigger date changes, the synced deadlines update. Around that calculator it builds a matter workspace in Microsoft 365, a Teams team, SharePoint site, shared mailbox and OneNote per matter, so the matter lives where lawyers already work.

The company is based in Denver. It was founded in 1998 and says 8,000+ firms have used it. InfoTrack bought a majority stake in August 2020, and the Grow family still runs it as an autonomous unit. Carol Lynn Grow is co-CEO.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It is docketing without a separate docketing system.** Aderant's Milana and CompuLaw assume a central docketing department working in a dedicated application. LawToolBox assumes the lawyer or assistant never leaves Outlook. For a litigation group with no docketing staff, that is the difference between rules-based calendaring and a spreadsheet.
- **It turns Microsoft 365 into matter storage.** Documents, emails, deadlines and Teams chats sit in one container per matter, inside the firm's own tenant. Firms that standardized on Microsoft 365 but never bought a matter system get [matter management](/en/learn/matter-management/) without a migration.
- **Its MCP server exposes that matter data to any AI.** On August 24, 2026, during ILTACON, LawToolBox announced an MCP connector with 70+ tools. They cover matter lookups, deadlines and appointments, reports, SharePoint and Microsoft 365 Groups. Claude, Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT, Harvey, Legora, CoCounsel and Lexis AI can query a matter container directly, with ethical walls enforced by the container. A firm that wants [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) to answer "what is due on this matter in the next 30 days" gets it without building its own connector. See [what an MCP server is](/en/learn/mcp-server-explained/).
- **It plugs into the systems firms already run.** It has document integrations with [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/), [NetDocuments](/en/tools/netdocuments/), [Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/), ECFX and Centerbase. Practice-management connectors cover Actionstep, [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/), PracticePanther, Smokeball, LEAP and others, and an August 2025 partnership extended it across the ProfitSolv suite.

## Pricing reality

LawToolBox is one of the few docketing vendors that publishes a price. It requires a one-year minimum commitment, and the rate falls with volume:

- 2–9 users: $35/user/month billed annually ($42 monthly)
- 10–19 users: $33 ($40)
- 20–79 users: $23 ($30)
- 80+ users: $19 ($22)

The catch is what counts as a user. The pricing page says the rate applies to every Microsoft 365 licensed mailbox in the firm or legal department, not only the people who docket. On top of that, each iManage, NetDocuments, ECFX or InfoTrack integration adds $4/user, AI features cost extra, and training is billed separately. A 25-mailbox litigation boutique on iManage should budget about $8,100 a year before AI and onboarding (25 × ($23 + $4) × 12). The MCP server has no published price.

## Best for

Litigation managers and practice-support leads at firms of roughly 5 to 150 lawyers that run Microsoft 365, have no dedicated docketing department, and want court deadlines on lawyers' Outlook calendars without adding another application. It also fits government and corporate legal teams that run litigation in Microsoft 365 and want their AI tools grounded in matter data.

Skip it if you already run [Clio](/en/tools/clio/). Clio's court rules come from CalendarRules, which Clio acquired in 2021, and cover about 2,300 jurisdictions inside the system your firm already pays for. Skip it too if you are a large firm with a centralized docketing team that reconciles every ECF notice. That is Aderant's market.

## Versus the alternatives

**Aderant Milana and CompuLaw** is the large-firm incumbent. Aderant says its rules are built and maintained in-house by licensed attorneys across 2,900+ jurisdictions, and Milana adds ECF reconciliation and court data feeds. Pick it when a docketing department owns deadlines and you need attorney-maintained rules plus ECF reconciliation. Pick LawToolBox when lawyers calendar their own deadlines in Outlook and Aderant's quoted pricing is out of proportion to your headcount.

**Clio with CalendarRules** is the fastest-growing option. Clio passed $500 million ARR in May 2026, and court rules are sold as a per-user add-on (reported at $15/user/month). Pick it when Clio is already your practice-management system. Pick LawToolBox when your matters live in Microsoft 365 and a document system, not in Clio.

**Practice-management-native deadlines** in MyCase, Smokeball or Filevine cover a smaller firm that only needs reminders, not court-rule chains. If none of these fit, the fallback is manual docketing with a mandatory second-person check on every calculated deadline, which is slow but auditable.

## Watch-outs

- **Mailbox-based pricing inflates the bill.** Paralegals, finance staff and anyone else with a licensed mailbox counts as a user under the published terms. *Guard:* count your licensed mailboxes before the demo, and get the licensed user definition written into the order form. Scoping it to the litigation group is the first thing to ask for.
- **You carry the accuracy risk.** The deadline calculator's terms say you rely on it at your own risk and disclaim any warranty of accuracy. LawToolBox also doesn't publish how rules are maintained, unlike Aderant's attorney-maintained process. *Guard:* in the pilot, calculate 20 deadlines by hand across your five busiest courts and compare. Get the jurisdiction list and the rule-update turnaround in writing. Some reviewers reported gaps in Texas coverage and multi-party cases.
- **Sync has been fragile.** Capterra reviews (4.2/5 from 53 reviews) mention Outlook having to stay open for sync, calendar clutter from unneeded deadlines, and weak PracticePanther integration. Most are from 2021–2022. *Guard:* test sync with the new Outlook, Outlook on the web and mobile, with the desktop client closed, before you sign.
- **The security story is mostly Microsoft's.** Matter content stays in your own tenant. LawToolBox's Azure servers in the US hold matter names, usernames and the calendar events it creates, and its security page cites Azure's certifications rather than its own audit. *Guard:* ask for LawToolBox's own SOC 2 Type 2 report. If you practice outside the US, confirm data residency for the metadata it stores.
- **An AI client with 70 tools over matter data needs its own review.** *Guard:* before firm-wide rollout, test the MCP server against a walled matter to prove a walled user's Claude or Copilot session cannot read it. Get the MCP pricing and the authentication model in writing.
