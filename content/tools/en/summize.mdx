## What it is

Summize is a contract lifecycle management platform from Manchester-based Summize Ltd that puts intake, review, approval and repository search inside Microsoft Word, Outlook, Teams, Slack, Gmail, Salesforce, HubSpot and Jira — rather than inside a legal portal the rest of the business has to be persuaded to log into. The 2026 product is organized in three layers the vendor calls the Summize System: a Knowledge Layer holding playbooks, policies and clause libraries; a Contract Operations Layer running intake through signature and storage; and an Agentic AI Layer branded SIA (Summize Intelligence Agents), which answers contract questions inside those same tools. The review engine, Review Pro, redlines a counterparty draft against your playbook inside the Word add-in and returns suggested amendments and omissions, with a vendor-stated first pass in as little as 60 seconds. Summize closed a £40m funding and reinvestment round in early 2026 backed by Maven Capital Partners, Kennet Partners, Federated Hermes Private Equity and YFM Equity Partners, then acquired the assets and staff of Seattle CLM consultancy InnoLaw Group in June 2026 to build out its US implementation team.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Adoption is the product, not a feature.** [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/), [Icertis](/en/tools/icertis/) and [Conga CLM](/en/tools/conga-clm/) all start a contract by asking a salesperson to enter a legal system. Summize's intake surfaces are Outlook, Teams, Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot and Jira, so the request starts where the requester already is. That is the specific reason to shortlist it: a stalled CLM rollout is nearly always a business-side adoption failure, not a legal-side capability gap.
- **Review stays in Word.** Playbook positions load into the Word add-in and the redline comes back in-document, so a lawyer does not switch tools mid-review. This is the same bet [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/) makes with VerifAI, and the two are the only mid-market CLMs where in-Word review is the primary path rather than a companion to a web app.
- **SIA absorbs the inbound legal questions.** Three agent types ship: embedded agents grounded in your live and historical contracts, customizable knowledge agents built on your own policy documentation, and an analytics agent that queries the contract estate for payment terms, liability exposure and renewal windows.

## Pricing reality

Summize publishes no prices — `summize.com/pricing` 301-redirects to the demo booking form, and the vendor's stated position is that quotes are tailored per business and built from license mix. Anonymized buyer data from Vendr puts the median Summize contract at **$32,000 per year**, with the observed band running $28,917 to $39,917. That sits below Ironclad's $40,000 median across 363 recorded purchases and in the same territory as SpotDraft's $25,277 average, so the commercial argument against enterprise CLM is real but narrower than the marketing implies. The number worth negotiating is time-to-value, not just price: Summize sells 3-4 week implementation sprints with the first use case live in roughly 4 weeks and full rollout inside 12 weeks. Put those dates in the order form — a multi-quarter rollout is the failure mode this product is sold against.

## Best for

In-house legal teams of roughly 3-25 lawyers at 200-3,000 employee companies, where the bottleneck is the volume of routine commercial paper — NDAs, MSAs, DPAs, order forms, supplier terms — arriving from sales, procurement and HR, and where a previous CLM died because nobody outside legal logged in. The fit is strongest when the business already runs on Microsoft 365 or Slack plus Salesforce or HubSpot, because those are the surfaces Summize embeds into natively.

## Not for

Enterprises whose core problem is sell-side obligation management, multi-party negotiation at scale, or ERP-grade supplier governance across tens of thousands of agreements — [Icertis](/en/tools/icertis/) and [SirionLabs](/en/tools/sirionlabs/) own that ground. Also not for teams whose security review requires a SOC 2 Type II report, and not for teams that want to build their own automation on top of the CLM: there is no public developer API or MCP server.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Ironclad** — the share leader in mid-market and upper-mid-market CLM, with a documented developer platform and a deeper workflow designer. Pick Ironclad when you need custom integrations your vendor will not build for you, or when contract workflow branching is genuinely complex. Pick Summize when the deciding constraint is whether sales and procurement will use it at all.
- **Icertis** — enterprise supplier-side governance, obligation tracking and contract-value assurance. Pick Icertis above roughly 10,000 active agreements, or where a procurement organization rather than legal owns the system. It costs multiples of Summize and takes multiples of the time to deploy.
- **SpotDraft** — the fastest-growing entrant in this band, raising $8M from Qualcomm Ventures in January 2026 at about a $380M post-money valuation, with contract volume up 173% year over year. VerifAI also redlines in Word. Pick SpotDraft when you want the lower entry band and one vendor for review plus repository; pick Summize when intake breadth across Teams, Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot and Jira is what decides adoption.

## Watch-outs

- **ISO 27001, but no SOC 2.** Summize holds ISO 27001 accreditation and states on its own security page that it intends to work toward SOC 2 in future — it does not hold that attestation today. Guard: if your procurement gate requires SOC 2 Type II, treat this as a hard stop rather than a negotiation; otherwise request the information-security whitepaper and the most recent third-party penetration test summary before the pilot, and get the ISO 27001 certificate scope in writing.
- **The productivity figures are vendor-published.** "6x more contracts reviewed", "50%+ of time saved" and the 60-second first pass all come from Summize's own marketing, not an independent audit. Guard: make the pilot measure your own first-pass-to-approval cycle time across 20 real counterparty drafts, and hold the sprint plan open until that baseline exists.
- **No public API and no MCP server.** Connectivity is whatever Summize has built — Word, Outlook, Gmail, Teams, Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, Jira, Docusign, Adobe Sign, SharePoint, Google Drive. Anything outside that list is a vendor roadmap conversation. Guard: confirm your CRM and document management system are on the supported list in writing, and get the bulk export format for the repository named in the order form so the contract estate stays portable.
- **AI runs on OpenAI models hosted in Azure.** Summize states customer prompts, completions, embeddings and training data stay exclusive to each customer and are not used to improve underlying models. Guard: if your AI policy constrains subprocessors or data residency, confirm the Azure region and the OpenAI subprocessor entry in the DPA before contracts move, and record that position now that EU AI Act obligations are landing on in-house teams.
