## What it is

Workday Contract Lifecycle Management is the CLM product Workday built from Evisort, the AI contract-analytics company it agreed to buy on 17 September 2024 and closed on 8 October 2024 (reported at about $310M; Workday's 10-K calls the deal not material). Workday sells two products on that base: **Contract Intelligence**, a post-signature repository that pulls clauses, dates and obligations out of existing contracts, and **Contract Lifecycle Management**, which adds intake, drafting, approval workflows, negotiation and signature on top. G2, Capterra and AWS Marketplace still list it as "powered by Evisort AI", and existing customers still log in at clients.evisort.com.

The product is mid-rebuild. Workday shipped a new platform it calls **CLM R2** in September 2026: a redesigned Workflow Builder you can drive with plain-language prompts, "Requests" replacing tickets (the API route moves from `/tickets` to `/requests`), custom review stages replacing the old fixed four-stage process, and a Word add-in that now runs in the native task pane. The in-app document editor is deprecated in favour of Word. Agents sit on top: the **Contract Workflow Agent** routes a plain-language request (an NDA, a renewal) to the right workflow and pre-fills the intake form, the **Contract Negotiation Agent** does playbook-based risk review and redlining, and the **Contract Intelligence Agent** works across the post-signature repository. Gartner placed Workday as a Visionary, not a Leader, in its November 2025 CLM Magic Quadrant.

## Why it shows up in legal-ops stacks

- **Extraction depth from the Evisort years.** Workday cites a library of 120+ pre-trained clause and field models, which is the reason Evisort won repository and contract-analytics deals before the acquisition. Its own customer stories include NetApp analysing 90,000+ agreements and FlexGen finding over $50M of tariff exposure in about 1.5 days instead of 2.5 weeks (vendor-reported).
- **One vendor for HR, finance and contracts.** Workday says users of Workday HCM, Financials or Procurement will recognise the CLM interface, and Workflow Builder has an "Integrate with Workday" step for Workday Strategic Sourcing and Procurement customers. If your CFO already signs one Workday order form a year, CLM can be added to it.
- **Compliance paperwork is already done.** Workday claims ISO 27001, 27701 and 42001 (the AI management-system standard), and since 26 February 2026 offers EU data residency in Frankfurt with AI language support for German, French and Spanish.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price. Vendr's anonymised transaction data puts the median buyer at **$41,850 a year**, with a middle band of roughly $35K to $49K. Third-party buyer guides report mid-size deployments at $30K-$60K and large enterprise contracts at $120K-$250K+ (estimate; quote-dependent). The number moves on three things: whether you buy Contract Intelligence alone or the full CLM, contract volume, and how many modules (Salesforce connector, SAP Ariba, e-signature) you switch on.

The real lever is the Workday relationship. If you already run Workday HCM or Financials, negotiate CLM at your main renewal, not as a separate deal, and get the price-increase cap written into the same order form. If you do not run Workday, you are a standalone CLM buyer to a vendor whose roadmap is built around its suite, so ask the account team in writing what support you get without HCM or Financials.

## Best for

Best for a legal-ops or procurement lead at a 1,000+ employee company already on Workday HCM or Financials, with a backlog of signed contracts nobody can search and a mostly standard paper flow (NDAs, MSAs, vendor agreements, renewals). It also fits teams whose first problem is post-signature visibility (renewal dates, obligations, tariff or change-of-control clauses) rather than drafting speed.

Do not buy it if you are on SAP, Oracle or Microsoft Dynamics for HR and finance and want the most configurable workflow engine on the market, or if you have fewer than five people touching contracts and fewer than a few hundred a year. A $40K median contract is hard to justify at that size.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/)** is the default for in-house teams whose hard problem is workflow design across sales, procurement and HR contracts. Pick it over Workday when you need the deepest no-code workflow builder and a large integration catalogue, and you are not committed to the Workday suite.
- **[Icertis](/en/tools/icertis/)** is the enterprise, procurement-heavy pole, usually priced in the low six figures and up. Pick it at $1B+ revenue when contracts are a sourcing and obligation-management system that spans SAP or Oracle ERP.
- **[SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/)** is the fastest-growing challenger (contract volume up 173% year over year by its own figures; Vendr average about $25K a year). Pick it when you want a modern CLM with in-Word AI review at roughly 60% of the Workday median.
- **[Sirion](/en/tools/sirionlabs/)** and **[Docusign IAM](/en/tools/docusign-iam/)** were both named Leaders in Gartner's 2025 CLM Magic Quadrant; shortlist Sirion for post-signature obligation and supplier performance management, Docusign when signature volume drives the whole process.

See [alternatives to Ironclad](/en/vs/alternatives-to-ironclad/) and [the best CLM platforms](/en/vs/best-clm-platforms/) for the wider field.

## Watch-outs

- **The platform underneath is changing.** New customers who bought after 30 September 2026 start on CLM R2; Workday is enabling migration for existing customers from November 2026 through June 2027, with renamed objects, a new API route and a new review model. Guard: if you are already on the legacy product, get your migration window and the `/tickets` to `/requests` API change in writing, and do not build new integrations against the legacy API.
- **The Word add-in needs setup.** Workday flags the new Word integration as "Setup Required"; if IT does not deploy it, users lose AI review, editing and drafting in Word. Guard: put the Microsoft 365 add-in rollout on the implementation plan with a named owner before go-live.
- **Agent status is not uniform.** Workday's blog calls the Contract Workflow Agent "released", while independent coverage in September 2026 described early access, and the planned alerts-and-escalations agent is still future. Guard: demo each agent on your own contracts and get GA status per agent in the order form.
- **Gartner rates it a Visionary.** Gartner scored its vision ahead of its ability to execute, which in CLM usually means workflow depth and deployment track record. Guard: run a two-week pilot on your most complex approval flow, not just on NDA extraction.
