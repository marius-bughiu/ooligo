## What it is

Pigment is a business-planning platform in which finance, sales, HR and supply chain teams build their plans on one shared modeling engine. For RevOps, the sales-planning side covers territory design with map views, quota setting, sales capacity planning, account scoring and segmentation, sales forecasting and an incentive compensation module. The company was founded in Paris in 2019 by Eléonore Crespo (CEO) and Romain Niccoli, has raised close to $400 million, and passed a $1 billion valuation in 2024. In March 2026 Fortune reported it was approaching $100 million in ARR after doubling ARR for a third straight year. Customers named on its RevOps pages include Gong, Miro, Airtable, Brex, Algolia, dbt Labs and 6sense.

The question this page answers: **Pigment or Anaplan for sales and territory planning at a scale-up that has outgrown spreadsheets?**

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **The same pitch as Anaplan, with a shorter build.** Quota, territories and capacity sit in the same model as the finance plan, so a cut to the bookings target flows straight into quotas. Independent reviewers (CFO Shortlist, Metapraxis) put a standard Pigment deployment at 6 weeks to 4 months, against 3 to 6 months for a single Anaplan use case.
- **The AI builds the model, not only answers questions about it.** The Modeler Agent, launched in March 2026, turns a plain-English description into dimensions, metrics and formulas. On 2026-09-10 Pigment added Frames, which generates input forms, scenario planners and presentations from a prompt on top of live model data. SiliconANGLE reported an Uber workshop where a whiteboard sketch became a working application in about an hour. CFO Shortlist still expects 20 to 30% manual refinement of what the Modeler Agent produces.
- **The connectors a scale-up actually runs.** Native connectors cover Salesforce, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, SAP, Workday, HiBob, BambooHR, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Snowflake and BigQuery. Pipeline, bookings and the hiring plan land in the model without a middleware project.

## Pricing

Pigment publishes no prices. Contracts combine a platform fee, planning modules (financial, sales, workforce and so on) and seats split into power users, contributors and viewers. Vendr's buyer data (117 purchases, updated February 2026) shows a median annual contract of $74,000, with a range of $33,640 to $168,100 and an average negotiated saving of 10.24%. That median is about 65% of Anaplan's $114,608 on the same dataset.

Two cost lines sit next to the license. Vendr puts professional services at 20 to 50% of first-year cost, far below Anaplan's typical 1.5 to 3 times license. CFO Shortlist reports a default annual escalator of 8 to 12%. Vendr also notes that seats added mid-contract are often trued up at list price. Fix the escalator and the price of added seats in the first order form, because RevOps headcount grows faster than the plan you sign on.

## Best for

RevOps and sales-finance leaders at venture-backed scale-ups where territory, quota and capacity planning still runs in spreadsheets and the CFO wants the sales plan tied to the finance plan. The strongest fit is a company buying finance and sales planning at the same time, or one whose finance team already runs Pigment.

**Do not buy it** if the real pain is paying commissions accurately every month, if nobody on the team will own model logic, or if your models already run into billions of cells.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Anaplan](/en/tools/anaplan/)** is the enterprise incumbent. It was named a Leader in Gartner's 2026 Magic Quadrant for Sales Performance Management, and its engine handles far larger models (Metapraxis cites 10B+ cells against Pigment's 500M+). Pick Anaplan when you are a large enterprise with a model-builder team, or when finance already runs on it.

**[Varicent](/en/tools/varicent/)** puts territory, quota and comp on one SPM data model and was also a Leader in the 2026 SPM Magic Quadrant. Pick Varicent when territory planning and payouts are the purchase and the finance plan can stay in another system.

**[Xactly](/en/tools/xactly/)** and **[CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/)** are the comp-first picks. Pigment's analyst credentials sit in finance planning (a Visionary in Gartner's 2024 and 2025 Magic Quadrants for Financial Planning Software), not in commissions. Pick one of them when payout accuracy and rep statements are the pain.

**[Everstage](/en/tools/everstage/)** is the fastest-growing entrant among the SPM specialists. Pick it when you need commissions live in weeks and planning can wait.

**[Fullcast](/en/tools/fullcast/)** is the pick when territory design and routing inside [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) are the whole problem and the finance plan is out of scope.

If none fit, fix the process first. Written [territory design](/en/learn/territory-design/) rules and a [capacity model](/en/learn/capacity-planning/) in a spreadsheet will tell you more about your requirements than an RFP will.

## Watch-outs

- **Faster than Anaplan still means someone owns the model.** G2 reviewers cite a steep learning curve on complex models and Pigment's own formula syntax, and the Modeler Agent output still needs refinement. *Guard:* name an internal owner for model logic before you sign. In the proof of concept, have that person change a territory rule and a quota formula without help from the vendor or partner.
- **Commissions are the least proven module.** Pigment lists incentive compensation management, but its analyst recognition is in financial planning, and it was not among the Leaders of the 2026 SPM Magic Quadrant. *Guard:* if payouts matter, make the vendor run your two most complex comp plans, including a clawback and a mid-period territory change, through statements and payroll export in the POC. Otherwise feed quotas from Pigment into Xactly, CaptivateIQ or Everstage.
- **Scale ceilings arrive with success.** Reviewers report slowdowns on highly dimensional or sparse models under concurrent updates. *Guard:* load a full year of opportunity-level data at your real account and product granularity during the POC, and time a territory re-carve with several planners editing at once.
