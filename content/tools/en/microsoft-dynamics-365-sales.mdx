## What it is

Dynamics 365 Sales is Microsoft's sales CRM: accounts, contacts, leads, opportunities, forecasting and sequences, stored in Dataverse — the same data platform under Power Apps, Power Automate and Copilot Studio. That shared platform is the whole argument for it. If your company already runs Entra ID, Outlook, Teams and SharePoint, Dynamics is the CRM whose records, permissions and audit trail live in the tenant your security team already governs. It is the main enterprise alternative to [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), and the one procurement will put in front of you when the Microsoft enterprise agreement comes up for renewal.

## The Sales Agent question

The AI story changed on 2026-07-07, when Microsoft made Sales Agent (formerly Copilot for Sales) and Service Agent generally available across Microsoft 365 Copilot, Outlook, Teams and Dynamics 365. Sales Agent summarizes email threads and drafts replies in Outlook, records and recaps Teams sales meetings with action items, and answers questions like "summarize account Fabrikam" in Copilot chat, grounded in CRM records.

Is it real? Yes, with two conditions buyers routinely miss. First, it needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot license on every seller — $30/user/month on the enterprise plan — on top of the Dynamics seat. It auto-installs for Sales Enterprise and Premium users, but nobody gets answers without that second license. Second, a CRM admin has to configure which tables Sales Agent can read and set up the account, opportunity and lead summaries; out of the box it says "CRM Knowledge hasn't been set up." Plan on configuration work, not a switch flip. It does not run in US GCC, GCC High, DoD or other sovereign clouds, which rules it out for many public-sector sellers.

One detail changes the comparison math: Sales Agent also connects to Salesforce Sales Cloud (Professional and above). If your reason to move to Dynamics is "the Copilot experience in Outlook," you can get most of that without migrating your CRM.

## The agent and MCP layer

Microsoft publishes a Dynamics 365 Sales MCP server with tools such as `get_opportunity_health`, `get_opportunity_top_risks`, `get_lead_qualification_assessment` and `draft_outreach_email`, plus CRUD over Dataverse records through the Dataverse MCP server. External agents, including [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), can call it. AI tool calls consume Copilot Credits unless the user holds a Sales Premium or Microsoft 365 Copilot license; a 25,000-credit pack lists at $200/month. For building custom sales agents on the same data, the tool is [Microsoft Copilot Studio](/en/tools/microsoft-copilot-studio/).

Microsoft also retired the twice-yearly release waves: from September 2026 new capabilities publish to the roadmap continuously, and Release Planner retires on 2026-11-15. Your admin's change-review cadence needs to move from two dates a year to a standing weekly look.

## Pricing

Per user per month, annual commitment, checked on Microsoft's pricing page 2026-10-04:

- **Sales Professional** — $65. Core sales force automation, Microsoft 365 integration, dashboards.
- **Sales Enterprise** — $105. Customization, forecasting and Copilot in Dynamics 365. The realistic baseline.
- **Sales Premium** — $150. Adds Sales Insights and 1,000 Copilot Credits per user per month.
- **Microsoft Relationship Sales** — quote only, Sales Enterprise bundled with LinkedIn Sales Navigator, 10-seat minimum.

Every plan includes access to the prebuilt Sales Qualification, Opportunity, Research and Close agents.

At list, Sales Enterprise costs 60% of Salesforce Enterprise ($105 against $175). That gap narrows once you add the $30 Microsoft 365 Copilot seat that Sales Agent requires ($135 total) and closes if you also need Premium.

Real-world band: implementation, data migration and training push year one to 2-5x the license line, settling near 1.3x from year two (Costbench 2026 TCO guide). Partners typically quote $500-2,000 per user for implementation. A 100-seat Sales Enterprise org lists at $126K/year and lands at $250-630K in year one. Storage is the cost line nobody budgets: database capacity beyond the tenant's default runs about $40 per GB per month at list.

## Best for

RevOps and sales operations leaders at mid-market and enterprise companies standardized on Microsoft 365, where the CRM must sit inside Entra ID governance, sellers live in Outlook and Teams, and IT already owns Power Platform skills.

## Watch-outs

- **Dataverse storage overages.** Email and attachment sync, audit logs and activity history fill database capacity faster than seat growth suggests. Guard: route attachments to SharePoint from day one, set audit-log retention policies before go-live, and review the Power Platform admin center capacity report monthly.
- **Partner-dependent implementation.** Most deployments go through a Microsoft partner, and partner quality varies more than the product does. Guard: require a fixed-scope phase one (accounts, contacts, opportunities, forecast) with a named solution architect, and keep custom plugin code out of scope until reps are using the core.
- **AI licensing stacks quietly.** Dynamics seat, Microsoft 365 Copilot seat, Copilot Credits for agents and MCP calls. Guard: build a per-seller AI cost line before the renewal and pilot Sales Agent on one team for a quarter before licensing everyone.

## Alternatives

[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) is the market-share leader; pick it when your ecosystem (Outreach, Clay, Gong, ZoomInfo) is configured Salesforce-first and you need the deepest third-party integration catalog. [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) is the other top-two CRM; pick it under roughly $25M ARR or when marketing and sales share one platform and you want admin work done by ops, not developers. [Attio](/en/tools/attio/) is the fastest-growing entrant; pick it for teams under about 50 people that want an AI-native data model and will trade enterprise governance for speed.
