## What it is

Transcend is a privacy platform built for companies where engineering, not the privacy office, has to make deletion, consent and data-use rules actually happen in production systems. Ben Brook (CEO) and Mike Farrell (CTO) founded it in San Francisco in 2017. Its last disclosed round was a $40 million Series B in May 2024, led by StepStone Group with Accel and Index Ventures, bringing total funding to about $90 million.

The core modules are DSR automation (access, deletion and opt-out requests fulfilled through integrations, which Transcend says cover more than 1,500 systems), consent and preference management, data discovery and mapping, and assessments. The architectural choice that separates it from most rivals is Sombra, a gateway that runs inside your own environment. It classifies data at the source and holds the API keys to your systems, so Transcend's cloud never handles them.

Four recent releases define what you are buying today:

- **Do Not Train and Deep Deletion** (expanded August 20, 2025) let a B2B AI company promise, record by record, that a customer's data is kept out of model training, and prove deletion across systems. Enterprise buyers increasingly write this into the master services agreement.
- **Agentic Assist and the Transcend MCP Server** (announced March 30, 2026 at the IAPP Global Summit) add an in-product assistant that pre-fills assessments from what Transcend already knows about your data map. The MCP server lets admins run Transcend from Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Both were opt-in, with rollout starting in April 2026, and the AI features can be switched off.
- **Transcend Rails** (October 6, 2026) applies the same policy engine to AI agents. Before an agent acts, Rails checks whether it is allowed to take that action, for that purpose, at that moment. It is waitlist-only, with a 30-day free trial.
- **Gartner recognition**: on October 9, 2026 Transcend announced it was named a Leader in Gartner's first Magic Quadrant for Privacy UX.

Named customers on Transcend's own site include Robinhood, Patreon and Brex.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Privacy work increasingly lands on engineering.** When a deletion has to reach a data warehouse, a product database and a dozen SaaS tools, the bottleneck is integration work. Transcend sells to that bottleneck: custom functions, workflows and a code-first setup that a platform team can own.
- **AI companies need Do Not Train as a contract term.** If your customers' MSAs forbid training on their data, Transcend is one of the few vendors with a product built to enforce and evidence it.
- **It is the engineering-led second quote against [OneTrust](/en/tools/onetrust/).** OneTrust wins on module breadth; Transcend competes on how deep its enforcement goes into your own systems.

## Pricing reality

Transcend publishes no prices; its pricing page is a demo form (checked November 5, 2026). The commercial model is a platform fee plus per-module pricing. DSR automation is metered by connected systems and a baseline of monthly requests, with overages above it. Consent is metered by monthly page views or unique visitors. Professional services are quoted separately.

Third-party data points are thin and inconsistent. Software Advice and GetApp list a $10,000-a-year starting price. Vendr's Transcend page shows a $76,075 average contract value, but it links to a different company's domain, so treat that figure loosely. Our working estimate: a mid-market deal for DSRs plus consent lands in the low-to-mid five figures a year, and an enterprise deal with several modules and many integrations goes well into six figures. Get the quote broken out by module, by meter and by services hours.

## Best for

Privacy engineers, security leads and the privacy counsel who work with them at data-heavy tech companies (fintech, marketplaces, consumer apps and B2B AI vendors), where deletion and consent have to reach warehouses, product databases and internal systems, not just SaaS tools. It is the strongest fit when engineering has time to own the integrations.

Do not buy it if the privacy program is run by a lawyer with no engineering support (DataGrail or [Ketch](/en/tools/ketch/) will be live faster), or if the program is assessment- and research-led ([TrustArc](/en/tools/trustarc/) fits better).

## Versus the alternatives

**[OneTrust](/en/tools/onetrust/)** is the market leader by a wide margin. Pick OneTrust when you need third-party risk, AI governance programs and the broadest module catalog under one contract. Pick Transcend when enforcement inside your own systems matters more than breadth.

**Securiti** is the other large platform, now owned by Veeam. Pick it when data security posture management and privacy have to share one discovery engine across large unstructured estates.

**DataGrail** is the fast-growing mid-market entrant. Pick DataGrail when a lean team mainly needs DSRs and a data map live quickly, with less engineering effort.

**[Ketch](/en/tools/ketch/)** wins when consumer consent and Do Not Sell enforcement across ad tech is the job, and when you want public pricing.

**[TrustArc](/en/tools/trustarc/)** wins when the program needs assessments, regulatory research and certifications.

## Watch-outs

- **Integration effort is real.** The most common G2 complaint is that connecting existing systems takes time, and the payoff depends on that work. *Guard:* list your top 20 systems that hold personal data before the demo, and make the vendor name which ones are native integrations, which need custom functions and who builds them.
- **Pricing is opaque and meter-driven.** Request volume, system count and visitors each scale on their own. *Guard:* model renewal cost at double your current request volume and system count, and negotiate overage rates and a renewal cap before signing.
- **The AI layer is new.** Agentic Assist rolled out from April 2026, and Rails is still waitlist-only. *Guard:* treat agent-filled assessments as drafts with a named reviewer, and keep a human-run [DSAR intake flow](/en/workflows/dsar-intake-triage-n8n/) until the output holds up in an audit.
- **Consolidation risk.** Privacy vendors have been changing hands (Securiti to Veeam, TrustArc to Main Capital), and Transcend's last disclosed round was in 2024. *Guard:* add a change-of-control termination right and data-export commitments, and keep the term at 24 months or less.

G2 rates Transcend 4.6/5 from about 110 reviews; reviewers most often praise how quickly DSR workflows can be automated once systems are connected. For the legal basics behind DSRs, see [GDPR for legal teams](/en/learn/gdpr-for-legal-teams/).
