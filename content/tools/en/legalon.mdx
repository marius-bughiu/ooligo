## What it is

LegalOn is an AI contract-review platform for in-house legal teams, organized around attorney-written playbooks instead of a general-purpose chat box. The core loop is **Review**: you drop a counterparty draft into the web app or the Microsoft Word add-in, LegalOn checks it against a playbook, and it returns redlines with the reasoning and the fallback language attached. Around that sit **Assistant** (questions, summaries, drafting), **Playbooks** (50+ pre-built review standards plus your own), **Matter Management** for tracking legal work from intake to completion, **Vault** for pulling data out of signed agreements, and **Translate** for reviewing and redlining in other languages and handing the changes back in the original. Entity and board management arrived with the October 2025 acquisition of Fides. The company was founded in Tokyo in 2017, closed a $50M Series E led by Goldman Sachs Growth Equity in July 2025 — total funding $200M, SoftBank Vision Fund among the backers — alongside a non-equity technology partnership with OpenAI, and announced in October 2025 that it had passed ¥10 billion in ARR, roughly $67M. Its April 2026 release put the customer count at 8,000+ organizations.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **The playbook ships with the product.** 50+ review standards written by LegalOn's own attorneys are live on day one, which is the difference between buying a review engine and buying a review engine plus a quarter of playbook authoring. The Playbook Agent, launched February 2026, generates a playbook from templates or review guidelines you already have.
- **Jurisdiction coverage is the differentiator.** The April 2026 International Playbooks release added NDA and MSA review standards for 23 countries outside the US, each reviewed by attorneys in that jurisdiction, plus EU and Singapore data-residency options. That is governing-law coverage shipped as content rather than as a prompt you write yourself, and it is the reason a team with EU, UK, and APAC paper shortlists LegalOn at all.
- **The agents cover intake and triage, not only redlining.** Intake Agent chases business stakeholders for the facts missing from a request, and Triage Agent (early access as of February 2026) auto-approves low-risk agreements or escalates them. That pushes LegalOn past the Word-add-in category and into the queue management that eats a legal-ops manager's week.

## Pricing reality

LegalOn's pricing page names three tiers — Core Review, Contracting Suite, Productivity Suite — and posts no number against any of them. Everything runs through a demo, with a free trial on Core Review. The last self-serve figure the vendor published was a single-seat Individual plan at $550/month billed annually ($6,600/year), and it is no longer on the page. Breakdowns published by competitors put an all-modules team seat near $8,000 per user per year and a five-seat deployment near $40,000 a year; those are estimates from interested parties, not vendor numbers, and should be treated as a ceiling to negotiate against rather than a quote. What matters for budgeting is the shape: a per-seat base for Review, plus paid modules — Matter Management, Vault, Translate, the international playbook pack — that each move the line. Price the modules you will actually switch on in year one, and get that module list onto the order form rather than into the email thread.

## Best for

In-house legal and legal-ops teams at companies that sign the same agreements repeatedly across more than one country — NDAs, MSAs, DPAs, order forms — and want a review standard enforced without authoring it first. LegalOn is strongest where the constraint is review throughput on cross-border paper and the team is 3-25 people: large enough that consistency between reviewers matters, small enough that hiring another lawyer is not the answer.

Don't buy LegalOn as a law-firm platform. It is built for the in-house side of the table; litigation, firm-grade research, and matter billing belong elsewhere. It is also not a CLM — it reviews and analyzes agreements, but your system of record for storage, approval routing, and renewal dates stays where it is. And a solo counsel or a two-lawyer team reviewing a handful of contracts a month will not clear the price floor.

## Versus the alternatives

[Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) is the volume leader in Word-native contract AI and publishes a $99 floor — pick it when the work is drafting-led, the budget is small, or you are a firm rather than a department. [Ivo](/en/tools/ivo/) is the closest match on the same in-house review-and-playbook job — pick it when your agreements sit in one jurisdiction and you want portfolio intelligence over what you have already signed; pick LegalOn when the governing law changes from deal to deal. [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) is the enterprise and BigLaw pole, scoped to research and litigation rather than high-volume commercial review. [GC AI](/en/tools/gc-ai/) publishes a $500 per-seat floor and carries advisory work — pick it when you want employment and compliance questions answered in the same surface and a price you can model before a sales call. [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) is the fastest-growing entrant in the segment, at $100M ARR about 18 months after its first $1M, and is the bake-off to run when Word-native drafting quality outranks playbook breadth. Against the status quo — a senior lawyer reading every draft by hand — LegalOn wins specifically when the review queue is the bottleneck and the paper is repeatable enough for a standard to hold.

## Watch-outs

- **The 85% time-saving figure is vendor-reported.** It comes from LegalOn's own press releases and named customer stories, not an independent audit. *Guard:* run a paid pilot on 30-50 of your own live agreements, have a senior reviewer grade the redlines against what they would have written, and gate the contract on the miss rate you measured rather than the one you were shown.
- **Module stacking is where the quote grows.** The base tier is review; Matter Management, Vault, Translate, and the international playbooks are separate lines. *Guard:* ask for the fully loaded per-seat number with every module you intend to enable, in writing, before you set it against a published floor from [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) or [GC AI](/en/tools/gc-ai/) — comparing a LegalOn base tier to a rival's all-in list price flatters LegalOn by a wide margin.
- **No published MCP server, and no named DMS integration.** The interface is the Word add-in, the web app, and Slack or Teams intake; there is no announced connector for iManage or NetDocuments and no MCP endpoint to wire an agent into. *Guard:* if your executed contracts live in a DMS, confirm the ingest and export path during the pilot, and assume manual upload for anything the add-in does not reach.
- **Once you fork a playbook, its currency is yours.** LegalOn maintains its own standards as law changes, but a custom playbook you build on top inherits your maintenance, and a stale one produces confident redlines against a position you no longer hold. *Guard:* name an owner per custom playbook, and sample a handful of its redlines monthly to catch drift.
