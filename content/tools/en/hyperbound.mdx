## What it is

Hyperbound turns a description of your ICP into an AI buyer a rep can call, then scores that call against your scorecard. It shipped that product in January 2024. It spent the two years since building outward from it, and now sells three things: **Practice**, the roleplay layer; **Perform**, which scores real customer calls and coaches on live deals; and **Kota Activate**, an agent that answers questions across both.

The correction to make before you evaluate it: roleplay is no longer the product. The vendor's own pricing FAQ says measurable improvement comes only from running Practice and Perform together, and the two are separate per-user licenses. What is being sold is a two-license commitment. What the demo shows you is one of them.

The company raised a $15M Series A on 16 September 2025, led by Peak XV Partners with Y Combinator, Snowflake Ventures, Roble Ventures and Fellows Fund, taking total funding to $18.3M. It said it had added more than $1M in new ARR in each of the two months before the term sheet. Named customers include Autodesk, Monday.com, Bloomberg, G2, Vanta, Airwallex and Hub International.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

The trigger is ramp time, not call visibility. A team hiring 30 SDRs a quarter cannot put a manager on every first call, and certification in most onboarding programs is a manager listening once and signing a form. Hyperbound replaces that with a bot the rep has to get past — run as many times as needed, scored the same way every time. The vendor's AppExchange listing claims teams ramp 50% faster. Treat that as vendor-reported until your own cohort data says otherwise.

The second reason is the width of the practice surface. Multiparty roleplays put several buyer personas in one call, the bots run in 25+ languages, and prebuilt packs cover outbound, inbound, objection handling and competitive positioning. Kota, launched 18 December 2025, sits on top with a Command+K prompt for questions like a coaching snapshot for one rep, or the objection themes across last month's discovery calls.

## Pricing reality

Quote-only above the free tier — and the free tier is real: 45 prebuilt roleplays and example scorecards, no card. Practice and Perform both read `ENTERPRISE` with no number attached. Pricing is per user, with volume discounts by team size and a further cut for multi-year terms.

The structural fact that decides your budget: **CRM and call-recorder integrations are Perform-only.** Salesforce, HubSpot and Dynamics, plus Gong, Zoom and Orum capture, do not exist on a Practice license. Seismic, Highspot, Workramp, SSO/SCIM and Slack ship with both. Live Call Coaching, a data-export API, EU data residency and implementation services are priced as add-ons above that.

Anchor on published comparables, since this vendor publishes none:

- **Yoodli** lists $8/user/month for Pro and $20/user/month for Advanced, both billed annually, and quotes Team and Enterprise separately.
- **Second Nature** is also quote-only; third-party trackers put it near $30-40/seat/month with a roughly $20K annual floor. That band appears in no vendor source.
- **Gong** runs $1,200-1,800 per rep/year, about $100-150/seat/month, with a 25-50 seat minimum.

Estimate, anchored on those three and marked as one: expect Practice to quote in the **$30-60/seat/month annual** band at 50-150 seats, and Practice plus Perform together to land at **$85-140/seat/month**. That upper band is Gong money. Ask for both license prices at signature even if you only buy Practice — the upgrade quote that arrives after 200 reps are trained is not a negotiation you win.

## Best for

An enablement or RevOps leader on a **50-500 rep team with a quarterly hiring cohort and a named ramp metric**, already standardized on Salesforce or HubSpot. The scoped case where it wins outright: onboarding certification for SDRs and AEs where the pass mark has to be identical across 40 new hires and three managers, and where someone owns the number that ramp time is supposed to move.

## Not for

Teams under 20 reps. The free tier's 45 roleplays cover the real need, and an enterprise license buys governance you have nobody to run. It is also the wrong pick when the mandate is visibility into real calls. Gong, [Clari](/en/tools/clari/) and [Attention](/en/tools/attention/) own that process, and buying Perform to reach conversation intelligence means paying for a roleplay engine you did not need.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Gong** — the bundling risk, and the reason this entry exists. Gong announced Dry Run on 24 June 2026 alongside Mission Big Dipper, for July 2026 availability: a rep launches a rehearsal straight from an upcoming calendar invite, and Gong builds the buyer persona from that account's own context and conversation history. Pick Gong when you already pay it, the practice need is pre-call rehearsal on live deals, and nobody owns a formal certification program. Pick Hyperbound when onboarding is a program with a curriculum, a scorecard and a pass mark.
- **Second Nature** — the incumbent in structured roleplay, and the name enterprise L&D buyers shortlist by habit. Pick it when the buyer is corporate training rather than RevOps and the rollout spans non-sales roles. Pick Hyperbound when the scorecard has to match your sales methodology and write back to the CRM.
- **Yoodli** — the published-price challenger. Pick it when you need a number on paper this quarter, when the need is communication coaching rather than buyer simulation, or when a two-license enterprise commitment is more risk than the ramp problem justifies. The tradeoff is real: no deal coaching, no CRM layer.
- On the support side, [Solidroad](/en/tools/solidroad/) runs the same practice-and-score loop for CS and support teams.

## Watch-outs

- **The demo is Practice; the value case is Practice plus Perform.** The vendor says so in its own pricing FAQ, and the two are separate per-user licenses. **Guard:** get both prices in the first paper, with the Practice-to-Perform upgrade rate fixed for 24 months. Do not let the second license be quoted after your reps are trained.
- **A Practice-only license has no CRM and no call capture.** Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics, Gong, Zoom and Orum sit on the Perform tier. Bought alone, Practice is a training island your scorecard data never leaves. **Guard:** name every integration you need in the first demo email, and make the CRM write-back a scripted step in the trial rather than a slide.
- **Roleplay adoption decays after the first month.** Reps practice during onboarding and stop unless a manager assigns the next one — the most common complaint in third-party reviews across this category, not of this vendor alone. **Guard:** tie roleplays to a certification gate with a date, use Manager Assignments, and review completion in the weekly forecast call instead of a quarterly enablement review.
- **Quote-only pricing with no published floor removes your anchor.** You will negotiate against a number the vendor picked after reading your headcount. **Guard:** open on Yoodli's published $20/user/month and Second Nature's reported $30-40 band, and ask what replicating the free tier's 45 roleplays as custom bots costs before you agree to a seat count.
- **The performance numbers are vendor-reported.** The 6.9% close-rate lift, 50% faster ramp and 30% more productive talk time all come from Hyperbound's own Kota announcement, with no named cohort. **Guard:** hold one team out of the rollout for a quarter and compare its ramp-to-certification against the trained cohort. If nobody will fund the holdout, the metric was never the point.
