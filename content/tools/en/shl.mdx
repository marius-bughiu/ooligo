## What it is

SHL sells the psychometric layer that other hiring vendors point at when asked whether their scores mean anything. Founded in 1977, it publishes the Occupational Personality Questionnaire (OPQ), the Motivational Questionnaire, the SHL Verify cognitive battery, the Global Skills Assessment (GSA), Job-Focused Assessments, situational judgment tests, and SHL 360 — delivered through the TalentCentral platform with a catalog running past 1,000 assessments.

Ownership is worth stating plainly, because SHL's own company page names no parent. Exponent Private Equity bought it in March 2018 in a $400m deal and still lists it as a current Fund III holding. Unrelated "SHL acquires…" headlines refer to SHL Medical, a drug-delivery manufacturer with no connection to this vendor.

Scale claims come from the owner and the vendor, not an auditor: Exponent's portfolio page puts SHL in front of 80% of the FTSE 100 and more than half the Fortune Global 500, and SHL's cognitive product pages cite 2,500+ clients. Treat those as directional. The load-bearing fact is narrower — when a selection decision gets challenged, this is the vendor whose technical manuals get cited.

2026 added an AI-readiness line. With The Josh Bersin Company, SHL ran the Superworker Skills Program from September to December 2025 and published findings in the first half of 2026. It scores four overarching factors and eight skills beneath them — adaptability, critical thinking, learning orientation, responsible AI use among them — through the roughly 15-minute GSA, and reports an AI-Ready Workforce profile and an AI-Ready Leader profile. SHL's published result: 1 in 3 employees and 1 in 5 leaders clear the bar.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Defensibility is the product, not a feature of it.** Every other assessment vendor in this catalog competes on speed, candidate experience, or price. SHL competes on whether the instrument holds up under an adverse-impact challenge, and it prices accordingly. If your reason to buy is anything other than that, you are overpaying.
- **Verify Interactive is built to be measured rather than guessed.** The cognitive tests use drag-and-drop and gamified items instead of multiple choice, which removes the guessing floor, and SHL's partial-scoring method lets the test reach a stable score in fewer questions. Delivery is automated and remotely proctored.
- **It attaches to the ATS instead of replacing a stage.** The [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) integration fires an assessment the moment an application is submitted, or at a named step in the interview plan, and writes invite status and results back to the candidate profile. The same pattern exists for [Workday](/en/tools/workday/), [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/), [SmartRecruiters](/en/tools/smartrecruiters/), and [Avature](/en/tools/avature/).
- **The skills data outlives the requisition.** SHL's Talent Mobility product, launched 29 April 2025, scores 96 behavioral skills against the SHL Universal Competency Framework and maps them onto 600+ O*NET-validated roles. That is the argument for buying assessment once and using it for [skills-based hiring](/en/learn/skills-based-hiring/), internal mobility, and succession from the same instrument.

## Pricing

Quote-only, no free tier, no published US price list. The usable public anchor is the UK G-Cloud framework, where SHL lists its psychometric, behavioural and cognitive testing service at **£4,500 to £1,000,000 a licence** — approximately $5,700 to $1.27M. Subscriptions are metered per annum by candidate volume band, and SHL's G-Cloud price sheet frames its worked examples as "Essential" tier, no add-ons, on a 24-month contract.

Read the floor and the ceiling as the actual information. The £4,500 entry buys a small volume band on a bare tier, which is why SHL rarely wins evaluations under about 500 assessed candidates a year — [TestGorilla](/en/tools/testgorilla/) publishes a $75/month Starter plan for that shape of buyer. The ceiling is where custom SJTs, bespoke reports, video interviewing, and virtual assessment centres land. Budget separately for the configuration work; it is not bundled into the subscription line.

## Best for

TA leaders and assessment owners at 1,000+ employee companies hiring at volume — graduate intakes, contact centre, retail, BPO — where selection decisions get challenged and someone has to produce a validity argument in writing. It is equally the right pick when leadership hiring and succession planning have to run on the same instrument as front-line selection.

## Alternatives and when to pick them instead

- **[HireVue](/en/tools/hirevue/)** — the other name on nearly every enterprise assessment shortlist. Pick it when video interviewing is the centre of the process and assessment rides along, rather than the reverse.
- **Aon Assessment (cut-e) and Korn Ferry's Hogan instruments** — the rest of the incumbent field. Pick Aon when European volume hiring and local-language norms dominate the requirement; pick Hogan when the deliverable is executive personality and derailment risk, not selection at scale.
- **[Maki People](/en/tools/maki-people/)** — the fast-growing AI-native entrant. Pick it when candidate experience and time-to-decision are the problem and you can accept a shorter validity track record.
- **[Harver](/en/tools/harver/)** — pick it for hourly and high-volume front-line hiring where realistic job previews and scheduling matter more than psychometric depth.
- **[Crosschq](/en/tools/crosschq/)** — pick it when the question is [quality of hire](/en/learn/quality-of-hire/) measurement across the whole funnel rather than the selection instrument itself.

If none fit, the fallback is [structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) plus a scored work sample, run on a fixed rubric. It costs interviewer hours instead of licence fees, and it tells you whether a paid instrument would beat what you already do.

## Watch-outs

- **Unproctored cognitive testing is under active attack.** SHL's own 2026 data puts 70% of candidates using AI at some stage of the hiring process. Its answers are automated remote proctoring and an AI-detection framework it reports as 7x more accurate at flagging AI-generated language responses — and SHL has published that candidates edit AI output specifically to evade those detectors. *Guard:* treat unproctored scores as a ranking signal, never a cut score. Verify the top of the funnel with a supervised retest or a live structured interview before any offer, and write that step into the process design rather than leaving it to the hiring manager.
- **The bias audit is your obligation, not SHL's.** Under [NYC Local Law 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/) the employer commissions the annual independent audit, and a vendor-supplied audit only counts if it covers the version and configuration you actually deployed. [Colorado SB 205](/en/learn/colorado-ai-act-sb205/) and Illinois AIVIA add their own duties, and under the [EU AI Act](/en/learn/eu-ai-act-for-legal-teams/) the Digital Omnibus moved Annex III employment obligations to 2 December 2027. *Guard:* make the contract name the specific instruments and configuration in scope, require the technical manual and adverse-impact data for each one at signature, and re-request them whenever you change a cut score or add a module.
- **Configuration effort is the hidden line item.** Reviewers cite customization complexity and integration work as the recurring friction — on a thin sample, 86% satisfaction across 19 reviews on two sites, so read it as a theme rather than a rate. *Guard:* scope the first deployment to one job family, agree the go-live date and the SHL consulting hours in the order form, and refuse a multi-family rollout in year one.
- **Long batteries cost you candidates.** OPQ plus a Verify test plus an SJT is a real time ask at the top of a funnel, and drop-off lands hardest on the volume roles SHL is sold into. *Guard:* instrument completion rate by stage from day one, set a drop-off threshold that triggers shortening the battery, and run the AI-readiness GSA — roughly 15 minutes — as the screen rather than the full stack where throughput matters more than precision.
