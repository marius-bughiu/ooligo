## What it is

Bettermode is a hosted B2B customer community platform: discussion spaces, a knowledge base, a wishlist and roadmap for ideation, events, direct messages, and a badge-and-leaderboard reputation system, all assembled in a visual layout builder the vendor calls Design Studio. Everything behind it is reachable through a GraphQL API with webhooks and a React SDK. The category leader it most resembles is [Higher Logic Vanilla](/en/tools/higher-logic-vanilla/), a branded customer community that sits next to the help center rather than inside it, with one structural difference that decides most evaluations: Bettermode publishes its prices, and Higher Logic Vanilla, [Khoros](/en/tools/khoros/), and Gainsight Customer Communities do not.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **It is the only self-serve entry in a quote-only segment.** A CS leader building a deflection business case can price Bettermode in an afternoon, at $1,788 or $18,000 a year, while the enterprise incumbents require a discovery call before a number exists. That difference is worth more than any feature on the page when the budget cycle is short.
- **The meter is community members, not CS seats.** Plans cap members (10,000 / 25,000 / 100,000+) and, separately, collaborators (admins and moderators) at 5, 10, and 20+. Your CSMs do not each need a paid seat to answer in the community; your moderator bench is what the tier governs.
- **MCP ships on every plan, including Starter.** The connector launched in August 2026 with 16 tools. Eleven read across spaces, posts, members, badges, post types, and analytics, so Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor can draft a weekly digest, list members who have gone quiet, or flag suspected spam against live data. Five write: publish a post or reply, send a direct message, assign a badge, add members to a space, and hide a post. Each write asks for approval individually and runs as the authorizing admin. It is admin-only and has no delete tools.
- **Federated search reaches outside the community.** On Growth and above, search spans [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) help-center content and Salesforce, so a member asking a question hits your documentation and your community in one query instead of choosing a silo first.

## Pricing reality

By November 2026 Bettermode had cut its entry price: Starter fell from $399 to $149 a month, and Growth did not move. Anything quoting $399 or $4,788 a year for Starter is the previous price.

- **Starter**: $149/month billed annually ($1,788/year), $199 month-to-month. Up to 10,000 members, 5 collaborators. Limited API access, social login only, no Ask AI, no federated search, and MCP capped at 10 requests per day per connector. Migration support is a paid add-on.
- **Growth**: $1,500/month billed annually ($18,000/year), $1,750 month-to-month. Up to 25,000 members, 10 collaborators. Adds Ask AI, federated search, full API with webhooks and a sandbox, OAuth2 SSO, hands-on onboarding, and unlimited MCP within the standard API rate limits.
- **Premium**: quote only. 100,000+ members, 20+ collaborators, SAML, a 99.9% uptime SLA, a 90-day audit log, subfolder URLs, and invoicing on net terms.
- **Toggy**: a separately sold add-on billed as an AI team that runs your community, from $750/month. The pricing page gives no tier detail beyond that floor.

A 14-day trial runs without a credit card on Starter and Growth. There is no free plan.

The cut changes who Starter is for, not what most CS teams end up paying. The gap between the two published tiers is now roughly tenfold, and nothing in the deflection business case lives in the cheap one: Ask AI, federated search, SSO, and a usable API are all Growth-and-up. Starter is now a reasonable price for a small branded community whose job is peer discussion and feature requests. For a CS org that is buying the platform to cut tickets, the number to budget against is still **$18,000/year**, and the 5-collaborator ceiling (two community managers, a docs owner, a support lead, one product manager) binds before the member cap does.

## Best for

A Customer Success or support leader at a B2B SaaS company with roughly 2,000 to 25,000 users, running a 3-10 person CS team, who wants a branded self-service community for ticket deflection and structured feature requests, and who needs a defensible budget number before procurement will open a vendor evaluation. Starter now also fits a founder-led or single-community-manager team that wants a branded home for a few hundred to a few thousand customers and does not need AI answers.

**Not for you if** you need moderation across millions of members, if your community must be self-hosted, or if your actual requirement is feature-request intake alone; [Canny](/en/tools/canny/) does that job from $79/month. Under about 200 members, a customer Slack channel or the free hosted [Discourse](/en/tools/discourse/) plan is still the correct answer.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Higher Logic Vanilla](/en/tools/higher-logic-vanilla/) and [Khoros](/en/tools/khoros/)** hold the enterprise B2B community share (Khoros has been owned by IgniteTech since May 2025). Pick either when you need moderation at scale, deep SEO control over community content, or a member base in the millions, and you have the procurement patience for a quote-only cycle. Their floor is well above Bettermode's; their ceiling is far higher too.
- **[Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) Customer Communities** is the pick when community activity has to write back into health scores in a Gainsight CS platform you already run. Buying Bettermode instead means building that link yourself over the GraphQL API and webhooks, which needs Growth.
- **[Discourse](/en/tools/discourse/)** is the pick for data sovereignty, self-hosting, or a technical audience. Hosted Pro is $100/month with 5 staff seats, so it now sits $49 a month under Bettermode Starter on the annual rate; Business is $500/month. You trade Design Studio and the packaged integrations for a plugin ecosystem you maintain, and for an exit path Bettermode does not offer.
- **Circle** is the fastest-growing entrant in adjacent territory, from $89/month on annual billing, but it is built for creators selling courses and paid memberships. Pick it when you are monetizing an audience, not deflecting support tickets; its branding control and CRM depth are behind Bettermode's for B2B customer communities.

## Watch-outs

- **The cheap tier and the AI tier are now ten times apart.** It is easy to start on Starter at $1,788 and find out at go-live that answers, federated search, and SSO were never in it. *Guard:* run the 14-day trial against the Growth feature set with your own imported content, measure answer quality on your ten most-asked support questions, and write down which Growth features your business case depends on before you sign either tier.
- **Monthly billing costs a third more on Starter.** $199 against $149 is a 34% premium; on Growth it is 17%. *Guard:* sign Starter annually once the trial proves the community has an owner, and treat month-to-month as a pilot rate only.
- **MCP writes are deliberately slow, and Starter's MCP is a sample.** Every write prompts for approval one at a time, and Starter allows 10 requests a day per connector, which a single digest-and-follow-up session can exhaust. *Guard:* scope MCP for reads and analysis, and route any bulk action through the GraphQL API or webhooks where you control batching; budget for Growth if MCP is part of the operating plan.
- **Toggy can cost more than the platform it runs on.** At $750/month it costs five times Starter's annual-billed monthly price, and the pricing page does not say what it does by tier. *Guard:* do not let it into a first-year contract; get a written scope of tasks and measure your moderator hours for a quarter first, so you know what an AI team would be replacing.
- **Reviewers consistently flag in-community search and the mobile app** as behind the rest of the product. *Guard:* import a representative slice of your existing forum or help-center corpus during the trial and search it as a member would; treat mobile as a browser experience and do not promise an app in your rollout plan.
