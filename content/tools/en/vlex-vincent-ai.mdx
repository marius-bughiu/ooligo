## What it is

Vincent AI is vLex's legal assistant, and since Clio closed its US$1 billion acquisition of vLex on 10 November 2025 it ships under the name Vincent by Clio inside the Clio for Enterprise line. It is not a chat window pointed at your own documents — it runs over vLex's editorial corpus of more than one billion documents from over 100 countries, the library that formed when Fastcase merged with vLex in 2023. The surface is a set of 20-plus prebuilt workflows in four groups: research (Ask a Research Question, 50-State Survey, Compare Jurisdictions), litigation (Analyze a Complaint, Analyze Pleadings, Analyze Judicial Proceedings), transactional (Analyze a Contract, Redline Analysis, Compare Documents), and litigation intelligence (Profile a Judge, Profile a Lawyer, Profile a Law Firm, Profile a Party). The closest category comparables are [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) on Westlaw and [Lexis+ AI with Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/) — research-corpus AI, not a document-review point tool.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **One subscription covers more than one jurisdiction.** The corpus spans 100-plus countries, and the 50-State Survey and Compare Jurisdictions workflows are built on it. A US-only research contract forces a second purchase the first time a matter crosses a border; this is the entry that does not.
- **The research base is already free to a lot of lawyers.** vLex partners with 60-plus bar associations to bundle vLex Fastcase as a membership benefit, and prices that base at $995+ per year. Where the bar benefit applies, the buying decision is the AI line only — a much smaller number than a fresh Westlaw or Lexis contract.
- **It reaches the drafting surface, not just the browser.** vLex documents DMS connectors for iManage, NetDocuments, and SharePoint/OneDrive, and the Clio for Word add-in — in beta since May 2026 — runs Vincent in a Word sidebar that reads the full document including existing redlines and comments, returning edits as native Track Changes.

## Pricing reality

vLex publishes no price for Vincent. There is no pricing page on vlex.com; every path ends at "Book a demo" or "Start a free trial." Two published anchors bound the conversation. First, the research base: vLex values the bar-association bundle at $995+ per year, and the Missouri Bar states the service "normally costs $1,145 per year" before the member benefit zeroes it out. Second, the AI line sits on top of that base as a separate monthly subscription — the Missouri Bar's own description lists AI-powered research, draft memos, redlines, contract review, brief drafting and M&A due diligence as the paid additions, sold to members at a discounted rate.

For a firm buying outright, expect two lines on the quote: the research seat plus the Vincent seat. Public third-party reporting puts self-serve seats that include Vincent near $65-100 per user per month, and firm-negotiated Vincent seats several times that; both are estimates, not vendor-published figures, so make the vendor break out each line before you compare against a Westlaw or Lexis renewal. Vincent Studio — the no-code workflow builder announced 12 January 2026 that lets a firm codify its own processes as reusable workflows — is restricted to large law firms and enterprise legal teams, so it is not part of what a small firm is quoted.

## Best for

Litigators and in-house counsel at solo through mid-market firms whose work crosses jurisdictions, and who already hold a bar-bundled vLex Fastcase seat — for them the incremental purchase is the AI line rather than a whole research platform. It also fits the knowledge or practice-innovation lead at a larger firm who wants firm-specific procedure encoded as Vincent Studio workflows instead of prompt discipline nobody follows.

Don't buy Vincent as your contract-drafting engine. In the independent Vals Legal AI Report (February 2025), Vincent scored 72.7% on document Q&A and 64.8% on transcript analysis but 53.6% on redlining — the research end is where it earns its keep. If Word-native drafting against a negotiation playbook is the actual job, [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) or [Ivo](/en/tools/ivo/) pencil better. Skip it too if your firm has standardized on KeyCite or Shepard's for citator workflow and will not run a second research platform: the base subscription is the expensive half of that decision, not the AI.

## Versus the alternatives

[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) holds the largest US share and brings KeyCite and Practical Law — pick it when the work is US-only and citator plus secondary-source depth is the requirement you cannot trade away. [Lexis+ AI with Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/) is the other incumbent pole — pick it when Shepard's is already the firm's citator of record and the Lexis contract is signed regardless. [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) is the AmLaw and large-enterprise choice, sold at matter and firm level with opaque pricing — pick it when the buyer is a BigLaw innovation team, not a practising lawyer with a card. [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) is the fastest-growing entrant, European in origin and built around collaborative drafting — pick it when several lawyers need to work the same document with AI in the room. The status quo alternative is a bar-bundled vLex Fastcase seat with no AI line plus a general assistant; choose Vincent over that when you need answers cited to primary law you can open and check.

## Watch-outs

- **The headline performance numbers are the vendor's own.** The "38% productivity gain" and "3.7x more reliable than leading LLMs" figures come from vLex and Clio marketing, not an independent audit; the one public third-party benchmark (Vals Legal AI Report, February 2025) is more mixed, with redlining at 53.6%. Guard: run the free trial on live matters in your own practice area, score the outputs against a partner's work, and gate the purchase on that instead of the deck.
- **No published price means an asymmetric negotiation.** Guard: anchor to the numbers vLex has published — $995+ per year for the research base, $1,145 per year list per the Missouri Bar — ask for the Vincent line quoted separately from the research line, and check whether your bar association already gives you the base and a member rate on the AI.
- **The Word add-in is a gated beta.** Clio for Word is in beta for large firms and in-house teams, access is being extended in waves, it is English (US) only, and there is no mobile version. Guard: if Word is your deployment plan, get beta access confirmed in writing for your named users before signing, and keep the web app as the fallback for anyone still queued.
- **There is no public MCP server and no documented Vincent API.** Integration runs through the Word and DMS connectors, so you cannot call vLex research from your own agents the way [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) now allows. Guard: if agent access is on your roadmap, keep a separate research path for it and treat Vincent as an end-user surface rather than infrastructure.
- **Vincent Studio is enterprise-only.** If codifying firm workflows is the reason you are buying, a small-firm quote will not include it. Guard: confirm Studio eligibility in the order form before you commit budget to a workflow-standardization project.
