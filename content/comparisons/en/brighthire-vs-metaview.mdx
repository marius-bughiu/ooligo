[BrightHire](/en/tools/brighthire/) and [Metaview](/en/tools/metaview/) both started from the "[Gong](/en/tools/gong/) for recruiting" pitch: join the interview, transcribe it, turn the conversation into a structured scorecard instead of scribbled notes. Neither is that product anymore. Zoom acquired BrightHire in December 2025 — it still ships under its own brand, with CEO Ben Sesser and the team inside Zoom — and its 2026 headline capability is candidate fraud detection running inside the live interview. Metaview repriced and repositioned as an "Agentic Recruiting Platform," selling Sourcing, Application Review, Notetaker, and Reports as separate agents on their own published tiers. Both still write good notes, so that is not the question. The question is whether your gap is *interview integrity and defensibility* or *a low-friction layer you can buy one agent at a time*.

## Where BrightHire wins

- **Fraud detection inside the interview — nothing on Metaview's side answers it.** BrightHire surfaces impersonation, AI-assisted answers, multi-device cheating, and identity inconsistencies across three stages — the application, the AI screen, and the live interview — consolidated into one risk view. Deepfake detection is powered by Zoom's own models and went to early access for Zoom Workplace customers with an active BrightHire subscription in June 2026. Coverage exists on Microsoft Teams and Google Meet but is thinner; the identity, device, and deepfake layer is the Zoom-only part. If your reqs have started attracting fraudulent candidates, this is currently a one-vendor decision.
- **Structured-hiring enforcement, not just capture.** BrightHire makes [structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) operational: standardized scorecards per interview type, required questions the platform expects interviewers to cover, and topic-coverage analysis that flags when a panel skipped a competency. Metaview drafts an excellent scorecard from the transcript, but it documents the interview that happened; BrightHire is built to change the interview that happens.
- **Decision-grade evidence and a real coaching loop.** When a panel debates a candidate, BrightHire surfaces the exact moment the contested signal appeared, with candidate-comparison views across the slate — debriefs move from "I felt it went well" to "here's the clip." The coaching agent flags interviewers who talked over the candidate, ran most of the call, or skipped required topics, per interviewer over time. Metaview surfaces similar calibration signals; BrightHire packages them as a managed program, which is what an enterprise TA function buying a behavior change actually wants.
- **Async AI screening from the same vendor.** BrightHire Screen runs first-round interviews as a configurable AI interviewer — the same structured questions for every candidate, scored against your rubric — and can be bought standalone or bundled with Interview Intelligence.

## Where Metaview wins

- **Published prices and a free tier that is actually usable.** The Notetaker agent is free for 25 calls a month with basic transcription, and $60 per user per month on Pro for unlimited calls and priority processing. BrightHire publishes no list price at any tier. For a team that wants to prove value before opening a procurement cycle, that difference alone decides it.
- **Platform-neutral by default.** Metaview does not care what you run your interviews on. BrightHire works across platforms too, but its strongest 2026 capability is gated behind Zoom Workplace — so a Teams or Meet shop pays BrightHire's premium for a partially reduced version of the thing that justifies the premium.
- **Buy one agent, not a suite.** The Sourcing agent is priced on its own ladder — free for the first 100 profiles, $100 per user per month for 200 profiles, $300 for unlimited — so a lean team can switch on sourcing without a new vendor or a new contract. The guard: this works agent by agent, not for the whole platform. The "all agents" option is Enterprise-only at custom pricing, so the self-serve path stops when you want everything at once.
- **Fastest time-to-value.** Metaview joins the call and the structured notes are waiting when it ends; a recruiter can be live the same afternoon, pushing competency-aligned scorecard fields into [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), [Lever](/en/tools/lever/), and [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/). BrightHire's value is real but lands after a 30–60 day rollout and an adoption mandate.

## Pricing reality

The gap is narrower than third-party trackers suggest, and the direction of the error matters. Metaview's notetaker is widely quoted at $20 per user per month; the vendor's own pricing page no longer shows that number anywhere. Pro is $60 per user per month — roughly $720 per seat per year — with the free tier capped at 25 calls a month. BrightHire prices on seats plus interview volume across three tiers (Recruiters, Teams, Enterprises), all demo-gated. Vendr transaction data puts typical annual contracts from about $15K for small teams running fewer than 100 interviews a month to $100K-plus for enterprise deployments, with the common 10–30-user band landing at $20–50K a year. Normalize a 20-seat team and BrightHire runs roughly $1,000–$2,500 per seat per year against Metaview's $720 — call it 1.5x to 3.5x, not the order-of-magnitude spread the stale $20 figure implies. Two things move the math: BrightHire's seat count usually includes hiring managers and interviewers, not just recruiters, and its volume component makes a heavy hiring quarter cost more. Metaview's per-agent model cuts the other way — adding Sourcing at $100–300 per user per month overtakes the notetaker line item fast.

## How you operate them

This remains the deciding axis, with one new variable. BrightHire rewards an org that will *mandate* a hiring-process change and wants the vendor to enforce and measure it — structured interviews, coached interviewers, evidence-backed decisions, an audit trail a legal or DEI review can lean on, plus AI screening and fraud checks at the front door. The more of that program you run, the more the price pays back; buy it as a passive recorder and you have overpaid badly. The new variable is your meeting platform: on Zoom you get BrightHire's full fraud stack, off it a reduced one. Metaview rewards a team whose constraint is note-taking friction today, that wants to be live this week on a published price, and that may later switch on another agent without procurement. Adoption is the shared risk — both are shelfware if leaders don't require interviewers to use them.

## Verdict

- **Pick BrightHire** when interview integrity and defensibility is the goal — candidate fraud you can actually detect, enforced structured hiring, decision-grade evidence for committees, a managed coaching program, or async AI screening at mid-market-to-enterprise scale. The case is strongest if you already run interviews on Zoom Workplace.
- **Pick Metaview** when the gap is note-taking friction and debrief quality, when you need a published price and a free tier to prove value, when time-to-value beats enforcement, or when you want to add a sourcing agent later without a new contract.
- **Pick neither** when [interview intelligence](/en/learn/interview-intelligence/) isn't really your gap: if you need raw transcripts, a generic notetaker or a [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) Skill run over meeting transcripts is far cheaper; if you want a third pole on the shortlist, [Pillar](/en/tools/pillar/) is the one to add; and if your ATS scorecards are already disciplined, you may not need a dedicated layer at all.

Default pick: choosing in a vacuum, start with Metaview. Most teams' real bottleneck is interviewers typing instead of listening and debriefs running on memory — Metaview fixes that the same day, on a price you can read without a sales call. Two conditions flip it: a mandate to enforce structured hiring and defensible decision evidence, or candidate fraud showing up in your funnel while your interviews already happen on Zoom. The second is the one requirement Metaview has no answer for today.
