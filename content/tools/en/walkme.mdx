## What it is

WalkMe is the digital adoption platform that defined the category: a JavaScript and desktop overlay that draws walkthroughs, tooltips, launchers and a self-help menu on top of software you did not build — Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, an ERP, a homegrown portal. Founded in 2011 and listed on Nasdaq in June 2021, it was bought by SAP for $14.00 a share, about $1.5B in equity value; the deal was announced on 5 June 2024 and closed on 12 September 2024, and WalkMe was delisted.

It is still sold on its own, including to companies that run no SAP at all, and SAP has committed publicly to keep supporting non-SAP applications. What changed is where the roadmap points. WalkMe's copilot concept, WalkMe X, was folded into SAP's Joule, and WalkMe is now SAP's replacement for its own training product, SAP Enable Now: no new Enable Now contracts since April 2025, the successor WalkMe Learning Arc generally available on 8 December 2025, and Enable Now Cloud renewals and extensions ending on 30 April 2027 (McCoy Partners, citing SAP). That ownership is the buying question this page answers.

## What you get today

- **The overlay.** Smart Walk-Thrus, SmartTips, Smart Highlights, the Action Bar and WalkMe Menu, on web and desktop, built on WalkMe's DeepUI element recognition.
- **Agentic authoring and analytics (Q3 '26 release).** AI Authoring builds and edits content from a free-text prompt — images, conditions, CSS, bulk edits — though Smart Walk-Thrus, SmartTips and element selection are not supported yet. AI Insights answers adoption questions in prose and returns charts you can pin to a dashboard. AI Chat added a SharePoint integration, and the Action Bar now takes file uploads. US and EU tenants got the release on 31 August 2026; SAP-hosted tenants on 28 September 2026. The AI features roll out only to accounts that have signed WalkMe's AI terms.
- **Learning Arc.** AI-built courses, simulations and assessments, with a one-click transfer into SAP SuccessFactors Learning scheduled for Q4 2026 (WalkMe, 6 October 2026).
- **Discovery analytics.** App discovery, form and workflow analytics, and license-optimization reports — useful for finding shelfware across a large application estate.

## Pricing reality

WalkMe publishes no rate card; every deal is quoted. Third-party contract data built on Vendr purchases puts the median at **$39,000–$44,000 per year** depending on the snapshot, with reported contracts running from about **$14,400 to $197,000**. Third-party estimates add 20–30% of first-year contract value for implementation services, plus the content labor to build and maintain flows, and report renewal uplifts of 5–10%. Treat $40K a year for one application as the realistic entry point and model year one at 1.3x that before content headcount.

If you are an SAP customer, the price question is different: SAP sells WalkMe editions tied to its own applications (WalkMe for SAP SuccessFactors is one), and an Enable Now customer is being moved toward WalkMe either way. Price it against what you already hold before taking a standalone quote.

## Best for

Enterprise IT, L&D and Customer Success leaders rolling SAP applications — SuccessFactors, Ariba, Concur, S/4HANA — out to thousands of employees, especially teams leaving Enable Now before the 2027 renewal cutoff. It is also a defensible pick for a large mixed estate where SAP is the system that matters most, because no other DAP will be as close to SAP's release cycle.

## Do not buy it if

You are a product-led SaaS company onboarding signups into your own product. A ~$40K floor and a multi-month implementation are the wrong shape against [Userpilot](/en/tools/userpilot/), [Appcues](/en/tools/appcues/), [Userflow](/en/tools/userflow/) or [Chameleon](/en/tools/chameleon/), which install in a day and start in the hundreds per month. Skip it too if your estate is mostly non-SAP and you want the adoption layer owned by a vendor with no stake in which applications you buy.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Whatfix](/en/tools/whatfix/)** — the second-largest DAP and the largest independent one. Pick it when the portfolio is mixed (Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, an EHR) and SAP is not the anchor; its standard contracts land in a lower band, roughly $24K–$37K a year on third-party data.
- **[Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/)** — pick it when the application is one your engineers ship and product analytics is the primary job, with guidance second.
- **Guidde** — the fastest-growing entrant, on a $50M Series B led by PSG Equity in February 2026. Pick it when the deliverable is quick video documentation made by non-specialists, not a governed in-app guidance layer.
- **[Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/)** — pick its in-app engagement module when the buyer is a CS team already on Gainsight CS and the goal is customer adoption of your own product rather than employee adoption of purchased software.

## Watch-outs

- **Non-SAP investment is a promise, not a roadmap.** The recent headline features — Learning Arc, SuccessFactors transfer, Smart Highlights pulling SAP CX Sales Cloud and Ariba data — point at SAP. *Guard:* if your critical applications are non-SAP, get named non-SAP connectors, support terms and a price cap for the renewal written into the order form, and re-check the release notes before every renewal.
- **Flows break when the host UI changes.** G2 reviewers (4.5/5 across roughly 570 reviews) name setup difficulty and overlay misfires on slow-loading pages. *Guard:* assign one content owner per major application, treat each host-app release as a regression test, and use publish version history (added in Q3 '26) to roll back a broken flow instead of hot-fixing in production.
- **Enable Now content does not carry over cleanly.** Simulations import into Learning Arc, but SAP Companion in-app content has to be rebuilt natively. *Guard:* inventory Companion content now and budget the rebuild before 30 April 2027, not at the renewal deadline.
- **No MCP server.** WalkMe exposes APIs and an AI Insights layer, but nothing an external agent connects to natively. *Guard:* if agent-driven adoption reporting is planned, scope a wrapper over the export APIs instead of waiting for a connector.
