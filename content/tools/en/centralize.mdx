## What it is

Centralize is an AI account-mapping and relationship workspace for enterprise deal teams. It builds an org chart for each target account from your CRM, email, calendar, and call recordings, marks which buying-committee seats are covered and which are empty, and suggests contacts to fill multithreading gaps. It then looks for warm paths in: who on your team already knows someone at the account, through synced LinkedIn connections, email history, or shared past employers. An assistant called Centra answers questions like "who actually owns the budget?" and flags when a champion leaves or a new decision-maker arrives. The founders call it a "deal GPS."

The closest category reference is LinkedIn [Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/)'s Relationship Map plus TeamLink, rebuilt around your first-party data instead of LinkedIn's graph.

The company is Collate Labs, Inc., founded in 2023 in San Francisco by Rachit Kataria (CEO) and Will Wang (CTO, who launched Slack Huddles), and went through Y Combinator's Winter 2024 batch. On 2026-07-29 it announced a $15M Series A led by NEA, which together with a $4M seed led by Salesforce Ventures brings total funding to $19M. Named customers include Brex, CoreWeave, Cognition, Decagon, Webflow, LangChain, and MaintainX.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

Enterprise deals stall on single-threading: one champion, no economic buyer, and nobody noticing until the champion changes jobs. Most stacks spread the fix across three tools: a CRM with stale contact roles, a call recorder that knows who attended, and Sales Navigator for the org chart. Centralize joins those into one live map per account. It also ships an MCP server on the Pro plan, so reps can create and update account tasks from Claude or ChatGPT, plus a read-only beta API with seven endpoints (accounts, connections, org chart, suggested contacts). API access requires contacting the team.

## Pricing reality

The pricing page, checked 2026-11-23, lists three tiers:

- **Free, $0:** 5 Standard accounts, automatic org charts, LinkedIn warm-connection sync, Chrome extension, email and calendar integrations.
- **Pro, $49 per user per month:** 50 Standard accounts and 5 Smart accounts, Centra, MCP access, suggested contacts, drafted outreach, and 1,000 email plus 100 phone enrichment credits per user. Extra Smart accounts cost $5 per account per month. 30-day trial with no card.
- **Enterprise, custom:** adds the Salesforce integration, call-recorder and sequencer integrations, SSO, MFA, and Slack support. Billed annually.

Two numbers matter more than the $49. First, only Smart accounts get live org charts, relationship scoring, and missing-stakeholder detection. An enterprise AE with 30 strategic accounts needs 25 extra Smart accounts, which takes Pro from $49 to $174 per user per month, about $2,090 per year. Second, the Salesforce integration is Enterprise-only, so any team that wants maps written back to the CRM is on a custom quote. Centralize publishes no Enterprise figure. When the round was announced, Crunchbase reported pricing by accounts under management with unlimited seats, while the current page prices Pro per user; ask which model your Enterprise quote uses.

## Best for

**Best for:** enterprise AEs and RevOps leads at B2B software companies selling six-figure deals to buying committees of five or more people, on Salesforce, with 10-50 named strategic accounts per rep. The ROI is clearest when warm relationships, often an executive's, already exist inside the company but nobody can see them.

**Not for you if** you run a velocity motion with one or two stakeholders per deal, or your system of record is HubSpot. HubSpot is not on the published integrations list, and the native CRM integration is Salesforce-only and Enterprise-only.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[LinkedIn Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/)**: the default most teams already pay for. Relationship Map and Relationship Explorer cover org charts and warm paths from LinkedIn's graph, and TeamLink (Advanced and Advanced Plus only, about $1,800 per seat per year on annual Advanced) shows colleague connections. Stay on it when LinkedIn data is good enough; add Centralize when the relationships that matter live in email, calendars, and call recordings that LinkedIn cannot see.
- **Introhive**: the incumbent in relationship intelligence, strongest in professional services and on Microsoft Dynamics or Intapp. Quote-only, no self-serve. Pick it for firm-wide relationship capture across partners; pick Centralize for deal-level buying-committee work in software sales.
- **[UserGems](/en/tools/usergems/) and [Champify](/en/tools/champify/)**: pick these when the problem is champion job changes across the whole CRM, not mapping a handful of big deals. They complement Centralize rather than replace it.
- **[Day AI](/en/tools/day-ai/)**: an AI-native CRM that builds relationship context from email and meetings. Pick it when you want to replace the CRM, not add a layer on top of Salesforce.

## Watch-outs

- **Inbox and calendar access is the whole product.** Maps are only as good as the mailboxes connected, and some reps will refuse. *Guard:* get legal sign-off on the SOC 2 Type 2 report and data-retention controls before rollout, then require connection for the pilot team only.
- **Free and Pro maps stay outside Salesforce.** Without Enterprise, contact roles and stakeholder gaps never reach the CRM or forecast reviews. *Guard:* price Enterprise before the trial ends, or limit the pilot to accounts where reps work from Centralize directly.
- **Young vendor, shifting pricing model.** A nine-person team at YC listing time, with a pricing unit that changed within months. *Guard:* sign 12 months at most, with a price cap on renewal, and export org charts through the API each quarter.
- **Warm-path scoring is vendor-reported.** The win-rate claims come from Centralize's own launch materials. *Guard:* run a 60-day A/B on matched accounts and measure stakeholders engaged per opportunity before expanding seats.
