## What it is

Alt Legal is cloud docketing software built for trademarks. It pulls status and deadlines from IP offices, calculates the dates, sends reminders, and gives clients and foreign associates a view-only window into the docket. It also handles patents, designs, copyrights, domains, and disputes, but its founder and CEO Nehal Madhani calls the product "trademark forward." Patents ride along; they are not the design center.

The company is New York-based, founded in 2013 by Madhani (formerly an attorney at Kirkland & Ellis) with an IP paralegal, and has grown by buying smaller competitors. On 16 April 2026 it acquired UK-based WebTMS (founded 1998, 500+ clients), its sixth acquisition after MarkTend (2019), Towergate's §2(d) Citation Watch (2022), TM Cloud's docketing business (2023), and WeberMark (2024), which became the Alt Legal Assist paralegal service. A growth investment led by SSM Partners followed in December 2025, amount undisclosed. Customer counts vary by source: LawNext reported "more than 1,300 organizations" in April 2026, and the vendor homepage now says 2,000. Treat both as vendor-supplied.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **The docket updates itself.** Alt Legal claims data connections to 180+ IP offices, with direct USPTO and CIPO connections for the deepest automation. For a US-heavy trademark docket, status changes and deadlines arrive without a paralegal keying them in.
- **Filing starts in the docket.** Intake forms collect application details from clients or colleagues, a TEAS connection auto-fills the USPTO application, and the Goods & Services Composer builds ID Manual-compliant descriptions with the class fees shown.
- **Watching comes from the same vendor.** The Trademark Protection add-on combines §2(d) Watch, which alerts you when one of your US marks is cited against someone else's application, with Application Match, a weekly scan of new filings worldwide for overlapping marks.
- **Clients see the docket at no extra cost.** Unlimited users and unlimited third-party access are in every tier, so outside counsel and clients stop emailing for status.
- **Integrations cover the basics.** Calendar sync with Outlook, Google, and Apple; an Outlook add-in that files emails to matters; a [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) sync for dates, matters, and contacts; and an API with no public documentation.

## Pricing reality

This is one of the few IP management vendors with a public price list. Pricing is by docketed matter count, not seats (altlegal.com/pricing, checked 30 October 2026):

| Matters | Docketing | Trademark Protection add-on |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 50 | $60/month | +$30/month |
| Up to 100 | $100/month | +$40/month |
| Up to 200 | $195/month | +$60/month |
| Up to 400 | $295/month | +$95/month |
| 400+ | Quote | Quote |

The real-world band runs from about $1,080 a year (50 matters with watching) to $4,680 a year (400 matters with watching). There is no setup fee, and migration, support, and training are included. Above 400 matters you get a quote, so a 2,000-mark corporate portfolio cannot budget from this page. For contrast, enterprise IP management systems such as [Anaqua](/en/tools/anaqua/) start in the tens of thousands of dollars a year.

A matter is one IP right, active or inactive, in any jurisdiction. Users, deadlines, classes, and reference-only records such as Madrid designations are not counted.

## Best for

Trademark attorneys, paralegals, and brand-protection leads at boutique IP firms, general-practice firms with a trademark book, and in-house teams managing roughly 50 to a few thousand marks, where trademarks are most of the job and the budget will never clear an enterprise IPMS business case.

**Not for you if** you run a large patent portfolio that needs annuity payments, foreign-associate instruction workflows, and patent-office data feeds. Alt Legal stores patents but does not automate them the way it automates trademarks.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Anaqua](/en/tools/anaqua/)** — one of the two enterprise leaders in IP management. Pick it when patents and trademarks are both large, you need annuities and spend forecasting in the same record, and you can staff an administrator.
- **Clarivate** — the other leader, through IPfolio, FoundationIP, and the CompuMark trademark data behind [IPOne](/en/tools/clarivate-ipone/). Pick it when you already license CompuMark for clearance and want one vendor for search and docket.
- **[Questel](/en/tools/questel/)** — pick Equinox plus Markify when the portfolio is centered on Europe and you want search, docketing, and renewal services from one supplier.
- **[Tradespace](/en/tools/tradespace/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in IP management ($15M Series A, January 2026), but it is patent-first. Pick it only when patent drafting and portfolio management are the real problem; for a trademark docket it is the wrong tool.

## Watch-outs

- **AI is thin.** The only named AI feature is a natural-language search across matters (AI Filtering Assistant). There is no office-action drafting and no MCP server. **Guard:** buy Alt Legal for docketing and watching, and budget AI drafting separately; don't let an "Automation, AI & Expertise" banner stand in for a feature list.
- **Serial acquisitions mean product consolidation is coming.** WebTMS customers were told "business as usual" in the near term, with integration steered by a customer advisory board and no published end date for either product. **Guard:** if you are on WebTMS, TM Cloud, or another acquired product, get the support horizon and migration terms for your platform in writing at your next renewal.
- **Prices above 400 matters are a quote.** The transparent pricing stops at 400 matters. **Guard:** count your matters the way Alt Legal counts them (inactive rights included) before the call, and ask for the per-tier price to be fixed for the contract term.
- **Coverage claims are uneven.** "180+ IP offices" is the vendor's number, but the deepest automation is USPTO and CIPO. **Guard:** before migrating, test your five highest-volume foreign jurisdictions against the docket data in the free trial and compare deadlines with your current system.
