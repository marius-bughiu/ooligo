## What it is

HeyReach is LinkedIn sending infrastructure for teams that run one campaign across many LinkedIn accounts. You connect accounts as "senders," and the platform rotates outreach across all of them, so each account stays at low daily volume while the campaign as a whole scales. It runs server-side rather than as a browser extension, and it does not send email — [Smartlead](/en/tools/smartlead/) and [Instantly](/en/tools/instantly/) integrations carry that channel. The tool it most resembles in function is [Expandi](/en/tools/expandi/); the difference is the account and pricing model, which is built for agencies rather than for individual reps.

Since mid-2026 it is also something it was not before: a LinkedIn sending layer an AI agent can drive. HeyReach ships an official MCP server on every paid plan and an open-source CLI that wraps the full API, so a [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) session can build a campaign from a lead segment, write per-lead openers, and pull replies without anyone opening the HeyReach UI.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Sender rotation is the product, not a feature.** Every LinkedIn account carries its own invitation ceiling. One rep on one account hits it and stops. Twenty senders on one campaign multiply throughput without pushing any single account harder — the only structural way to raise LinkedIn volume.
- **Billed per sender account, not per human.** Users, VAs and client logins are free on every tier, so an agency's whole delivery team works in one workspace and the bill tracks connected LinkedIn accounts. Expandi and Dripify bill per seat, the wrong axis for a team of four managing thirty client accounts.
- **It is the LinkedIn step in an agent or automation, not a separate UI.** REST API, webhooks and the MCP server are included from the Growth plan up. That makes HeyReach the LinkedIn action in a [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) table, an [n8n](/en/tools/n8n/) flow, or a Claude chat — each workspace gets its own MCP connection URL and key, so client data stays separated per workspace.

## Pricing reality

Three self-serve tiers, priced monthly, with 10% off for quarterly and 20% off for annual billing:

- **Growth** — $79 per sender per month for 1-9 senders, $59 per sender once you have 10 or more (annual: $64 and $48). Capped at 50 senders. Each sender gets a dedicated residential proxy, and 100 one-time enrichment credits.
- **Agency** — $999 a month for 25 senders or $1,399 for 50 (annual: $800 and $1,120). Adds one white-label brand, done-for-you onboarding and a dedicated Slack channel, plus 1,000 enrichment credits. You bring your own proxies.
- **Unlimited** — $2,999 a month (annual $2,399), 3,000 enrichment credits, extra white-label brands at $500 each. "Unlimited" is capped at 300 senders under the fair-use policy.

A custom "Done for you" tier runs the outbound motion for you, and an early-stage program discounts accounts under $250,000 ARR with fewer than five people. The trial is 14 days, no card, up to 3 LinkedIn accounts, and does not convert to paid automatically.

The volume price produces two numbers worth knowing. First, **10 senders cost less than 9**: nine at $79 is $711, ten at $59 is $590, so a team sitting at nine should add the tenth. Second, **Agency 25 becomes cheaper than Growth at 17 senders** — 17 × $59 is $1,003 against $999, and the annual crossover lands at the same count ($816 versus $800). Agency 50 at $1,399 is cheaper than Growth from 24 senders. Above 50 senders both Growth and Agency are closed, and the only self-serve step is Unlimited at $2,999: about $10 per sender at the 300 cap, but still around $50 per sender at 60.

That crossover has a cost the plan table hides: Growth includes a residential proxy per sender and Agency does not. Price your own proxy supply for 17-25 accounts before switching, or the "cheaper" plan is not. Every sender also needs a real LinkedIn account behind it, and most teams at scale buy [Sales Navigator](/en/tools/sales-navigator/) per sender for search and lead export. Model cost per active sender all-in, not the plan line.

## Best for

Agencies and outbound teams running five or more LinkedIn sender accounts against shared campaigns — lead-gen agencies with client accounts, and in-house GTM teams that have put founders, AEs, and SDRs on the same sequence. It is also the right pick for GTM engineers who want LinkedIn steps fired from Clay, n8n or an AI agent through an API or MCP rather than managed by hand.

**Do not buy it for a single sender.** At one connected account, $79 a month buys rotation you cannot use; [Waalaxy](/en/tools/waalaxy/) or Dripify do single-account sequencing for $40-60. The economics turn in HeyReach's favor at three or more senders, and decisively at ten, where the per-sender price drops to $59.

## Versus the alternatives

**Expandi** is the revenue leader in cloud LinkedIn automation — roughly 3,700 customers and around $10M ARR on the last public figures — and it wins on sequence logic and personalization depth. Pick Expandi when reps send fewer, more considered messages and each rep runs their own account; pick HeyReach when many accounts feed one campaign and per-account economics decide the bill.

**Waalaxy** has the largest installed base in the category, reporting over 150,000 users and around 10,000 paying customers, and it is the cheapest way for one person to run LinkedIn sequences. Pick Waalaxy for solo prospecting or a two-rep team. It has no answer for multi-account rotation, so it stops scaling exactly where HeyReach starts.

**[lemlist](/en/tools/lemlist/)** is the fastest-growing threat to the standalone category, because it absorbs LinkedIn steps into an email sequencer instead of selling LinkedIn separately. Pick lemlist when email is the primary channel and LinkedIn is a touch inside the sequence. Pick HeyReach when LinkedIn is the primary channel and email is the follow-up — running both tools is common rather than contradictory.

If LinkedIn is not already producing replies for you manually, none of these fix that. Automation multiplies an existing response rate; it does not create one.

## Watch-outs

- **LinkedIn deplatformed the vendor, not the product — and the distinction is doing a lot of work.** On 25 March 2026 LinkedIn removed HeyReach's company page and restricted four executives' personal profiles with no notice. HeyReach states customer campaigns and infrastructure were untouched, and the pricing page still sells LinkedIn senders in November 2026. The signal is that LinkedIn now enforces against vendors, not only against end users. **Guard:** pipe connections, replies and conversation history out through the API or webhooks into your CRM continuously, so a vendor-level action never takes your outreach history with it. Treat competitor-written roundups claiming HeyReach left LinkedIn as wrong; check the live pricing page instead.
- **The channel is rented and your sender accounts are the collateral.** HeyReach's own documentation is explicit that the daily caps you configure are maximums, and that actual delivery depends on account health rather than the number you typed. The platform will not stop you from setting limits that get an account restricted. **Guard:** ramp new senders over two to three weeks, set per-action caps well under the maximum, and spread each campaign across enough senders that one restriction costs throughput rather than a pipeline.
- **An agent with the MCP key can launch campaigns across every sender in the workspace.** The MCP server is not read-only: it creates campaigns, writes messages and acts on replies. A bad prompt or a wrong lead list goes out under real LinkedIn identities. **Guard:** give agents their own workspace, keep a human approval step before any campaign goes live, and rotate the key when access changes — generating a new key also changes the connection URL, so old configs stop working.
- **No native email means a second bill and a second tool.** Multichannel here is an integration with Smartlead, Instantly or EmailBison, not a built-in channel. **Guard:** budget the email sending layer alongside HeyReach from the start, and decide up front which tool owns the reply — running both inboxes without a rule produces duplicate follow-ups to the same prospect.
