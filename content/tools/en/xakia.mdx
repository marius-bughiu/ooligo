## What it is

Xakia is [matter management](/en/learn/matter-management/) for in-house legal teams that cannot fund an enterprise legal management rollout. Matters, tasks, deadlines, [legal intake](/en/learn/legal-intake/), contract records, outside counsel spend, and corporate entities live in one system with dashboards over the top. Jodie Baker, a former in-house lawyer, founded it in Melbourne in 2016; it runs from Melbourne and Kansas City and names Coca-Cola Europacific Partners, Nissan, Virgin, Southwire, and Axiom as customers. The design centre is a team of roughly 2 to 20 lawyers.

What separates Xakia from [Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/), [Onit](/en/tools/onit/), and [LawVu](/en/tools/lawvu/) is not a feature. It is that Xakia publishes its prices and the other three do not. For a legal ops manager who has to put a number in a budget before anyone will approve a vendor evaluation, that is the whole difference.

Xakia is still founder-owned and privately held, with no venture funding, in a segment where Brightflag now belongs to Wolters Kluwer. That independence is why the pricing page reads like a price list rather than a lead-capture form.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **You can size the contract before you take a sales call.** Four tiers are published at a per-user monthly rate, so a 6-lawyer team can compute its own annual number in about thirty seconds and walk into procurement with it. Every direct competitor requires a discovery call to learn the same thing.
- **Requesters and outside counsel do not consume seats.** Unlimited internal requesters and the Xakia Connect portal for external counsel carry no additional fee, and law firms are not charged per invoice. The seat count is your legal team, not your company — this is the mechanic that makes per-seat pricing viable here at all.
- **It covers four jobs, not one.** Intake and triage, matter and task tracking, [outside counsel spend](/en/learn/outside-counsel-management/), and [entity management](/en/learn/entity-management/) sit in one record set. Small teams otherwise buy three products or run two of the four in Excel.
- **The document integrations are the real ones.** iManage and NetDocuments from the Professional tier, plus an Outlook add-in rebuilt for Microsoft 365 in the first half of 2026 that files outgoing email to the matter and auto-files replies. Filing from the inbox is where matter management either becomes habit or dies.
- **Matter data now reaches your AI workspace.** The Xakia MCP Connector works from Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot Studio, so a GC can ask "which open matters with firm X are over budget" without opening Xakia. It is included on Enterprise and All-In; Advance and Professional get it only for a limited trial window.
- **2026 shipped work is intake, search, and narrow AI.** Conditional logic and routing on intake forms, a rebuilt Smart Search that reads inside documents with saved searches and proximity operators, and Xakia AI: playbook-based contract review and redlining in Word, contract extraction and summaries, and AI invoice review against billing guidelines.

## Pricing reality

Xakia publishes four per-user monthly tiers: **Advance $100**, **Professional $125**, **Enterprise $155**, and **All-In $230**. Spend management, intake, the contract repository, dashboards, API access, and SSO start at Advance. DMS integrations and document automation start at Professional. Contract approval and signing workflows, intake workflows, Smart Search, entity management, and the MCP Connector require Enterprise. All-In is the only tier sold through a demo rather than a self-serve trial.

AI is metered separately and this is where the sticker price moves. AI access is **+$50/user/month on Advance** and **+$30 on Professional**, both with usage caps; Enterprise includes it with caps; All-In removes the caps, subject to fair use. The AI-inclusive real price is therefore $150-230 per seat per month, not $100.

Terms are month-to-month, annual billing takes up to 10% off, and there is a 14-day trial with no credit card. At list monthly rates, a 5-lawyer team on Enterprise runs **$9,300/year** ($8,370 at the full annual discount), or $13,800 on All-In. Non-legal contract users are a separate flat annual **Stakeholders** add-on — **$2,000/year for up to 5 users**, $16,000 for up to 50 — covering the contract repository, intake, approval and signing, and entities but not matters or spend, with some features requiring the legal team to be on Enterprise. For contrast, Vendr puts the median Brightflag contract at **$38,359/year**. The crossover is around 20 legal seats: above that, Xakia's per-seat meter costs more than a spend-based one.

## Best for

Legal ops managers and general counsel running a 2-20 lawyer in-house team at a company of roughly 100-2,000 employees, who need matters, intake, and spend off spreadsheets this quarter and have a budget in the $10K-40K range rather than the $50K-150K an ELM implementation consumes. It is the strongest pick when the buying constraint is approval speed, because you can show finance the price before you show them the vendor.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Thomson Reuters Legal Tracker](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-legal-tracker/)** — the market-share incumbent in in-house e-billing, with the largest base of firms already submitting invoices through it. Pick it when firm-network coverage is the requirement and you have the budget and the implementation runway.
- **[Onit](/en/tools/onit/) / SimpleLegal** — the other volume incumbent in mid-market ELM. Pick it when you need configurable workflow breadth (legal holds, approval matrices, complex matter intake) rather than a fixed product shape; SimpleLegal is its spend-priced mid-market entry point.
- **[LawVu](/en/tools/lawvu/)** — the fastest-moving entrant in the segment and Xakia's closest structural rival: a matter workspace first, with e-billing attached, an agentic LegalOS layer launched in June 2026, and a write-capable MCP server. Pick LawVu when you want agents acting on your records rather than answering about them, and you are willing to run a quoted sales process to get it.
- **[Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/)** — now part of Wolters Kluwer. Pick it when outside counsel spend above roughly $5M is the actual problem and AI invoice review is the reason you are buying. Its meter is spend, not seats, so it does not reprice as your legal headcount grows.
- **[Athennian](/en/tools/athennian/) or [Diligent Entities](/en/tools/diligent-entities/)** — pick these when entity and governance management is the primary job rather than one module of five.

Below about two full-time lawyers, the honest answer is a shared inbox, a spreadsheet, and a naming convention. Matter management pays back when the intake volume is no longer trackable in one person's head.

## Watch-outs

- **The CLM is a repository with signing attached, not a contract workflow engine.** Reviewers are consistent that you can store and execute contracts but cannot build good contract workflows in it. Guard: run your worst contract type — the one with a four-approver matrix — through the 14-day trial before signing, and plan to pair Xakia with [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/), [Juro](/en/tools/juro/), or [Agiloft](/en/tools/agiloft/) if [contract lifecycle management](/en/learn/contract-lifecycle-management/) is a first-order requirement rather than a filing cabinet.
- **Per-seat pricing inverts the advantage as the team grows.** At 25 legal seats, Enterprise is $46,500/year at list, above Brightflag's reported median, and every lawyer you hire raises the bill by $1,860/year. Guard: model the cost at your three-year headcount rather than today's, and get annual-commitment pricing written for the seat count you expect to reach, not the one you start at.
- **The MCP Connector is an Enterprise feature that you can try on any tier.** Advance and Professional teams can wire it into Claude or Copilot during the trial window, build a habit around it, and then lose it. Guard: if querying matter and spend data from an AI workspace is part of the reason you are buying, budget Enterprise from day one, and confirm in writing which objects the connector reads and whether it can write before you plan workflows on it.
- **AI usage caps are real and the caps are not published.** Advance, Professional, and Enterprise all carry AI "usage caps", and only All-In at $230 removes them. Guard: get the cap expressed as documents or reviews per month in the order form, and measure your true monthly contract-review volume during the trial before choosing between Enterprise and All-In.
- **Migration off an incumbent is the reported friction point.** Teams moving from another system describe the transition as tedious and the reformatting as a real adjustment cost. Guard: scope historical import as a fixed-fee implementation line with a named field-mapping document, and migrate open matters only — leave closed matters in the old system as a read-only archive.
