## What it is

Copper (formerly ProsperWorks) is a per-seat CRM built for companies that run on Google Workspace. The pitch is that the CRM lives where the work already happens: a Gmail sidebar shows the contact, company and open opportunity next to the thread, Calendar and Drive files attach to records, and new contacts are suggested from email traffic instead of typed in. Copper's own comparison material says it plainly: it works with Google Workspace only, and it has no native calling.

That narrowness is the product. A five-person agency or services firm living in Gmail gets a usable pipeline in days with almost no admin. A team on Microsoft 365, or one that wants the CRM to dial, sequence and forecast, is shopping in the wrong aisle.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks now

- **The Zendesk Sell retirement.** Zendesk will retire Sell in August 2027, and Copper is actively selling into that migration with a January 2026 guide to Sell alternatives. Copper's pull for Sell teams is its Zendesk integration, which shows support tickets inside the Copper record, so a support-led SMB keeps the ticket-to-deal view it had in Sell.
- **CopperGPT.** Since 20 November 2025 every Copper plan can use CopperGPT, a custom GPT inside ChatGPT that answers questions and writes summaries over leads, people, companies, pipelines, tasks and activities. It is strictly read-only, goes through the Developer API, respects each user's record permissions, sees email metadata but not email bodies, and needs a paid ChatGPT plan on the user's side.
- **AI email help on the paid tiers.** An AI template generator and an AI email rewriter ship on Professional and Business. Copper also publishes prompt guides for using Google Gemini against Copper data in Chrome and Sheets, but there is no Copper-built Gemini connector and no first-party MCP server.
- **A plain REST API.** `https://api.copper.com/developer_api/` with API-key or OAuth 2.0 auth, webhooks, and a limit of 180 requests per minute (bulk endpoints add a 3-requests-per-second cap). That is enough to wire Copper into n8n or Make for the automation the lower tiers lack.

## Pricing

Per seat per month in USD, before tax, checked on copper.com today. Annual billing is up to 26% cheaper. There is no free plan; the trial needs no card, and Copper's own blog describes it as 14 days.

- **Basic** — $29 monthly, $23 annual. **2,500 contacts.** Pipelines, task automation, project management, contact enrichment.
- **Professional** — $69 monthly, $59 annual. **15,000 contacts.** Adds workflow automation, bulk email, reporting, integrations, and the AI email template generator and rewriter.
- **Business** — $134 monthly, $99 annual. **Unlimited contacts.** Adds email series, custom reports, multi-currency and premium support.

The contact cap, not the seat count, decides the tier. A 5-seat team on Professional annual pays $3,540/year. The same team with 20,000 contacts is forced onto Business at $5,940/year, 68% more for the same people. Ten seats on Business annual is $11,880/year. For comparison, ten seats on [Pipedrive](/en/tools/pipedrive/) Growth annual run $4,680/year and ten on [Zoho CRM](/en/tools/zoho-crm/) Enterprise $4,800/year, while [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) Sales Hub Professional is $10,800/year plus a required $1,500 onboarding fee.

## Best for

Owners and ops leads at 3–20 person agencies, consultancies and B2B services firms that run entirely on Google Workspace, sell through relationships rather than outbound volume, and want the pipeline to fill itself from Gmail. Also a fair landing spot for a small, support-led Zendesk Sell team that must move before August 2027 and wants to keep Zendesk.

## Not for

Teams on Microsoft 365 or Outlook. Outbound teams that need a dialer, multi-step sequences at volume, or a sales engagement platform: Copper has no native calling, and its own comparison post says it does not support mass outbound email even though Professional lists bulk email. RevOps teams that need agents writing to the CRM: CopperGPT reads, it does not write, and there is no MCP server.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/)** — the market-share leader at SMB size. Pick it when marketing and sales must share one system or you expect to outgrow 20 seats. Pick Copper when the team is Gmail-native, sales-only, and the HubSpot Professional onboarding fee and credit model are more platform than you need.
- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/)** — the other top-share option, and wrong for most Copper buyers. Pick it only when approval chains, territories or a finance-grade data model are already requirements.
- **[Pipedrive](/en/tools/pipedrive/)** — the cheapest credible Sell replacement and, per Copper's own guide, Zendesk's official Sell migration partner. Pick Pipedrive when you need Outlook support, forecasting, sequences or an MCP server at $39 a seat. Pick Copper when Gmail-sidebar capture and the Zendesk ticket view matter more than those.
- **[Attio](/en/tools/attio/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in the segment, and the destination several 2026 write-ups by teams leaving Copper name. Pick Attio when you want custom objects, agent workflows and an object model you shape yourself, at $35 a seat on Plus. Pick Copper when nobody on the team wants to design a data model.
- **[folk](/en/tools/folk/)** — pick folk when the records really are people and companies and you want $24 seats with a hosted MCP server and no contact cap on the entry plan.

## Watch-outs

- **Contact caps force upgrades that seats don't.** Basic stops at 2,500 contacts and Professional at 15,000, and email-driven contact capture fills both faster than a team expects. Guard: before migrating, count contacts in the source CRM plus 12 months of growth from Gmail auto-capture, and price the tier that number lands on, not the tier your features need.
- **CopperGPT is read-only and billed on the other side.** It summarizes and answers but cannot create or update a record, and every user needs a paid ChatGPT seat for it to work. Guard: treat it as a reporting convenience, and if you want an agent updating pipeline, budget an n8n or Make flow against the REST API and its 180-requests-per-minute limit.
- **"Bulk email" is not outbound.** Professional includes bulk email and Business adds email series, but Copper itself says it does not support mass outbound. Guard: keep cold outbound in a dedicated sending tool and use Copper's email features only for customer and warm-pipeline sends.
- **A Zendesk Sell exit has a hard deadline.** Sell retires in August 2027, and Copper's migration guide names no Copper-run migration service or discount. Guard: export Sell data and run a 200-record test import into a Copper trial this quarter, and ask Copper's sales team in writing what migration help comes with an annual Professional or Business contract before you sign.
