## What it is

Zowie is an enterprise AI customer-service agent for chat, email and voice, built for companies whose tickets end in an action: a refund, a parcel redirect, a subscription change, a card block. It sits in the same category as [Ada](/en/tools/ada/), [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) and [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/), but its architecture is its pitch. The language model handles the conversation; a separate Decision Engine executes the business logic. In Zowie's own words, the LLM extracts intent, the deterministic engine runs the rule (refund eligibility, return window, identity check), and the LLM phrases the result. The model never decides whether a refund is allowed.

The product today is a set of modules: Agent Studio (Knowledge, Flows, Playbooks, and Agent Connect for plugging in other agents over REST, A2A or SDKs), an Orchestrator, an Inbox for human handoff, Supervisor for QA and CSAT, Tester, Traces, and an Audit Log (released 2026-05-12) that records who changed which rule. Channels are chat, email, voice and Hello, a voice-and-text layer that replaces site navigation. On 2026-09-15 Zowie shipped a remote MCP server (EU and US endpoints, OAuth login, edits land in staging by default), so a CX ops lead can inspect and change the agent from Claude or ChatGPT. A Conversation SDK for web, iOS, Android, React Native and Flutter followed on 2026-11-09.

Founded in 2019 in Poland by Maja Schaefer (CEO) and Maciej Ciołek, Zowie raised a $5M seed led by Gradient Ventures and 10xFounders in January 2022 and announced a $14M Series A in May 2022. It claims about 100 million conversations a year and names InPost, Decathlon, Allianz, Total Wine, KRUK and Empik as customers. It is independent; we found no acquisition or shutdown reports as of 2026-11-26.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Policy you can prove.** Because refund and eligibility rules run in a rule engine, not a prompt, the same input gives the same decision every time. That suits regulated customers such as Allianz and KRUK, and it means the agent cannot invent a 60-day return window.
- **Voice in production, not a demo.** InPost runs Zowie's voice agent in Poland, the UK and France. Voice settings moved into the UI in May 2026, and voice profiles arrived in October.
- **Audit trail and change control.** Audit Log, Tester and staging-by-default MCP edits give compliance teams a record of who changed which rule and when, something most mid-market agents still lack.
- **Bring your own model.** Zowie lists Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta and Mistral models, and ElevenLabs, Cartesia, Deepgram and Azure Speech for voice. Its site displays SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA and DORA badges and offers deployment inside the customer's own cloud.

## Pricing

Zowie publishes no rate card; its pricing page is a demo form. The only public number is its AWS Marketplace listing: **$34,500 for 12 months, including 25,000 automated conversations**, which works out to about **$1.38 per automated conversation**, with up to 3% off on 24-month and 6% off on 36-month terms. The unit is the automated conversation, not the resolution, so a conversation the agent handles and then escalates may still count. Get the definition in writing.

Treat $34,500 a year as the realistic floor. At 25,000 conversations a year (about 2,100 a month), Intercom's Fin at about $0.99 per resolution would cost roughly $24,750 if every conversation resolved, and less in practice; Zendesk's AI agents at $1.50 per committed resolution land near $37,500. Above that floor Zowie sits well below the enterprise agents: Ada's reported median is about $73,500 a year, Decagon starts with a roughly $50,000 platform fee, and Sierra contracts usually run $150K–$350K+. Zowie's premium over a native agent pays back only if the Decision Engine automates actions the native agent can't, such as refunds, redirects and cancellations.

## Best for

Heads of CX or support operations at ecommerce, logistics, fintech and insurance companies handling roughly 2,000–50,000 automatable conversations a month, where most contacts end in a transaction governed by written policy and a wrong answer costs money or creates regulatory exposure. It is the wrong pick for teams under about 1,500 conversations a month, for pure FAQ deflection where a native helpdesk agent is enough, and for B2B software support that needs reasoning over technical documentation more than policy execution.

## Watch-outs

- **Rules have to be written before they can run.** The deterministic engine only executes what you model in Flows and Playbooks, so the first months are spent translating policy documents and edge cases into logic. The guard: launch with the three highest-volume intents only, have the policy owner sign off on each Flow in staging using Tester, and add intents one at a time once the escalation rate holds for two weeks.
- **Seasonal volume can break a fixed package.** A conversation package sized on an average month runs out early when peak season spikes volume, and reviewers cited by third parties describe costs climbing with seasonality. The guard: size the package on your peak quarter, set a usage alert at 75% on the Billing page, and put the per-conversation overage rate in the contract.
- **Integration depth varies.** Native integrations confirmed on Zowie's own pages include Shopify, Zendesk, Gladly and Dixa; others need Agent Connect or custom API work. The guard: list every system the agent must write to (OMS, payments, carrier), and get a working staging demo of a write action in each before signing.
- **Thin public review base.** G2 shows 4.7/5 on 73 ratings; older reviews mention uneven translation quality and slow vendor support. The guard: test your non-English queues during the pilot and get a named support contact with response-time terms in the contract.

## Alternatives, and when to pick them instead

[Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) and [Intercom](/en/tools/intercom/) Fin hold the most market share and sell native agents per resolution (about $1.50 and $0.99). Pick the native agent first if your helpdesk is already one of them and most volume is informational. [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) is the fastest-growing enterprise entrant; pick it for software or fintech support at scale where agent operating procedures written in natural language suit your team better than modelled rules. Pick [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) when budget is above $150K and outcome-based billing matters. Pick [Ada](/en/tools/ada/) for very high, rising volume where per-conversation pricing at scale wins. For Shopify brands on Gorgias, [Siena AI](/en/tools/siena-ai/) or [Yuma AI](/en/tools/yuma-ai/) are cheaper overlays with lighter setup. If none of these fit, because volume is low, keep macros, a self-serve order-status page and a returns portal until the queue justifies a $34,500 floor.
