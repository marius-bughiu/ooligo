## What it is

PartnerStack is a partner relationship management (PRM) platform built for B2B SaaS companies that grow through affiliates, referral partners and resellers. It tracks partner links, leads and registered deals, calculates commissions, and pays partners out of one balance, so finance doesn't run a payout spreadsheet every month. Its other half is a marketplace: a network of more than 138,000 commission-earning affiliates, influencers, co-sell and channel partners that a vendor can recruit from, instead of building a partner list from scratch. Monday.com, Stripe and Freshworks are named customers.

The company started in Toronto as GrowSumo. AppDirect, the subscription-commerce platform that also owns Tackle, acquired it on 2026-04-14 for undisclosed terms. PartnerStack's roughly 110 staff moved over, the Toronto office stayed open, and the product is still sold on its own, now branded "An AppDirect Company." The announcement describes one combined platform for partner-led growth but gives no integration timeline.

The question this page answers: PartnerStack now belongs to AppDirect. If partner-sourced pipeline runs on it, do you stay, or move to Impartner or Crossbeam? For a SaaS affiliate and referral program, staying is the default. Reseller and distributor programs with tiers, MDF and partner quoting are better served by [Impartner](/en/tools/impartner/). And Crossbeam is not a replacement at all: it maps account overlap with partners, which PartnerStack doesn't do.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **Payouts are built in.** Partner Payments pays partners out of PartnerStack, and the Stripe, Chargebee and Recurly integrations feed billing events into commission calculations. This is why most SaaS teams buy it over a general PRM.
- **Partner revenue reaches the CRM.** Native Salesforce, HubSpot and Pipedrive integrations write partner-sourced leads and deals into the system you forecast from.
- **Recruiting is part of the product.** The marketplace and its AI Matches feature rank partners by fit (industry, commission structure, similar products they promote), which shortens the first months of a new program.
- **Agents can read program data.** An official remote MCP server (mcp.partnerstack.com/mcp, OAuth with your PartnerStack login) exposes partnerships, customers, leads, deals, transactions, rewards, links and report exports to Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients. For now only Admin and full Integration users can connect.

## Pricing reality

PartnerStack publishes plan names, not prices. Launch covers a new program but makes you pick one tracking method: affiliate links or lead and deal registration. Growth adds a learning management system, partner challenges and incentives, MDF management and payments, and advanced segmentation. Enterprise adds every tracking method, custom workflows and a designated CSM. The AI features, MCP connector and partner payments are in every plan.

What teams pay: Vendr's data puts the median annual contract at $19,616, with a range of $9,186 to $37,770 and average negotiated savings of about 21%. Vendr also estimates a transaction fee of 1.5% to 3.5% of partner payouts on top of the subscription, plus $5,000 to $20,000 or more for an enterprise implementation. PartnerStack doesn't publish its payout fee, so budget around $20,000 a year plus the fee on every dollar you pay partners, and expect what third-party buyer data reports: a one-year minimum that renews automatically.

For scale: Impartner's Vendr median is about 2.8 times higher ($54,598). Rewardful runs $49 to $149+ a month with no transaction fee (3% if it manages payouts). Dub Partners runs $90 a month (up to $2,500 in monthly payouts) or $300 a month (up to $15,000), with a 5% payout fee.

## Best for

Partnerships and RevOps leads at B2B SaaS companies running an affiliate or referral program with roughly 50 to several hundred active partners, where paying commissions on time and recruiting new partners are the main work, and partner-sourced deals have to show up in Salesforce or HubSpot.

Don't buy it for a reseller or distributor channel where deal-registration conflicts, partner tiers and partner quoting are the daily work; that is Impartner's ground. Don't buy it for a program under about 25 affiliates paid from Stripe, where Rewardful does the job for about $500 a year. And don't buy it for account mapping; pair it with [Crossbeam](/en/tools/crossbeam/) as in the [partner ecosystem stack](/en/stacks/partner-ecosystem-stack/).

## Versus the alternatives

**Impartner** leads structured channel programs. Pick it for reseller and distributor programs with tiered benefits, MDF, compliance and partner CPQ, and accept the higher price and longer implementation.

**impact.com** is the best-known partnership-automation platform across affiliates, influencers and commerce content partners. Pick it when the program spans consumer creators and publishers, not just B2B SaaS partners.

**Dub Partners** is the fastest-growing entrant, built on Dub's link-attribution product with published self-serve prices. Pick it when you want to launch an affiliate program this week without a sales cycle and the 5% payout fee costs less than an annual contract. Rewardful and FirstPromoter fill the same role for Stripe-billed programs.

If none of them fit, check whether the program has rules yet. Without commission terms, attribution windows and an owner, any PRM becomes a payout tool. [Ecosystem-led growth](/en/learn/ecosystem-led-growth/) covers the program design that comes first.

## Watch-outs

- **The roadmap now belongs to AppDirect.** The deal was announced as one combined platform with no integration timeline. *Guard:* before a multi-year renewal, ask for a written product roadmap and a clause that lets you exit or renegotiate if PartnerStack is retired, rebranded into another product or repriced.
- **The payout fee is not on the price page.** Third-party estimates vary, and the fee scales with partner revenue, not seats. *Guard:* get the payout-fee schedule and payment-method costs in the order form, and model them at your expected payout volume for year two.
- **Launch makes you choose one tracking method.** Running affiliates and deal registration together needs Enterprise. *Guard:* map every partner type you plan to run in the next 12 months to a tracking method before choosing a plan.
- **Setup and partner onboarding take work.** G2 reviewers name setup complexity and payout options as the main complaints. *Guard:* launch with one partner type and one commission rule, and test a full referral-to-payout cycle with a pilot partner before inviting the rest.
- **Affiliate traffic brings fraud risk.** Referral programs that pay on signups or first payments attract fake accounts. *Guard:* pay commissions on a paid, past-refund-window event from your billing system, not on signup.
