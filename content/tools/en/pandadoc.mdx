## What it is

PandaDoc is a document automation platform for sales proposals, quotes, contracts, and forms, with e-signature and payment collection built in. It started as a proposal tool for SMB sales teams and now covers enough of the agreement workflow — approval routing, a searchable document store, renewal notifications — to act as a lightweight CLM for sales paper. It is still an independent company: 56,000+ customers and 700+ employees by its own September 2025 count, with Keith Rabkin as CEO since January 2026 and co-founder Mikita Mikado moved to Chief Product Officer. Its closest peers are DocuSign on e-signature and Proposify and Qwilr on proposals.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops and RevOps stacks

- **Proposal, order form, signature, and payment in one document.** When the customer contract is the last page of the proposal, PandaDoc keeps the flow in one tool. Legal Ops sees the terms without buying a separate CLM for sales paper.
- **Agents can create and send agreements.** PandaDoc launched an official hosted MCP server on 16 September 2025 (`mcp.pandadoc.com`, with an EU endpoint at `mcp.pandadoc.eu`), authorized per user through OAuth. It covers document creation from templates, sending, recipients, status, search, and templates, and can write: create, send, update fields, mark paid or declined, expire, archive. PandaDoc is also listed in Claude's connector directory (July 2026) and has shipped a ChatGPT app (June 2026). The vendor says both run on every plan, including Free, with no usage limits on paid plans for now.
- **Compliance options above basic e-signature.** QES for eIDAS, 21 CFR Part 11 alignment, KBA and ID verification, and remote online notarization through PandaDoc Notary sit on the same platform. That matters to legal teams sending regulated agreements, not just order forms.

## Pricing

- **Free — $0.** 60 documents a year (5 a month) with legally binding e-signatures, the drag-and-drop editor, and tracking.
- **Starter — $19/seat/month billed annually** ($35 on monthly billing, per third-party trackers; the vendor page says annual saves "up to 46%"). Unlimited uploads and e-signatures, up to 5 templates. It is an e-signature and forms plan: no quotes, CRM integrations, content library, or custom branding.
- **Business — $49/seat/month billed annually** ($65 monthly). This is the plan the proposal use case needs: quotes and pricing tables, CRM integrations, content library, approval workflows, payments, up to 3 deal rooms. The Salesforce integration, bulk send, web forms, and SSO are listed as optional add-ons here, not inclusions.
- **Enterprise — custom**, per seat or per document. Adds smart content, team workspaces, custom roles, webhooks, and US or EU data residency; CPQ, workflow automation, Notary, and API access are add-ons. A per-document package with unlimited seats (launched September 2025 as the Launch plan) is sold through sales.
- **API is priced separately.** PandaDoc's API page sells a free tier (60 documents a year) and an API Developer plan at $40/month for 40 documents a month; the standard Business plan does not include API access.
- **What teams pay.** Vendr's median annual contract is $14,868, in a $3,662-$37,580 range, with buyers saving 26% on average. List math for 10 Business seats is $5,880/year before add-ons; the Vendr median implies most buyers add seats or add-ons on top of that, typically Salesforce, CPQ, or Enterprise.

## Best for

- RevOps and sales ops leaders at sales-led B2B companies of 5-100 reps where the customer agreement is attached to the proposal and the goal is shorter time from verbal yes to countersigned
- Legal Ops teams that want visibility into sales paper without running a CLM for it
- Teams that want an agent in Claude or ChatGPT to draft from approved templates and send for signature
- Not for procurement, vendor MSAs, or anything negotiated both ways: PandaDoc is built for documents the seller controls, and its redlining and post-signature obligation tracking are thin

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[DocuSign](/en/tools/docusign-iam/)** — the e-signature market leader. Pick it when signature volume, counterparty familiarity, and enterprise agreement management matter more than proposal design. Its eSignature plans run $25 (Standard) and $40 (Business Pro) per user/month on annual billing, capped at 100 envelopes per user per year, per third-party trackers; the IAM platform adds Iris AI and its own MCP server.
- **Adobe Acrobat Sign** — the other e-signature incumbent. Pick it if the company already licenses Adobe Acrobat and signature is the whole requirement.
- **Proposify** — the closest proposal-first rival. Basic is $19/user/month annual with 10 sends a month and Team is $41 with 30. Pick it for a small team sending under 30 proposals per rep a month that wants proposal analytics over document breadth.
- **Qwilr** — web-page proposals instead of PDFs, Starter at $35/user/month annual. Pick it when the proposal is a marketing asset; note that Salesforce needs its $75 Scale tier with a 10-user minimum.
- **[Concord](/en/tools/concord/) or [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/)** — pick a CLM when Legal Ops owns every contract type, not just sales paper. See [PandaDoc vs Concord](/en/comparisons/pandadoc-vs-concord/).

## Watch-outs

- **The Salesforce integration is not in the Business price.** The pricing table marks it optional on both Business and Enterprise. Guard: get the Salesforce add-on price into the quote before comparing PandaDoc to DocuSign or Qwilr on seat price.
- **Document limits read two ways.** The plan table says unlimited documents on Starter and Business, while the pricing FAQ still quotes overage at $2 per document on Starter annual, $3.50 on Starter monthly, and $2.60 on Business monthly. Guard: have the send allowance for your plan written into the order form.
- **An agent can send a legally binding document.** The MCP server and connectors can create and send for signature from a single prompt, and they hold the user's own permissions. Guard: have agents send only from locked templates, turn on approval workflows, and confirm in a test workspace that a connector-created document stops for approval before anyone relies on it.
- **The MCP server is not an integration layer.** It runs only inside a chat a person starts, has no triggers, and cannot retrieve the sealed signed PDF or subscribe to webhooks; those are API-only, and webhooks require Enterprise. Guard: anything that must fire on "document signed" goes through the API or [Zapier](/en/tools/zapier/), not the connector.
- **Not a CLM.** Renewal notifications are optional on Business and there is no obligation tracking. Guard: route vendor, procurement, and negotiated paper to a CLM, and export signed PDFs with metadata monthly so the repository doesn't depend on the PandaDoc seat count.
- **AI Assistant is not generally available.** The in-app assistant for search, Q&A, and summaries is in closed beta for select Business and Enterprise accounts. Guard: evaluate on features you can switch on today; for clause-level legal review, pair PandaDoc with [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) or [Claude](/en/tools/claude/).
