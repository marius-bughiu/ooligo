[Granola](/en/tools/granola/) and [Fathom](/en/tools/fathom/) both turn meetings into text without anyone writing them up afterward, and as of 2026 both can do it without a bot appearing in the participant list. That convergence retired the differentiator most buyers still choose on. The real split is what each one leaves behind: Granola merges the notes you typed into the transcript and then discards the audio; Fathom keeps the recording and writes the summary for you. Pick on whether the artifact your team needs is your own thinking, cleaned up, or a record somebody else can verify.

## The bot-free argument is over

Granola's founding pitch was device-level capture — nothing joins the call, so in-person conversations and any app that makes sound are covered, not just Zoom, Meet, and Teams. Fathom now ships its own bot-free capture alongside the bot it has always used, but that mode is in beta and Mac-only. Granola transcribes on macOS and Windows on the desktop, plus mobile.

Two consequences. If your team runs Windows, Fathom still puts a bot in the room and Granola's advantage holds exactly as it did. If your team is on Mac, "no bot" stops being a reason to pay either vendor, and the decision moves to the two questions below.

## Where Granola wins

- **Your notes survive the summarization.** Granola fills in the sparse bullets you typed during the call using the transcript, so the document follows your structure. Fathom writes to its own template. For discovery calls, user research, or a partner conversation where the thing worth keeping is your interpretation, these are not two styles of the same document — they are different documents.
- **Capture is source-agnostic.** Device audio covers a phone call on speaker, an in-person meeting, a webinar playing in a browser tab. Fathom's bot covers Zoom, Meet, and Teams; its bot-free beta covers Mac.
- **No audio is stored at all.** Granola's security documentation states it transcribes in real time on macOS and Windows and keeps only the transcript and your notes. For teams whose customers decline recording, or interviews where "this call is being recorded" changes the answers, an absent file is easier to defend in a security review than a retention policy is.
- **The integration list skews to startup GTM.** Granola Business names Attio, Notion, Slack, HubSpot, Affinity, and Zapier. Affinity and Attio are the tell — this was built for venture and startup teams, not Salesforce shops.

## Where Fathom wins

- **There is a recording to go back to.** Clips, playlists, coaching metrics, AI scorecards, and "play me the 30 seconds where they said that" all need retained audio. Granola cannot backfill any of it, because the file was never kept.
- **The free tier is a deployment, not a trial.** Fathom Free is unlimited recordings, unlimited transcription, AI summaries, clips, and search, per user, with no expiry. Granola's free tier limits meeting history — you can evaluate on it, you cannot run on it.
- **API access on every plan, including Free.** Fathom's developer FAQ states that API keys and webhooks are included on all plans, with TypeScript and Python SDKs, OAuth, and official Claude and ChatGPT MCP connectors. Granola gates MCP and API access behind Business at $14/user/mo. If the point is piping meeting context into an internal agent, Fathom lets you prove it works before paying.
- **Salesforce, and field-level CRM writes.** Fathom pushes summaries and notes into Salesforce and HubSpot, and its Business tier maps them into specific CRM fields. Granola's published integration list has no Salesforce entry at all.
- **Sales-shaped summaries out of the box.** Fathom's advanced summaries draw on more than 15 meeting templates including BANT and Sandler. Granola gives you templates you write yourself.

## Pricing, quantified

Granola bills flat monthly per user with no annual discount: Basic $0, Business $14, Enterprise $35. Fathom bills lower annually than monthly — Free $0, Premium $16 against $20, Team $15 against $19, Business $25 against $34, Enterprise quoted. Team and Business both carry a two-seat minimum.

At the tier most teams actually buy, they cost the same. Granola Business at $14/user/mo against Fathom Team at $15/user/mo annual is a 7% gap: $1,680 versus $1,800 a year for 10 seats. The gap opens where CRM sync lives. Fathom's CRM field sync is Business-only at $25/user/mo annual, so those same 10 seats run $3,000 a year — 79% more than Granola Business, whose integration tier is the same $14.

The detail that catches people mid-rollout: below Business, Fathom caps CRM sync at 3 users per domain. A 10-seat Team plan does not sync 10 reps' calls into HubSpot.

## Watch-outs

- **Granola discards audio and cannot recover it.** If any downstream workflow needs playback — dispute resolution, call coaching, QA sampling — settle that before rollout. Guard: run one week of real calls through both and count how many times somebody reached for the recording.
- **Fathom's bot-free mode is beta and Mac-only.** Guard: pilot on your actual OS mix rather than the founder's laptop. A Windows-heavy team is buying the bot.
- **Fathom's 3-user CRM cap sits below the tier most teams price.** Guard: if CRM sync is the reason you are buying, budget Business at $25/user/mo annual from day one, not Team.
- **Granola has no Salesforce integration.** Guard: if Salesforce is the system of record, price the Zapier hop and its per-task cost, or pick Fathom.
- **Both bill per seat, and note-takers spread by word of mouth.** Guard: scope the rollout to people who run external meetings. A 40-seat deployment where 12 people take customer calls is 28 seats of waste.

## Verdict

- **Pick Granola** if the output you care about is your own thinking — founder and exec conversations, user research, partner calls, in-person meetings — you live in Attio or Affinity, and you would rather store no recordings at all.
- **Pick Fathom** if the calls are customer-facing and repeatable, somebody other than the attendee needs to review them, Salesforce or HubSpot is the system of record, or you want a real deployment at $0.
- **If you cannot decide, start on Fathom Free.** It costs nothing, it keeps the recordings, and its API works at the free tier, so you can test whether meeting data actually reaches your agents before anyone signs anything. Granola is the deliberate second purchase for people whose notes are the product — a weak default, because $14 buys a tool whose entire value depends on you typing during the call, and most teams will not.
- **Running both** means paying twice to capture the same meeting. The one split that works is by person, never by meeting: Granola on the exec and research side, Fathom across the GTM team.

If neither fits, the question was conversation intelligence, not note-taking. Talk ratios across the whole rep team, deal scoring, forecast signal pulled from call content — that is [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) territory, and [Gong vs Fathom](/en/vs/gong-vs-fathom/) is the page that routes it. Neither Granola nor Fathom scores a pipeline.
