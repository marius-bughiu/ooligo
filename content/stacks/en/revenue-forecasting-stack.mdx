The Friday forecast call fails the same way in most companies. The CRO asks why a seven-figure deal is still in commit, the AE says the champion is engaged, and nobody in the room can check the claim. The number gets defended with adjectives, the board hears it, and the quarter lands 12% under.

This stack replaces the adjective with a record. Salesforce holds the deal. Gong holds what the buyer actually said. Clari Forecast holds the commitment and measures it against quota. A hygiene layer keeps the fields the other three depend on from going stale. The chain runs one direction: evidence → risk flag → forecast category → commit.

Two things changed in the second half of 2026 that this page now reflects. Clari stopped being a company: on September 2, 2026 the merged Clari + Salesloft business took the single name Salesloft, and Clari Forecast is the only product that kept the Clari name. And Salesforce retired the Enterprise and Unlimited editions this stack used to be priced on. Read the overlap section before you take either revenue-AI sales meeting, because the two vendors still sell each other's job.

## How the pieces fit

- **[Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) is the record.** Amount, close date, stage, forecast category, owner. Collaborative Forecasts rolls those up a hierarchy and does it adequately; what native forecasting does not do is tell you whether the roll-up is believable. Since September 3, 2026 new Sales Cloud contracts are sold as Core, Advanced and Max, which replace Enterprise, Unlimited and Agentforce 1. Core at $195 per user per month is the baseline edition for this stack, because the API and the hosted MCP servers live at that level and above. Those servers went GA on April 29, 2026, so an external AI client can read the pipeline out of Salesforce over OAuth without a middle tier you maintain.

- **[Gong](/en/tools/gong/) is the evidence layer.** Calls, emails and meetings become structured signal — no next step scheduled, a single thread into a buying committee, a competitor named in week three. That signal is what makes a risk flag arguable instead of a vibe. Gong's MCP Server lets an external agent in Salesforce Agentforce or Microsoft Copilot query Gong, and the MCP Gateway pulls partner data into its own AI features. The June 24, 2026 release added the Revenue Harness and Custom Agents that reason from the Gong Revenue Graph with scoped data access and approval gates; on September 30, 2026 Gong added event-driven triggers, so a custom agent can fire when a deal changes rather than when someone remembers to run it.

- **[Clari Forecast](/en/tools/clari/) is the commitment layer.** Rep commit rolls to manager, manager to CRO, and the roll-up is measured against quota rather than a spreadsheet copy of it. Inspect, now sold under the Salesloft name, is where a specific deal gets challenged and the category change gets logged with a reason. An AI Forecast Agent summarizes where the number moved and why. The product has not changed method since the rebrand; what changed is that it is now sold, roadmapped and supported by an engagement vendor.

- **[Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/) is the execution layer, whether you planned one or not.** Every Clari Forecast contract is now a Salesloft contract. From inside Forecast and Inspect a manager can create a Salesloft Cadence task or an AI follow-up email on a deal carrying the gap, and the Salesloft MCP server exposes forecast, deal-inspection, Cadence and call data to Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini. If you already run Salesloft for engagement, this is the cheapest layer to add. If you run Outreach, this is the layer that forces a decision.

- **[Rattle](/en/tools/rattle/) is the hygiene layer — for existing customers.** Reps update Salesforce from Slack or Teams through interactive forms, RevOps builds workflows on CRM events without writing Apex, and the CRM Data Agent proposes field values for the rep to confirm. In 2026 the company moved its forward product effort to Von, an AI analyst for revenue teams, and gorattle.com now says Rattle is "still fully supported for current customers" while steering new buyers to Von. Rattle holds no state of its own — it writes into Salesforce — which is why it was always the most swappable layer, and why a new buyer should now price the native alternative first.

### Named handoffs

1. **Call ends → risk flag.** Gong transcribes, detects the missing next step or the single-threaded committee, and raises deal risk within hours. With event triggers, a custom agent can post the flag to the deal owner's manager the same day. The flag reaches the forecast review that week, not the quarterly business review.
2. **Risk flag → category change.** A flagged deal sitting in commit gets challenged in Inspect. Either the manager moves it to best case, or the AE writes the counter-evidence into the deal. Both outcomes are logged; "we discussed it" is not an outcome.
3. **Missing field → Slack prompt → CRM write.** A stage change without a close date, or an open opportunity with no activity for 30 days, fires a hygiene workflow into the rep's DM — Rattle if you have it, Salesforce Flow plus Slack if you do not. The value lands in Salesforce the same day, which is the only reason the layers above have inputs at all.
4. **Commit submitted → measured against quota.** Clari Forecast rolls rep to manager to CRO against the quota record, so a segment committing under target is visible before the last week of the quarter rather than after it.
5. **Forecast gap → Salesloft task.** From Forecast or Inspect, a manager creates the Cadence task or the AI follow-up email on the deals carrying the gap. Coverage action happens in the same session as the diagnosis.

## The overlap you have to resolve first

Gong sells Forecast. Salesloft sells conversation intelligence — Clari Copilot is now Salesloft Conversation Intelligence, and its recordings, transcripts and summaries land in the Salesloft deal record and meeting-prep plays. Each vendor can credibly quote you the other's half of this stack, and since the rebrand Salesloft will also quote you the engagement layer on top.

Buying both without a written boundary produces two deal-risk scores, two sets of call data, and a quarterly argument about which one the CRO looks at. The boundary that holds:

- **Gong owns the evidence.** Recording, transcript, Revenue Graph, what the buyer said and when. It is the upstream publisher of signal.
- **Clari Forecast owns the commitment.** Categories, submission history, roll-up, quota measurement, and the audit trail of who changed what and why.
- **Salesforce owns the record.** Neither revenue-AI vendor is the arbiter of what closed.
- **The hygiene layer owns nothing.** It is an input mechanism, and treating it as a system of record is how teams end up with workflow logic nobody can find.

**If you cannot defend two revenue-AI contracts, run one.** The rule for choosing: if the expensive problem is the forecast call — repeated misses, categories that mean different things to different managers — keep Clari Forecast and take Salesloft Conversation Intelligence for call capture. If the expensive problem is rep behavior — ramp time, discovery quality, a coaching program with no curriculum — keep Gong and forecast in Gong or Salesforce until the miss rate justifies a second vendor. Running both is defensible when the forecast is a board-committed number *and* call evidence is the primary input to it, which is a $100M+ ARR enterprise-sales pattern, not a Series B one. If you run Outreach for engagement, the rebrand tilts the choice toward Gong: Clari Forecast's roadmap is now set by Outreach's main competitor.

## Cost reality

Salesforce is the only published number here. Sales Cloud lists at $25 (Starter Suite), $100 (Pro Suite), $195 (Core), $395 (Advanced) and $550 (Max) per user per month billed annually. A 100-seat Core org is $234K/year list. Existing customers keep legacy Enterprise pricing ($175) until they change editions, so check which one your renewal is quoted on. Treat it as the platform you are extending, not a line this project adds.

The layers on top are all quote-only:

- **Gong** publishes its shape but not its numbers: licences priced per user, plus a platform fee that scales with the number of users supported. Vendr reports a median annual contract of $54,900 across 1,127 analyzed purchases, a $11,184-$204,033 range, and average savings of 14.17% off the opening quote. Third-party buyer guides place effective per-seat cost at $1,200-$2,400 per year, with the low end reachable only at 100+ seats.
- **Clari Forecast** is quoted through Salesloft, whose pricing page is a "Talk to Sales" form. Docket estimated it at about $100-$120 per user per month on annual billing in April 2026. Vendr's data across 291 Clari purchases puts the median annual contract near $76,000 in a $19,065-$415,001 range. Expect the account team to price Cadence and Conversation Intelligence against it; a Forecast-only order is still possible but is no longer the default quote.
- **Rattle** is custom-quoted. Per Vendr's February 2026 data across 71 purchases, the median contract lands near $16K/year across a $6K-$71K range. Renew it if it works; for a new buyer, Salesforce Flow plus Slack costs nothing beyond the Core licence.

For 100 quota carriers the revenue-AI layers land at roughly **$130K-$175K/year** on the median anchors above, on top of Salesforce. A lean configuration — Gong for AEs only, Clari Forecast without Conversation Intelligence, native Flow for hygiene — reaches about $85K. An enterprise configuration with both agent layers licensed and Salesloft Cadence for every rep clears $400K.

The cost nobody budgets is definition work. Stage exit criteria and forecast category definitions have to be written and agreed before Clari Forecast is configured, because the roll-up inherits whatever ambiguity is in them. Budget a quarter of a RevOps lead's time for that, and do it before the implementation kickoff, not during.

## Common variations

- **Drop Clari Forecast; forecast in Gong.** One vendor across evidence and commitment, one contract, no boundary document, and no engagement vendor in the forecast. The swap rule: take it under about 50 quota carriers with a single forecast hierarchy, or whenever Outreach is your sequencer. What you give up is submission history and the audit trail — Gong Forecast will tell you what the number should be; it is weaker at showing who changed the commit, when, and on what argument.

- **Drop Gong; run all of Salesloft.** Clari Forecast, Inspect, Conversation Intelligence and Cadence on one contract. Take it when calls feed the forecast rather than a coaching program and Salesloft is already the execution layer. What you give up is the Revenue Graph and the Custom Agents built on it, and you take on a single vendor that now owns engagement, call data and the commit.

- **Replace Rattle with Salesforce Flow and Slack.** Take it when you are a new buyer, or when the hygiene problem is notification-shaped rather than workflow-shaped — reps who need a reminder, not an approval chain. Since July 2026 Salesforce-hosted MCP servers connect Slackbot to the CRM on Core and above (legacy Enterprise and above), which covers more of the "ask in Slack, write to Salesforce" pattern natively. Keep Rattle if you already run multi-step approval workflows on it and its support commitment covers your renewal term.

- **HubSpot instead of Salesforce.** Gong, Salesloft and Rattle all support it, but the depth argument in this stack — custom objects, opportunity-level write-back, MCP servers against the record graph — is a Salesforce argument. On HubSpot, evaluate native forecasting plus Gong before adding a third vendor.

- **Independent forecasting specialists: [Aviso](/en/tools/aviso/) or [Terret](/en/tools/boostup/) (formerly BoostUp).** Evaluate them when the six-figure Clari line is the blocker, or when you want a forecasting vendor that does not also sell your sequencer. The rule: run the same three deals through each vendor's inspection view during the trial and compare what each one flags against what actually happened last quarter.

## What this stack does NOT replace

- **Stage exit criteria.** Every risk flag and category rule reads the definitions you wrote. If Stage 4 means five different things across five managers, the forecast is wrong at higher cost. See [deal stage definitions](/en/learn/deal-stage-definitions/) and [forecast categories explained](/en/learn/forecast-categories-explained/).
- **The CRO's judgment.** These tools produce a defensible number and the argument behind it. Committing to it is still a person's decision.
- **Sales methodology.** Nothing here improves discovery. Gong shows you that discovery was thin; the fix is a program, not a licence.
- **Quota and capacity planning.** The target the roll-up is measured against comes from somewhere else — see [quota coverage](/en/learn/quota-coverage/) and the [RevOps planning stack](/en/stacks/revops-planning-stack/).
- **Finance's revenue recognition.** A Clari Forecast commit is a sales number. It is not ASC 606 revenue, and treating the two as interchangeable in a board deck is a mistake auditors catch.
- **Data ownership.** A hygiene layer enforces a standard; somebody still has to write it. Start from [CRM hygiene](/en/learn/crm-hygiene/) and name the fields that are load-bearing for the forecast before you build a single workflow.

## Match rules

**Use this stack when:**

- You carry 25+ quota carriers and the quarterly commit is a number the board holds you to. Both halves matter — headcount alone justifies conversation intelligence, not a forecasting layer.
- You have missed the commit by more than 10% twice in a row. That is the trigger event to buy on, and it is also the business case that gets the budget approved.
- Deals run 3+ months with multi-threaded buying committees. Call evidence is worth paying for when the deal is long enough for the story to change mid-cycle; in a 30-day cycle it is not.
- Someone owns the forecast definition. A stack with two revenue-AI vendors and no named owner regresses to a spreadsheet within two quarters, and now it is an expensive spreadsheet.

**Do not use this stack when:**

- You are under $10M ARR with fewer than 15 reps. Salesforce reports plus a weekly pipeline review get you inside 10% — see [forecast accuracy](/en/learn/forecast-accuracy/) for what to measure first.
- Deals close in under 30 days at high volume. Your forecast is a conversion-math problem, and [pipeline velocity](/en/learn/pipeline-velocity/) instrumentation beats deal-level inspection.
- Revenue is majority self-serve. Product usage, not call signal, is the leading indicator, and the money belongs in a product-led motion instead.
- Outreach is your engagement platform and you will not switch. Buy Gong, Aviso or Terret for the forecast instead of handing the commit to Outreach's competitor.
- Stage exit criteria are unwritten. Buy nothing until they exist. Configuring Clari Forecast on ambiguous stages produces a confident number built on a definition nobody agreed to, which is worse than the honest guess it replaced.
