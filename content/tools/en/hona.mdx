## What it is

Hona is the client-facing layer for high-volume plaintiff firms. It sits on top of the case management system you already run and turns case events into client communication. When a project moves phase in [Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/), Hona advances the client's case tracker and sends the update text you wrote for that phase, with an explainer video if you have one. Clients open a link (no app to download), see where their case stands in plain language, upload documents, and text the team. Replies and activity are logged back to the case file.

The company is a Y Combinator alum based in Utah. It raised a $9.5M Series A led by Costanoa Ventures in June 2024, with Ludlow Ventures, Soma Capital and Y Combinator participating. Its homepage says 500+ personal injury firms use it; a July 2026 Hona blog post says "more than 700 firms". Either way it is a mid-size vendor, not a startup testing its first customers. It lists HIPAA, TCPA and SOC 2 Type II compliance, which matters because PI case updates routinely touch medical treatment.

The problem it solves is specific: clients calling and emailing to ask "what's happening with my case?" Hona's CEO, Matthew McClellan, told the ABA Journal that clients ask "the same 10 questions every day". Every one of those calls lands on a case manager who should be chasing records and treatment.

## What it does now

- **Automated case-status updates.** Phase changes in the case system trigger branded texts and tracker updates. You write the copy once per phase; Hona sends it to every client whose case hits that phase.
- **Two-way texting and document upload.** Client messages land in one inbox tied to the case, instead of in staff cell phones.
- **Engage and reviews.** Drip campaigns, education content and review requests. Hona reports 57% more five-star Google reviews within 30 days for firms on Engage (vendor figure).
- **Lia, the AI case agent** (launched September 2026 after a 20-firm beta). Lia texts or calls clients during medical treatment: appointment check-ins, reminders, reschedules, provider follow-ups. She introduces herself as AI, is configured never to give legal advice, answers in Spanish when a client writes in Spanish, and escalates to a case manager under plain-language rules you set, re-pinging every hour on high-priority alerts. Provider drafts need human sign-off before they go into the file. Beta results Hona published: 86% attendance on appointments Lia tracked to resolution (against roughly 70% typical), 74% of follow-ups answered within 24 hours, 1,500+ appointments tracked. The beta covered only the treatment phase, so treat those numbers as a treatment-phase result, not a whole-case result.
- **Voice AI.** A 24/7 receptionist that answers calls, starts intake, schedules consultations and routes hot leads live to an attorney, in 31 languages.

## Integrations

Hona lists 17 integrations: Filevine, [CASEpeer](/en/tools/casepeer/), [SmartAdvocate](/en/tools/smartadvocate/), [Litify](/en/tools/litify/), [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) (including Clio Grow), [MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/), [Smokeball](/en/tools/smokeball/), [PracticePanther](/en/tools/practicepanther/), [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), Assembly Neos, Lawcus, GrowPath, LeadDocket, Cerenade, Lexamica, Attorney Share and Neostella, plus Zapier. Needles is not on the list. Filevine setup needs a Filevine Account Admin to switch the integration on; Hona's own FAQ names an expired Personal Access Token as the most common cause of a broken sync.

## Pricing

Hona does not publish core-platform pricing; its "Pricing" link goes to a demo form. Third-party listings say the core subscription is sized by monthly active cases. A $15/user/month figure on Capterra sits next to a "contact vendor" note, so don't budget on it. Lia is priced separately from the core platform with its own contract terms, and Hona says setup "depends on how many cases you want Lia working".

The one published price is Voice AI: $200/month plus a $500 setup fee, month-to-month with no contract, including 100 minutes; higher usage tiers run up to 3,500 minutes/month at unlisted prices. A discounted trial costs $99/month plus $249 setup. The page calls it a 30-day trial in one place and 3 months in another, so confirm the length before you sign.

Budget rule: price the core platform against your active-case count, and ask for the per-case rate in writing. A firm with 400 open PI cases should get a quote and divide it by 400. If the per-case cost exceeds what one avoided status call per case per month costs you in case-manager time, the core product isn't paying back yet.

## Best for

PI and mass-tort firms with a few hundred or more open cases, already on Filevine, CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate or Litify, where case managers lose real hours to status calls and treatment gaps. Hona's own figure is that 20%+ of cases see a two-week treatment gap in the first six weeks. The buyer is the operations director or managing partner who owns client experience and staff ratios.

## Alternatives

- **Your case system's free client portal.** Filevine and others ship a portal at no extra cost. Pick it when your clients actually log in and your staff ratio is already fine. Hona's argument is adoption: it claims 92% portal adoption across customers, but that's a vendor number. Run your existing portal for 60 days and measure logins before buying another layer.
- **Case Status.** The closest head-to-head rival: a branded iOS/Android app with AI message triage, drafted replies and translation in 140+ languages, integrated with Filevine, Clio, CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate, Litify and others. Pick it when you want a native mobile app and broader language coverage. Pick Hona when you want link-based access with nothing to install and an AI agent working the treatment phase.
- **[Eve](/en/tools/eve-legal/) or [Supio](/en/tools/supio/) agents.** The fastest-moving plaintiff AI platforms are adding agents and intake voice. Pick one of these when the bottleneck is demand drafting or medical-record work, not client communication. Hona does not draft demands.
- **[Lawmatics](/en/tools/lawmatics/).** Pick it when the gap is intake and marketing automation across practice areas rather than post-sign client updates.

## Watch-outs

- **Automated texts are only as accurate as your case data.** If a phase in Filevine is wrong, Hona tells the client the wrong thing, instantly and in writing. *Guard:* before launch, audit phase definitions and status hygiene on 20 live cases, and have an attorney approve every phase message template.
- **AI follow-up on a TCPA-regulated channel.** Lia texts and calls clients, and the firm, not Hona, owns consent and the content. *Guard:* confirm opt-in language in your retainer covers automated texts and AI calls, keep enrollment per client (Hona supports opt-in by case), and review the escalation queue daily during the first month.
- **Unbundled, quote-only pricing.** Core, Lia and Voice AI are priced and contracted separately, so the first quote may not be the real total. *Guard:* ask for all three in one written quote, at your active-case count, with renewal uplift capped, and confirm data export terms for message history.
- **Integration breakage is silent.** A token expiry stops updates without clients noticing until they call. *Guard:* assign an owner to check the sync log weekly and set a calendar reminder for token renewal.

## The verdict

Hona is the right buy when a PI firm's case managers spend measurable time answering status calls and chasing treatment, and the firm already keeps clean phases in a supported case system. It is the wrong buy when your free portal already gets used, or when your case data is too messy to automate client messages from. Start with the core updates, add Lia only after a quarter of clean phase data, and price Voice AI on its own merits against your current answering service.
