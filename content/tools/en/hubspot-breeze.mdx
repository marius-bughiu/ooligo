## What it is

Breeze is HubSpot's AI brand, and the agent layer inside it now has a different name. On 2026-07-23 HubSpot launched **Agent Hub** in public beta for every Professional and Enterprise customer, folding what it had been selling as Breeze Agents into one place to activate, monitor, and build agents. The same release renamed Breeze Studio to Agent Builder and Commerce Hub to Revenue Hub. Breeze still names the assistant and the enrichment layer; the agents moved. Most third-party buying guides you will find still say "Breeze Agents," because that was the name until nine days ago.

The meter changed earlier, and it matters more. Since 2026-04-14 the two flagship agents bill on outcomes instead of attempts: Customer Agent went from $1.00 per conversation to $0.50 per *resolved* conversation, and Prospecting Agent moved off a recurring monthly charge per enrolled contact to $1.00 per lead it recommends for outreach. Both carry a 28-day free trial. That shipped alongside the Spring 2026 Spotlight on the same date, which also brought HubSpot AEO ($50/month standalone, included with Marketing Hub Professional and Enterprise) and Smart Deal Progression.

## Why it shows up in RevOps and CS stacks

- **The agent runs inside the permission model you already administer.** No copy of the record graph gets shipped to a separate index before the agent can read it, which is the argument that ends most security reviews at a company already standardized on HubSpot.
- **HubSpot publishes the rate sheet.** Per-action credit costs sit in the public Products & Services catalog, so you can price a pilot before you talk to a rep. Agentforce is the only comparable platform that does the same.
- **Two official MCP servers, both GA.** The remote CRM server at `mcp.hubspot.com` reached GA in April 2026 for read and write access from any MCP client; the local developer server for app and CMS work went GA on 2026-02-19. That is the honest path to running [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) against governed HubSpot data without buying agent SKUs at all.

## Pricing reality

Everything meters in HubSpot Credits at $0.010 each — capacity packs of 1,000 credits per month at $10, or Pay-as-You-Go billed in 10-credit increments. From the rate sheet, checked 2026-08-02:

- **Data Agent** — 10 credits ($0.10) to answer one prompt against one record
- **Customer Agent** — 50 credits ($0.50) to resolve one conversation on a text channel
- **Prospecting Agent** — 100 credits ($1.00) to recommend outreach for one lead
- **Content Agent (beta)** — 1,000 credits ($10) per piece of content
- **Custom agents (beta)** — 1 credit per action unit

Included credits reset monthly, do not roll over, and are **not additive across products**: 500 at Starter, 3,000 at Professional, and 5,000 at Enterprise on Smart CRM or any single hub, rising to 500 / 5,000 / 10,000 on Data Hub or Customer Platform.

Do the arithmetic before the demo. Sales Hub Professional runs $100/seat/month billed monthly, $90 annually, and carries 3,000 credits — that is 60 resolved support conversations, or 30 recommended leads, or 300 Data Agent answers, or three pieces of Content Agent output. A ten-rep team running Prospecting Agent seriously clears the Professional allowance in the first fortnight and lands on the capacity pack.

## Best for

RevOps and support leaders at $1M-50M ARR companies already on HubSpot Professional or Enterprise, whose highest-value agent job is deflecting inbound support volume at a known unit cost. Customer Agent is the strongest of the four: HubSpot reports it resolving 65% of conversations and cutting resolution time 39% across 8,000 accounts, and at $0.50 a resolution the payback arithmetic is legible without a business case.

Skip it when HubSpot is not your system of record, when cold outbound to a purchased list is the actual gap, or when you need agent behavior that Agent Builder cannot express while it remains in beta.

## Versus the alternatives

[Salesforce Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/) is the enterprise pole and the direct price comparison: $2 per conversation against Breeze's $0.50 per resolved conversation is 4:1 on a unit that also favors Breeze, since Agentforce bills the conversation whether or not it ends anything. Agentforce wins on cross-cloud orchestration and on orgs where Salesforce holds the record graph. Data Agent and an Agentforce standard action both land at $0.10, so the gap is narrower than the headline.

Purpose-built CX agents are the real competition for the Customer Agent budget. [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) and [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) go deeper on resolution quality and multi-step workflow execution, and neither publishes a rate — third-party reporting puts both in six-figure annual commitments. Take one of them when deflection quality is the business rather than a cost line, and take Breeze when the support volume rides on a HubSpot portal you already pay for. [Day.ai](/en/tools/day-ai/) is the fastest-growing entrant in agentic CRM and argues the opposite case: rebuild the system of record around agents instead of layering them on. Weigh it only if you are willing to leave HubSpot.

If none fit, run [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) against the remote MCP server and pair [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) for prospecting depth. You keep the agent layer outside the CRM and pay nothing per resolution; you build and monitor the orchestration yourself.

## Watch-outs

- **The definition of "resolved" is the meter.** HubSpot counts a resolution when the agent shares a source or takes an action and no human picks the conversation up within 72 hours — or when it qualifies a lead. A customer who gives up and leaves bills the same as one who got an answer. **Guard:** for the first 60 days, reconcile billed resolutions against reopen rate and CSAT on the same tickets, and treat any conversation reopened after the 72-hour window as an unresolved one you paid for.
- **The credit allowance is smaller than the number you have read.** The widely-quoted 5,000 and 10,000 figures apply to Data Hub and Customer Platform; a team on Sales Hub Professional alone gets 3,000, and buying a second hub does not add to it. **Guard:** read the allowance in your own portal rather than a pricing blog, then model blended burn at rate-sheet rates before enabling agents portfolio-wide.
- **Content Agent is an order of magnitude off the others.** At 1,000 credits it consumes a third of the Professional allowance for one piece and costs $10 marginal. **Guard:** leave it off by default and price the same job as a Claude plus n8n pipeline first; turn it on only for formats where you have measured what the output replaces.
- **HubSpot's own docs disagree on entitlement.** The Agent Hub page scopes the layer to Professional and Enterprise, while the Prospecting Agent product page lists Starter, Professional, and Enterprise. **Guard:** confirm the entitlement inside your portal before you plan a Starter-tier rollout, and get the tier written into the order form.
- **Agent Hub and Agent Builder are both in public beta.** Surfaces, names, and limits have moved twice in four months. **Guard:** keep every agent's job reproducible outside HubSpot — a documented prompt and a fallback workflow — so a beta change costs you a rebuild rather than the process itself.
