## What it is

BigHand sells business-of-law software to law firms: the tools that decide what a matter should cost, who should staff it, and whether it made money. It started in digital dictation and grew by acquisition into three groups. **Financial productivity** covers Matter Pricing, Budgeting and Forecasting, Business Intelligence (built on the 2022 Iridium acquisition), Partner Performance, PrebillManager and AlertManager. **People productivity** covers Resource Management, Workflow Management for support teams, Impact Analytics, and dictation. **Document productivity** covers templates, metadata cleaning, stamping, redaction and email-recipient checks. BigHand claims more than 3,300 law-firm clients and 81% of the Am Law 200. Private equity firm Levine Leichtman Capital Partners has owned it since 2020.

The question BigHand is now selling into is how firms price and staff matters when AI shrinks the hours they bill. Its own 2026 finance report, published 22 January from a survey of more than 800 legal finance leaders, found about two-thirds of firms reporting falling billable hours while 99% planned to raise targets, and 89% reporting higher write-offs. Matter-level pricing and profitability tracking is the fix it recommends, and that is the product it sells.

## What changed in 2026

- **It bought an AI pricing company.** On 4 August 2026 BigHand acquired Ayora, a UK legal-pricing start-up, for undisclosed terms, after a partnership announced the quarter before. Ayora drafts fee proposals by finding comparable historic matters and adjusting for current scope, staffing and fee assumptions. Its co-founder Stefan Ciesla-Grain now runs AI and data product strategy at BigHand.
- **Matter Pricing gets the AI first.** BigHand says the first combined Ayora and Matter Pricing capabilities ship later in 2026, with Resource Management and Business Intelligence to follow. It markets the combined direction as "Predict".
- **AI is arriving across the suite, unevenly.** Workflow Management's Spring Update '26 added AI email routing. Impact Analytics applies AI to unstructured timecard data, the job Digitory Legal did before BigHand bought it in August 2022.

## Pricing

Quote-only. BigHand publishes no prices, G2 lists none, and Capterra shows Workflow Management and Resource Management as priced per feature, which in practice means per module. We found no credible third-party price band, so do not trust any figure you see quoted without a source. Expect the quote to scale with the number of modules and timekeepers. The vendor's headline Matter Pricing claim, a 23% average reduction in write-offs within 12 months, is its own marketing figure; ask for the customer behind it.

## Best for

Pricing, finance and practice-management leads at mid-size and large firms, roughly 100 lawyers and up, who already run [Aderant](/en/tools/aderant/) or [Elite 3E](/en/tools/elite-3e/) and need to quote AFAs and fixed fees from their own matter history instead of a partner's spreadsheet. The specific use case: a pricing team that builds a budget from comparable past matters, tracks burn against it weekly, and reports realization to partners from the same data.

Not for: firms under about 50 lawyers, where [Clio](/en/tools/clio/) reporting plus a spreadsheet covers pricing, or corporate legal departments, whose problem is outside-counsel spend and belongs to [Brightflag](/en/tools/brightflag/).

## Alternatives

- **Elite 3E Matter Pricing and [Intapp](/en/tools/intapp/), the two largest vendors that compete for the same budget.** Elite's pricing module recalls budgets from past matters and models hourly or fixed-fee scenarios inside the financial system. Pick it when you want pricing and billing from one vendor. Pick Intapp when it already runs your intake and conflicts and its Celeste agents cover pricing well enough.
- **[Laurel](/en/tools/laurel/), the fastest-growing entrant.** Laurel Signal, launched in February 2026, reports profitability and cost of delivery by matter from captured time. Pick it when the root problem is time data quality, not the pricing model.
- **Litera Foundation Scoping** (formerly Clocktimizer) when the firm already runs Litera's Foundation data platform.
- **[Entegrata](/en/tools/entegrata/)** when reporting must join HR, CRM and intake data, not just finance.

## Watch-outs

- **The AI is acquired, not yet integrated.** The Ayora features are promised for later in 2026, and the extension to staffing and BI has no date. Guard: price the renewal on what is generally available in your tenant on the contract start date, and get the Ayora roadmap written into the order form as a dated deliverable or a price adjustment.
- **The module count drives the bill.** Seventeen-plus products sold per feature turn a pricing purchase into a platform purchase. Guard: ask for every module as a separate line item with its own unit price, and cap annual uplift on multi-year terms.
- **Its pricing is only as good as your matter history.** A comparable-matter model fed with inconsistent task codes and late time entries will produce confident wrong budgets. Guard: before the pilot, pull 50 closed matters from one practice group, check phase and task coding coverage, and run new quotes side by side with the pricing team's own numbers for a quarter.
- **Public reviews are thin.** Capterra and G2 show little or no review volume for the pricing and staffing products. Guard: ask for two reference firms of your size on your practice-management system, and speak to their pricing lead, not IT.
