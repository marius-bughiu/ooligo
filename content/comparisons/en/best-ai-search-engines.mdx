Three AI search products are worth paying for in 2026: Perplexity, Claude, and ChatGPT. They win at different jobs, and the gap between them is sharper than it was a year ago — mostly because the free baseline moved.

## What changed, and why it changes the decision

On 2026-01-27 Google made Gemini 3 the default model behind AI Overviews worldwide and wired an AI Overview into a conversational follow-up with AI Mode, so a free Google query now returns a synthesized answer rather than a link list. Perplexity moved too: a $200/mo Max tier with Model Council, a first-party MCP server, and the Comet browser dropped behind no paywall as of March 2026.

Both moves push the same way. A paid AI search subscription now has to beat Google's free answer — not ten blue links. That raises the bar on what you buy a subscription *for*: citations you can defend, documents Google can't see, and the ability to call the thing from an agent instead of a browser tab.

## 1. Perplexity — search-first, cited by default

ooligo score: 8.3. Perplexity is the only one of the three whose entire product is organized around retrieval and citation rather than chat with search attached. Every answer carries clickable sources, Spaces keep a research project's pinned sources across sessions, and Internal Knowledge Search on the Enterprise tiers puts your own docs alongside the live web.

**Where it pulls ahead:** repeated, shareable research where someone downstream will ask where a number came from. Account research before a QBR, competitive intel a CRO will forward, regulatory scans a GC will re-check. The Space is the artifact, not the chat log.

**Where it loses:** long-document synthesis. Drop a 200-page filing on it and you get a good summary; drop the same filing on Claude and you get an analysis that holds the whole thing at once.

**Where to start:** make it your default search for one week on research tasks only. If nobody on the team clicks a citation in that week, you didn't need the tier.

## 2. Claude — long-document synthesis with search bolted on the right side

ooligo score: 9.5. Claude searches the web with mandatory citations and has a Research mode that opens several sources, reads them, and returns a structured brief. The differentiator is what surrounds the search: 1M-token context on the current Opus and Sonnet models, so the retrieved material and your own corpus sit in the same prompt without retrieval scaffolding.

**Where it pulls ahead:** any question whose answer lives in documents rather than on the open web — a deal room of contracts, a quarter of CRM activity, a year of support tickets, an 80-page RFP response. Also agentic work: Skills plus MCP mean the same research routine runs from claude.ai, Claude Code, or your own client.

**Where it loses:** fast factual lookup. Asking Claude for a vendor's current price list is slower and no more accurate than asking Perplexity.

**Where to start:** take the next task that involves more than one long document and route it to Research mode instead of a summarizer.

## 3. ChatGPT — the one your team already has

ooligo score: 8.8. ChatGPT's web-grounded search and Deep Research mode are both credible, and Developer mode gives it full MCP client access — read *and* write — against connected systems, on Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Education accounts. That last part matters: it moved ChatGPT from "assistant that reads your tools" to "assistant that changes things in them."

**Where it pulls ahead:** distribution. If 40 people already have Plus seats and log in daily, the cheapest working AI search deployment is the one nobody has to adopt. Custom GPTs let you codify a recurring ops routine — an ICP scoring rubric, a redline checklist, a candidate scorecard — into something a non-technical teammate can run.

**Where it loses:** citation discipline. It cites when asked and when the mode calls for it; Perplexity cites because that is the product. For output headed to a regulator, a board, or opposing counsel, that difference is not cosmetic.

**Where to start:** if the team is already on Plus or Business, run the next thorny multi-source question through Deep Research before you buy anything else. Write-enabled MCP connectors deserve a separate decision — see the watch-out below.

## Pricing, side by side

Individual entry is a three-way tie at roughly $20/mo: Perplexity Pro at $20 ($200/yr), Claude Pro at $20 ($17/mo billed annually), ChatGPT Plus at $20. The spread opens at the top and at the team tier.

- **Power tier:** Perplexity Max $200/mo, ChatGPT Pro $200/mo (with a lighter $100/mo Pro tier since April 2026), Claude Max $100-$200/mo. Same band, roughly 10× the entry price.
- **Team/business seats:** ChatGPT Business $20/seat/mo annual with a 2-seat minimum; Claude Team $25/seat/mo ($20 annually) with a 5-seat minimum; Perplexity Enterprise Pro $40/seat/mo. Perplexity's team seat runs about 2× the other two — you're paying for Internal Knowledge Search and the no-training data guarantee.
- **Top of the enterprise ladder:** Perplexity Enterprise Max at $325/seat/mo is roughly 5× ChatGPT's ~$60/seat Enterprise floor. That gap is only defensible if research volume is the job itself.

Running all three at individual tiers costs $60/mo. For a research-heavy role that is one hour of loaded salary, which is why most operators stop optimizing and just buy all three.

## What's not on this list, and why

- **Google AI Mode / Gemini in Search** — excluded because it isn't a purchase decision. It is the default you already get, worldwide, on Gemini 3. Treat it as the baseline every paid option has to beat, not as an option. Google AI Pro at $19.99/mo and AI Ultra at $99.99-$199.99/mo raise the limits, but they don't change the shape of the answer.
- **[Gemini Enterprise](/en/tools/gemini/)** — a different job. From $21/seat/mo it does permission-aware search over *your* systems, competing with Glean, not with web AI search. Buy it for internal retrieval; it won't replace any of the three above for open-web research.
- **Microsoft Copilot** — reasonable inside a committed Microsoft 365 estate, and the estate is the reason to pick it. On open-web research quality it trails Perplexity.
- **You.com, Andi, Phind** — real products, too small a share to put on an ops shortlist. If one lands on your desk, benchmark it against Perplexity on ten of your own queries before taking it seriously.

## Watch-outs

- **Write-enabled MCP connectors.** ChatGPT Developer mode can delete and update records in connected systems, and prompt injection through a fetched page is the live attack. *Guard:* enable it on a service account with read-only scopes on anything you can't restore, and review the connector list quarterly.
- **Citation theater.** All three will produce a source list containing a page that doesn't support the claim. *Guard:* for anything going to a regulator, a board, or opposing counsel, click through every citation that carries a number.

## The minimum viable choice

If you can't justify going deeper: **Perplexity Pro at $20/mo.** It is the one whose output survives being forwarded, and the switching cost to either of the others is a month.

Then split by job. Fast factual research with defensible citations: Perplexity. More than one long document: Claude. Already standardized on ChatGPT and unwilling to add a tool: ChatGPT with Deep Research.

If none of the three fits — because the answers live entirely inside your own systems rather than on the web — you don't have an AI search problem. You have an enterprise retrieval problem, and the shortlist is Gemini Enterprise and Glean.
