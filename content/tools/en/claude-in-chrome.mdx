## What it is

Claude in Chrome is Anthropic's browser extension that lets [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) navigate, click, type and fill forms in your own Google Chrome tabs, with the logins you already have. It became generally available on every paid Claude plan on 26 August 2026: Pro, Max, Team, Ultra and Enterprise. It is not on Free. It runs from a side panel, installs from the Chrome Web Store, and Anthropic's GA announcement says it doesn't run on other Chromium browsers or on mobile yet.

On Max, Ultra and Team the side panel now runs as a [Claude Cowork](/en/tools/claude-cowork/) session, with history, skills, plugins and connectors. On Enterprise that happens only once an admin enables Cowork in the cloud; until then users get the classic panel. Developers can also drive it from [Claude Code](/en/tools/claude-code/) to test a site they are building.

It has a sibling. The Claude desktop app ships its own built-in browser that needs no install, keeps its cookies apart from your other browsers, and in Cowork hides recognized password, one-time-code, card and Social Security number fields from Claude. New browser-use users get the built-in browser by default. Claude in Chrome is the choice when the work has to happen in the Chrome profile where you are already signed in to Salesforce, Greenhouse or the e-billing portal.

## Why it shows up in ops stacks

Most of the admin work in ops has no API worth calling: a vendor portal, a legacy ATS screen, a settings page in [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) that a connector doesn't expose. Claude in Chrome reaches those screens with no integration project, on a seat the company may already pay for.

The GA release changed how much a human has to sit and watch. There are three modes, picked from the chat input:

- **Manually approve**: Claude pauses before each action. You allow it for that site, for this time only, or deny it.
- **Automatically approve**: the default in the Cowork side panel. A classifier checks each action Claude is about to take against what you originally asked for and blocks anything that doesn't match. Separate probes scan page and email content for prompt injection. If Claude keeps hitting blocks, it drops back to asking you at every step.
- **Skip all approvals**: nothing checks Claude's actions.

Anthropic's published numbers are the reason Auto mode is usable. On its current red-team evaluation, attacks that reached the model succeeded 3.8% of the time against Opus 5 before extra safeguards. With probes plus the classifier, no attacks succeeded against Sonnet 5 or Opus 5, and 0.3% did against Fable 5. Some actions are refused in every mode: purchases and financial transactions, creating accounts, handling card or ID data, trades, and following instructions found in emails or web content.

## Pricing reality

There is no separate price. You buy a Claude seat: Pro at $20/mo ($17 on annual billing), Max at $100/mo, Ultra at $200/mo, plus Team and Enterprise seats. Browser tasks draw from the same 5-hour and weekly usage limits as everything else on the plan, and a screenshot-heavy multi-step task burns through them faster than chat. Budget like a Cowork user: the $20 seat is fine for a trial, and $100–$200 per person is realistic for someone who runs browser tasks daily.

## Best for

RevOps admins, legal-ops managers and recruiting coordinators who spend hours a week on repetitive work inside SaaS screens that have no usable connector: re-tagging records in a CRM list view, pulling invoice PDFs from three outside-counsel portals, copying interview slots between a scheduling tool and an ATS. It wins when the person running it already has the right access and the task needs that person's own signed-in session.

## Not for

Unattended, high-volume jobs over structured data. If the system has an API or an [n8n](/en/tools/n8n/) node, use that: it is cheaper, faster and easier to audit than a model taking screenshots. It is also the wrong tool when an agent needs its own identity. Claude in Chrome acts as the user, in the user's browser, so look at [Microsoft Agent 365](/en/tools/microsoft-agent-365/) or [Salesforce Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/) for agents with their own lifecycle.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) Chrome extension**: the fastest-moving entrant. OpenAI shut down its Atlas browser on 9 August 2026 and moved browser agents into the ChatGPT desktop app, a cloud browser and a Chrome extension. That extension gained tab control on 15 October 2026 and also runs in Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi. Pick it when the company is standardized on ChatGPT, or when staff use a browser other than Chrome.
- **[Gemini](/en/tools/gemini/) in Chrome (auto browse)**: the widest distribution, because it is built into Chrome itself. Auto browse is limited to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the US, has a daily cap on agentic actions, and needs an administrator to enable it on work accounts. Pick it for US teams on Google Workspace that want no extension to deploy.
- **[Perplexity](/en/tools/perplexity/) Comet**: a separate AI browser. Comet Enterprise installs silently through MDM and lets admins allow answers only, or actions only on approved domains. Pick it when you want the agent in a browser that isn't the one holding everyone's production logins.

## Watch-outs

- **It is on by default for organizations.** On Team the extension is enabled by default, and on Enterprise it is on unless an owner has already disabled it, and users aren't notified when an admin enables it. The exception is HIPAA-enabled orgs, where it stays off and is not covered by the BAA. Guard: before anyone announces it, an owner sets a restrictive allowlist under Organization settings > Claude in Chrome, which Anthropic itself recommends. The same list governs the built-in browser. Pin the extension to your org with the `forceLoginOrgUUID` Chrome policy so staff can't use it from a personal account.
- **Data handling is not enterprise-default.** Zero data retention is not supported, the same as Cowork. Claude screenshots every tab it works in, and Auto-mode checks may keep a copy of the latest screenshot. Guard: keep it off pages that show regulated data such as candidate medical notes, privileged matter files or full card numbers. Put those domains on the blocklist rather than relying on users to stay away.
- **Skip mode and scheduled tasks remove the human.** In Skip mode nothing reviews Claude's actions, and a scheduled task can finish without a connector's "Always ask" prompt. Guard: set the rule that write actions on systems of record run in Manual or Auto mode only, and run scheduled browser tasks against read-only views or a least-privileged service login in a dedicated Chrome profile.
