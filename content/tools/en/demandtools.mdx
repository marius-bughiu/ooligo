## What it is

DemandTools is Validity's data-quality toolkit for [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/): a downloadable app (Windows and, since the 2021 V release, Mac) that an admin signs into with Salesforce credentials and uses to find and merge duplicates, mass-update fields without an export round trip, import and export records while matching against what already exists, and score data quality with the Assess module. A scheduler runs saved scenarios unattended. The bundle also covers email validation (powered by Validity Engage) and change notifications, and third-party guides report that DupeBlocker, Validity's Salesforce-native app that blocks duplicates at entry, is included with DemandTools subscriptions. The product has been on the market since 2000, and G2 lists it at 4.6 out of 5 across 284 reviews.

What it is not: a multi-CRM tool, an enrichment source, or an AI product. It works on Salesforce only, it does not fill empty fields with third-party data, and Validity's product page names no AI features. The MCP connector Validity promotes in 2026 belongs to its email product, Validity Engage, not to DemandTools.

## Why RevOps teams buy it

Salesforce duplicate rules block or flag records at entry, but they do not clear the backlog. Native merge handles three records at a time, by hand. DemandTools closes that gap for the admin who owns a large org: match rules across any field combination (including cross-field comparisons, such as a phone number stored in the wrong field), master-record rules, contact-to-account relationship merging, and bulk merges that run on a schedule.

A typical scoped job: before a territory rebuild, a Salesforce admin at a 300-seat org converts 40,000 orphaned leads, matches them to existing accounts by normalized domain and company name, merges the duplicate contacts that years of list imports created, and then sets a weekly scheduled dedupe so the backlog does not come back.

## Pricing

Validity publishes no price list. Checked 2026-10-24 against Vendr's marketplace data and public listings:

- **Licensing basis.** Priced per seat in your Salesforce org, not per DemandTools user, so two admins in a 200-user org pay for 200 seats.
- **Full DemandTools.** Vendr lists $132 per seat per year (about $11 per seat per month). Its median negotiated price is $4,825 a year for 50 seats (27% off the $6,600 list) and $16,764 for 200 seats (36% off $26,400). Vendr reports 40-55% discounts at 200+ seats.
- **DemandTools Elements** (the dedupe-focused edition): $801 for 25 seats per Vendr, consistent with the $2.67 per seat per month G2 and Capterra show.
- **What changed.** In December 2025 a Validity customer-success director confirmed in the company's community forum that webstore plan types are being retired in favour of Enterprise plans. Self-serve card purchases are effectively gone; expect a sales quote and an annual (or one-to-three-year) contract.

## Best for

- A Salesforce admin or RevOps lead at a 100-2,000 seat org with a duplicate and standardization backlog in the hundreds of thousands of records, who wants the deepest dedupe and merge rules on the market and is comfortable running a desktop app
- Teams cleaning a Salesforce org before a migration, an org merge after an acquisition, or a territory and routing rebuild
- Not for HubSpot-first teams, not for a 20-seat org with 15,000 records (Salesforce's native rules plus an afternoon cover it), and not for teams whose real problem is empty fields

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **Salesforce duplicate and matching rules**: free and already in your org. Pick them when the backlog is small; pick DemandTools when you need bulk merges, scheduling, and cross-field matching.
- **Plauti Deduplicate** (formerly Duplicate Check): runs natively inside Salesforce, with a free edition and paid tiers that Plauti's own 2023 comparison started at $588 a year. Pick it when the data must never leave the org or the seat count makes DemandTools expensive.
- **Cloudingo**: the other long-standing Salesforce dedupe specialist, with third-party listings putting entry pricing between $2,500 and $10,000 a year. Pick it when you want a web app instead of a desktop install.
- **[Insycle](/en/tools/insycle/)**: per-record pricing, unlimited users, HubSpot and Salesforce. Pick it when the org is HubSpot-first or seat-heavy and record-light: 50 seats with 80,000 records costs about $4,800-$6,600 a year on DemandTools versus $1,440 on Insycle Growth.
- **[Openprise](/en/tools/openprise/)** or **[ZoomInfo](/en/tools/zoominfo/) Operations** (formerly RingLead): pick them when data quality spans marketing automation, the CRM, and routing, and the budget is in the tens of thousands. Pick [Traction Complete](/en/tools/traction-complete/) for Salesforce-native account hierarchies, and [LeanData](/en/tools/leandata/) when routing is the real problem.
- **An agent against the Salesforce API**: the fastest-growing approach in the category. The [Salesforce data cleanup skill](/en/workflows/salesforce-data-cleanup-skill/) drafts match rules and merge plans from Claude Code for the cost of tokens. Pick it for one-off cleanups run by a technical admin; keep DemandTools for scheduled, audited hygiene.

## Watch-outs

- **The plan you have may not renew.** With webstore plans retired, a self-serve customer can reach renewal and find no matching plan. Guard: check your renewal date now, ask for an Enterprise quote 90 days ahead, and bring the Vendr medians above to the negotiation.
- **Seat pricing punishes large, light-use orgs.** You pay for every Salesforce seat, even though one or two admins run the tool. Guard: run the per-record math against Insycle and the native-app pricing of Plauti before you sign, and negotiate seat count separately from term.
- **Bulk merges are hard to undo.** Salesforce cannot unmerge records; merged-away records go to the Recycle Bin, but restoring them does not move back the related activities. Guard: export the affected objects before every bulk run, test the scenario on a filtered sample of 50 groups, and review the surviving masters by hand.
- **Runs consume your Salesforce API allocation.** DemandTools works through the Salesforce API, so a large scheduled run competes with integrations for the org's daily limit. Guard: schedule big jobs overnight, run them under a dedicated integration user, and check API usage in Salesforce Setup after the first full run.

For the operating model around any of these tools (who owns hygiene, which fields to standardize first, how often to run), see [CRM hygiene](/en/learn/crm-hygiene/).
