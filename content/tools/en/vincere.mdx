## What it is

Access Vincere Evo is an ATS and recruiting CRM for recruitment agencies that run permanent, contract, temp, executive-search, and RPO desks. It keeps clients, job orders, candidates, submissions, and placements in one record set, and it adds timesheets and pay-and-bill for contract work. Vincere was founded in Singapore in 2012. The Access Group, the UK business-software group, bought it in January 2022 and now sells it as Access Vincere Evo. It is still sold as its own product, not folded into another Access suite. Access says 22,000+ recruiters use it.

The AI layer is Access Evo, the group-wide assistant that Access is building into all its products. In Vincere, the current plan page lists AI candidate matching and shortlisting, "AI agents and autopilots", a Meeting Assistant that transcribes live calls, syncs them to the CRM, and drafts follow-ups, and AI-formatted candidate presentations.

The fact this page turns on: **Vincere's packaging changed in 2026.** Until mid-2026, Vincere sold a seat licence from £69 per user per month. AI came as a separate "Smart Pack" priced per agency, from £25 (Base) to £349 (Elite) a month, on a 12-month minimum term. Most third-party pricing guides still quote that model. The plan page we checked on 17 October 2026 shows a different one: AI is built into the seat, and the two smaller plans run on 30-day rolling terms.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Perm and contract desks on one system, at mid-market size.** Core includes temp and contract management with scheduling and timesheets. Workers submit hours, expenses, and leave through a web portal, and client managers approve them by logging in or by email. Pro adds built-in Pay & Bill, which generates branded invoice and payslip PDFs. Payroll runs through FastTrack360, another Access product, which has a rates-and-rules engine for multi-condition pay. Recruiterflow and Loxo have no back office. Bullhorn licenses its Middle Office separately.
- **UK compliance in the box.** Core includes onboarding with DBS and Right to Work checks, the two checks a UK agency has to run before a candidate starts. A US-built ATS usually needs a partner integration for both.
- **AI included in the seat, not metered per agency.** On the current plans, a two-person agency gets the same matching, Meeting Assistant, and presentation formatting as a seven-person one. Under the old Smart Pack model, the small agency paid the same £349 for Elite as a large one.
- **A large back catalogue.** Spott, a competitor, counts about 66 named marketplace integrations across job boards, sourcing, telephony, payroll, and e-signature, plus a Zapier connector.

## Pricing reality

Checked on [theaccessgroup.com's Vincere pricing page](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/products/vincere/pricing/) on 17 October 2026. Prices are in pounds sterling, and the page does not say whether they exclude VAT:

- **Startup.** One user. CRM, business-development tools, email and calendar sync, reporting dashboards, and self-serve onboarding, with no AI features listed. The vincere.io pricing page put it at £150 a month flat, as recorded by a third-party comparison on 12 October 2026. We could not load that page ourselves.
- **Core — £95 per user per month, for 2-7 users, on a 30-day rolling term.** Adds all the AI features above, an advanced KPI dashboard, onboarding with DBS and Right to Work checks, temp and contract management with timesheets, a branded careers site, and guided implementation.
- **Pro — custom quote, above 7 users.** Adds multi-brand support, built-in Pay & Bill, a client portal, full-service implementation, a dedicated CSM, a same-day SLA on critical issues, and monthly business reviews. The vincere.io page showed "from £85 per user", per the same third-party record.

Concrete numbers: five recruiters on Core cost £475 a month, or £5,700 a year at list. Ten recruiters on Pro start at £850 a month, or £10,200 a year, before whatever the quote adds for implementation. Under the old model the same ten seats with the Elite pack cost £12,468 a year. Against dollar-priced peers, ten Bullhorn Core seats list at $19,800 a year with no AI, and ten Recruit CRM Business seats at $16,200.

The list price still leaves out several costs. Pin, a competitor, collects third-party estimates of $2,000-$10,000 for setup and $3,000-$15,000 for a ten-user data migration (ITQlick, February 2026). A Capterra reviewer reported paying over €1,000 to export their data on exit (December 2025). Reviewers also reported a 6.5% price rise in April 2025.

## Best for

Owners and ops leads at UK and APAC recruitment agencies with about 2-50 recruiters that run contract or temp desks next to permanent placements, and want timesheets, compliance checks, and AI matching in one system. Concretely: an eight-recruiter London agency with a few dozen weekly contractors that runs DBS and Right to Work checks on every start.

**Not for you if** you hire for your own company. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) or [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) handle in-house hiring better. It is also the wrong pick if your team works through Claude or another AI assistant. Vincere ships no MCP server, and its API keys are issued per tenant by request, not self-serve.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/)**: the market-share leader in staffing. Pick it above about 200 seats, or when you need VMS connectivity, a 350+ partner marketplace, and a US-grade middle office. Its AI starts only on the quote-only Pro tier.
- **JobAdder**: the Australian-founded agency ATS that most often meets Vincere in UK and APAC deals. It is quote-only. Get a JobAdder quote alongside Vincere's so you can compare two real prices.
- **[Recruit CRM](/en/tools/recruit-crm/)**: pick it if your team wants a read-and-write MCP server and published dollar pricing. Since July 2026 it also has its own timesheets and invoicing.
- **[Spott](/en/tools/spott/)**: the fastest-growing entrant in agency ATS, with a $21M Series A in September 2026. Pick it if semantic candidate search and an AI note-taker matter more than back office. It has no timesheets or pay-and-bill.
- **[Loxo](/en/tools/loxo/)**: pick it for permanent-only desks where sourcing new candidates is the bottleneck.

**The rule:** if you are a UK agency with a contract desk and fewer than about 50 recruiters, put Vincere on the shortlist first. If you place permanent hires only, or your team works through AI assistants, start with Recruit CRM or Spott.

## Watch-outs

- **Third-party pricing is out of date.** Most 2026 guides still quote £69 plus Smart Packs and a 12-month minimum term. **Guard:** quote from the live plan page, and get the plan name, seat price, and term written into the order form. If you are an existing customer on the old model, ask in writing whether moving to Core or Pro changes your renewal date or notice period. Pin reports a three-month notice window on the old contracts.
- **The 7-user boundary is ambiguous.** Core is listed for 2-7 users, but Pro is described as 7+, and Pro is quote-only. **Guard:** if you have 6-9 recruiters, ask for both a Core and a Pro price, and get it in writing which plan applies when you hire your next recruiter.
- **Search is keyword-first.** Spott, a competitor, says that as of July 2026 Vincere's AI pages did not advertise natural-language search across the candidate database. Its search runs on tags, fields, Boolean strings, and maps. Capterra reviewers also mention a cluttered UI. **Guard:** in the demo, load 1,000 of your own CVs and run three real job orders through AI matching before you sign.
- **Exit costs are not published.** **Guard:** before signing, get the format, timing, and price of a full data export written into the contract, including attachments and notes.
