## What it is

SAP Fieldglass is a vendor management system (VMS) plus a services-procurement tool: the software an enterprise uses to request, source, onboard, time-track, invoice and offboard people who are not on payroll, and to buy project work under a statement of work (SOW). Fieldglass was a Chicago company until SAP bought it in 2014. It now sits in SAP's spend management family next to SAP Ariba and SAP Concur, and next to SAP SuccessFactors on the HR side.

It ships as three products. **Contingent Workforce Management** handles agency temps: requisitions, supplier distribution, rate cards, timesheets and consolidated invoicing. **Services Procurement** handles SOW work, where the statement of work, its deliverables and milestone payments are the record rather than an hourly worker. **Worker Profile Management** keeps one record for every external person who never shows up on a Fieldglass invoice, such as outsourced cleaners or vendor-paid consultants who still need a badge and system access.

The question Fieldglass answers: **how does procurement see and pay for all external labor through the same purchase orders, cost centers and suppliers that run the rest of our SAP spend?**

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks

TA operations rarely buys a VMS, but it inherits one the moment the company starts direct sourcing or converting contractors to employees, and staffing agencies live inside their clients' instances. On the supplier side, ATS tools such as [Bullhorn](/en/tools/bullhorn/), [Ceipal](/en/tools/ceipal/) and [JobDiva](/en/tools/jobdiva/) sync with Fieldglass so recruiters receive requisitions and push submittals without re-keying them.

The reach is the selling point. In SAP's January 2026 summary of analyst coverage, Staffing Industry Analysts (SIA) credits Fieldglass with operating in more than 180 countries, 22 languages and invoicing in 118 countries, and SIA's 2025 landscape, built on 23 VMS surveys and 36 customer assessments, rated it strong on SOW, supplier management and time, expense and billing. Ardent Partners' 2025 VMS Technology Advisor named it a Market Leader and an Elite Performer in AI innovation, SOW and services procurement, direct sourcing and total talent management. Both are vendor-cited analyst results.

The AI layer is newer than the marketing implies. Joule, SAP's assistant, drafts requisitions and job descriptions and speeds approvals, and Document AI turns uploaded documents into structured SOWs. AI-assisted SOW deliverables creation shipped in Q1 2026; SAP claims it cuts manual creation time by 70%. SAP's Sapphire 2026 innovation guide listed Joule agents for Fieldglass Services Procurement for general availability in June 2026, and trade coverage from 25 June still described them as planned. SAP has said Joule agent runtime is free for current Ariba and Fieldglass customers through 31 December 2026.

Integration is native when the rest of the estate is SAP. S/4HANA connects through prebuilt connectors, directly or through SAP Integration Suite, with approvals flowing both ways. Combined with Ariba, services and goods can land on one purchase order. [Icertis](/en/tools/icertis/) plugs in as an SAP solution extension for contract intelligence.

## Pricing reality

SAP publishes no price list. Its own training material describes two licensing methods: a **subscription** paid up front monthly or annually, and a **legacy pay-as-you-go** model billed in arrears on the spend managed through Fieldglass. Funding is a separate choice. **Supplier-funded** programs short-pay each supplier an agreed percentage, so you never see a software invoice. **Buyer-funded** programs pay implementation, subscription and transaction fees directly. **Hybrid** programs split it, for example the client pays for contingent and suppliers fund the SOW module.

License advisors that negotiate Fieldglass deals quote fees from roughly 0.35% to 2% of managed spend, falling with volume. Treat about 1% as the working midpoint: some $500K a year on a $50M program. A managed service provider running the program adds 2% to 3.5% of spend, and the systems integrator implementation is billed on top.

## Best for

Procurement-led contingent workforce programs at enterprises with $50M+ a year in external labor and services spend that already run S/4HANA, Ariba or SuccessFactors, and especially those buying a lot of SOW work. Skip it if HR runs on Workday or your finance stack is mixed, or if you manage fewer than about 50 contractors at a time. It is not an applicant tracking system: employee hiring stays in [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/) or [Workday](/en/tools/workday/).

## Versus the alternatives

**[Beeline](/en/tools/beeline/)** is the other half of the category's top two by spend under management. Pick Beeline when HR and finance are not SAP, or when independent contractors matter: its MBO Partners deal adds employer-of-record services Fieldglass does not sell. Pick Fieldglass when procurement owns the program and SAP owns the ledger.

**Workday VNDLY**, bought by Workday for $510M in December 2021, puts contractors in the same worker record as employees. Pick it when Workday HCM is your system of record.

**SimplifyVMS** is the fastest-rising independent, a Leader and Star Performer in Everest Group's 2025 and 2026 VMS PEAK Matrix. Pick it for a vendor-neutral VMS without a suite attached, at a mid-market budget.

## Watch-outs

- **The agent dates are a roadmap, and the free runtime expires.** Joule agents for Services Procurement were listed for June 2026, and free runtime ends 31 December 2026. *Guard:* confirm in your tenant's What's New release notes that the agent is live before building a process on it, and get 2027 runtime pricing in writing before renewal.
- **Supplier-funded does not mean free.** SAP's own material warns that suppliers may build the platform fee into their rates. *Guard:* compare fully loaded bill rates at renewal and price a buyer-funded subscription next to the percent-of-spend offer.
- **Setup is heavy.** Complex setup and a dated, hard-to-learn interface are the recurring complaints in G2 and Capterra reviews. *Guard:* contract a fixed-scope implementation, go live with contingent labor first and add SOW in a second phase, and budget hiring-manager training.
- **Worker sync breaks on bad HR master data.** Employee Central to Fieldglass integrations fail until discrepancies are fixed in the source system. *Guard:* clean cost centers, org units and manager assignments before go-live, and run a weekly reconciliation between SuccessFactors and Fieldglass for the first quarter.
- **Legacy pay-as-you-go contracts get pushed to subscription.** SAP labels pay-as-you-go legacy. *Guard:* model both options against three years of forecast spend before the renewal conversation starts, not during it.
