## What it is

ADP is the largest payroll provider in the US. In January 2026 it said its platform serves 1.1 million clients across 140 countries and territories and pays 42 million wage earners. **ADP Workforce Now** is its HR and payroll suite for mid-sized companies. **Workforce Now Recruitment** is the hiring module inside it: requisitions, job posting, an applicant tracker, offers, and a handoff into onboarding and payroll in the same system. Larger ADP clients get a separate product, **ADP Recruiting Management**, sold as part of the ADP Talent Acquisition suite. It adds resume parsing, branded career sites, and background-screening services.

The AI features are assistive, not agentic. ADP's Workforce Now talent page lists AI-generated, skills-based job descriptions, candidate recommendations with qualification summaries each time a requisition is created, and "smart profile relevance scores" for ranking applicants. It also lists two-way text, candidate self-scheduling, distribution to more than 25,000 job sites across North America, and salary benchmarks drawn from more than 39 million US employee pay records. ADP Assist launched AI agents in January 2026 for payroll variances, tax IDs, and HR policy answers. We found no ADP-built recruiting agent. On 2 March 2026 ADP opened an AI agents section in ADP Marketplace with a "Finding Talent" category. Those agents come from partners, not from ADP.

This page answers one question: **we already run payroll on ADP, so is its recruiting add-on good enough, or do we need a real ATS on top?**

## Why it shows up in recruiting stacks

- **The hire is already in payroll.** An accepted offer in Workforce Now Recruitment becomes the employee record that ADP pays. Nobody re-keys a name, start date, or pay rate, and no sync can drop a field. A dedicated ATS has to write the hire across an integration. Greenhouse's connector for Workforce Now has to be bought through ADP Marketplace, exports a candidate when they reach hired status, and supports US-based hires only.
- **The price is published, which is rare at ADP.** Most ADP pricing is a sales quote. The recruitment add-on has a list price on ADP Marketplace (see below).
- **Unlimited users.** The listing includes unlimited users, so every hiring manager can get a login without a seat count.
- **The marketplace is the extension path.** ADP Marketplace lists ATS, assessment, and screening apps that connect to Workforce Now, including [Workable](/en/tools/workable/) and [Criteria](/en/tools/criteria-corp/). When the built-in tracker runs out, you can swap it out without leaving ADP payroll.

## Pricing reality

ADP Marketplace lists Workforce Now Recruitment at **$3.00 per employee per month** for companies with 50–999 employees, plus a **$2,000 setup fee** due at completion (checked 19 October 2026). Billing follows active employee count, not recruiter seats. Clients on ADP's fully outsourced Workforce Now service tier cannot buy it.

You need the Workforce Now base first, and ADP publishes no price for it. Vendr's ADP benchmark lists $10–18 per employee per month for core payroll and HR, $2,000–8,000 in implementation, and says 100–250-employee buyers typically pay $1,200–3,500 a month. Across 103 ADP deals, Vendr puts the median contract at $51,372 a year, with buyers saving an average of 22.07% off the first quote. That figure covers all ADP products, not Workforce Now alone.

Worked example: a 200-employee company pays $3 × 200 × 12 = **$7,200 a year** for recruiting, plus $2,000 once. That is the same whether it makes 10 hires a year or 150. For comparison, Vendr's median [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) contract is $26,587 a year, and you still need payroll underneath it. **The add-on is the cheapest credible ATS a Workforce Now customer can buy.** The question is whether it does enough.

## Best for

HR generalists and People Ops leads at 50–500-employee US companies that already run payroll on ADP Workforce Now, make fewer than about 50 hires a year, and mostly need to post jobs, collect applicants, text candidates, and hand the hire to payroll without re-entry. Concretely: a 180-person manufacturer whose office manager runs hiring part-time and still copies offer details into ADP by hand.

**Not for you if** you run structured interview loops with scorecards, source passive candidates, or hire across several countries. The tracker processes inbound applicants. ADP does not market sourcing, interview kits, or structured scorecards for it. G2 summaries of user reviews also flag a less modern interface than Greenhouse. It is also the wrong pick if you are not on ADP payroll. The native handoff is the whole advantage.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) on top of ADP payroll** is the usual upgrade. Pick it when hiring managers need interview kits and scorecards, when you hire more than about 50 people a year, or when recruiting reports go to leadership. Keep ADP for payroll and buy the Greenhouse connector. Pick the ADP add-on when one generalist runs hiring and roughly $19,000 a year in savings matters more than interview discipline.

**[Workday](/en/tools/workday/) and [UKG](/en/tools/ukg/)** are the other payroll-native suites with built-in recruiting. Workday is the enterprise pick above about 1,000 employees. UKG is the pick for hourly-heavy employers that need conversational screening (UKG Rapid Hire). Neither is a reason to leave ADP payroll just to get a better tracker.

**[Rippling](/en/tools/rippling/)** is the fastest-growing entrant. It crossed $1B in annualized revenue in March 2026, and its recruiting module shares one data model with payroll and device provisioning. Pick Rippling when you are re-platforming HR and payroll anyway and want IT onboarding to fire from the same new-hire record. Do not switch payroll vendors for the ATS alone.

**[Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/) or [Workstream](/en/tools/workstream/)** fit when you hire hourly staff in volume. Workforce Now Recruitment has texting and self-scheduling, but no conversational screening flow.

## Watch-outs

- **The PEPM price charges you for employees who never hire.** The $3 applies to every active employee, so a 900-person company with 20 hires a year pays $32,400 a year, or $1,620 per hire. Guard: divide the annual add-on cost by your planned hires before signing. If the cost per hire passes what a per-seat ATS would cost, price [Workable](/en/tools/workable/) or Greenhouse with the ADP connector instead.
- **The AI is assistive, and the agent story belongs to partners.** ADP's recruiting AI drafts job descriptions and scores relevance. It does not screen, interview, or schedule on its own, and the "Finding Talent" agents in ADP Marketplace come from third parties with their own contracts and data processing. Guard: score the deal on the features on ADP's product page today. For any Marketplace agent, get the partner's DPA and bias-audit evidence before turning it on. In New York City, Local Law 144 requires a bias audit for automated hiring tools that rank candidates.
- **Third-party ATS connectors are US-hire only.** Greenhouse's Workforce Now connector supports only US-based hires. Guard: if you hire outside the US, map where each country's new-hire record lands before you buy any ATS on top of ADP, and budget manual entry or a separate payroll provider for those hires.
