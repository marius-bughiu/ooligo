## What it is

Microsoft Copilot for Legal is the legal vertical of [Microsoft 365 Copilot](/en/tools/microsoft-365-copilot/), launched on 15 October 2026. It has three parts: more than 20 legal skills, two Microsoft-built plugins that deliver them (one in Word desktop, one in Copilot Cowork), and a registry of partner plugins from more than two dozen legal tech vendors. It is the fourth frontier-AI legal vertical in five months, after [Claude for Legal](/en/tools/claude-for-legal/) in May, [Gemini Enterprise for Legal](/en/tools/gemini-enterprise-for-legal/) in August and [OpenAI Astra for Law](/en/tools/openai-astra-for-law/) in September.

It is not a separate product or a separate licence. Microsoft's rollout notice says the plugins reach users who already hold a Microsoft 365 Copilot commercial licence. The Word plugin is on by default, and the Cowork plugin has to be added by the user. So the question it answers for most legal departments is not "should we buy it?" but "we already pay for Copilot, so do we still need [Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/) or [Legora](/en/tools/legora/)?"

## What you actually get

**The Word plugin** replaces Legal Agent, the contract-review tool Microsoft put into its Frontier early-access programme in April 2026. That tool followed Microsoft's January 2026 hiring of former Robin AI engineers into the Word team. In Word, Copilot reviews an agreement against a playbook, explains a counterparty's redline, drafts from approved precedents and templates in SharePoint, and applies its suggestions as native tracked changes that keep the document's formatting. That last point matters more than it sounds. Most legal AI add-ins hand back a suggested clause that someone has to paste in.

**The Cowork plugin** handles multi-file work. It extracts obligations and deadlines across a contract portfolio, compares key terms across related agreements, builds case chronologies, and turns meeting records into minutes and resolutions.

**Partner plugins** come through Microsoft's plugin registry: research (Bloomberg Law, Trellis, Descrybe, Courtroom5), document and matter management ([iManage](/en/tools/imanage/), [NetDocuments](/en/tools/netdocuments/), [Box](/en/tools/box/), [Litera](/en/tools/litera/), Thomson Reuters HighQ), agreements and e-signature (Docusign, [Juro](/en/tools/juro/), [LawVu](/en/tools/lawvu/), Adobe Acrobat Sign, PandaDoc), legal AI (Harvey, Legora, [DeepJudge](/en/tools/deepjudge/)) and eDiscovery ([Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/), [Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/)). Westlaw, CoCounsel and LexisNexis are absent at launch. Thomson Reuters appears only through HighQ.

Microsoft's own numbers for Legal Agent from April to October: more than 1,800 users made nearly 100,000 edits and kept roughly seven in ten suggestions. Those are vendor figures from internal evaluation. No independent legal benchmark has been published.

## The scoped use case that justifies it

An in-house team of eight lawyers at a 2,000-person company on Microsoft 365 E5, handling about 150 inbound NDAs and vendor agreements a month. The playbook sits in SharePoint and every agreement arrives as a Word file. With the Word plugin, a counsel opens the counterparty paper, runs the playbook review, and gets a marked-up draft with tracked changes and an issues list. There is no upload to a second tool and no new security review, and Copilot reads only what that user can already open. Microsoft's own senior corporate counsel describes an hour-long review often dropping to several minutes. Treat that as a vendor anecdote, but it is the right job to pilot.

## Pricing reality

The legal plugins carry no separate price. What you pay is the Copilot seat, checked on Microsoft's pricing pages today:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot (enterprise)**: $30/user/month on annual billing, or $31.50 paid monthly on an annual commitment, on top of a qualifying Microsoft 365 base licence.
- **Microsoft 365 Copilot Business** (up to 300 seats): $21/user/month list, with a first-year $18 promotion through 31 December 2026. Microsoft's notice says "commercial licences" without singling out this SKU, so confirm eligibility before you plan around it.
- **Cowork** is billed in Copilot Credits on top of the seat. The Cowork plugin's portfolio-wide work is exactly the metered part.
- **Partner plugins** need the partner's own licence. The Harvey plugin does nothing without a Harvey contract.

Worked band: 25 lawyers and legal-ops staff on enterprise Copilot cost 25 × $30 × 12 = **$9,000/year** before Cowork credits. The same 25 seats on Harvey, at the roughly $1,200/seat/month third-party reports cite, would cost about $360,000. That gap is why this launch matters. If your company has already rolled Copilot out to the legal team, the marginal cost of the legal skills is zero.

## Best for

General counsel and legal-ops leads at Microsoft 365 companies whose contract work happens in Word and whose precedents live in SharePoint or iManage. The fit is strongest for high-volume commercial review (NDAs, MSAs, vendor paper) and obligation tracking. It is weaker for litigation research, because primary-law content is missing at launch.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/)** is the segment leader by revenue and firm count. Pick it when you need a vendor accountable for legal output, legal research with citation validation, and lawyer-facing onboarding. It also ships a Copilot plugin, so the two can coexist: Copilot for routine Word review, Harvey for the matters that justify the seat.

**[Legora](/en/tools/legora/)** is the fastest-growing entrant. Pick it when associates need a collaborative tabular review and drafting surface across hundreds of documents, which Word-centric review does not give you.

**[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/)** is the pick when Westlaw research is the core job. Copilot for Legal does not reach Westlaw.

**[Claude for Legal](/en/tools/claude-for-legal/)** is the pick when you want to fork and own the prompts (it is Apache 2.0 on GitHub) or when your team does not run on Microsoft 365. **[Gemini Enterprise for Legal](/en/tools/gemini-enterprise-for-legal/)** is the equivalent for Google Workspace firms.

Take Copilot for Legal first when you already pay for Copilot seats. Run your highest-volume contract type through the Word plugin for 30 days before signing anything new, and buy a specialist only for the work it demonstrably fails.

## Watch-outs

- **It is on by default in Word.** Every Copilot user in Word desktop gets the legal skills, including people outside legal who will happily "review" a contract against no playbook. **Guard:** decide the scope before the rollout reaches your tenant. Turn off "Legal Skills in M365 Copilot in Word" in the Microsoft 365 Apps admin center until the legal team has published its playbooks in SharePoint.
- **The quality evidence is Microsoft's own.** Keep rates and fluency scores are internal evaluations, not an independent benchmark. **Guard:** re-review 50 of your own past agreements with the plugin, compare its issues list against what your lawyers actually flagged, and record the miss rate before you trust it on live matters.
- **Documents can steer it.** In July 2026 a researcher showed hidden white-on-white text making Copilot in Word alter figures and propagate itself into new documents. Counterparty paper is untrusted input by definition. **Guard:** require a lawyer to accept each tracked change individually, never "accept all", and apply Purview sensitivity labels so privileged libraries are not pulled in as automatic grounding.
- **Cowork is metered.** Portfolio-wide obligation extraction is the feature that consumes Copilot Credits. **Guard:** set a legal-department spending limit in Cost Management in the Microsoft 365 admin center before enabling the Cowork plugin, and budget credits as their own line.
