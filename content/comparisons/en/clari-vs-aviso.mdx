[Clari](/en/tools/clari/) and [Aviso](/en/tools/aviso/) used to be the two independent, forecasting-first revenue platforms an enterprise RevOps team shortlisted. In late 2026 only one of them is still that. Clari merged with [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/) on 3 December 2025, and on 2 September 2026 the combined company moved to a single Salesloft brand. What you buy today is **Clari Forecast**, one module on Salesloft's menu next to Cadence, Conversation Intelligence, Deals, Inspect and Rhythm. Aviso is still independent and still sells forecasting as the core of what it now calls a revenue operating system. So the choice is no longer "which forecasting vendor". It is whether your forecast should live inside your engagement vendor's platform, or with a vendor whose main product is the forecast.

## Where Clari Forecast wins

**Roll-up rigor that enterprise RevOps already knows.** Clari's forecast hierarchy is still its centerpiece and did not change in the rebrand. Commits roll up from rep to manager to VP to CRO, with documented overrides at each layer and a snapshot of every submitted call. When the board asks why the quarter closed below Commit, you can trace the gap to specific overrides at specific layers. Most enterprise RevOps people have run a Commit / Best Case / Pipeline call in Clari at some point, so the adoption cost is low.

**One contract with your engagement layer.** If Salesloft already runs your cadences and calls, Clari Forecast is now an add-on to a contract you have, not a second vendor. Cadence activity and conversation signals feed deal inspection through Salesloft's own pipes rather than a third-party connector. Spring 2026 releases linked forecasting to seller execution, though deeper feature-level integration was still rolling out through mid-2026. For a team not on Salesloft, this advantage reverses: you are buying into an engagement vendor's roadmap to get its forecast.

**Forecast data reachable from an LLM.** The Salesloft MCP server launched on 14 April 2026 and now exposes forecast and deal-inspection data alongside Cadence activity and call intelligence. It is listed in [Claude](/en/tools/claude/)'s connector directory, with ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini connectors after it. A RevOps lead can ask "which Commit deals have had no buyer activity in two weeks" from a chat client. We found no published MCP server or LLM connector from Aviso (checked 26 November 2026).

## Where Aviso wins

**Forecasting is the product, not a module.** Aviso is independent and forecasting-first. In the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Revenue Action Orchestration (published 13 October 2026), Aviso was one of four Leaders among 13 vendors. In the companion Critical Capabilities report it scored highest in two of four use cases, one of them "Manage Pipeline and Forecast". Forrester also named it a Leader and Customer Favorite in its Q3 2026 Wave for Revenue Orchestration Platforms. Analyst placements are vendor-reported marketing, but they are evidence that Aviso's forecasting depth has kept pace while Clari's roadmap merged into Salesloft's.

**Less CRM dependency.** Aviso builds on a time-series data foundation and a context graph that reads across CRMs and data lakehouses, and it positions itself for teams that want forecasting "without high CRM dependency". Its models weight historical deal patterns and engagement signals rather than relying on rep-set stages, so they degrade more gracefully on messy CRM data. Aviso also has stronger coverage outside Salesforce, including Microsoft Dynamics and Oracle CX, which matters for enterprises not standardized on Salesforce.

**Displacement terms.** Aviso's pricing page offers free data migration, white-glove onboarding, and a buyout of your current vendor contract. For a team locked into a multi-year Clari or Salesloft term, the buyout can turn a switch from a budget question into a timing question. Get it in writing, with the bought-out amount and any new term length stated.

**An accuracy claim you can interrogate.** Aviso advertises "98+%" forecast accuracy. That is a vendor claim with no published method. Ask for the reference customer, the forecast horizon (week 1 or week 10 of the quarter), and the error metric before you treat it as a benchmark. Clari Forecast's accuracy claims deserve the same questions.

## Pricing reality

Neither vendor publishes prices. `salesloft.com/pricing` and `aviso.com/pricing` are both request-a-quote forms (checked 26 November 2026). Third-party procurement data puts them much closer together than they were:

- **Clari**: Vendr's data across 291 purchases puts the median annual contract near $76,000. Docket estimated Clari Forecast at about $100-$120 per user per month on annual billing in April 2026. Implementation services run about $15,000-$75,000 over 8-16 weeks.
- **Aviso**: Vendr's median is $73,662 per year, but from only 10 purchases. Vendr's buyer guide gives a range of about $38,000 to $95,000, and its marketplace shows average negotiated savings near 15%.

At the median, Aviso comes out about 3% cheaper, a gap that disappears in negotiation. An earlier version of this page said Aviso cost about half as much. That rested on an older ~$160,000 Clari median that current Vendr data no longer supports. The real pricing difference is structural. Post-rebrand, Salesloft account teams will price engagement and conversation intelligence alongside Clari Forecast, so a forecast-only quote is the exception. Aviso sells per-user plans with what it calls hybrid pricing, and adds the buyout. Compare total contract value over the full term, including implementation and any bundled modules you would not otherwise buy.

## Verdict

**Pick Clari Forecast when** Salesloft is already your engagement platform, or will be. Your board expects an auditable Commit / Best Case / Pipeline roll-up with an override trail. And you want forecast data available in Claude or ChatGPT through a vendor-supported MCP server today.

**Pick Aviso when** you want an independent, forecasting-first vendor whose roadmap is not tied to an engagement platform. Also when your CRM data is uneven or spread across Dynamics, Oracle or a lakehouse. Or when you are on a Clari or Salesloft contract and the buyout offer makes switching cheap.

**Pick neither when** you have fewer than about 20 reps or under $10M ARR. A median contract above $70,000 is a material share of GTM budget at that size. Salesforce or HubSpot native forecasting, plus a disciplined weekly pipeline review, gets you most of the forecast discipline for a fraction of the cost. If you are already on Gong for conversation intelligence, price its forecasting module before buying either; see [Clari vs BoostUp](/en/vs/clari-vs-boostup/) for the mid-market alternative.

**The pick if you cannot decide:** for a standalone forecasting purchase, default to Aviso. This reverses this page's earlier default, and the reason is the vendor change, not the model. Clari Forecast is now sold, roadmapped and supported by an engagement vendor, and its price advantage is gone. The exception is a team already on Salesloft. There, Clari Forecast's integration advantage is real, so take it.
