## What it is

GUIDEcx is a client onboarding and implementation platform: an internal project workspace for the implementation team, a white-labelled portal where the customer sees and completes their own tasks, and — in its top tier — a professional services layer for time, capacity and margin. It is the product the rest of the category is measured against. Rocketlane, OnRamp, Arrows and Dock all publish "GUIDEcx alternatives" pages, and G2 has ranked it a Leader in Client Onboarding for years; it holds a 4.6/5 rating across roughly 460 G2 reviews.

The company is based in Draper, Utah, and is led by founder and CEO Peter Ord. It raised a $10M Series A in November 2020 and a $25M Series B led by Meritech Capital Partners in February 2022, $35M in total. It reports more than 500,000 client onboarding projects run on the platform.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Multi-stakeholder visibility without customer licences.** Every plan includes unlimited projects and unlimited customers. Customer contacts get secure login links, email and SMS nudges, and a portal view limited to their own tasks, so you pay for the implementation team, not for the six people on the customer side who each own one step.
- **GUIDE 2.0 rebuilt the product in 2025.** Rolled out to all accounts between 4 and 29 August 2025, the new architecture added a home screen, a portal view for individual contributors, and the Coach: an AI layer that scores each project Red, Amber or Green from live data and proposes a mitigation plan. Tempo AI followed on 6 May 2026, producing coaching insights on completed projects. The legacy GUIDE 1.0 experience is supported until 31 December 2026.
- **It has moved into PSA.** The Advanced tier adds time tracking, resource management, utilisation, margin tracking and an AI dispatching engine that assigns tasks by role, availability and capacity. Estimated Capacity reached general availability on 2 September 2026. That narrows the gap with Rocketlane for teams that bill implementation hours.
- **Integrations ship inside the licence.** Premium includes native Salesforce, HubSpot, Gainsight, Jira, Slack and NetSuite connectors plus an embedded Workato subscription with 250,000 automation tasks; Advanced adds another 250,000.

## Pricing reality

GUIDEcx no longer publishes prices. Its pricing page, checked on 18 September 2026, lists Starter, Premium and Advanced, all "speak to sales". Third-party data fills the gap: Starter has been reported at $100–$143 per licence per month with a four-licence minimum, which is roughly $4,800–$6,900 a year, and Vendr buyer data puts the low end of real contracts around $6,800. Most teams that need integrations or the AI Coach land on Premium, so budget $15,000–$30,000 a year for a five-to-ten-person implementation team, and more for Advanced if you want PSA. Plan on about 45 days to deploy.

## Best for

Implementation and onboarding leaders at mid-market B2B software and services companies running 50–1,000 concurrent implementations of four weeks or longer, where the customer side has several stakeholders who each own tasks and the team needs one view of which projects are slipping and why.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Rocketlane](/en/tools/rocketlane/)** is the fastest-growing challenger, with a $60M Series C led by Insight Partners in March 2026 and published per-user prices from $19 to $99 a month. Pick it when a professional-services team runs onboarding and PSA — utilisation, financials, billing — is the core requirement rather than an Advanced-tier add-on.

**[OnRamp](/en/tools/onramp/)** is the pick when onboarding paths must branch by segment and customer answers, and a flat annual contract from $15,000 is easier to approve than per-licence pricing.

**[Arrows](/en/tools/arrows/)** fits lighter, HubSpot-native onboarding plans, and **[Dock](/en/tools/dock/)** fits one shared workspace spanning the sales cycle and onboarding.

Pick GUIDEcx when you run many long, multi-stakeholder implementations and want the most mature customer-facing project model in the category. If none fit — fewer than 20 onboardings a year — run a CRM project template and a shared tracker until volume justifies a platform.

## Watch-outs

- **Opaque, tier-gated pricing.** Starter has no integrations; the CRM connectors, AI Coach, SSO and API sit in Premium, and PSA sits in Advanced. **Guard:** list the three features you cannot live without before the first call, get a written quote for the tier that carries all three, and cap the renewal uplift in the order form — reviewers report a roughly 5% annual increase as the negotiable default.
- **Templates are built from scratch.** No starter templates ship with the product, and reviewers cite limited task editing once projects run. **Guard:** budget the 45-day deployment for template design, and in the trial change scope on a live test project to see what breaks.
- **The GUIDE 1.0 cutoff.** Legacy customers must be on 2.0 by 31 December 2026, and templates need migration. **Guard:** if you are on 1.0, schedule the migration this quarter and re-test your automations on 2.0 before renewal.
- **No MCP server.** Agent access runs through the REST API (Premium) and the AI-Ready Analytics API (Advanced). **Guard:** if Claude or an internal agent should read project risk, confirm API tier access in the contract and build a read-only wrapper rather than exposing write scopes.
- **It stops at go-live.** Reviewers want post-launch task tracking the product does not cover. **Guard:** define the handoff event to [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) or your CS platform and end the GUIDEcx project there.
