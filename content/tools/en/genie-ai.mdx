## What it is

GenieAI is a London contract AI built for companies that draft and review their own contracts without a big legal department. It started in 2017 as a legal-drafting research project (founders Rafie Faruq and Nitish Mutha, out of Entrepreneur First), built a free open-source template library, and today sells "Genie": a web app that drafts, reviews, redlines, researches and negotiates contracts inside your own Word files, with an add-in that brings the assistant into Microsoft Word. It raised a $17.8M Series A led by GV with Khosla Ventures in October 2024 and reports more than 280,000 users. That figure is self-reported and counts users, not paying customers.

Two launches in 2026 changed what you are buying. Genie 3.0 (public trial from May 2026) added "Company Knowledge": you upload past contracts, policies and negotiation history by department, and review runs against your own positions rather than a generic market view. On 5 November 2026 GenieAI shipped Agentic Legal Specialists, where a lead agent plans each task and assigns drafting, negotiation, risk, research and proofreading agents with quality gates between them. The vendor's own example is 2 agents and 1 gate for a two-page NDA, 12 agents and 3 gates for a 40-document due diligence. The benchmarks GenieAI publishes against ChatGPT and Claude Cowork are vendor-run; treat them as a reason to pilot, not as evidence.

## What it costs in practice

GenieAI is one of the few contract AI tools in this segment that still publishes prices (USD, checked 20 November 2026):

- **Free:** $0, one user, enough AI tokens for about one task, no document exports and no PDF upload. It is a demo, not a working tier.
- **Pro:** $75/month, or $59/month billed annually ($708/year). One user, about 5 documents a month on the included tokens, top-ups available.
- **Business:** $320/month, or $259/month billed annually ($3,108/year) for up to 5 users, about $52 per user. About 50 documents a month shared across the team, plus the Word add-in, integrations, team playbooks and the contract repository.
- **Enterprise:** quoted, billed annually, with unlimited tokens, role-based department spaces, SLA and a pilot on your own contracts.

The real-world band for a small in-house team is therefore $3,100-3,900 a year on Business, against quote-only pricing from [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/), where third-party estimates for the entry seat range from roughly $20 to $99 per user per month and nobody outside its sales team knows the real number. The catch is that the plans meter AI tokens, not seats. "About 5 documents a month" on Pro means a founder reviewing one or two contracts a week will hit the cap.

## Best for

- Founders, finance leads and first in-house lawyers at UK and EU SMEs who draft NDAs, MSAs, employment contracts and supplier terms themselves and want what one G2 reviewer called a draft about 90% ready for a lawyer to check
- Two-to-five-person legal or commercial teams that want playbook-based review of incoming paper at a published price, with a Word add-in and DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign in the same tool

## Watch-outs

- **The Word add-in and integrations sit on Business, not Pro.** On Pro you work in GenieAI's web app and export to Word. Guard: if your lawyers live in Word, budget Business from day one and pilot the add-in on a real redline before committing.
- **Your data's treatment depends on the plan.** Zero data retention with OpenAI and Anthropic is listed for Business and above only, and the pricing FAQ says you opt out of AI training by emailing help@genieai.co, which implies it is on until you do. Guard: send the opt-out request and get the DPA signed before uploading any client or counterparty contract.
- **ISO 27001, not SOC 2.** GenieAI lists ISO 27001 and UK/EU hosting on AWS; SOC 2 does not appear anywhere on its site. Guard: if a US customer or your own security team requires a SOC 2 report, ask for it before the trial, not after.
- **The agentic layer is weeks old.** Legal Specialist Agents launched on 5 November 2026 and the Genie MCP server for Claude and ChatGPT is marked "coming soon". Guard: buy on the drafting and review that work today and pilot multi-document work on a closed matter before relying on it.

## Alternatives and when to pick them instead

[Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) is the most widely adopted Word-native contract AI for lawyers (4,500+ legal teams by its own count) and wins on Word-native depth and precedent search across iManage; pick it if your users are practising lawyers redlining all day and you can accept a sales-led quote. [Juro](/en/tools/juro/) is the other default for SMEs and scale-ups and wins when the problem is the contract lifecycle (approvals, signature, repository, renewals) rather than drafting quality. [Legora](/en/tools/legora/) is the fastest-growing entrant in legal AI; test it if you are a firm or a large department that wants a collaborative agent workspace and a research layer, not a published SME price. For a comparison of the drafting-first options, see [alternatives to Spellbook](/en/vs/alternatives-to-spellbook/).

If you only need clean first drafts of standard documents and never review counterparty paper, GenieAI's free open-source template library may be all you need; the Free plan, with no exports, will not be. Pay for Pro only once you review more than a contract or two a month.
