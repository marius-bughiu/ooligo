## What it is

HackerRank is a technical-assessment platform that spent 2026 rebuilding around a different question: not whether a candidate can write an algorithm from memory, but how they direct, check, and correct an AI coding assistant. Founded in 2009 as InterviewStreet, it remains independent — last raise March 2022, roughly $110M total — and reports 2,500 customers and a 26M+ developer community.

Three products sit on one platform. Screen runs take-home assessments, Interview runs live pair programming, and Chakra, an AI interviewer launched January 2026, runs conversational screens that probe a candidate's reasoning with follow-up questions and now opens an inline code editor mid-interview.

The July 2026 release changed the assessment surface itself. Candidates work in an agentic IDE and choose their model — Claude, Gemini, or GPT. Scoring reads IDE activity rather than chat logs: which AI suggestions the candidate accepted, rejected, or rewrote, surfaced through a Diff View of initial versus final code. Plan Mode has candidates shape an approach with AI before writing anything. A set of 159 new plan-build-review repository tasks drops candidates into production-style codebases — MERN, Spring Boot, Django, Go, .NET — to resolve support tickets.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **It scores how candidates use AI instead of trying to stop them.** Guarded and unguarded assistance modes are configured per question, and the AI Fluency score comes from what the candidate did with the model's output. The alternative — ban the assistant and run plagiarism detection as an arms race — measures a behavior that will not appear on the job.
- **The entry tier now has a published price.** HackerRank sold this quote-only until 2026; both self-serve tiers sit on the pricing page as of September 2026, which removes a procurement cycle for teams making their first technical hires.
- **Unlimited platform users at every tier.** HackerRank meters candidate attempts, not seats, so every engineer on the panel gets an account without changing the bill. Vendors that meter seats make it expensive to let the people who will work with the candidate read the submission.

## Pricing

Verified against [hackerrank.com/work/pricing](https://www.hackerrank.com/work/pricing) on 2026-09-03.

- **Starter — $79/month billed annually** ($948/year, 60 candidate attempts) or **$99/month billed monthly** (5 attempts/month). Unlimited users, 2,000+ library questions, Chakra, Screen, Interview, plagiarism detection, session replay, device detection. No ATS, no calendar, no API, no SSO.
- **Pro — $419/month billed annually** ($5,028/year, 360 attempts) or **$529/month billed monthly** (30 attempts/month). Adds [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), [Lever](/en/tools/lever/) and [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) ATS integrations, Gmail and Outlook calendar sync, and a 4,000+ question library.
- **Enterprise — custom.** The only tier carrying API access, the HackerRank MCP server, leaked-question protection, certified assessments for 80 roles, SSO/SCIM, audit logs, the desktop proctoring app, and 22+ ATS integrations.
- **Overage is $20 per extra attempt** on both published tiers. Pro on annual billing can pre-purchase attempts at $15.
- Annual billing runs about 20% below monthly on both tiers.

Real-world band: SpendHound's de-identified contract data across 160 HackerRank customers puts average SMB spend at $4,860/year (17 companies, 50–1,000 employees) and average enterprise spend at $84,947/year (35 companies, 1,000+ employees).

**The attempt math that picks your tier.** Pro costs $4,080/year more than Starter and adds 300 attempts. At the $20 overage rate, buying those attempts on top of Starter costs that same $4,080 at 204 extra attempts — so on attempt cost alone, Starter plus overage stays cheaper up to roughly 264 attempts a year. Move to Pro before that point only because you need the ATS integrations, which Starter does not carry at any price.

## Best for

- Engineering teams hiring 25–60 engineers a year on Pro — at six assessments per hire, 360 attempts covers the funnel
- Teams whose loop already assumes candidates use AI, and who want the evaluation to reflect that rather than police it
- Small teams making their first technical hires, where Starter's $948/year and unlimited seats beat waiting on a quote

Not the pick when programmatic access is a requirement: API and MCP are Enterprise-only here, while [Codility](/en/tools/codility/) sells MCP task authoring on its $6,000/year Scale tier.

## Watch-outs

- **MCP and API sit behind an Enterprise quote.** The HackerRank MCP server shipped in July 2026 as read-only and limited-availability. Guard: if piping assessment results into an internal dashboard or agent is a hard requirement, price the Enterprise quote before committing to Pro, and confirm MCP availability for your account in writing.
- **Leaked-question protection is Enterprise-only, and the library is the screening signal at every tier.** Guard: on Starter and Pro, write custom questions for any role you screen more than 20 times a year, and rotate them quarterly.
- **The entry tier is a commodity, so choosing on sticker price is a coin flip.** [CodeSignal](/en/tools/codesignal/)'s Build plan is $948/year for the same 60 credits and the same $20 overage. CoderPad's Starter is $960/year for 60. Codility's Starter is $1,200/year for 120 invites — $10 an invite against HackerRank's $15.80 — but caps you at one platform user. Guard: decide on what each vendor gates above the entry line and on the per-attempt rate at your real volume, then check the sticker.
- **AI Fluency scores do not compare across guarded and unguarded modes.** Guard: fix the mode per role before rollout and re-baseline the cohort if you change it; a candidate scored unguarded against a guarded cohort is not a like-for-like read.
- **Coding-challenge performance correlates poorly with on-the-job engineer effectiveness.** Guard: pair the assessment with [structured interviews](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) and a design exercise, and treat the score as a floor filter rather than a ranking.
