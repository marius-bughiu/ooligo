## What it is

Handshake is the early-career job network that more than 1,600 universities, most of them in the US, run their career services on. Students and recent graduates sign in through their school, employers post internships and entry-level roles into that network, and career services staff approve which employers can reach their students. Handshake's employer site now claims 25 million job seekers, 1,600+ university partners and 97% of the Fortune 500; many third-party guides still quote the older 18 million students and 1,400+ schools. Sacra estimates roughly 750,000 employers on the platform, most of them on the free tier.

The company was founded in 2014 by Garrett Lord, Ben Christensen and Scott Ringwelski and is based in San Francisco. Its last priced round was a $200M Series F in January 2022 at a $3.5B valuation.

The employer product has four parts: job posting into school networks, messaging and sourcing against student profiles, event management for virtual and on-campus fairs, and employer branding. The Spring 2026 release added Hiring Intelligence (in beta — you describe the ideal candidate and it ranks matches on skills, interests and engagement, learning from your feedback), Promotions (job placements at high-intent moments in search and the student inbox) and Insights, a live dashboard with peer benchmarks across 1M+ employers. ATS integrations cover Greenhouse, iCIMS and Workday, one ATS per employer account, and only on the top plan. We found no public employer API and no MCP server.

## The part that matters: recruiting is no longer the main business

The question buyers now ask is whether Handshake still cares about them. In January 2025 the company launched Handshake AI, which recruits students, PhDs and domain experts from its own network to produce training data for AI labs; TechCrunch reported it had supplied eight top labs, including OpenAI. Sacra estimates the company reached about $1.1B in annualized gross revenue by April 2026, up 349% year over year, with the legacy recruiting business at roughly $150M. Sacra's history shows recruiting revenue growing 6% in 2024, down from 50% in 2023. Three acquisitions followed in six months — Cleanlab (January 2026, for its research team), Taro (February 2026, engineering career content) and Uplimit (30 June 2026), which became AI Skills Studio, a free AI training hub for job seekers.

Two things follow. First, the recruiting product still ships: the Spring 2026 release and the repackaged plans show continued investment. Second, Handshake now competes with you for the same students. Expert contributors are paid $100-125 an hour for specialist labelling work, which is more than many internships offer, and the network you pay to recruit from is also the supply side of Handshake's largest business. That does not make the network less useful for posting jobs. It does mean your contract carries a roadmap risk that did not exist in 2024.

## Pricing reality

Handshake publishes three plans. **Basic** is free: unlimited job posts to the network and 10 messages a month. **Pro** is $450 a month: 200 messages a month, AI applicant ranking and summaries, multiple users and workflow automation. **Enterprise** is quoted: advanced messaging, promotions, event support, the full brand page, team management with analytics, and the Greenhouse, iCIMS or Workday integration. Promoted Jobs is a variable-rate add-on for Basic and Pro and is included in Enterprise.

Enterprise is the renamed Premium tier, and legacy Premium contracts ran $10,000 to $250,000+ a year with a one-year minimum. Vendr data cited by Pin puts the median contract at $29,835 a year. Expect the larger numbers to come from event bundles, promotions and multi-school targeting, not seats.

## Best for

A university-recruiting or early-talent lead at an employer hiring 20 or more interns and new graduates a year across several US schools, who needs the career-centre relationship as much as the applicant flow. Handshake is the default because career services run their job postings, fair registration and employer approvals through it. At partner schools fair registration runs through it, so skipping Handshake usually means skipping the fair.

For a company hiring fewer than 10 early-career people a year, Basic plus a few school relationships is enough. Pro makes sense once your team sends more than 10 messages a month or needs more than one recruiter on the account. Enterprise is for programs that need the ATS sync and event tooling.

## Versus the alternatives

**[LinkedIn Recruiter](/en/tools/linkedin-recruiter/)** is the other network by market share. Pick it when you hire graduates with one to three years of experience, or when your roles are not tied to specific schools. Handshake reaches current students better; LinkedIn reaches graduates once they have left.

**Yello** is the campus-program CRM rather than a network. Pick it when events, interview days and pipeline tracking across schools are the problem, not reach. It launched an AI Campus Recruiting Agent on 27 May 2026, partnered with Symplicity in June 2026 for access to 600,000+ early-talent candidates, and owns WayUp and Symba. It complements Handshake rather than replacing it.

**RippleMatch** was the main AI-matching challenger. JobGet acquired it on 28 May 2026 and is folding it into its own platform, so treat any new RippleMatch contract as a JobGet contract.

**Jack & Jill** is the fastest-growing agent-led entrant: separate AI agents for candidate and employer, a $40M Series A in September 2026. It is not campus-specific. Pick it for early-career technical roles where you want pre-screened introductions instead of an applicant queue.

**[Gem](/en/tools/gem/)** is the pick when early-career hiring is one pipeline in a CRM you already run for experienced hires. If none fit, the problem is usually the school list, not the platform: a target-school strategy plus direct career-centre relationships covers most of what Basic does, at no cost.

## Watch-outs

- **Your vendor also recruits your candidates.** Handshake AI pays students and graduates to label data, from the same network you pay to reach. *Guard:* add a clause to Enterprise contracts that your job, message and applicant data is not used to recruit for Handshake AI or to train its models, and ask for the data-use terms in writing before signing.
- **Roadmap risk on a multi-year deal.** Recruiting is now about 14% of gross revenue by Sacra's estimate. *Guard:* sign one-year terms only, add an exit right if named features (ATS sync, events, Insights) are withdrawn, and review the release notes before each renewal.
- **Application volume is rising faster than quality.** Handshake's own data showed postings down 16% and applications per job up 26% in August 2025, and 85% of the Class of 2026 uses AI tools. *Guard:* add two or three knockout questions and a short skills task to each posting, and judge the platform on interviews and hires per requisition, not applicants.
- **The ATS integration is narrow.** Only Greenhouse, iCIMS and Workday, only on Enterprise, one ATS per account. *Guard:* if you run another ATS, stay on Pro and tag Handshake as a source in your ATS; do not pay for Enterprise expecting a sync you cannot use.
- **Messaging limits drive upgrades.** Basic allows 10 messages a month and Pro 200. *Guard:* count last cycle's outreach before choosing a plan, and put the volume in the order form so you are not upsold mid-season.
