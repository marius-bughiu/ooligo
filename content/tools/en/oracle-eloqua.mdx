## What it is

Oracle Eloqua is the enterprise B2B marketing automation platform Oracle bought in 2012 for about $871 million. It covers email, forms, landing pages, lead scoring, segmentation, and the Campaign Canvas, a drag-and-drop flow builder that large marketing ops teams use to run multi-step nurture, event, and lifecycle programs. Its long-standing strengths are a mature Salesforce integration app, contact-level security for global teams, and a deliverability and data model built for databases in the millions.

It is not being retired. Oracle still ships quarterly releases and cites a Leader placement in Gartner's Magic Quadrant for B2B Marketing Automation Platforms. The 26D release (feature summary dated 2026-10-28, rolling out 2026-10-30 to 2026-11-14) does three things worth knowing:

- **A Redwood rebuild of the Campaign Canvas and Template Editor.** It was an opt-in preview in 26C; Oracle's readiness notes say it switches on for all customers on 2026-12-01, with multi-step copy and paste across canvases, favorite steps, notes, and faster performance on large campaigns.
- **An "agentic ecosystem."** Agents built in Oracle's Fusion Agent Studio can be installed and run inside Eloqua. It is in controlled availability, requires an existing Oracle Fusion environment, and ships with no Oracle-delivered agents at launch.
- **Email tracking consent.** Contacts can now opt in or out of open and click tracking through forms, emails, and the APIs, with audit reporting on tracked sends.

## The scoped use case

A marketing ops team at a large B2B company with 500,000+ contacts, several regions or business units, and Salesforce or Microsoft Dynamics as system of record, where an Eloqua instance already holds years of campaign history, scoring models, and integrations. For that team the 2026 question is usually renewal, not purchase: whether the new canvas and agent framework justify staying, or whether a 10-16 week migration (the range HubSpot partner The Pedowitz Group quotes for Eloqua-to-HubSpot moves) pays back.

## Pricing reality

Oracle publishes a global price list, unlike most rivals. Per that list (dated 2025-06-12, as reported by licensing advisor Redress Compliance and checked 2026-11-23), Eloqua is billed monthly by contact band: the maximum number of contacts stored at any one time, rounded up to the next band. List prices per month:

| Contacts | Basic | Standard | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | $2,000 | $4,000 | not offered |
| 100,000 | $4,500 | $9,000 | not offered |
| 500,000 | $7,300 | $14,600 | $29,200 |
| 1,000,000 | $10,000 | $20,000 | $40,000 |

Each edition costs twice the one below it. Additional marketing users list at $840 per user per year, an extra test environment at $12,000 per year, and the Profiler and Engage sales tools at $200 per user per year each. Negotiated contracts usually land below list, but uncapped renewal uplifts in the high single digits are common.

The bill follows stored contacts, not sends, and unsubscribed contacts still count until deleted. Redress reports that in the 15-25 Eloqua renewals it reviewed, 20-40% of stored contacts were inactive or duplicate. Add a dedicated admin or managed-service partner; Eloqua is not run part-time.

## Best for

Marketing ops leaders at enterprises already on Eloqua, with a full-time admin, a database above roughly 500,000 contacts, and either Oracle Fusion in the back office or a CRM integration that would be expensive to rebuild.

**Not for you if** you are buying marketing automation for the first time, or your database is under about 100,000 contacts. At that size the license and admin cost is hard to justify against HubSpot or Account Engagement.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) Marketing Hub**: the top alternative by customer count and the most common Eloqua exit. Pick it when time-to-value and usability matter more than campaign depth: Professional runs about $890/month for 2,000 marketing contacts plus a $3,000 onboarding fee. Stay on Eloqua when you need contact-level security across business units or run multi-million-contact sends.
- **[Adobe Marketo Engage](/en/tools/adobe-marketo-engage/)**: the closest like-for-like enterprise rival. Pick it when you want a native, generally available MCP server (Adobe shipped one in September 2026) and a larger admin talent pool. Third-party trackers put its packages between roughly $14,000 and $120,000+ per year, so the cost gap is smaller than the migration cost.
- **[Salesforce Marketing Cloud Account Engagement](/en/tools/salesforce-account-engagement/)**: the other incumbent by share. Pick it when the company is all-in on [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) and wants the marketing data inside the CRM; list runs $1,250 to $15,000 per org per month.

## Watch-outs

- **Contact-band creep inflates the bill silently.** Unsubscribed, bounced, and duplicate records keep counting toward your band. *Guard:* run a contact purge and dedupe (an [Openprise](/en/tools/openprise/) or in-house script pass) 90 days before renewal, and negotiate a renewal cap of around 3% in writing.
- **The agent framework depends on Oracle Fusion.** Agents require a Fusion environment with Agent Studio access, are in controlled availability, and come with no prebuilt Oracle agents; there is no MCP server. *Guard:* if you run Salesforce or Dynamics rather than Fusion, value Eloqua on what runs today and do not pay a renewal premium for agents.
- **The Redwood canvas arrives for everyone on 2026-12-01.** Builders trained on the classic canvas will hit new layouts mid-quarter. *Guard:* request the preview in a test environment now, and freeze major program launches the week of the switch.
- **Tracking-consent changes alter your metrics.** Contacts who opt out of tracking stop producing opens and clicks, which can break scoring rules and engagement-based segments. *Guard:* audit lead scoring models that weight email engagement before enabling the 26D tracking-preference forms.
