## What it is

Gusto is payroll, benefits and HR for US small businesses, used by more than 500,000 companies. Payroll is the product; everything else is built around the payroll record. Its hiring module, Gusto Recruiting, is included at no extra cost on the Plus and Premium plans: a company job board, job posts written by you or drafted by Gusto's AI, free syndication to 100+ job boards through partner Jobcase, free basic posting to LinkedIn, a hiring team with comments and scorecards, an offer letter, and a one-click hire that drops the new employee straight into Gusto onboarding and payroll.

The question this page answers is the one a 10–80 person company actually has: is that built-in ATS enough, or do you still need Workable, JazzHR or Breezy HR on top? Short answer: it is enough for low-volume, inbound hiring of generalist and hourly roles. It is not enough once candidate communication, interview scheduling or sourcing becomes the bottleneck.

## What the ATS does, and what it skips

Gusto's own help center is unusually direct about the gaps. Interview scheduling is not included. Email templates for applicants are not included. Gusto does not send automated emails when someone applies or moves stage; the only candidate email it sends is an optional rejection with fixed text from a no-reply address. The bulk CSV export covers one job at a time and leaves out resumes and cover letters, which you download one applicant at a time.

Screening is rules, not AI. You mark preferred answers on single- or multiple-select application questions, and Gusto tags every applicant as fully, partially or not qualified. That is useful for knockout criteria such as work authorization, shift availability or a licence, and it works retroactively on people who already applied. The AI in the module writes the first draft of the job overview and description from the job title; it does not rank candidates.

Distribution has fine print. Jobcase syndication keeps a post live for 30 days from when you enable it, and placement on any given board is not guaranteed. Sponsored Jobcase campaigns start at $25 and Gusto itself says they work best for non-specialized hourly roles. Free LinkedIn posting needs a Gusto account approved for more than 30 days and a job with a single work location.

## Pricing reality

Gusto publishes its prices and bills month-to-month with no long-term contract. Simple is $49/month plus $6 per person, Plus is $80 plus $12 per person, Premium is $180 plus $22 per person. US contractors count as a person on whichever plan you pick.

Recruiting is on Plus and Premium only, so the honest price of the ATS for a company on Simple is the upgrade delta: $31 a month plus $6 per person. At 30 people that is $211/month ($2,532/year); at 80 people it is $511/month ($6,132/year). Plus also brings time tracking, scheduling, multi-state payroll and next-day pay, so if you need any of those, the ATS is effectively free.

A 30-person company on Plus pays $440/month, or $5,280/year. At 80 people, Plus is $1,040/month and Premium is $1,940/month ($23,280/year). Premium adds a named advisor, HR guidance and Gusto Business Compliance, the state registration and annual-report product built on its April 2026 acquisition of Mosey.

## Best for

Owners and office or HR managers at US companies with 10–80 employees, already running payroll on Gusto, who open a few roles at a time, hire mostly inbound applicants, and want the hire to land in payroll without retyping. The pick is Plus: it is where Recruiting starts and where most small teams need time tracking anyway.

Skip Gusto Recruiting if you are hiring specialized or senior roles where sourcing matters more than inbound volume, if your recruiters live in the inbox and need templated candidate emails, or if you have employees outside the US; Gusto's own payroll covers US employees, and abroad it only pays contractors (in 120+ countries).

## Versus the alternatives

**Standalone ATS on top of Gusto.** [Workable](/en/tools/workable/) is one of the two SMB ATS market-share leaders, from $169/month for companies up to 50 employees, with a sourcing database and AI features Gusto lacks. [JazzHR](/en/tools/jazzhr/) costs $1,000/year on Hero and $3,480/year on Plus, priced flat rather than per employee, and syncs hires to Gusto. [Breezy HR](/en/tools/breezy-hr/) starts at $157/month on annual billing. The match rule: if the only reason to leave Simple is recruiting, compare the upgrade delta ($31 + $6 × headcount per month) with the standalone price. It is cheaper than Breezy's Startup plan below about 21 people and cheaper than JazzHR Plus below about 43. Above that, or as soon as you need scheduling and candidate emails, the standalone ATS wins on both price and capability.

**HRIS with an ATS built in.** [BambooHR](/en/tools/bamboohr/) has the stronger ATS-to-onboarding flow and an MCP connection, but its own payroll is a priced add-on and its Core plan caps you at 5 concurrent openings. [Rippling](/en/tools/rippling/) is the fastest-growing platform in the segment and the pick when IT provisioning should fire off the same hire event as payroll; expect roughly $15–$35 per employee per month all-in, several times Gusto Plus. Pick Gusto when payroll and benefits are the main job and hiring is occasional.

## AI and agents

Gusto launched Cofounder, an AI teammate that works from a company's payroll, team and calendar data, in early access on 2 June 2026. At its 22 October 2026 Fall Showcase it opened connections to Claude, ChatGPT and other assistants, plus an MCP server and a Gusto CLI, so an agent can ask payroll questions, prepare a payroll run and generate reports, with the owner approving every change before it is final. None of this reaches into Recruiting yet: no AI screening, no agent-driven scheduling.

## Watch-outs

- **Candidates hear nothing unless you write to them.** No automated acknowledgment, no stage emails, no templates. A team with 60 applicants per role will either ghost people or spend an hour a week copy-pasting. Guard: before relying on Gusto Recruiting, count expected applicants per role per month. Above roughly 30, add a standalone ATS with templates and scheduling, or at minimum keep a shared email draft and a weekly reply block on the calendar.
- **The plan is priced on every person, contractors included.** Upgrading from Simple to Plus to get Recruiting raises the bill on all workers, not on the two people who hire. Guard: price the delta with your real headcount, including contractors, against JazzHR or Breezy before upgrading for the ATS alone.
- **Resumes do not leave in bulk.** The CSV export omits resumes and cover letters, so migrating to a real ATS later means one download per applicant. Guard: if you expect to outgrow Gusto Recruiting within a year, start the standalone ATS now, or save resumes for shortlisted candidates to a shared drive as you go.
- **An AI connection reads payroll data.** The new Claude and ChatGPT connections and the MCP server sit on top of payroll, pay rates and personal data. Gusto requires approval for changes, but reading is the exposure. Guard: connect it with an admin account scoped to what the agent needs, and decide in writing which assistant workspaces are allowed to hold Gusto data before anyone turns it on.
