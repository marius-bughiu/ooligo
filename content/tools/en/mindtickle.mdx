## What it is

Mindtickle is a sales readiness platform: onboarding programs, certifications, AI roleplay, manager coaching, call recording and scoring, a sales content library, and buyer-facing Digital Sales Rooms, all tied to a per-rep "Readiness Index" that scores skills against CRM outcomes. It was founded in Pune in 2011, is headquartered in San Francisco, is run by co-founders Krishna Depura (CEO) and Deepak Diwakar (COO), and has raised about $281 million, including two $100 million rounds led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2 in November 2020 and August 2021, the second at a $1.2 billion valuation. Named customers include Cisco, Thomson Reuters, Johnson & Johnson, Adobe and Autodesk. The Digital Sales Room came from the Enable Us acquisition, announced 2023-05-02.

The current product layer is **ElevateOS**, launched 2026-04-21 and billed as an agentic operating system for enablement. It runs seven agents on shared rep data: AI Tutor, AI Roleplay, AI Manager Coach, AI Content Creation, Deal Guide, AI Roleplay Simulator and Copilot. Mindtickle says ElevateOS is available to existing customers, with individual capabilities rolling out through beta programs in Q1 and Q2 2026, and that it is built on the MCP and A2A standards.

The question this page answers: Seismic and Highspot became one company on 2026-08-18. Which enablement vendor do you buy if you want the big-platform feature set from someone who is not mid-merger?

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **It is the default independent alternative after the merger.** The Seismic–Highspot merger closed on 2026-08-18 after the US Department of Justice ended its review. The combined Seismic reports 2,500 customers and 3.5 million users, and Highspot now ships as "Highspot by Seismic". Mindtickle is the vendor with comparable enterprise depth, running at SoftBank-funded scale, whose roadmap is not being merged with anyone else's.
- **Its center of gravity is rep skill, not content.** Seismic started as a content system and added training. Mindtickle started as training and added content. If your problem is [ramp time](/en/learn/ramp-time/) and whether reps can actually run the discovery call, that origin matters more than the feature checklist, which now looks similar across all three.
- **Practice and real calls feed the same score.** Roleplay results, certification scores and recorded calls roll into the Readiness Index, so a manager can see that a rep passed the pricing certification and then fumbled pricing on three live calls. That is the core argument for buying [sales enablement](/en/learn/sales-enablement/) and [conversation intelligence](/en/learn/conversation-intelligence/) from one vendor.
- **It connects to the systems reps already live in.** [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), Microsoft Dynamics and [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) on the CRM side. Zoom, Teams, Webex and Google Meet for call capture. [Slack](/en/tools/slack/) and Gmail for nudges. Drive, SharePoint, Box and Adobe Experience Manager for content, plus Okta and Entra ID SSO, SCIM user sync and REST APIs.

## Pricing

No public price list. Every deal is an annual quote, and multi-year terms are the norm.

Vendr's buyer data, checked today, shows a median annual contract of $44,356 across 34 purchases, a range of $11,157 to $138,726, and average negotiated savings of 17.68% off the first quote. Vendr's observed quotes put the core readiness platform at $100–$200 per user per year for mid-market deployments, with Call AI adding $50–$100 per user per year and Digital Sales Rooms or revenue intelligence adding 20–40% to the base contract. Budget $10,000 to $50,000+ for onboarding and implementation. Competitor analyses citing Vendr also report 3–7% annual escalators written into contracts.

Read that as: a 100–200 rep team buying readiness, coaching and Call AI lands in the $20,000–$60,000 a year band before implementation. Modules move the number more than seats do.

## Best for

Enablement and RevOps leaders at companies with roughly 75 to 2,000 quota-carrying reps, where the measurable problem is ramp time, certification or coaching consistency rather than content findability. It is the strongest fit for teams with a dedicated enablement function, frequent product launches that reps must be certified on, and a leadership team that wants skill data next to pipeline data.

**Not for you** under about 30 reps, where the platform, implementation fee and admin time cost more than a sales manager's hour a week of structured call reviews. Also not for you if your real pain is that reps can't find the right deck: that is a content-management job, and Seismic or a lighter tool does it with less setup.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Seismic (including Highspot by Seismic)** — the top two by share, now one company. Pick Seismic when content governance, brand control and content automation across many business units are the requirement. Pick Mindtickle when rep skill is the requirement and you would rather not sign a multi-year term during a product consolidation. See [Seismic](/en/tools/seismic/).
- **Allego** — the other independent full-suite vendor, built video-coaching-first and named a Leader in Gartner's first Magic Quadrant for Revenue Enablement Platforms in November 2025. Pick Allego when asynchronous video practice and peer-shared "what good looks like" clips are how you coach. Pick Mindtickle when you want roleplay, certification and call scoring tied to a single readiness score.
- **Hyperbound** — the fastest-growing entrant, AI roleplay only. It launched in January 2024 and raised a $15 million Series A led by Peak XV on 2025-09-16. Pick it when practice reps is the whole problem and you want it live in weeks without an enablement platform. See [Hyperbound](/en/tools/hyperbound/).
- **Gong for calls** — if [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) already records every call, do not pay for Mindtickle's Call AI on top. Buy Mindtickle for training and roleplay and keep one system of record for recordings.

If none of these fit, the missing piece is usually an owner. A readiness platform runs programs; it does not write the talk track, the [battlecards](/en/learn/battlecards/) or the certification rubric.

## Watch-outs

- **"Available now" and "in beta" describe the same launch.** ElevateOS is live for customers, but individual agents rolled out through beta programs, and ooligo found no public documentation for a Mindtickle MCP server as of 2026-09-11 despite the MCP and A2A claim. *Guard:* ask which of the seven agents are GA in your tenant on your contract start date, get the list in the order form, and if agent access from Claude or Copilot matters to you, see it working in a trial tenant before you sign.
- **Admin load is the recurring complaint.** Mindtickle holds 4.7/5 on G2 across 2,200+ reviews, but the consistent negatives are a steep admin learning curve, complex setup when running many similar programs and audiences, and slow loading on heavy content. *Guard:* name a platform admin before signing, and limit the first 90 days to onboarding plus one certification track rather than migrating every program at once.
- **Modules and escalators compound.** Call AI, Digital Sales Rooms, premium support and a 3–7% annual escalator each look small on their own. *Guard:* get every module priced as a separate line item, drop any module another tool in your stack already covers, and cap the renewal uplift in writing.
- **A readiness score is not revenue.** A rep can certify on a pitch and still lose deals. *Guard:* capture ramp time, stage conversion and win rate by rep before rollout, and judge the platform at 6 and 12 months on those numbers, not on completion rates or Readiness Index scores.
- **Roleplay and call scoring process recorded voice and personal data.** *Guard:* confirm call-recording consent flows for every country you sell into, sign the DPA with explicit retention periods for recordings, and keep AI scores out of compensation or termination decisions until HR and legal have reviewed them.
