## What it is

CallMiner is the long-standing incumbent in contact-centre conversation analytics. Its Eureka platform captures calls, chats, emails and screen activity, transcribes and categorises every interaction, and turns the result into automated quality scores, coaching and compliance alerts. The product line is organised by stage: Record, Screen Record and Redact for capture; Analyze and Visualize for intelligence; Coach and RealTime for agent augmentation; Outreach and OmniAgent (voice and chat virtual agents) for automation. Founder Jeff Gallino is still CEO, the company is based in Waltham, Massachusetts, and its last disclosed round was a $75M growth investment from Goldman Sachs in December 2019, taking total funding to about $160M. It is an acquirer, not a target: in June 2025 it bought VOCALLS to add native voice and text AI agents.

2026 has been about bolting generative and agentic features onto that analytics core. At Enterprise Connect in March it showed AI classifiers wired into its natural-language assistant. On 22 June it added agent-initiated AI guidance to RealTime: agents ask for help mid-call, answers come from your own knowledge base, and every request is flagged in Analyze and Coach so supervisors see where agents get stuck. In September it was named a Strong Performer in its first Forrester Wave for Customer Feedback Management and Analytics (Q3 2026), and it was a Leader in the QKS SPARK Matrix for Conversational Intelligence for Q2 2026.

## Why it shows up in CX stacks

- **Analytics depth is the point.** CallMiner's strength is not the scorecard but the category and topic model underneath it: asking why contacts happen, which policies generate repeat calls, which phrases precede churn or complaints. AI-native QA tools score agents well; fewer of them are built for a CX analyst asking open questions across millions of calls.
- **Compliance and redaction are mature.** Redact removes card numbers and other sensitive data from audio and transcripts, including in real time. The vendor lists independent audits for SOC 2 Type II, HITRUST (covering HIPAA), ISO 27001 and PCI DSS, which is why banks, insurers, health plans and BPOs show up in its customer list — Santander, PennyMac, UPMC Health Plan, Teleperformance, SiriusXM.
- **It connects at the phone layer.** Real-time connectors exist for [Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/) AudioHook, [Five9](/en/tools/five9/) VoiceStream and [Amazon Connect](/en/tools/amazon-connect/), plus [Talkdesk](/en/tools/talkdesk/), [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) on the CRM side and an open standard for bringing your own transcription engine.
- **Users rate it well.** G2 shows about 4.6 out of 5 across roughly 230 reviews; the most-cited complaints are a learning curve and some accuracy issues before tuning.

## Pricing reality

No published price. CallMiner's own FAQ says packages are priced by user count or by interaction volume, start at midsize teams of more than 60 agents, and include its advanced AI at no extra cost. Third-party listings put the per-agent equivalent between $55 and $110 a month. Vendr's buyer data, from a small sample, shows an average contract of about $102,000 a year, a top end near $140,000 and about 15% average negotiated savings. Plan on $100,000-130,000 a year for a 100-agent centre and $200,000-400,000 for 300 agents once Coach and RealTime are in scope, with professional services on top.

For scale, the [Zendesk QA](/en/tools/zendesk-qa/) add-on lists at about $35 per agent per month and [Level AI](/en/tools/level-ai/) is estimated at $80-125. CallMiner is not the cheap option; you are paying for the analytics layer.

## Best for

Heads of CX analytics, QA and compliance in voice-heavy contact centres of 100 seats and up — financial services, insurance, healthcare, telecoms and BPOs — where the question is "why are customers calling and what are we saying to them" across every interaction, not only "how did this agent score."

Skip it under about 60 agents, where the vendor itself does not sell. Skip it if your queue is chat and email inside Zendesk or Intercom; a ticket-first QA tool will be cheaper and live in days.

## Versus the alternatives

The two installed-base incumbents are [NICE CXone](/en/tools/nice-cxone/) (Nexidia analytics) and [Verint](/en/tools/verint/). Pick them when you already run their recording and workforce management and the renewal bundles analytics cheaply; pick CallMiner when you want an analytics vendor that is independent of your CCaaS and recording stack. Among AI-native challengers, [Level AI](/en/tools/level-ai/) and [Observe.AI](/en/tools/observe-ai/) are the like-for-like auto-QA swaps: faster to configure and usually better at generative scorecards; pick one when QA scoring and coaching, not exploratory analytics, is the budget line. [Cresta](/en/tools/cresta/) is the fastest-growing entrant and the pick when real-time agent guidance is the priority — CallMiner's agent-initiated guidance shipped only in June 2026. For digital-only queues under 100 agents, [Solidroad](/en/tools/solidroad/), [Intryc](/en/tools/intryc/) or [Rippit](/en/tools/rippit/) cost a fraction.

If none fit, export a month of transcripts from your CCaaS and run a calibrated LLM rubric over one queue. If full coverage does not change a coaching or policy decision, no vendor in this category will pay back.

## Watch-outs

- **The analytics needs an analyst.** Categories, searches and dashboards are only as useful as the person maintaining them, and reviewers cite a learning curve. Guard: name an owner with at least half their time on CallMiner before signing, and write three business questions the first 90 days must answer into the success plan.
- **Two pricing meters.** Packages can be priced per user or per interaction volume, and volume growth can outrun headcount. Guard: get both models quoted, choose the one that fits your 3-year forecast, and cap the overage rate and renewal uplift in the order form.
- **The new agentic features are young.** RealTime's on-demand guidance and the AI classifiers are 2026 releases with vendor-reported results. Guard: pilot RealTime on one queue with a control group and measure handle time and first-contact resolution before buying seats for the floor.
- **Out-of-the-box scores drift from human judgment.** Accuracy complaints appear in reviews. Guard: double-score 200 interactions by hand and in Eureka before retiring human QA, and set a per-question agreement threshold — 90% is a reasonable bar — as a go-live criterion.
- **Redaction is a configuration, not a default guarantee.** Guard: test Redact on real calls containing card and health data during the pilot, and have your compliance team sign off on the transcripts, not on the datasheet.
