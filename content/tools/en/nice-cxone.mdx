## What it is

NiCE CXone is an enterprise cloud contact-center suite: routing, IVR, outbound, agent and supervisor workspaces, and the deepest workforce-engagement layer in the category — workforce management, quality management, recording, interaction analytics, and voice-of-the-customer feedback in the same tenant. NiCE (Nasdaq: NICE, the company now styles itself NiCE) built that WEM business first and moved it to the cloud, which is why CXone wins when the WFM and QA teams have a vote.

The AI layer is now two products. Native CXone AI agents — for self-service, sales and marketing, proactive outreach, and process automation, plus Copilot for agents and supervisors — launched as "CXone Mpower Agents" in June 2025. NiCE Cognigy, the Düsseldorf conversational-AI platform NiCE bought for $955M in a deal that closed on 8 September 2025, sits beside it and is still sold standalone; Cognigy's co-founder Philipp Heltewig runs it and is NiCE's chief AI officer. At Nexus on 10 March 2026, NiCE announced new Cognigy capabilities: agents generated from mined interaction data, multivariate testing of prompts, models, and guardrails before go-live, voice-plus-visual hybrid journeys, LLM-scored conversation analysis, and expanded MCP support so Cognigy services can be called from outside AI tools. The release gave no GA dates. Playvox, acquired in 2024, is now sold as Playvox by NiCE for WFM and QA on Salesforce, Zendesk, and Amazon Connect desks.

Scale is not in question. Q2 2026 revenue was $782.3M, cloud revenue $609.0M (up 12.6% year over year), and NiCE reported CX AI and self-service ARR of $362M, up 52%. It was placed highest for Ability to Execute in Gartner's 2025 CCaaS Magic Quadrant, alongside Genesys, AWS, Five9, and Talkdesk as Leaders.

## Why it shows up in CX and support stacks

- **WEM is native, not a partner.** Forecasting, scheduling, QA scoring, and recording live with the routing engine, so adherence and quality data never cross a vendor boundary. For a 300-seat center running separate WFM software, that consolidation alone often justifies the evaluation.
- **It is the incumbent the AI-native vendors plug into.** [PolyAI](/en/tools/polyai/), [Parloa](/en/tools/parloa/), [Cresta](/en/tools/cresta/), and [Kore.ai](/en/tools/kore-ai/) all list CXone as a supported platform. Keeping CXone does not lock you into NiCE's own agents.
- **Two AI paths under one contract.** Native agents cover routing and FAQ intents; Cognigy covers multi-step, integration-heavy journeys. You can start on the first and graduate to the second without changing CCaaS vendors.
- **The UCaaS gap is closing by partnership.** On 23 July 2026 NiCE agreed to resell RingCentral's RingEX under a multi-year deal, so a CXone buyer can now get the phone system on the same paper.

## Pricing reality

NiCE publishes list prices, which the enterprise peers mostly do not: Omnichannel Suite $110, Essential $135, Core $169, Complete $209, and Ultimate $249 per agent per month, with Ultimate and the banking, insurance, healthcare, and retail packages adding $0.25 per session. Billing is monthly in arrears with no prepay. Voice biometrics, EHR integration, and the proactive-engagement AI agent are unpriced add-ons, and Cognigy has no public rate card.

The session fee is what moves the number. Fifty agents on Ultimate list at $12,450 a month; add 8,000 AI sessions and the invoice is $14,450 — about $173K a year before implementation, which third-party analyses put at $10K-$100K+ for CRM integration and compliance work. Fifty seats on Omnichannel run $66K a year. For comparison, Genesys Cloud lists from $75, Talkdesk from $85, and Five9 from $119 per seat per month, so CXone's entry point is mid-pack and its top tier is the most expensive published rate in the segment.

## Best for

Contact-center and CX operations leaders running 200+ seats in regulated, voice-heavy industries — banking, insurance, healthcare, utilities, BPO — whose WFM and quality teams are part of the buying decision, and who want AI agents and human-agent operations under one vendor.

Skip it below about 50 seats, where per-agent list price and implementation overhead outrun the WEM benefit, or if digital-first support on a ticketing tool is the real job.

## Versus the alternatives

[Genesys Cloud](/en/tools/genesys-cloud/) is the other half of the enterprise duopoly. Pick Genesys when you plan to orchestrate several vendors' AI agents behind one front door; pick CXone when WEM and quality management drive the decision or your WFM team already runs NiCE. [Five9](/en/tools/five9/) wins for outbound-heavy and mid-market centers that want a simpler buying motion. The challenger with the most momentum is [Talkdesk](/en/tools/talkdesk/), the one vendor that moved up into Gartner's Leaders quadrant in 2025 — the pick for a 50-300 seat center that wants a lower entry price and vertical packages.

If you already run CXone, the live question is whether NiCE's own agents or an AI-native vendor should hold the call. Take Cognigy or the native agents when your top intents are routing, status, and FAQ. Bring in [PolyAI](/en/tools/polyai/) or [Parloa](/en/tools/parloa/) when the call must transact against a healthcare or hospitality system of record, and [Sierra](/en/tools/sierra/) when you want outcome-based billing across chat and voice. If none fit, keep CXone for routing and WEM, and pilot one AI vendor on your three highest-volume intents before committing to any agent contract.

## Watch-outs

- **Product names move faster than the contract.** "Mpower" no longer appears on NiCE's platform page, Cognigy is sold both inside CXone and standalone, and the Nexus 2026 features shipped without GA dates. Guard: list every AI capability by its current SKU name in the order form, with GA status and region, and attach the product description as an exhibit so a rebrand cannot change what you bought.
- **The $0.25 session fee scales with success.** Every contained conversation on Ultimate adds to the bill, so higher automation raises AI spend even as headcount falls. Guard: model sessions × $0.25 against your containment target before signing, negotiate a session-volume tier or cap, and get a definition of "session" in writing.
- **Reviewers flag call quality and reporting.** G2 averages 4.3/5 over 1,730 reviews, but recurring complaints cite dropped calls in the browser agent app and reporting that is hard to customize. Guard: run a two-week pilot on your own carrier and network with your real reporting pack rebuilt in CXone, and make SLA credits for audio incidents part of the contract.
- **Two AI stacks mean two roadmaps.** Native agents and Cognigy overlap, and NiCE has not published a convergence date. Guard: pick one path for your first year, get written confirmation of migration terms if NiCE consolidates them, and keep your intent map and test sets outside the platform.
