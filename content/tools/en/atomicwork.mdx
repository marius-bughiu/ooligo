## What it is

Atomicwork is an AI-native service management platform for internal requests: an ITSM and ESM system of record plus a set of AI agents, which it calls **AI Coworkers**, that answer and resolve employee requests in Slack, Microsoft Teams, email, a web portal, voice, and inside Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise. It covers IT first (incidents, requests, assets, access provisioning) and extends to HR, finance and legal workspaces, with example coworkers for onboarding, benefits, phishing triage and legal intake.

The design choice that sets it apart is that the agent layer is sold separately from the ticketing system. You can run Atomicwork as a full replacement for your helpdesk, or deploy only the AI Coworkers on top of an existing [ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/) or [Jira Service Management](/en/tools/jira-service-management/) instance, which the pricing page describes as "no migration, no platform fee." Identity and HR context comes from [Okta](/en/tools/okta/), Microsoft Entra and [Workday](/en/tools/workday/); the vendor lists 100+ connectors and exposes the platform to MCP-compatible agents, so a coding or desktop agent can file and query tickets through it. You can also bring your own models from OpenAI, Anthropic or Google.

**Company status:** founded in September 2022 by Vijay Rayapati, Kiran Darisi and Parsuram Vijayasankar. It raised a $25M Series A co-led by Khosla Ventures and Z47 in January 2025, for about $39M in total at the time, and Okta Ventures joined a Series A extension later that year. It listed on the Microsoft Marketplace in March 2026. It is independent; we found no acquisition or shutdown reports in the last 12 months. Compliance claims on its site: SOC 2, ISO 42001, CSA STAR, HIPAA and GDPR, with hosting in the US, EU or APAC.

## Why it shows up in ops stacks

It is the only AI-native service desk we cover that publishes both a starting price and per-outcome prices. That makes it the easiest of the new entrants to put into a business case, and the lowest-friction way to test AI resolution without a migration: buy outcomes on top of the desk you already run, and measure.

## Pricing reality

The pricing page, checked on 2026-11-22, offers two models.

- **Platform plans.** **Professional** starts at **$25,000 a year**, billed annually, with 25,000 credits, 2 AI Coworkers, up to 250 end users, 500 managed devices and 50 software applications, and business-hours email support. Extra coworkers cost **$499 per coworker per month**. **Business** (5 coworkers, up to 1,000 end users) and **Enterprise** (10 coworkers, unlimited users, custom data residency, 24x7 support) are quote-only.
- **Outcome plans.** **Knowledge Support at $1** per resolved query (minimum 25,000 a year), **Access Automation at $2** per access request handled end to end (minimum 12,000 a year), and **Service Resolution from $3** per resolved request (minimum 8,000 a year). Each minimum works out to roughly $24,000 to $25,000 a year.

What teams actually pay: at the 250-user cap, Professional is $100 per employee a year before extra coworkers, and the annual minimums put any outcome plan at about $25,000 a year. Business and Enterprise contracts are not public and we found no verified negotiated figure. For scale, [Serval](/en/tools/serval/)'s only public number is a $500,000 AWS Marketplace listing, ServiceNow's 50-fulfiller estate runs about $48,000 to $90,000 a year before AI, and [Freshservice](/en/tools/freshservice/) Pro for 25 agents is about $29,700 a year.

## Best for

IT and operations leaders at 150 to 2,000-employee companies, on Okta or Entra and Slack or Teams, who want to automate repeat access requests and how-to questions and need a published price to get budget approval. The sharpest use case is the overlay: a team on Jira Service Management or ServiceNow that wants to prove AI deflection on real tickets before deciding whether to migrate at all. Published customer results fit that band: Zuora reports a 50% reduction in ticket volume after moving off Jira Service Management, Pepper Money says it replaced ServiceNow in 6 weeks with a 75% cut in mean time to resolve, and Mary Ruth's reports 60% deflection.

Do not buy it if you have fewer than about 100 employees (the $25,000 floor buys more than you will use), if you need endpoint management (patching, remote control), or if the queue is external customer support rather than employees.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[ServiceNow](/en/tools/servicenow/)**: the market-share leader. Pick it at 2,000+ employees when IT already runs the CMDB and cases cross IT, HR and customer service under one data model.
- **[Jira Service Management](/en/tools/jira-service-management/)**: the other incumbent. Pick it when engineering lives in Jira, IT and dev tickets should share a backlog, and per-agent pricing is cheaper than a platform fee for your desk.
- **[Serval](/en/tools/serval/)**: the fastest-growing entrant. Pick it when you want vendor engineers to build deterministic access and IT workflows and can fund a six-figure platform line.
- **[Freshservice](/en/tools/freshservice/)**: pick it when you want a mature per-agent ITSM tool run by an internal admin, with AI as an add-on.

The default: if you already run ServiceNow or Jira Service Management, buy Atomicwork's Knowledge Support or Access Automation outcomes on top for a year before considering any migration. If you are replacing a helpdesk outright, pilot Atomicwork against Serval on the same 30 days of real tickets and take the one that hits your automation target at the lower 3-year cost.

## Watch-outs

- **Credits are opaque.** A credit is drawn each time a coworker completes a job, and the draw scales with steps, tools called, output length and reasoning complexity. The pricing page states no overage rate. Guard: before signing Professional, run your top 20 request types in the trial, record credits burned per request, and write the overage rate and a renewal cap into the order form.
- **Outcome definitions decide the bill.** At $1 to $3 per outcome, what counts as "resolved" matters more than the rate. Guard: get the outcome definition, the dispute window and the reporting you can audit in the contract, and sample 50 billed outcomes each month against the ticket history.
- **Thin independent review base.** G2 and Capterra each show a single review; the main complaints are limited customization and a UI that takes time to learn. Gartner Peer Insights has 11. Guard: ask for two reference customers in your headcount band and industry, and confirm the SOC 2 Type II report and data-residency region before go-live.
