## What it is

Ankar is a London AI platform for the patent lifecycle that has spent 2026 widening into something it calls an operating system for innovation. The site now sorts the product into three areas. **Explore** covers portfolio mapping, landscape monitoring, technology scouting and whitespace analysis. **Invent & Experiment** is the R&D side: inventor-facing disclosure capture and research intelligence on competitor filings. **Protect** is the patent-attorney side: drafting, office action responses, claim charts, and two life-science modules, one for small-molecule (Markush) analysis and one for biological sequences with ST.26 preparation. Every module runs on traceable citations back to the source document, and novelty and prior-art analysis runs across what Ankar says are 150M+ patent applications and 250M+ scientific publications.

Tamar Gomez (CEO) and Wiem Gharbi (CTO) founded it after building software at Palantir; Gomez also worked in defence tech. Index Ventures led a £3M seed in May 2025. Atomico led a $20M Series A on 17 December 2025, with Index, Norrsken VC and Daphni, for about $24M raised. The team was about 20 people at the Series A. In June 2026 it hired Harm van der Heijden, a patent attorney with roughly 17 years in private practice, as Head of Patent Innovation.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It starts upstream of the attorney.** Most drafting tools wait for a disclosure to arrive. Ankar puts invention capture and research intelligence in front of the inventors and engineers, so the same record carries from the lab notebook to the draft. That matters for an in-house IP lead whose real bottleneck is ideas that never get disclosed.
- **Chemistry and biology are first-class.** Markush expansion, compound-to-claim mapping and ST.26 sequence listings are native modules, not prompts. Named customers lean that way: L'Oréal, Valeo, Nexans, the life-science company Antheros, and the law firms Vorys and Prinz & Partner.
- **Infringement and claim charts sit in the same tool.** The Valeo and L'Oréal case studies describe infringement detection on the existing portfolio, which turns the platform from a cost-cutting tool into a licensing and enforcement one.
- **Security paperwork is ahead of its age.** SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001:2022, US or EU data hosting, and no training on customer data, per its trust centre.

## Pricing reality

There is no public price. Ankar sells through a demo and a forward-deployed rollout team, and we found no published tier, free trial or third-party price reference. Plan for an annual enterprise contract, and benchmark it against what you pay outside counsel today: Tradespace's 2026 fee guide puts a boutique firm's bill for drafting and filing a US non-provisional at $8,000–15,000 before USPTO fees. If Ankar cannot show the drafting saving on a handful of your own past filings, the platform fee is a research-tool fee, and it should be priced against [PatSnap](/en/tools/patsnap/) or [Questel](/en/tools/questel/) instead.

## Best for

In-house IP teams at European or US corporates with chemistry, materials, life-science or automotive portfolios who want invention capture, prior-art search and first drafts in one system, and who need EU data hosting. Second fit: patent boutiques doing heavy small-molecule or sequence work.

**Not for you if** your attorneys refuse to leave Microsoft Word, if you need a full docketing and annuity system, or if you need a published price before you take a demo.

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **[Solve Intelligence](/en/tools/solve-intelligence/)** — the best-funded drafting platform, about $55M raised. Pick it when attorney drafting and prosecution volume is the bottleneck and the inventor side is handled elsewhere.
- **[DeepIP](/en/tools/deepip/)** — $40M raised and the fastest-growing prosecution entrant, working as a Word add-in. Pick it when the drafting must happen inside Word.
- **[Patlytics](/en/tools/patlytics/)** — $40M Series B in April 2026, weighted towards litigation and evidence-of-use charts. Pick it when enforcement and licensing drive the budget.
- **[Tradespace](/en/tools/tradespace/)** — the closest lifecycle rival, with published drafting prices and a flat-fee attorney network. Pick it when you want the filing work priced up front and US hosting is acceptable.
- **[Anaqua](/en/tools/anaqua/)** or **[PatSnap](/en/tools/patsnap/)** — the incumbents by market share for portfolio management and patent data. Pick them when the job is a large global docket or search at scale, with AI drafting as a bolt-on.

## Watch-outs

- **Every performance number is the vendor's.** The 40% productivity gain and 96% would-recommend figure come from Ankar, and the published case studies contain no measured results. **Guard:** run a pilot on five inventions you have already filed through counsel, and compare claims, cited art and attorney hours side by side.
- **Drafting quality was uneven in 2025.** An August 2025 review by a patent attorney, who disclosed an affiliate relationship, reported inconsistent output depth, text inserted into the wrong section, a wrong figure description and a claim-reference format that does not meet 37 CFR 1.75(f). Word import and export with tracked changes arrived in August 2026. **Guard:** have a registered practitioner sign off every draft, and test current output against your house style before scaling.
- **Integrations are described, not documented.** Ankar says it connects to document repositories, lab notebooks and IP management systems, but names none and publishes no API or MCP server. **Guard:** name your IPMS and repository in the order form, and make bulk export of disclosures, drafts and citations a contract term.
- **A two-year-old vendor with a widening scope.** The product grew from patent drafting to R&D intelligence within eighteen months. **Guard:** pin your entitlements to the modules you buy, and ask for a written support horizon and roadmap for those modules before signing a multi-year term.
