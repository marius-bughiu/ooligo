## What it is

Bloomberg Law is the third full-service US legal research platform, next to Westlaw and Lexis: case law, statutes and regulations, the BCite citator, Points of Law, federal and state dockets, Litigation Analytics on judges, courts and counsel, Practical Guidance, transactional precedent with Draft Analyzer, and Bloomberg Law News. It is sold by Bloomberg Industry Group, an affiliate of Bloomberg L.P., on annual quoted subscriptions. The question this page answers: is it a real third option to [Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) with Westlaw and [LexisNexis Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/) for AI research and dockets? For dockets and transactional work, yes. For AI-first case-law research, it is still catching up.

**2026 is when the AI layer got a shape.** Bloomberg's generative features started as two betas on 14 January 2025: Bloomberg Law Answers, which answers questions typed into the search bar with linked citations, and the AI Assistant, a side panel that summarizes and answers questions about the one document you have open. On 20 August 2026, ahead of ILTACON, Bloomberg announced three pieces on top of that:

- **BLAW AI** — an AI mode for legal questions that returns structured, cited answers grounded in Bloomberg Law content and sources you select.
- **Workspaces** — a persistent area per matter, client, deal or investigation that holds research, your own documents and AI output together.
- **AI agents** — guided workflows for repeatable tasks with structured inputs and reviewable outputs, built partly on Dashboard Legal, the legal project-management tool Bloomberg acquired in April 2024.

On 5 October 2026 it added **Watchlists**, an AI-monitored feed of developments on the clients, companies and issues you follow, paired with **Bloomberg Regology**, the regulatory-change platform it acquired in June 2026 that monitors US federal and state rules plus more than 135 countries.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Dockets inside Claude.** The Bloomberg Law connector, listed in Anthropic's [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) directory since July 2026, exposes 17 docket tools: search, entries, filings, downloads, alerts, tracks, filing requests and an outcome-prediction call. That puts live federal and state docket data into an assistant without a Bloomberg screen open. Case-law research and judge analytics stay in the Bloomberg Law app.
- **Paid actions are gated.** Alerts, tracks, docket updates and filing requests each have a preview step before an execute step, plus `get_docket_cost` and `get_pleading_cost` calls. An agent cannot buy filings silently, which matters once a docket workflow runs unattended.
- **Firms with their own LLM get an API path.** Bloomberg's chief revenue officer, Kara DelVecchio, told Legal IT Insider in September 2026 that customers already use its dockets API and news API, and that MCP extends the same access to firms that built internal models.
- **Transactional and regulatory depth.** Draft Analyzer benchmarks clause language against EDGAR-filed agreements, and Regology now ties regulatory change to Watchlists. That is where Bloomberg's research pull is strongest, more than in litigation research.
- **Fewer surprise charges.** Bloomberg prices on a flat subscription rather than per search, with dockets, BCite, Points of Law and analytics included.

## Pricing reality

**Bloomberg publishes no price; every deal is quoted.** The $450-per-user-per-month figure that still circulates traces back to launch-era coverage in 2010 and 2011. Third-party estimates for 2026 run $300–$700 per attorney per month for small firms, on an annual commitment, with escalators of roughly 5–8% a year reported across all three incumbents. For comparison, ooligo's band for Westlaw with CoCounsel is $200–$500 per attorney per month, and Lexis+ with Protégé lands at $128–$494.

Two line items decide the real bill. First, PACER: Bloomberg absorbs docket retrieval fees, but it has historically capped that per user, at $1,500 a year in a 2020 academic arrangement, so a heavy docket practice can run past the flat fee. Second, the Claude connector's filing requests and docket updates carry costs that have no published rate card. A five-attorney litigation team should budget roughly $18,000–$42,000 a year before PACER overages and implementation.

## Best for

Litigation support managers, docket clerks and in-house legal ops teams who track federal and state dockets at volume and want that data inside Claude or their own LLM, and transactional and regulatory teams already on a Bloomberg Law contract who want AI answers, Workspaces and Regology monitoring in the same subscription.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Thomson Reuters CoCounsel](/en/tools/thomson-reuters-cocounsel/) with Westlaw** — the market-share leader. Pick it when KeyCite, Practical Law and the most mature agentic research are the requirement.
- **[LexisNexis Protégé](/en/tools/lexisnexis-protege/)** — the other incumbent. Pick it when Shepard's is your standard, or when you want judge analytics from Lex Machina in the same contract.
- **[Harvey](/en/tools/harvey/)** — the fastest-growing entrant in legal AI. Pick it for firm-wide drafting and analysis; Harvey licenses research content through partners such as LexisNexis rather than owning it.
- **[vLex Vincent AI](/en/tools/vlex-vincent-ai/)** — pick it when your bar association already bundles vLex Fastcase and you only need to budget the AI line.
- **[Midpage](/en/tools/midpage/)** — pick it at a published $100 a seat when the job is AI case-law research with PACER access and no annual contract.
- **[Trellis](/en/tools/trellis/)** — pick it when the dockets that matter are state trial courts and you want published self-serve pricing.

If you need case-law research grounded inside Claude, the Bloomberg connector does not cover it. Pair it with a research connector, or keep the research seat where it is.

## Watch-outs

- **The newest pieces are not yet fully shipped.** Bloomberg described the dockets-and-analytics interoperability as a preview in August 2026, and the company has published no general-availability dates for BLAW AI, Workspaces or agents. Guard: ask your account team for a written list of what your contract includes today, and demo each feature on your own matters before renewal.
- **AI research trails the leaders.** Answers and the AI Assistant launched as betas in January 2025, almost two years after CoCounsel, and ooligo's [legal AI research roundup](/en/vs/best-legal-ai-research-tools/) finds nobody switches to Bloomberg for the AI. Guard: score BLAW AI on ten of your own research questions against your incumbent's output before you count it as a reason to move.
- **Connector access follows your subscription.** Two users running the same docket query through Claude can see different results, and third-party reviews report license limits on building a retrieval corpus from returned content and on unattended bot use under a shared account. Guard: get written confirmation of connector entitlement and permitted automated use before wiring it into an agent.
- **Docket costs can outrun the flat fee.** PACER caps and per-request connector charges sit outside the headline price. Guard: put the PACER cap and the connector rate card in the order form, and route every filing request through the preview step with a human approval.
- **Annual escalators compound.** At 5–8% a year, a three-year term costs 10–17% more in year three than in year one. Guard: negotiate a capped escalator, or a one-year term while the AI features mature.
