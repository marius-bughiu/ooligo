A recruiting team buying AI in 2026 is buying five jobs, not five logos: a horizontal assistant for all the work that isn't recruiting, a system of record, a sourcing engine, something that captures what actually happened in the interview, and — only if you hire hourly — a conversational front door. Five products cover those jobs. The list is ranked by what pays back first, cheapest and most portable at the top, and the order moved this year for two reasons that have nothing to do with product quality.

## What changed since the last version of this list

**Paradox belongs to Workday.** The acquisition closed on October 1, 2025. Olivia now ships as the Workday Paradox Candidate Experience Agent, available for purchase through either Workday or Paradox, and founder-CEO Adam Godson now leads Workday's talent-acquisition platform. The product still deploys against non-Workday systems, but the roadmap is set inside Workday now. That moved Paradox from second on this list to fifth — not because it got worse, but because "who steers this in three years" is a real buying criterion and the answer changed.

**Metaview stopped being an interview-notes product.** It now calls itself an agentic recruiting platform and ships four named agents — Sourcing Agent, Application Review, Notetaker, and Reports — plus Fillmore, an agent that sources, runs outreach, and books screening calls without a recruiter in the loop. The Sourcing Agent carries published per-user pricing. That puts Metaview and Gem on the same job for the first time.

**Ashby and Gem both publish numbers now.** Ashby's pricing calculator opens at $400/month for Foundations and meters AI usage as credits rather than seats. Gem publishes a startup program and hands companies under 30 employees the whole platform free for six months. Two years ago every tool on this list was a sales call.

## 1. Claude — the horizontal layer, and the first thing to buy

ooligo score: 9.5. Most of what a recruiting team does in a week is not sourcing. It is turning a rambling intake call into a job description, summarizing eight candidate profiles for a hiring manager who will read one, writing the rejection note that doesn't sound like a form letter, and rebuilding the same offer-approval spreadsheet every quarter. Claude's 1M-token context handles whole-document work without retrieval scaffolding, and Skills plus MCP let you encode the recurring routines instead of re-prompting them by hand every time.

**Where it pulls ahead:** cost per useful task. At $20–$25 a seat it is a twentieth of anything else here, and it is the only entry on the list that keeps working if you change ATS.

**Where it loses:** it has no candidate data and no system of record. It will write an excellent outreach sequence and it cannot tell you who to send it to, or remember that you sent it.

**Where to start:** build three Skills — JD-from-intake, candidate-research-summary, outreach-personalizer — and point one MCP connector at your ATS. If your recruiters are pasting into a chat window by hand six months from now, the Skills were never built.

## 2. Ashby — the AI-native system of record

ooligo score: 9.1. Ashby bundles ATS, CRM, scheduling, and analytics into one product where none of the four feels bolted on, and it is the piece of this stack that everything else writes into. The 2026 shape of it is worth understanding before you buy: AI is metered as credits, not bundled per seat. Foundations includes 1,500 credits a month; Plus includes 2,500 per seat per year and Enterprise 12,500, and they power AI application review, candidate fraud detection, and talent rediscovery.

**Where it pulls ahead:** analytics that don't need a data team. Ashby answers "which source produced hires, not applicants, at what cost" natively — which is the question Greenhouse shops usually answer with an exported CSV and a contractor.

**Where it loses:** the credit model turns AI into a variable cost you have to forecast, and small line items add up. SSO is a $100/month add-on rather than a base feature, which is a hard conversation with a security team that assumed otherwise.

**Where to start:** if you are on Greenhouse or Lever and your analytics are the pain, run a 30-day pilot on one business unit and count credits burned. That number, not the license, is what determines the real annual cost.

## 3. Gem — sourcing and candidate CRM at outbound scale

ooligo score: 8.4. Gem is the outbound recruiting layer: it captures every candidate touch across LinkedIn, email, and your ATS, runs sequences with AI personalization, and — the part that pays for it — rediscovers the silver-medalist you rejected fourteen months ago before you pay to source a stranger. Its AI Sourcing Agent searches a pool the company puts at 800M+ profiles and returns a shortlist rather than a result set.

**Where it pulls ahead:** rediscovery against a candidate database you already own. Every other sourcing tool on the market sells you access to people you have never met; this one monetizes the pipeline you already paid to build.

**Where it loses:** price opacity above the startup tier. Both real plans — Gem alongside your ATS, and Gem All-in-One — are quoted by FTE count with no published rate, so budgeting requires a sales call the moment you pass 100 employees.

**Where to start:** import the last 12 months of rejected-but-strong candidates, run rediscovery against two open reqs, and compare response rates to cold outbound. If rediscovery does not beat cold by a wide margin, your ATS data is the problem and no sourcing tool will fix it.

## 4. Metaview — interview capture, now with a sourcing agent attached

ooligo score: 8.7. Metaview joins interviews on Zoom, Meet, or Teams and writes a structured, scorecard-aligned draft into Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby before the interviewer has closed the tab. That remains the reason to buy it. What is new is everything around it: an Application Review agent for inbound volume, a Reports agent sold as a flat-fee add-on, and a Sourcing Agent with the only fully published price on this list.

**Where it pulls ahead:** scorecard quality, measurably, within two weeks. Interview notes written from memory three hours later are the weakest input in the whole hiring process, and this is the only entry here that fixes them.

**Where it loses:** the platform story now overlaps Gem's, and paying two vendors for sourcing is a decision, not an accident. The Sourcing Agent's Pro tier caps at 200 profiles a month, so a real outbound motion lands on Max at $300 a user or on Gem.

**Where to start:** turn the Notetaker on for one hiring panel and compare scorecards before and after. Evaluate the Sourcing Agent separately and later — on the free tier's first 100 profiles, against whatever you source with today.

## 5. Paradox — high-volume conversational hiring, now a Workday product

ooligo score: 8.6. For hourly and frontline hiring, Olivia is still the strongest thing you can buy. Candidates apply, screen, and self-schedule over SMS or chat with no application form, and the drop-off a legacy careers-site form causes simply stops happening. 7-Eleven, Nestlé, Marriott, Chipotle, and Wendy's run hourly hiring on it, and no challenger has that depth of frontline reference.

**Where it pulls ahead:** funnel completion at volume. Nothing else here moves apply-to-hire from weeks to days for a warehouse req.

**Where it loses:** ownership and fit. It is a Workday product now, priced by quote, and it earns its keep on hourly funnels rather than on 20 senior reqs a quarter. If you hire knowledge workers, this slot in your stack should be empty.

**Where to start:** point it at one high-volume role family and measure time-to-schedule and no-show rate week over week. Ask the rep directly what the non-Workday integration roadmap looks like, and get the answer in the contract rather than the deck.

## Pricing, side by side

| Tool | Published? | Entry economics |
|---|---|---|
| Claude | Yes | $20/mo Pro; $25/seat/mo Team |
| Metaview | Partly | Sourcing Agent: free to 100 profiles, $100/user/mo Pro, $300/user/mo Max; platform is custom |
| Ashby | Partly | Foundations from $400/mo flat, up to 100 employees; Plus and Enterprise quoted |
| Gem | Startup tier only | $130/mo for 1–10 FTE on annual; free for six months under 30 employees; custom above that |
| Paradox | No | Quoted. Roughly $25K–60K/yr mid-market, $75K–150K+/yr enterprise, plus $15K–35K implementation |

Two structures hide inside that table. Claude, Metaview, and Gem's startup tier price per user, so cost scales with recruiters. Ashby prices flat with metered AI credits, so cost scales with hiring volume instead. A 12-recruiter team hiring lightly and a 4-recruiter team hiring 300 people a year will get very different answers, and neither is discoverable from a rate card.

## The pick

**If you cannot decide, buy Claude and Metaview.** Together they run under $325 a seat, neither needs an implementation project, and they attack the two things that actually consume a recruiting week: document and communication work, and the interview record. Everything else on this list should follow evidence from those two rather than precede it.

Then add by bottleneck, in this order:

- **You cannot answer basic funnel questions without exporting a CSV** → Ashby. The symptom is an analytics problem, not a sourcing one, and switching ATS is the only fix.
- **Pipeline is thin and outbound is manual** → Gem, and start with rediscovery rather than cold sourcing. If you are already on Metaview, price its Max tier against Gem before adding a second vendor.
- **You hire hourly at volume and candidates ghost between apply and interview** → Paradox, with the Workday question asked out loud.
- **You hire fewer than 50 people a year** → stop at Claude and Metaview. The rest is overhead.

## What's not on this list, and why

- **BrightHire** — the tool that created the interview-intelligence category, and genuinely good. Zoom acquired it in December 2025 and runs it as a standalone brand inside Zoom Workplace. It loses the slot to Metaview on ATS-native breadth, not on notes quality. See [BrightHire vs Metaview](/en/vs/brighthire-vs-metaview/).
- **Greenhouse, Lever** — solid systems of record with real ecosystems. Neither is AI-native. If you are on one and happy, stay; if you are picking fresh, Ashby.
- **hireEZ, SeekOut, Findem, Eightfold** — credible sourcing and talent-intelligence platforms, several with published entry pricing now. Gem keeps the slot on the integrated CRM plus rediscovery story, which is where the money is for in-house teams rather than agencies.
- **Sense, ConverzAI, Phenom** — the live alternatives in Paradox's lane. Sense is the closest ATS-neutral peer and now publishes floors; ConverzAI is the fastest-growing AI-native entrant. Any of the three is the right call if a Workday-owned roadmap is a problem for you. See [Paradox vs Sense](/en/vs/paradox-vs-sense/).
- **Raw ChatGPT for recruiting** — it works, and it leaves the Skills, MCP, and long-context advantages unused while your prompt library rots in a Notion doc.

## If none of these fit

The usual reason is that the constraint is not tooling. A team with no scorecards, no intake process, and no agreed definition of a qualified candidate will buy Metaview and generate structured notes about an unstructured process. The AI makes that failure faster and better documented, not smaller.

That team's correct next step is to write one interview rubric for one role family and run it manually for a month. Then buy Claude, then buy the rest against a bottleneck you can name. Every tool here works better against a defined process, and two of them are close to unusable without one.
