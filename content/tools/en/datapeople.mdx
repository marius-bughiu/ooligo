## What it is

Datapeople is job-posting software for talent acquisition teams. It checks every job post before it goes live for pay-transparency compliance, biased or exclusionary language, and job-board searchability. It also keeps a governed library of approved job templates and reports how each post performs through the hiring funnel. The company was founded in 2015, raised more than $21 million (First Round Capital, GreatPoint Ventures, New Markets Venture Partners), and says its models are trained on more than 100 million job posts and outcomes from 80 countries. Named customers include DoorDash, Remitly, Informatica and Twitch.

**It is no longer independent.** Payscale announced the acquisition on 16 September 2025, terms undisclosed. On 28 July 2026 Payscale relaunched the product as **JobNav Recruiter** inside its Payscale Intelligence Cloud, and its own FAQ calls JobNav Recruiter "the continuation" of Datapeople. The datapeople.io site, pricing form and G2 listing ("Datapeople (by Payscale)", 4.4 out of 5 across 323 reviews) are still live, so you will meet both names in the same procurement cycle.

The question this page answers: Textio or Datapeople, which one moves applicant quality and keeps your posts compliant with pay-transparency law?

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **Compliance is the lead product, not a feature.** The entry tier, Comply, is pay-transparency monitoring, anti-bias checks and an audit repository of what was posted when. JobNav Recruiter's pre-publish checks name California, New York, Colorado, Washington, Illinois and the EU Pay Transparency Directive. For a TA team posting in several of those, an audit trail is worth more than a better-sounding sentence.
- **It governs a job library, not one post at a time.** The Smart Editor, flagship-job templates and version control let one TA team set the approved text for 300 recurring roles and stop hiring managers from editing the salary range out of a copied post.
- **It reaches the posting screen in your ATS.** Native integrations cover Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Greenhouse, SmartRecruiters, iCIMS, Lever and Gr8People. Any other ATS gets the "Anywhere" browser extension or a daily auto-import.
- **The Payscale link is the new reason to buy.** JobNav Recruiter can pull posted ranges from Payscale's Peer data (4,500+ contributing organisations), so the range in the post matches the one compensation priced.

## Pricing

No public price. The pricing page lists three cumulative tiers on request: **Comply** (pay-transparency monitoring, anti-bias, audit repository), **Architect** (adds market data, ATS and HRIS integrations, First Draft writing AI, version control) and **Attract** (adds templates, collaboration and funnel insights). The FAQ says plans typically include unlimited hiring-manager collaborators, with no training or implementation fees.

The buyer data is thin. Vendr's marketplace page shows a median of about **$11,000 a year** from 3 purchases. Its buyer guide shows an average of $11,890 across 8 deals, with the top contract near $30,000. Treat $10,000 to $15,000 a year as the realistic band for one TA team on Comply or Architect, and anything above $25,000 as either Attract at enterprise scale or a first quote. That puts it at roughly half of Textio's $24,405 median contract (Vendr, February 2026).

## Best for

Heads of talent acquisition and TA operations leads at US or EU employers posting in several pay-transparency jurisdictions, with a library of recurring roles that hiring managers keep rewriting. Strongest fit: you already buy Payscale for compensation and want posted ranges to come from the same data.

**Not for you** if you post a few dozen roles a year in one state with no salary-range law. A Claude prompt with your style guide plus a required salary-range field in the ATS covers that for $0 marginal cost.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Textio](/en/tools/textio/)** — the category's other large incumbent. Its score predicts who will apply, including the demographic skew of the applicant pool, from hiring outcomes. Pick Textio when the goal is applicant mix and you hire mainly in English. Pick Datapeople when the goal is a defensible, audited posting process across jurisdictions.
- **Your ATS's native AI plus an LLM** — the fastest-growing substitute. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) and [Workday](/en/tools/workday/) both generate job descriptions in the editor, and [Claude](/en/tools/claude/) drafts well from a style guide (see the [JD writer skill](/en/workflows/jd-writer-claude-skill/)). Pick this when volume is low and no salary-range law applies to you. None of these keeps an audit trail of what was posted.
- **Ongig** — public pricing (Lite at $4,900 a year for 3 users and 100 jobs; job language translation on the Enterprise tier from $39,900). Pick Ongig when you need non-English job content or a price before the demo.

## Watch-outs

- **The brand is changing mid-contract.** You may sign for Datapeople and renew for JobNav Recruiter on different paper. *Guard:* ask Payscale in writing which SKU you are buying, whether the datapeople.io tiers will map one-to-one to JobNav Recruiter, and cap the renewal increase in the order form.
- **Compliance coverage is a claim, not a warranty.** Payscale's own release cites fines of up to $300,000 for pay-transparency violations without naming the law. *Guard:* get the list of jurisdictions the rules engine covers and its update cadence, and keep counsel's sign-off on the posting template. The tool flags; your legal team owns the decision.
- **The analytics only see what the ATS sends.** G2 reviewers report integration breaks, including with SmartRecruiters. *Guard:* during the pilot, reconcile funnel counts for 10 reqs against an ATS report before you trust any dashboard.
- **Bundling pressure.** Payscale's roadmap points at the Intelligence Cloud. *Guard:* buy JobNav Recruiter on its own term unless Ascent or JobNav already saves you money, and confirm it runs from your ATS without them, which Payscale says it does today.
