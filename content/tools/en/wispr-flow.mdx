## What it is

Wispr Flow is a voice-dictation layer that works in any text field on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android: you hold a key, talk, and Flow writes cleaned-up text into the email, CRM note, Slack message or prompt you were already in. Tanay Kothari and Sahaj Garg founded Wispr in 2021. In September 2026 it moved dictation onto Canto, its own speech model, which Wispr says beat Google, OpenAI, AssemblyAI and Deepgram on word error rate in its own test set of 2,300+ speakers — a vendor benchmark, not an independent one.

The reason it now lands on ops shortlists rather than personal-productivity lists is Notetaker, launched on August 5, 2026. Notetaker captures meeting audio from your own computer — no bot joins Zoom, Meet, Teams or a Slack huddle — and produces speaker-named transcripts and summaries, with meeting transcription in 21 languages as of the September release. It reached Windows on September 15 and iPhone on November 19, where it records in-person conversations only, not phone or video calls. In August Wispr raised a $280M Series B led by Menlo Ventures at a $2B valuation ($361M raised in total) and told Fortune that 100,000 businesses use Flow.

## Why it shows up in ops stacks

- **One tool replaces typing everywhere.** Reps, recruiters and CSMs spend their day writing call notes, follow-ups and ticket replies. Flow works inside whatever app they already use, with a shared team dictionary and snippets so product names, client names and canned answers come out spelled right.
- **Bot-free meeting notes on the same seat.** Pro includes unlimited Notetaker (subject to abuse limits), so a team buying dictation gets a [Granola](/en/tools/granola/)-style notetaker without a second vendor. Granola and [Otter](/en/tools/otter-ai/) imports exist for teams switching.
- **Meeting context reaches agents over MCP.** Transcripts connect to [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/), [Gemini](/en/tools/gemini/) or your own MCP client. The MCP connection exposes meeting transcripts only, not dictations — the right default, since dictation covers everything a user types by voice.
- **Admin controls arrived in 2026.** Org-wide Notetaker toggles, SCIM-driven joiner digests, department-level shared dictionaries, and Enterprise audit logs that record note access, including anonymous share-link views and MCP reads.

## Pricing

Per user per month from Wispr's pricing page, monthly vs annual billing (annual is 20% off):

- **Free — $0.** Dictation in 100+ languages with an unpublished weekly word limit, and limited Notetaker with 30-day meeting history on desktop.
- **Pro — $15 monthly, $12 annually.** Unlimited dictation and unlimited Notetaker, shared team workspace, dictionary and snippets, centralized billing, basic team analytics. No minimum seat count.
- **Growth — $23 / $18 dictation-only, or $33 / $26 with unlimited Notetaker.** Adds SSO with SAML, org-wide HIPAA enforcement with a signed BAA, multiple domains, per-user usage reporting and department admin. Model training is locked off for every member.
- **Enterprise — custom, annual only.** Adds SCIM, audit logs, MDM deployment, domain capture, free IT admin seats, the ability to block dictation in chosen apps, and data-retention controls.

Real-world band: a 50-person team pays $7,200 a year on annual Pro, $10,800 on dictation-only Growth, and $15,600 on Growth with Notetaker. Growth with Notetaker at $26 sits $12 above Granola Business ($14) and above Otter Business ($19.99 annually) — the premium buys dictation, which neither of those sells. Wispr states SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 for the whole service.

## Best for

- RevOps, recruiting and CS teams whose people write all day — CRM notes, candidate feedback, ticket replies — and who would take dictation and bot-free meeting notes from one vendor on one seat
- Legal ops and healthcare-adjacent teams that need a signed BAA and SAML SSO on a self-serve plan rather than a sales-gated tier
- Ops engineers who want meeting transcripts readable by Claude or an internal agent over MCP without buying a revenue-intelligence platform

## Alternatives — and when to pick them instead

- **Built-in OS dictation (Apple Dictation, Windows voice typing)** — the largest installed base by far, and free. Pick it when users dictate occasionally and the requirement is "no new vendor"; you give up cleanup, team dictionaries and admin control.
- **Dragon Professional** — the incumbent in professional dictation, now Microsoft-owned via Nuance, sold as a Windows-only perpetual license at around $699 through resellers. Pick it when dictation must run locally on Windows with deep custom vocabularies and a cloud processor is a non-starter.
- **Superwhisper** — the on-device challenger: about $84.99 a year or $249.99 lifetime, and it can run transcription models locally. Pick it for individual users whose audio must not leave the laptop; it has no comparable team admin layer.
- **[Granola](/en/tools/granola/)** — pick it when meeting notes are the job and dictation is not: $14 Business, a longer track record as a team notetaker, Spaces and a team API.
- **[Otter](/en/tools/otter-ai/) or [Fireflies](/en/tools/fireflies/)** — pick them when one connected calendar must cover every call org-wide without an install on each laptop. Both put a bot in the meeting, which is the trade.

## Watch-outs

- **Everything spoken is transcribed in the cloud.** Wispr's data-controls page states transcription always runs in the cloud; only Notetaker's audio capture is local. Third-party model providers sit under zero-data-retention agreements, but Notetaker's pre-meeting briefs are excluded from that, and usage statistics are collected regardless of settings. Guard: for privileged, PHI or deal-sensitive work, buy Growth so training is locked off and a BAA is signed, turn off Dictation Cloud Storage, and on Enterprise block dictation in the apps that hold regulated data.
- **No bot means no one is told.** Like Granola, Notetaker records from the device, so consent is your obligation in two-party-consent states such as California, Illinois and Pennsylvania and under GDPR. Guard: switch on the org setting that makes users confirm consent before recording starts, and add verbal-notice language to client and candidate call templates before rollout.
- **The pricing page disagrees with itself.** The Growth card lists SAML SSO and the HIPAA BAA, while the FAQ routes SSO and enforced HIPAA to Enterprise sales; the Free card says unlimited meetings while the FAQ cites a weekly meeting limit with no number. Guard: get the tier that carries SSO, the BAA and the training lock confirmed in the order form, not the web page.
- **Coverage is per-device and uneven by platform.** Notes exist only if an attendee ran Notetaker on their own machine, the iPhone app does not record calls, and Android has no Notetaker yet. Guard: scope the pilot to Mac and Windows users and audit install rates before you retire a bot-based notetaker.
