## What it is

Thought Industries is a customer-education platform built for teaching people outside your company: customers, partners, resellers, and paying learners. Its core design choice is multi-tenancy. One instance runs several branded learning portals, each with its own catalog, permissions, and storefront, so a software company can run a free customer academy, a gated partner certification program, and a paid public course catalog without buying three systems. Ecommerce, certification tracking, and SCORM support are native. The company was co-founded in Boston in 2013 by Barry Kelly, took a strategic growth investment from Luminate Capital Partners in September 2019, and has been run since 23 October 2025 by Irana Wasti, previously Chief Product Officer at BILL.

The reason it belongs on a 2026 Customer Success shortlist is ownership, not features. [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/) bought Northpass in July 2023 and Skilljar in April 2025, and on 27 May 2026 opened a Skilljar MCP server in beta as part of its agentic-stack release. The two best-known customer-education pure plays now sit inside a CS-platform vendor. If your academy should not be a Gainsight line item, the independent shortlist is Thought Industries and [Intellum](/en/tools/intellum/).

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Several academies, one contract.** Multi-tenant portals let you split audiences by segment, partner tier, or product line, each fully branded. This is the feature to buy it for. If you only ever need one academy for one audience, you are paying for architecture you will not use.
- **Monetization is built in.** Paid courses, subscriptions, and bundles run inside the platform. For a company where training is a revenue line, not only a retention cost, that removes a payments build from the rollout.
- **The April 2026 AI release moves learning out of the portal.** Thought Industries launched its AI Wave series on 22 April 2026 with two capabilities announced as available now. Conversational AI Learning answers learner questions in natural language from your approved content — videos, PDFs, articles, SCORM files — with citations back to the source, and can enroll the learner in a relevant course from the chat. Omnichannel Learning adds answer-engine optimization, publishing structured data so your course content can be cited by ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Perplexity. MongoDB, SOCi, Vetfolio, Skillpath, and IMA are the named early adopters.
- **A Claude plugin for authoring.** Thought Industries for Claude lets an admin browse the catalog, draft courses, and rewrite lessons from inside [Claude](/en/tools/claude/). Everything it produces stays a draft until a person approves it; the plugin does not publish on its own.
- **Learner data lands in Salesforce.** An AppExchange app lets you create, manage, and report on learner records inside [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/), next to account data.

## Pricing reality

No public prices. Contracts are quoted against active learners, tier, term, and add-ons. Vendr's anonymized purchase data puts the median annual contract at **$54,960**, across a range of $24,750 to $115,333, with tier bands:

- Growth — $30,000 to $60,000 per year, 500 to 2,000 learners
- Pro — $50,000 to $100,000 per year, 1,000 to 5,000 learners
- Enterprise — $100,000 to $200,000+ per year, 5,000+ learners

Add implementation at $5,000 to $30,000, overage at $2 to $10 per additional active learner per month, and optional premium support ($5,000 to $20,000) and advanced analytics ($3,000 to $15,000) a year. A realistic first-year number for a mid-market academy is **$45,000 to $90,000**. Multi-year commitments take 10% to 25% off the annual rate, and buyers who bring a competing quote report landing 15% to 30% below the opening number.

## Best for

A Customer Education or Customer Success leader at a B2B software company who needs more than one branded academy — customer and partner, or free and paid — wants course revenue handled in-platform, and does not want the education system of record owned by the CS-platform vendor.

**Not for you if** your program is a single external academy under roughly 500 learners: the Growth-tier floor of $30,000 does not pay back at that size, and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) help-center content with a [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/) or [Whatfix](/en/tools/whatfix/) in-app guide track answers most of the same questions for less. It is also the wrong buy if most of your learners are employees — Thought Industries is built for external audiences.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Skilljar by Gainsight** has the largest installed base in customer education. Pick it when you already run Gainsight CS and want course completions feeding health scores natively, or want an agent-ready LMS through the Skilljar MCP server, in open beta since May 2026. With Thought Industries you build that CS link yourself over the API and webhooks.
- **Docebo** is the broadest enterprise learning suite and the pick when employee learning is the main workload and customer education is secondary.
- **Intellum** is the other independent. Route on audience: Intellum argues for one platform spanning customers, partners, and employees; Thought Industries is narrower and deeper on external, multi-portal, monetized learning. Quote both — each is the other's sharpest negotiation lever.
- **WorkRamp**, now part of Learning Pool since October 2025, is the lighter option when you want a faster first launch and can give up multi-tenant depth and native commerce.

## Watch-outs

- **Reporting accuracy is the recurring reviewer complaint.** G2 reviewers say report data does not match what happened in the system, which forces manual validation. *Guard:* in the pilot, run one cohort through a course, then reconcile the platform's completion report against raw event data. If the numbers disagree, plan to pull data over the API into your BI tool and treat native reports as operational only.
- **The HubSpot connection is tracking scripts, not a sync.** Thought Industries documents HubSpot as tracking code pasted into its settings to fire events like course completions and ecommerce orders. *Guard:* if HubSpot is your CRM of record, scope a webhook or middleware build for contact-level learning data before you sign, and budget for it.
- **Active-learner overage can outrun the contract.** At $2 to $10 per extra active learner per month, a launch that doubles traffic lands on the renewal invoice. *Guard:* define "active learner" in the order form as someone who launched content in the period, contract 20% to 30% headroom over your forecast, and cap renewal uplift at a fixed percentage.
- **AI features and the Claude plugin need an itemized quote.** The April 2026 launches and the Claude plugin are sold through demos, with no public pricing or tier inclusion. *Guard:* have every AI capability named in the order form with its price and tier, and measure Conversational AI answers against 50 real learner questions from your support queue before the business case depends on them.
