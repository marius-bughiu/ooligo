## What it is

Tableau is the visual analytics platform Salesforce bought in 2019 and still sells on its own. Analysts build workbooks in Tableau Desktop or the browser, publish them to Tableau Cloud or a self-managed Tableau Server, and business users view, filter and subscribe. It connects to most warehouses and SaaS sources, including [Snowflake](/en/tools/snowflake/), [BigQuery](/en/tools/google-bigquery/) and [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/).

What changed in 2026 is the agent layer. At Tableau Conference on May 5, Tableau launched its Agentic Analytics Platform across Cloud, Server, Desktop and Next. The parts that matter to ops are Tableau Agent, which answers plain-language questions over published data, Pulse metrics with proactive alerts, Slackbot Q&A, and Agent Actions, which let a metric trigger a workflow such as opening a case. Tableau MCP connects [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), [ChatGPT](/en/tools/chatgpt/) and other MCP clients to governed Tableau data. It runs hosted at `mcp.tableau.com` with per-user OAuth, or self-hosted from the Apache-2.0 `tableau/tableau-mcp` repo.

Tableau Next is a separate product. It is an agentic analytics layer built on the Salesforce platform and Data 360, grounded in Tableau Semantics, and aimed at CRM users rather than analysts.

## Why it shows up in RevOps and CS stacks

- **The analysts already know it.** Many ops teams inherit a Tableau Server estate and a team trained on it. Pipeline, bookings and renewal dashboards often already live there.
- **Salesforce data is the shortest path.** Tableau Next reads CRM data through Data 360 without a separate pipeline, and its answers show up inside Salesforce and Slack.
- **It reaches people who never open a dashboard.** Pulse digests and Slackbot answers put a churn-risk or coverage metric in front of a CSM or AE where they already work.

## Pricing reality

Tableau Cloud lists per user per month, billed annually, with no monthly plans. Standard costs $75 for a Creator, $42 for an Explorer and $15 for a Viewer. Enterprise costs $115, $70 and $35, and adds Data Management, Advanced Management and up to 10 sites. Every deployment needs at least one Creator. Tableau Desktop Free Edition handles local authoring on files at no cost.

The agentic features are not in either of those editions. Tableau Agent in authoring, Prep and Pulse, plus Tableau Knowledge, require Tableau Cloud+, which is quote-only. The Tableau+ Bundle adds Tableau Next and Data 360 on top, also quote-only, and includes 250,000 Data 360 credits.

A September 30, 2026 update added capacity-based pricing. Creators and Explorers stay per user, and Viewer access, including AI agents and MCP traffic, is bought in shared blocks. You cannot mix capacity blocks with per-user Viewer licenses in one tenant.

Our example: a RevOps team with 3 Creators, 10 Explorers and 60 Viewers pays $18,540 a year on Standard and $37,740 on Enterprise at list, before Cloud+ for agents. Vendr's marketplace data puts the median buyer at about $29,400 a year, with about 13% average savings off quote.

**Do you get Tableau Next free with Agentforce?** Partly. The new Core, Advanced and Max editions of Sales, Service and Industries Clouds include the Tableau Next platform. The [Agentforce](/en/tools/salesforce-agentforce/) for Sales, Service and Industries add-ons include full-functionality Tableau Next Consumer licenses with Tableau Agent. Free, Starter and Pro Suites get limited Consumer licenses for prebuilt apps only. Consumers cannot build content, so someone still needs a Creator license. Standalone Tableau Next starts at $40 per user per month.

## Best for

RevOps and CS ops leaders whose company already runs Tableau, or runs Salesforce on the new editions, who need 50–500 people to view governed pipeline and account metrics and a small analyst team to build them.

Skip it if you are starting from zero BI with a cloud warehouse and no Tableau skills. A warehouse-native tool gets ops staff to answers faster. Skip it if users must write numbers back, such as quota or forecast overrides.

## Versus the alternatives

**Power BI** leads BI by installed base, at $14 per user per month for Pro. Pick it when you are a Microsoft shop or already own Microsoft 365 E5, where Pro is in the bundle. Pick Tableau when trained analysts do heavy visual exploration, or when Salesforce is the system of record.

**[Looker](/en/tools/looker/)** is Google's governed option. Pick it when shared metric definitions in code matter more than visual freedom, especially on BigQuery.

**Omni** is the fastest-growing entrant, raising a $120 million Series C at a $1.5 billion valuation in April 2026. Pick it for a Looker-style semantic layer with spreadsheet-style self-serve and no legacy estate to migrate.

**[Sigma](/en/tools/sigma/)** wins when ops staff must edit governed warehouse data. **[Hex](/en/tools/hex/)** wins when the work is exploratory analysis with an agent, not a dashboard catalog.

If none fit because nobody agrees what "pipeline" means, fix the model first. See [data warehouse vs CDP](/en/learn/data-warehouse-vs-cdp/).

## Watch-outs

- **The agents sit behind a quote.** A Standard or Enterprise pilot shows dashboards and Pulse, but not Tableau Agent in authoring or Tableau Knowledge. *Guard:* get the Cloud+ price as a written per-user delta over Enterprise before the pilot, and run the pilot on a Cloud+ trial site.
- **Licensing choices lock in per tenant.** Editions and licensing models cannot be mixed in one Tableau Cloud deployment, except Cloud+ with Tableau+ Bundle users. *Guard:* choose per-user or capacity Viewers before signing, and put external or embedded audiences in a separate tenant from day one.
- **"Included with Agentforce" usually means Consumer seats.** Data 360 and Flex credits can still apply to Tableau Next. *Guard:* confirm in writing which Tableau Next license type each Salesforce SKU carries. Check credit burn in Digital Wallet monthly for the first quarter.
- **MCP reach equals Viewer reach.** Every license type can use the hosted Tableau MCP, and Viewers can download full data. *Guard:* expose only certified published data sources to MCP users, apply row-level security there, and audit which sources agents query.
