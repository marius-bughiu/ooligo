## What it is

Common Paper is two things that are easy to confuse. The first is a set of free standard agreements, released under CC BY 4.0 and drafted by a committee of more than 40 attorneys from vendors, procurement teams, boutiques and large firms. There are 12 of them: Mutual NDA, Cloud Service Agreement (CSA), SLA, DPA, BAA, Terms of Service, Design Partner, Pilot, Professional Services, Software License, Partnership and an AI Addendum. Each one splits into a Cover Page that holds every negotiable variable and Standard Terms that nobody edits. The CSA is on version 3.0, which moved every variable onto the Cover Page.

The second is a paid product line from a small, YC-backed company (Winter 2023, Philadelphia, about $5M raised, led by Boldstart and Uncork). The contracting platform sends those agreements, collects e-signatures and tracks renewals. **Gerri**, sold separately, is an AI agent that reviews counterparty redlines or counterparty paper against a plain-language playbook, accepts or counters each change, writes the counter-language into the .docx, and sends anything outside the playbook to a named owner in legal, security, sales or finance for a human decision.

The specific job it does well: a one- or two-person legal team (or a founder with no lawyer) that wants NDAs, SaaS orders and DPAs to stop being negotiations. Standard paper removes most of the drafting, and Gerri handles the first pass on whatever the counterparty sends back.

## Why it shows up in legal ops stacks

- **The paper is the product.** Counterparties are more likely to accept a public, committee-drafted form than your own template, because they can read it once and reuse that reading. Common Paper claims a 47% reduction in counterparty paper and 63% of contracts signed within 24 hours. Both are vendor figures. The configuration logic sits in the Cover Page, which is the same move our [NDA playbook](/en/learn/nda-playbook/) recommends.
- **Benchmark data from real signed deals.** The 2026 SaaS Contract Benchmark covers 16,140 agreements from 2,223 companies signed between June 2025 and June 2026: 87% of CSAs auto-renew, 96% cap liability at 1x fees, and Delaware law is chosen in 74% of US CSAs. That gives a negotiator a market answer instead of an opinion. See [contract benchmarking](/en/learn/contract-benchmarking/) for how to use it.
- **Gerri escalates instead of guessing.** Items the playbook covers get resolved. Items it does not cover go to a person, with an approval step and an audit trail. Since November 2025 you can add custom instructions, run multiple playbooks, and since August 2026 have Gerri draft a playbook from your past contracts.
- **Built for AI workspaces.** Common Paper shipped an MCP server in April 2026, exposed Gerri through the API and MCP in May, and has an official Claude connector. Few contract tools at this price expose their data to an AI assistant at all.

## Pricing reality

Two separate bills.

- **Contracting platform:** Free sends 2 agreements a month per organization. Startup is $50 per user per month for 10 agreements per user per month. Growth is $100 per user per month for 30 per user and adds roles, payment links, the renewal workflow and Salesforce and HubSpot. Enterprise is custom and adds Okta, AD or SAML SSO and storage backup. No annual prices are published.
- **Gerri:** the first 3 contracts are free, then $500 a month for 10 files, with unlimited users, unlimited re-runs and up to 6 playbooks. Above 10 files a month is a sales conversation. Startups with 10 or fewer employees and no more than $5M raised get unpublished special pricing. In November 2025 the published plans were 5 files for $250 and 15 for $500, so the per-file price has risen.

In practice: a two-person legal team on Growth with Gerri pays about $700 a month, or $8,400 a year. That is roughly $50 per reviewed file. One seat of GC AI at its published $500 per month covers one lawyer for every kind of question but sends no agreements.

## Best for

Heads of legal and solo GCs at B2B SaaS companies under about 500 employees who sell on their own paper, want sales to send NDAs and order forms without a ticket, and need first-pass review of 10 or fewer counterparty contracts a month. It also fits founders who need a sound CSA and DPA before they hire counsel.

It is a poor fit for teams that live in Microsoft Word redlining enterprise MSAs all day, or for non-US contracts where the standards' assumptions do not hold.

## Alternatives and when to pick them

- **[Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/)** is the market-share leader in AI contract review, with 4,000 law firms and in-house teams and a $50M Series B in October 2025. Pick it when lawyers want review inside Word on every contract type, not a web upload.
- **[GC AI](/en/tools/gc-ai/)** is the fastest-growing entrant for small in-house teams ($60M Series B in November 2025). Pick it when contracts are only part of the job and the team also needs research, policy drafting and employment questions answered.
- **[LegalOn](/en/tools/legalon/)**: pick it when you want pre-built playbooks written by lawyers instead of writing your own.
- **[Juro](/en/tools/juro/)** or **[Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/)**: pick a full CLM when you pass about 50 contracts a month across several departments and need intake, approvals and a searchable repository more than you need standard paper. See [best CLM platforms](/en/vs/best-clm-platforms/).
- **Bonterms** is the other free, CC BY 4.0 standards project. Pick it if your counterparties already know it. It is a standards body with no platform or AI agent.

## Watch-outs

- **Very small vendor with almost no review record.** YC lists the team at 8 people, and there is no G2 or Capterra review base to triangulate against. Guard: keep the standards (they are CC BY and survive the vendor), export signed agreements and structured data every quarter with bulk PDF export, and avoid multi-year prepay.
- **Gerri reads files, not your Word session.** Redline review needs a .docx with tracked changes. Clean paper can be .docx or PDF, and there is no Word add-in. Guard: confirm during the 3 free reviews that counter-language lands cleanly in your counterparties' Word files, and keep Spellbook or GC AI in view if lawyers refuse to leave Word.
- **The 10-file ceiling arrives fast.** At $500 for 10 files, a busy quarter-end pushes you into custom pricing. Guard: count last quarter's inbound counterparty paper before buying, and get the overage price in writing.
- **US and SaaS assumptions.** The forms and benchmarks come from US SaaS deals, and Delaware dominates the data. Guard: have local counsel review the Cover Page defaults before using them for UK, EU or Asian counterparties, and add the DPA for GDPR deals.
