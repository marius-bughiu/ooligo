## What it is

eBrevia is contract AI for in-house legal teams, sold as three pieces that share one repository. **DraftPro** is a Microsoft Word add-in that checks a draft or counterparty paper against your playbooks, flags deviations and inserts suggested fallback language as tracked changes. **Command**, launched on 15 October 2026, is a contract-management layer on top: a searchable repository, AI extraction of clauses, obligations, renewal dates, pricing and rights, date and obligation alerts, and request tracking from drafting through signature. Underneath sit the older pieces — Contract Analyzer for bulk extraction across large document sets, **eBrevia Lens** for natural-language questions over one document or a whole repository, and **eBrevia Connect** for pushing extracted data into other systems. The company was founded in 2011 by Adam Nguyen and Jake Mundt to cut the cost of M&A due-diligence review, developed its first extraction technology with Columbia University, was bought by Donnelley Financial Solutions (DFIN) in December 2018, and was bought back by its founders in December 2023. The closest category comparison is [Kira Systems](/en/tools/kira-systems/) for the extraction heritage and [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) for the Word drafting surface.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It is pitched as CLM without the CLM project.** The Command launch line is CLM-style benefits without lengthy implementation, heavy customization or process redesign, and the vendor's stated rollout is training on day one, configuration in week one and scale by day 30. For a legal team that has watched a 6-month [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) or [LinkSquares](/en/tools/linksquares/) rollout stall, that is the reason it gets a demo slot.
- **One vendor covers pre-signature and post-signature.** DraftPro handles the Word negotiation; Command holds the signed estate and its obligations; the clause library in DraftPro searches agreements already stored in eBrevia by contract type, matter, jurisdiction or clause type. Most lightweight alternatives cover one side — Spellbook the draft, [ContractWorks](/en/tools/contractworks/) the repository.
- **Extraction depth from a decade of diligence work.** The bulk-review engine is the original product, marketed for 37 languages, with logos from Baker McKenzie, Norton Rose Fulbright, McDermott Will & Emery, PwC, KPMG and MUFG. That lineage matters when the first job is loading and tagging several thousand legacy contracts.

## Pricing reality

eBrevia publishes no prices; every product page routes to a demo. The only public figure is a directory listing on Capterra showing a $1,000 starting price on a per-user model, with zero user reviews behind it and no indication of term or tier — treat it as a stale placeholder, not a quote. There are no recorded G2 reviews and no published Vendr benchmark either, so you will not be able to triangulate a market rate before the first call. Plan the budget conversation around the shape you need rather than a number: DraftPro seats for the lawyers who negotiate, and Command for the repository and the business users who receive alerts. Ask whether Command is licensed per user, per contract volume or as a platform fee, because that choice decides whether pushing renewal alerts to sales, procurement and finance — the main point of buying it — costs nothing or multiplies the bill.

## Best for

Heads of legal and legal-ops managers in in-house teams of roughly 2-15 lawyers who need a searchable, AI-tagged contract repository with renewal and obligation alerts this quarter, and who also want playbook-driven redlining in Word, but who do not have the appetite or the budget for a full CLM implementation. The fit is strongest at companies with a few thousand legacy agreements sitting in SharePoint, iManage or a shared drive and no structured data on any of them.

## Not for

Teams whose real problem is high-volume intake and approval routing across sales and procurement — eBrevia's request tracking is new and narrow next to a workflow designer. Also not for teams that want their AI assistant to drive the contract system directly: there is no public MCP server, and integration runs through eBrevia Connect rather than a documented developer API.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Ironclad** — the share leader in mid-market CLM, with typical contracts running $50K-$500K+ a year and a real workflow designer. Pick Ironclad when intake, approvals and conditional routing across departments are the job. Pick eBrevia when the job is knowing what is in the signed contracts and redlining the next one, and a multi-quarter rollout is not acceptable.
- **LinkSquares** — the other large mid-market CLM with an analytics-first repository. Pick LinkSquares when you want one established platform for repository, intake and reporting with a bigger customer reference base; pick eBrevia when time-to-live is the deciding constraint.
- **Spellbook** — the fastest-growing entrant in Word-based contract AI, reporting 4,500+ legal teams and a $350M post-money valuation from its October 2025 Series B. Pick Spellbook when drafting and review in Word is the whole need and you want a 7-day trial before talking to sales. Pick eBrevia when the same purchase also has to deliver a repository with obligation tracking.

## Watch-outs

- **Command is brand new.** It launched on 15 October 2026, so there are no independent reviews and no reference customers yet for the CLM layer specifically — the logos on the site come from the extraction product. Guard: ask for two references using Command in production, and run a 30-day pilot on 200 of your own signed agreements that measures extraction accuracy on renewal date, notice period and liability cap before you sign a multi-year term.
- **The AI stack is undisclosed.** The security page lists SOC 1, SOC 2, GDPR and HIPAA compliance but does not name hosting regions, model providers or whether customer documents train the models. Guard: get the subprocessor list, hosting region and a no-training clause written into the DPA before any contract leaves your environment.
- **Ownership history means a smaller company than the logo wall suggests.** eBrevia spent five years inside DFIN and has been founder-owned again only since December 2023. Guard: confirm support coverage hours and the named implementation contact, and put a bulk export of the repository and extracted fields, in a documented format, into the order form so the contract data stays portable.
- **Vendor-stated speed claims.** The "90% faster review" figure and the day-30 scale timeline are eBrevia marketing, not audited results. Guard: baseline your current first-pass review time on 20 real counterparty drafts before the DraftPro pilot and compare on the same set.
