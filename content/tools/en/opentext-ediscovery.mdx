## What it is

OpenText eDiscovery is the product most buyers still call Axcelerate. OpenText renamed its legal line from Cloud Edition 24.4 onward, and its own release blog now writes "OpenText eDiscovery (Axcelerate)". It sits in a legal-tech family that also includes OpenText Investigation, OpenText Core Legal Hold and OpenText Core Insight. The forensics side used to be EnCase. OpenText retired that brand on February 4, 2026, and the technology now ships as OpenText Endpoint Investigator, OpenText Forensic and OpenText Endpoint Forensics & Response.

The AI layer is Aviator. Release 26.3 added eDiscovery Aviator Agents: a dashboard where a review team describes a multi-step search, review or analysis job in plain language and an agent runs it. Five templates are production-ready (Custom Agent, Query Support, Person Discovery, Research Assistant, Timeline Reconstruction). Nine more are beta, including Privilege Log, Privilege Discovery, DSAR Report and Data Breach Investigation. Release 26.4, published in early November 2026, added a Deep Review Agent in beta that analyzes a responsive set of up to 30,000 documents and writes one consolidated report. Every agent feature is labeled cloud only.

The question buyers are asking is whether the product line has a future at OpenText. The company sold eDOCS, its legal document-management unit, to [NetDocuments](/en/tools/netdocuments/) for $163M (closed January 12, 2026) and sold Vertica to Rocket Software for $150M (closed May 11, 2026). Ayman Antoun took over as CEO on April 20, 2026. In Q1 of fiscal 2027 OpenText moved about $116M of revenue out of "Core" into a non-core "Portfolio" category of mature, mostly on-premise products, and it says an Enterprise Assessment of its portfolio completes in December 2026 with announcements in early 2027. eDiscovery has not been put up for sale. OpenText has also not said where eDiscovery sits in that review.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Corporate investigations and discovery on one evidence chain.** OpenText is one of the few vendors selling endpoint forensics, investigation and review from the same company. When HR misconduct, insider-threat and litigation matters share custodians, collection does not have to be redone between teams.
- **Legal hold that fills a Microsoft gap.** Core Legal Hold is a SaaS [legal hold](/en/learn/legal-hold-process/) tool that preserves Microsoft 365 mailboxes in place through Purview and syncs custodians from Entra, Workday, ServiceNow, Google Workspace, Slack and Box. OpenText says Microsoft retired Purview's legacy legal hold communications in 2025 and pitches Core Legal Hold as the replacement. That is a vendor claim; check it against your own Purview tenant.
- **AI cost visibility by matter.** Release 26.3 added an Aviator token consumption dashboard by project and client, refreshed every 24 hours, and changed enrichment jobs to process one item per duplicate group to cut processing cost. Firms that bill AI review through to clients need exactly that.
- **Purview exports go straight in.** The 26.4 parser reads the Purview direct search export and can pull the cloud attachment as it existed when the message was sent. That is the version question that [EDRM](/en/learn/edrm-model/) collection disputes turn on.

## Pricing reality

OpenText publishes no rates. Its subscription collateral describes a fixed fee sized to your forecast discovery and investigation volume over two or three years. The only public per-unit figures are on the UK G-Cloud 13 framework from May 2022: £25 per GB ingested, £65 per GB published to analytics, and £16 per GB per month for active storage with unlimited review, with lower nearline and archive tiers. Treat those as a public-sector ceiling from four years ago, not a quote. They are still useful as an anchor: a 200 GB matter at those rates costs £18,000 to ingest and publish before anyone reviews a page.

Aviator usage is tracked in tokens by project and client, but OpenText publishes nothing on how that consumption is billed or whether the fixed fee includes it. Get the token rate, the included allowance and the overage price in writing before the first agent run.

## Best for

Litigation support and investigations leads at large enterprises and government bodies that already run OpenText for content or records, handle internal investigations and litigation with the same custodians, and want collection, hold and review from one vendor. It is also a fit for a law firm or service provider whose clients require OpenText.

It is the wrong pick for a mid-market legal team buying its first [eDiscovery](/en/learn/ediscovery/) platform. Contact-sales pricing, a multi-year fixed fee and an unresolved portfolio review are three reasons to start elsewhere.

## Versus the alternatives

**[Relativity](/en/tools/relativity/)** is the share leader and the platform outside counsel already know. Pick it when matters are large, outsourced and review-heavy. Pick OpenText when investigations start in-house on endpoints and the same team carries them into litigation.

**[Exterro](/en/tools/exterro/)** is the other incumbent with forensics in the box (FTK), plus legal hold and privacy requests on one data map. Pick Exterro when privacy requests and subpoena volume are the driver. Pick OpenText when the company already owns OpenText content management and procurement wants one vendor.

**[Everlaw](/en/tools/everlaw/)** is the fastest-growing entrant and made generative review free in 2026 rather than metering it. Pick Everlaw when review speed and usability are the complaint and you do not need forensics.

**Microsoft Purview eDiscovery** is the do-nothing option for an all-Microsoft 365 company. Start there and upgrade only when volume or defensibility outgrows it; [Purview vs Relativity](/en/vs/microsoft-purview-ediscovery-vs-relativity/) covers that line. [Reveal](/en/tools/reveal/) is the pick for a mid-market team that wants AI review without an enterprise contract.

If none fit, the problem is usually upstream: a [collection workflow](/en/workflows/evidence-collection-ediscovery-n8n/) and a clear hold process cut volume before any review platform bills on it. The [eDiscovery platform roundup](/en/vs/best-ediscovery-platforms/) ranks the full field.

## Watch-outs

- **Portfolio risk is live, not theoretical.** OpenText has divested two units in 2026, reclassified $116M of revenue as non-core, and will announce its portfolio plan in early 2027. *Guard:* do not sign a term past 24 months before that announcement. Negotiate a change-of-control clause that lets you exit without penalty if the eDiscovery line is sold, plus a data-export obligation in load-file format at no charge.
- **The agents are new, cloud only and partly beta.** Nine of the 26.3 templates and the 26.4 Deep Review Agent are labeled beta, and none apply to on-premise deployments. *Guard:* if you run on-premise or private cloud, price the migration to OpenText cloud separately before counting on any Aviator feature. Pilot one production-ready template on a closed matter with known results before using it on a live one.
- **AI consumption is outside the published fee structure.** Nothing public says a fixed platform fee caps Aviator token use. *Guard:* set a per-matter token budget, have the dashboard reviewed weekly by the matter owner, and contract a pre-agreed overage rate instead of list price.
- **Naming churn hides what you are buying.** Axcelerate, Investigation, Core Legal Hold, EnCase and its three successors are separate SKUs. *Guard:* ask for a quote that lists each module under its current name and SKU, and confirm which ones share a single evidence store in your tenant.
