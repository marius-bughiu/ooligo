[Pipedrive](/en/tools/pipedrive/) and [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/) are usually the first two CRMs on an SMB sales leader's shortlist, but they are different kinds of product. Pipedrive is a sales CRM: one product built around a drag-and-drop pipeline, priced per seat with no platform fee and no free plan. HubSpot is a platform: Sales Hub, Marketing Hub, Service Hub and Data Hub share one record graph, and there is a free tier underneath. So this is not a feature-by-feature contest. The question is scope. Are you buying a tool to move deals through stages, or a system of record that sales, marketing and service will run together?

That question decides most purchases, and the price gap makes it sharper. If you only need sales pipeline management (logging activity, forecasting, automating the obvious handoffs), Pipedrive covers it at Growth for $39/seat/month on annual billing. If marketing automation, forms, nurture or support ticketing will be in scope within a year, HubSpot's single graph is the more durable choice, even though its jump to the automation tier is steep. Two things changed in 2026 that older comparisons miss. Both vendors now ship an official MCP server. And Pipedrive is in the middle of the largest restructuring in its history.

## Where Pipedrive wins

- **Automation and forecasting for less than half the price.** Two-way email sync, automations, Sequences and revenue forecasting all sit in Growth at $39/seat/month annual ($49 monthly). HubSpot puts sequences and forecasting in Sales Hub Professional, at $90/seat/month annual or $100 monthly plus a required $1,500 onboarding fee. For a sales-only motion, Pipedrive gets you the same core capability for well under half the cost.
- **Reps adopt it in days.** The pipeline board is the main screen, not a report. Every card shows stage, value and next activity, so a team under 30 reps can go live within a week without an admin. Higher voluntary logging means clean data, and clean data means reporting you can use from the first month.
- **The cheapest CRM to put behind an AI assistant.** Pipedrive's MCP server launched on 30 June 2026 and is included on every plan, starting with Lite at $14/seat. Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor can read and update deals, contacts, organizations and leads and log activities and notes, within the connected user's permissions. Nova, the AI meeting assistant that went generally available on 16 September 2026, is also included on all plans at no extra cost. The REST API is open from Lite up as well.

## Where HubSpot wins

- **One record graph across sales, marketing and service.** Companies, contacts, deals and tickets live in one schema. If you run nurture campaigns, landing pages, forms or a support queue, HubSpot does it natively. Pipedrive's answer is Campaigns, which is basic email, plus Outfunnel, the marketing-automation app it acquired in August 2026 and has not yet folded into the core product.
- **A free on-ramp and a stable vendor.** The Free CRM costs $0 for up to two users, and Starter is $20/seat month-to-month, with a $7 annual rate offered to new customers. You can stand up a working CRM before spending anything and upgrade only the hub you outgrow. HubSpot is also not reorganizing itself while you sign.
- **Depth as you scale.** HubSpot's remote MCP server went GA on 13 April 2026, with write access to contacts, companies, deals, tickets, line items and products. Add the app marketplace, Data Hub for programmable sync, and Breeze agents for prospecting, customer service and data work. HubSpot keeps up as you pass $10M ARR, which is where Pipedrive's reporting and automation start to feel thin.

## Pricing reality

Pipedrive sells four plans per seat per month, as of November 2026: Lite **$14** annual / **$24** monthly, Growth **$39** / **$49**, Premium **$59** / **$79**, Ultimate **$79** / **$99**. There is no free plan and no onboarding fee, and the trial is 14 days. Premium now bundles LeadBooster (chatbot, live chat, web forms), Smart Docs (quotes and e-signature) and Projects. That changes the add-on math. On Growth, LeadBooster and Smart Docs cost $32.50/month each for the whole company, so $65/month total, while Premium costs $20/seat/month more. Above three seats, Growth plus those two add-ons is cheaper than Premium. Move up only if you need Premium's permission sets, required fields or AI email writing.

HubSpot Sales Hub, checked 7 November 2026: Free **$0** (up to 2 users), Starter **$20/seat/month** month-to-month (500 HubSpot Credits), Professional **$90/seat/month** annual or **$100** monthly with a required **$1,500** onboarding fee (3,000 credits), and Enterprise **$150/seat/month** with a required **$3,500** onboarding fee (5,000 credits). Starter includes up to 50 workflows with restricted triggers. Sequences and forecasting start at Professional. Credits are the meter for Breeze agents; extra credits cost $0.010 each, and the allowance resets monthly with no rollover. Teammates who only need CRM access can take Core Seats ($50/month on Professional) or free view-only seats rather than full Sales Seats.

Compare the plans where the features match. A 10-rep team that needs automation and forecasting pays **$4,680/year** on Pipedrive Growth (10 × $39 × 12), or **$5,460** with LeadBooster and Smart Docs added. The HubSpot equivalent is Sales Hub Professional: **$10,800/year** plus the **$1,500** onboarding, so **$12,300 in year one**. That is 2.6× Pipedrive Growth in year one and 2.3× from year two. Against Pipedrive Premium ($7,080/year), the gap narrows to 1.7×. The trap is the jump from Starter to Professional. HubSpot Starter at $20/seat looks cheaper than Pipedrive Growth, but it has no sequences and no forecasting, and when you need them the seat price goes up 5× before onboarding. Price the tier that has the features you need, not the entry tier.

## Implementation and risk

The two risks are different. Pipedrive's risk is the vendor and the ceiling. On 29 October 2026 Pipedrive announced cuts affecting the "majority of the roles", and Estonian press reported 267 jobs cut in Tallinn and Tartu, a figure Pipedrive has not confirmed. Prices have also moved twice in 16 months: Growth went from $24 to $39 and Premium from $49 to $59. Automations allow 3 if/else steps on Growth, and Premium caps active automations at 150. Guard: get the support SLA and your account contact in writing, cap renewal increases at 5–7% in the order form, prefer annual terms over a deep multi-year discount until the new structure has shipped a quarter of releases, and map your three most complex automations against the builder during the trial.

HubSpot's risk is the cost curve. Starter brings teams in, then the features they assumed were included force the jump to Professional, and both seats and credits grow with headcount. Programmable automation (custom code actions and webhook triggers) is a separate Data Hub Professional purchase at $720/month annual. Guard: model year-one cost at Professional with onboarding before you commit based on the Starter price, run any Breeze agent on one segment for a full billing cycle before enabling it portal-wide, and audit seat types at every renewal.

On both, the native AI trails specialist tools. Pair [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) for enrichment or [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) for conversations rather than relying on Nova or Breeze for anything a revenue number depends on.

## Verdict

- **Pick Pipedrive** when you are a sales-led SMB with 2–50 reps buying a pure sales CRM, you want automation, forecasting and an MCP-connected assistant without HubSpot's Professional price and onboarding fee, you have no dedicated admin, and fast adoption matters more than platform breadth.
- **Pick HubSpot** when marketing automation, forms, nurture or support ticketing share the same team and budget as sales, when you want a free tier to start from, or when you are standardizing a $1–50M ARR GTM on one record graph and need reporting depth Pipedrive does not reach. Also pick HubSpot when vendor stability is a board-level requirement this year.
- **Pick neither** if you sell complex multi-stakeholder enterprise deals that need account hierarchies, deep custom objects or CPQ; [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/) earns its overhead there. If you are an API-first or PLG team that wants a custom data model without a consultant, evaluate [Attio](/en/tools/attio/) instead. If the budget is the binding constraint, [Zoho CRM](/en/tools/zoho-crm/) has a free plan for three users.

Default pick: **Pipedrive**, for a sales-first SMB torn between these two and optimizing for cost and adoption. The per-seat economics and fast rollout still make it the cleaner choice, and you can add marketing tooling later. Sign annually, not multi-year, while the restructuring plays out. Switch to HubSpot as soon as marketing or service is genuinely in scope: one record graph and a free starting tier beat stitching Pipedrive add-ons to a separate marketing platform. For the full reasoning on each, see [Pipedrive](/en/tools/pipedrive/) and [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/).
