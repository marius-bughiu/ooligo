[11x](/en/tools/11x/) and [Artisan](/en/tools/artisan/) are the two names that come up whenever a RevOps leader asks about AI SDR platforms. Both promise to automate the outbound prospecting motion — find prospects, research them, write and send sequences, handle replies, and book meetings — without a human SDR in the loop. The real split is in scope and architecture: 11x is building a multi-agent workforce (Alice for outbound, Julian for inbound voice, plus others), while Artisan is betting on a single deeply capable agent — Ava — with email and LinkedIn as the primary channels.

Neither platform is cheap or fast to deploy. Choose carefully; annual commitments are the norm.

## Where 11x wins

- **Multi-agent suite depth.** 11x offers Alice (outbound SDR), Julian (AI inbound voice agent), James (inbound SDR), and Serena (RevOps agent). If you need to cover inbound qualification via phone and AI-driven outbound from the same vendor, 11x has the product surface to do it. Artisan's current offering centers on Ava; phone is not on the current channel list.
- **Data sourcing model.** 11x does not ship its own proprietary contact database; it integrates third-party data providers, so coverage depends on the data contracts you bring or buy. Artisan, by contrast, includes a 300M+ contact database in the platform. The practical implication is about how you source records, not a fixed size advantage for 11x — any "larger database" claim for 11x reflects the third-party data it connects to, not an owned asset.
- **Phone outreach for the AI SDR motion.** Alice handles email and LinkedIn; Julian handles inbound voice. For teams that want AI-driven phone touchpoints in the outbound sequence — not just voicemail drops, but actual AI voice calls — 11x has a native path. Artisan currently does not.
- **Enterprise GTM integrations.** 11x holds SOC 2 Type II certification and supports deeper integrations across enterprise GTM tools. For RevOps teams at 200+ rep companies with complex Salesforce environments and compliance requirements, 11x's enterprise posture is more mature.

## Where Artisan wins

- **Built-in data reduces stack cost.** Ava's 300M+ contact database is included, which means teams don't need a separate ZoomInfo, Apollo, or Lusha contract to feed the agent. 11x relies on third-party data providers, so you're paying for 11x plus data on top.
- **Faster onboarding.** Artisan targets a 1-week onboarding window; 11x typically runs 4 weeks. For RevOps teams that can't absorb a month-long ramp, Artisan's faster time-to-sequence matters.
- **Email-first focus that benefits deliverability.** Artisan's Ava is an email and LinkedIn specialist. The platform invests heavily in warmup, inbox health monitoring, and dynamic send limits. 11x is expanding channel coverage; Artisan's email infrastructure is more mature at this point.
- **Entry price point.** Artisan is accessible from approximately $2,000/month — a lower floor than 11x's ~$5,000/month starting point. For teams that want to validate AI SDR ROI before committing to the higher price tier, Artisan's entry price is more forgiving.
- **Intent-based timing.** Ava detects buying signals — funding rounds, leadership changes, product launches — and optimizes send timing around those triggers. 11x has signal coverage, but Artisan's signal-to-sequence automation is more tightly integrated at the Ava layer.

## Pricing reality

11x: ~$5,000/month, annual commitment standard — $50,000-$60,000/year for the Alice package covering approximately 3,000 contacts/month. Median contract value per Vendr benchmark data is $45,000-$65,000/year. Multi-year commitments (24 months) are now offered as a discount mechanism — expect 11x's sales team to lead with those.

Artisan (Ava): $2,000-$5,000+/month on custom, volume-based pricing — $24,000-$86,000/year depending on lead volume tier. A lower entry floor, but the ceiling is comparable. Neither vendor publishes exact per-seat pricing; both require a sales conversation.

The cost gap at entry is real: 11x starts at roughly 2.5× Artisan's minimum. At mid-tier volumes, pricing converges. Note that Artisan's total cost of ownership is lower when you factor out the third-party data contract that 11x requires.

## Implementation effort

11x: 4-week onboarding standard. ICP configuration, sequence setup, CRM integration, Alice training on your product and positioning. The multi-agent surface means more configuration, but also more coverage once live.

Artisan: 1-week target onboarding. Ava is designed to get operational faster. The tradeoff is narrower initial scope — you're launching email + LinkedIn, not phone + inbound + RevOps coverage from day one.

Both require annual commitment. Neither offers meaningful month-to-month flexibility.

## Verdict

- **Pick 11x** when you need a multi-agent outbound motion that spans email, LinkedIn, and inbound voice qualification; you're at a company with 100+ reps where SOC 2 and enterprise CRM compliance requirements are non-negotiable; you already have a data provider you want the agent to draw from rather than a bundled database; or you want a single vendor to cover multiple outbound and inbound AI functions rather than assembling point solutions.
- **Pick Artisan** when you want to start with email and LinkedIn AI automation at a lower initial commitment, your team can absorb a 1-week onboarding rather than 4, you want to avoid a separate third-party data contract (Ava's database is included), or you're validating AI SDR ROI before scaling up budget.
- **Pick neither** if your outbound motion is under 500 contacts per month — at that volume, an AI SDR platform is the wrong price tier. Clay + Apollo or Amplemarket + a human SDR running tight sequences covers the same ground at a fraction of the cost, and you maintain more control over message quality during the early-stage ICP iteration.

Default pick: Artisan. The lower entry floor, faster onboarding, and included database make it the lower-risk starting point for teams new to AI SDR platforms. Move to 11x when your use case genuinely requires voice agents or multi-agent GTM coverage that goes beyond email and LinkedIn.
