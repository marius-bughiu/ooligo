## What it is

LegalSifter is a Pittsburgh contract-AI company, founded in 2013 out of Carnegie Mellon, that now sells two products. **ReviewPro** is a pre-signature review tool: an add-in for Microsoft Word and Google Docs that reads counterparty paper against a playbook, flags missing or off-standard terms, and proposes redlines. It ships with 100+ standard playbooks, a Playbook Builder that generates playbooks from your templates, past redlines or policy documents, an AI Assistant for chatting with the contract and drafting new terms, plus a light repository and an intake queue with a Kanban board. LegalSifter describes the engine as hybrid deterministic code plus generative AI, and its security page says the generative features run on OpenAI under OpenAI's business terms.

**Contract Logix** is a full contract lifecycle management platform (repository, workflow, creation, negotiation, dashboards) founded in 2006 and positioned at regulated industries. LegalSifter bought it in October 2024, a year after buying Akorda, a San Francisco CLM, in September 2023. Akorda no longer appears under its own name on either site. Carrick Capital Partners took a growth stake in LegalSifter in March 2023 and is still the backer; there is no sale or shutdown on record as of November 2026.

The closest category comparison for ReviewPro is [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) or [LegalOn](/en/tools/legalon/). The closest for Contract Logix is a mid-market CLM such as [Juro](/en/tools/juro/) or [LinkSquares](/en/tools/linksquares/).

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It is where LawGeex's enterprise customers went.** In September 2023 LegalSifter took over a group of [LawGeex](/en/tools/lawgeex/) enterprise clients, 17 of them Global 2000. Robin AI, which bought the LawGeex technology, has since collapsed, so LegalSifter is the one live company that inherited LawGeex customer relationships.
- **It publishes prices.** Almost every playbook-review tool in this category, Spellbook and LegalOn included, now quotes through sales. ReviewPro's pricing page lists every tier, which makes it the easiest tool in the segment to budget before a demo.
- **People sit behind the software.** LegalSifter calls its model "Combined Intelligence": its playbook architects are lawyers who build your playbook, and it sells a contract redlining service where its staff mark up the contract for you. That suits a two-person legal team that wants output, not a configuration project.

## Pricing reality

All plans are annual and list prices are on the page. **Consumption plans** include unlimited seats: Basic $2,750 for 50 contracts ($55 each), Team $7,500 for 150 ($50), Business $18,000 for 400 ($45), Enterprise $40,000 for 1,000 ($40). Extra contracts are billed at the tier's per-contract rate. **Seat plans** include unlimited contracts: $6,000 per seat per year for one user, $4,500 per seat (3-seat minimum, $13,500), $4,000 (8 seats, $32,000), $3,500 (15 seats, $52,500).

The choice between the two is a volume question. A three-lawyer team reviewing 400 contracts a year pays $18,000 on consumption or $13,500 on seats, so seats win. The same team reviewing 100 contracts pays $7,500 on the consumption Team tier, which wins by $6,000. Run your own last twelve months of counterparty paper through that math before the call. Custom playbook work is a separate one-time fee on top, and that is where quotes grow. The signup page still shows an older ladder of $80, $72 and $65 per contract; the pricing page is the current one. Contract Logix publishes no price at all.

## Best for

In-house legal teams of one to ten lawyers that review a steady flow of inbound NDAs, vendor agreements and MSAs in Word, want a playbook enforced on every one, and want a vendor that can build the playbook or redline overflow for them. It is also the natural first call for former LawGeex customers who never moved.

**Not for** law firms doing drafting-heavy transactional work (Spellbook fits that job better), teams that want software to run the negotiation itself ([Luminance](/en/tools/luminance/) is built for that), or teams that want one modern CLM with review built in: ReviewPro and Contract Logix are two products on two clouds (AWS and Azure) sold under one demo form.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/)** is the volume leader in Word-native contract AI. Pick it when lawyers draft from their own precedent more than they review counterparty paper, or when you are a firm rather than a legal department.
- **[LegalOn](/en/tools/legalon/)** is the stronger pure-software reviewer, with attorney-written playbooks and a Triage Agent that auto-approves low-risk agreements. Pick it when you want auto-approval and do not need a services layer.
- **[Ivo](/en/tools/ivo/)** is the newer in-house review platform that most mid-market shortlists now include. Pick it for a bigger legal team that wants search across its executed contracts as well as review.
- **[Juro](/en/tools/juro/), [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/) and [LinkSquares](/en/tools/linksquares/)** are the CLM alternatives to Contract Logix. Pick one of them when the business side self-serves its own contracts and review is one step in a single platform. Pick Contract Logix when you sit in a regulated industry and need HIPAA- and FISMA-ready hosting.

## Watch-outs

- **"95% accuracy" is the vendor's number.** *Guard:* run the 7-day free trial on 30 of your own live agreements and have a senior reviewer grade every flag and redline before you sign the annual contract.
- **Your contracts reach OpenAI.** The generative features run on OpenAI's business terms; customer data trains LegalSifter's own models only on opt-in. *Guard:* get the subprocessor list and a written no-training clause into the order form, and confirm in writing that the training opt-in stays off for your account.
- **Two products, two clouds, one sales team.** ReviewPro on AWS and Contract Logix on Azure run on separate clouds, and LegalSifter.s product pages describe no built-in sync between them. *Guard:* if you buy both, write the review-to-repository sync into the statement of work with a named owner and a test date, rather than assuming it exists.
- **The playbook is the asset.** LawGeex customers lost theirs when that platform shut down. *Guard:* require an export of every playbook rule and clause position in a readable format on demand, and test the export during the trial.
