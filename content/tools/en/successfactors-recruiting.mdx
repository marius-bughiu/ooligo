## What it is

SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting is the applicant tracking module inside SAP's HXM suite: requisitions, candidate pipelines, offer approvals, and the Recruiting Marketing side that runs the careers site through Career Site Builder. It reads the worker record from Employee Central and hands new hires to SuccessFactors Onboarding. It is the SAP analog of [Workday](/en/tools/workday/) Recruiting, and it gets bought for the same reason: the requisition, the position budget, and the org chart already live in the HR system of record.

One fact reorders every evaluation of it in 2026. SAP has named its replacement. SmartRecruiters, which SAP acquired in September 2025, was released as SmartRecruiters for SAP SuccessFactors on March 3, 2026, and SAP's own recruiting topic page states that as innovation moves to that product, the existing Recruiting module transitions to a "Core Stability Phase."

## What Core Stability Phase means for a buyer

Maintenance, not roadmap. SAP has not announced a forced migration and has not published an end-of-life date. SuccessFactors president Daniel Beck told CIO that customers get three to five years to complete the migration, and that SAP would signal a migration contract for existing clients by the end of Q1 2026. Migration tooling was slated for early 2026, and Phase 2 of the integration, around June 2026, automates candidate-to-hire conversion into Employee Central and Onboarding and supports running SmartRecruiters and SuccessFactors Recruiting side by side for a phased cutover.

The 1H 2026 release shows what core stability produces. Recruiting got OnChange business rules that fire when a recruiter changes candidate status, automatic requisition closure when a candidate reaches Hired, and styling options in Career Site Builder. The one new candidate-facing capability is an embed of SmartRecruiters' Winston chat on the SuccessFactors career site — the successor product arriving inside the incumbent.

## Pricing reality

SAP publishes no price for the module; its HCM packages page lists packages with no numbers. Every figure here is a third-party negotiation benchmark, not vendor-published MSRP. Employee Central, which Recruiting requires, is benchmarked at $6–8 PEPM list and $4.50–6.00 PEPM negotiated at 5,000+ employees. Each talent module, Recruiting included, adds $2–8 PEPM before 20–30% discounting, and a negotiated full HXM suite lands at $28–38 PEPM at enterprise scale. Bundle discounts of 15–28% apply once three or more modules are taken together, escalators run 3–5% a year, and implementation partners charge 100–125% of annual license fees, with multi-country programs passing $2M.

The commercial mechanic matters more than the rate. SuccessFactors bills on the employees you have, not on the people who open the module. At 5,000 employees and $3 PEPM, Recruiting costs roughly $180K a year whether 12 recruiters use it or 60. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) and [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) bill per user. If you staff fewer than one recruiter per 200 employees, that difference is the largest line in the comparison, and negotiation does not fix it because the meter is headcount. Negotiate the terms that do move: annual employee true-downs, a step-down clause, an inflation cap at 4%, and a three-year rather than five-year commitment.

## Best for

Enterprise TA leaders at 5,000+ employees already live on Employee Central and Onboarding, running a stable hiring program, whose real question for the next 24 months is whether to keep what works while the migration path matures. Keeping it is defensible: no forced migration announced, stable behavior, and cost already sunk into a suite contract.

Do not buy it new. If you are selecting an ATS in 2026 and you run SAP, the product SAP is building is SmartRecruiters, and implementing the frozen module means paying for a rollout you redo inside the three-to-five-year window. If you do not run Employee Central, it is not a candidate at all — standalone, it loses to every modern ATS on recruiter speed and configuration turnaround.

## Versus the alternatives

[SmartRecruiters](/en/tools/smartrecruiters/) is the successor and the default for anyone staying on SAP: same vendor, native Employee Central and Onboarding flows, and the roadmap. [Workday](/en/tools/workday/) Recruiting is the other HRIS-bundled incumbent — pick it only if you are re-platforming the HRIS itself, because buying the suite to get an ATS is a large implementation aimed at a small problem. [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/) wins when structured hiring quality is the goal and you accept an HRIS integration instead of suite adjacency; [SmartRecruiters vs Greenhouse](/en/vs/smartrecruiters-vs-greenhouse/) is the same decision one layer down. [Ashby](/en/tools/ashby/) is the fastest-growing entrant moving upmarket, and the right pick at 200–2,000 employees where reporting and scheduling in one product beats proximity to the HR record.

## Watch-outs

- **Migration is a reimplementation, not an export.** Business rules, form configurations, SSO wiring, and the Employee Central and Onboarding integrations get rebuilt rather than transferred. Guard: get a partner-scoped migration quote before you sign the next renewal, and price that work inside the same term instead of after it.
- **The careers site is the largest single migration item.** SmartRecruiters brings its own career site, and teams that want one careers site end up on a big-bang cutover rather than a phased one. Guard: inventory Career Site Builder pages, localizations, and job-board integrations now, and treat the site as its own cutover date.
- **Deprecations still land during core stability.** The Top Jobs and View All Jobs link types in Career Site Builder ended maintenance on November 15, 2024 and are scheduled for deletion on May 14, 2027. Guard: audit career site link types against the deprecation list each release cycle rather than at cutover.
- **The commercial path is announced, not contracted.** "Three to five years" and "no forced migration" are statements from SAP leadership, not clauses in your agreement. Guard: ask your account team in writing for the migration contract terms SAP said it would signal, and get the no-forced-migration position into the renewal before agreeing to a multi-year commit.
