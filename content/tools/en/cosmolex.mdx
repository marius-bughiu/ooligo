## What it is

CosmoLex is cloud practice management for solo and small US law firms, built around one claim: your trust accounting and your firm's own books live in the same system as your matters, time and invoices. It has sold to law firms since 2013, was bought by Tabs3 in 2018, and is now one of the ProfitSolv brands alongside Tabs3, Rocket Matter and TimeSolv. Matter management, time and expense tracking, invoicing, online card and eCheck payments, a client portal and trust ledgers with compliance reporting are in every plan.

The question most buyers bring is whether they still need QuickBooks. The answer depends on the plan. CosmoLex's own plan table puts **General Ledger & Payables** and **bank feeds** on the top Pro tier only. On the cheaper tiers you get trust accounting and bank reconciliation inside CosmoLex, and you keep a separate ledger, usually QuickBooks Online, for operating and business accounting.

On 2026-08-13 CosmoLex added its first AI features (vendor press release): an AI Intake Form Builder that generates intake forms from a prompt or converts existing PDF and Word forms, AI document summaries for files stored in CosmoLex's own document storage, and plain-English filters over matters, invoices and time entries. These help with intake and admin work, not with legal drafting or research.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **Trust compliance is the core strength.** Reviewers on G2 and Capterra consistently praise how hard it is to break trust rules in CosmoLex: client ledgers, trust-to-operating transfers and three-way reconciliation reports run in the same place billing happens. For a solo lawyer who has been reconciling IOLTA in a spreadsheet, that is the reason to buy.
- **One system instead of two, on Pro.** A Pro firm can drop QuickBooks: billing posts to the general ledger, payables live next to invoices, and bank feeds pull transactions in. That removes the monthly export, the double entry and the reconciliation drift between a practice management tool and an outside ledger.
- **A real QuickBooks bridge on Core.** Firms that want their CPA to keep working in QuickBooks Online can connect it on Core or Pro. The sync runs one way, CosmoLex to QuickBooks, and switching it on turns off CosmoLex's built-in business accounting and matter budgets.
- **Published prices.** Unlike Smokeball or Centerbase, CosmoLex lists its rates, offers a 10-day trial with no card, and includes live onboarding with guided data migration in the plan rather than as a quoted project.

## Pricing reality

cosmolex.com/pricing, checked 2026-11-20, per user per month:

- **Launch — $49.** Matters, time and invoicing, online payments, trust accounting and ledgers, compliance reporting, client portal. No LEDES or split billing, no bank reconciliation, no AI features.
- **Core — $99.** Adds LEDES and split billing, bank reconciliation, matter budgets, intake, document assembly, dashboards, the three AI features and the QuickBooks Online integration.
- **Pro — $129.** Adds General Ledger & Payables, bank feeds, advanced compliance reporting, intake workflow automation, calendar sync with Outlook and Google, and the open API.

The page's billing toggle says yearly billing saves $120 per user per year; confirm whether your quote is the monthly or yearly rate. CRM, Websites, Google Ads and LexShare/LexSign file sharing and e-signature are add-ons with no published price. Review aggregators still quote an older $119/$139 two-plan ladder, so budget off the live page.

In practice, five lawyers on Pro pay $7,740 a year at list. The same firm on Core pays $5,940, plus QuickBooks Online Plus at about $140 a month ($1,680 a year after Intuit's August 2026 increase), for $7,620. The two routes cost almost the same, which is the useful finding: if your firm doesn't need a CPA working inside QuickBooks, Pro replaces it for roughly the price of keeping it. For comparison, five lawyers on [PracticePanther](/en/tools/practicepanther/) Business Pro, its accounting tier, pay $6,840 a year on annual billing.

## Best for

Solo lawyers and firm owners at US practices of roughly 1-10 attorneys who keep their own books, or have one bookkeeper, and want trust accounting, operating accounting and billing in one system they can trust at audit time. The strongest fit is a general practice, estate planning or real estate firm with heavy trust activity and simple, hourly or flat-fee billing.

**Not for:** firms whose outside CPA insists on QuickBooks (pick Core with the integration, or a lighter tool), litigation-heavy firms that need deep case workflow, and firms above about 25 timekeepers, where [Centerbase](/en/tools/centerbase/) or [Actionstep](/en/tools/actionstep/) fit better.

## Alternatives

- **[Clio](/en/tools/clio/)** — the market-share leader, past $500M in annual recurring revenue in May 2026. Pick it when integrations and ecosystem matter more than built-in accounting; Clio expects you to keep QuickBooks or Xero for the firm's books.
- **[MyCase](/en/tools/mycase/)** — the other large incumbent. Pick it when you want intake, texting and built-in AI writing help in one bill, and are fine adding MyCase Accounting at $39 per user per month.
- **[PracticePanther](/en/tools/practicepanther/)** — the closest like-for-like. Pick it when you want trust and operating accounting with a general ledger for less than CosmoLex Pro ($114 versus $129 per user per month on annual billing).
- **[LeanLaw](/en/tools/leanlaw/)** — pick it when your firm is committed to QuickBooks Online and wants billing and trust compliance built on top of it rather than replacing it.
- **[Filevine](/en/tools/filevine/)** — the fastest-growing platform in the category after a $400M raise in September 2025. Pick it for high-volume litigation or plaintiff work, where case pipelines matter more than the general ledger.

## Watch-outs

- **The marketing copy and the plan table disagree.** The accounting page says bank feeds are "included free", and the vs-Clio page headline says all features are included, yet the same site's plan table puts General Ledger & Payables and bank feeds on Pro only. *Guard:* before you sign, get the list of features for your plan in writing, and test the general ledger and a bank feed during the trial (the trial allows one bank feed connection).
- **The QuickBooks switch is one-way and exclusive.** Turning on the QuickBooks Online integration disables CosmoLex's built-in business accounting and matter budgets, and data does not flow back. *Guard:* decide which system holds the firm's books before go-live, with your CPA in the room, and don't run both ledgers in parallel.
- **The general ledger is legal-first, not a full accounting suite.** Some Capterra reviewers find the operating-accounting side clunkier than QuickBooks, and one asks for accrual accounting. *Guard:* have your accountant run a month-end close and the reports they need for tax filing in the trial before you cancel QuickBooks.
- **Integrations are thinner than Clio's.** Lawyerist lists limited integrations and limited intake as cons; the published list covers Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Dropbox, Box, NetDocuments, QuickBooks Online and Zapier, and the API is Pro-only. *Guard:* list your must-have connections (calendaring rules, e-filing, phone, marketing) and confirm each one works natively or through Zapier before migrating.
- **The AI features are new and narrow.** They shipped in August 2026, and document summaries only work on files in CosmoLex's own storage. *Guard:* treat AI as a bonus, not a buying reason; if your documents live in Dropbox or NetDocuments, summaries will not reach them.
