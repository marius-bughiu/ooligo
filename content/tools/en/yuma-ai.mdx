## What it is

Yuma AI is an autonomous support agent for ecommerce brands that sits on top of an existing helpdesk and takes actions in the store, not only answers. Its homepage lists nine helpdesks — Gorgias, Zendesk, Kustomer, Salesforce Service Cloud, Re:amaze, Gladly, Front, Sprinklr, and Zoho Desk — plus HubSpot Help Desk on the integrations page. Intercom and Freshdesk are not listed. Tickets stay in the queue your team already works, and Yuma resolves, drafts, or hands off from inside it.

The actions come from a long integrations grid: Shopify, WooCommerce, and BigCommerce for orders; Recharge, Skio, Loop Subscriptions, and Stay AI for subscriptions; Loop Returns, Redo, and Happy Returns for returns; ShipStation, ShipBob, AfterShip, and parcelLab for tracking; Klaviyo, Attentive, and Postscript for marketing. For back-office tools with no API, a feature called Web Actions drives a real Chrome browser. The vendor's own pages disagree on the count (50+, 60+, and 100+ integrations), so check your specific stack rather than the headline number.

The current product is Yuma 2.0. Per-intent "Auto-Pilots" were replaced by one AI-managed Handbook of brand pages, playbooks, and procedures, which you edit by talking to **Ask Yuma** in the dashboard or, through Yuma's MCP server, from inside Claude or ChatGPT. Every change is a draft until published, each playbook carries replay tests, and rollout can start at 10% of traffic. The same Handbook now powers chat, social, outbound event messages, and Yuma Voice, a phone agent that verifies callers and edits orders or subscriptions during the call.

The founder and CEO is Guillaume Luccisano, previously a founder of Socialcam (sold to Autodesk) and Triplebyte (sold to Karat). Yuma went through Y Combinator, is backed by Alt Capital and Google's Gradient Ventures, and was reported to have raised $5M in October 2024. It is headquartered in the Boston area with a European office in Barcelona, claims 140+ brands, and lists Glossier, Petlibro, Clove, and Javvy Coffee among its customers.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Pay per resolution, nothing else.** No seats, no platform fee, no implementation fee. A "full resolution" is counted once, only when Yuma closes the ticket with no human involvement; the moment an agent steps in, the ticket drops out of usage. That is the cleanest billing definition among the overlay vendors.
- **Breadth of helpdesk coverage.** Nine-plus supported helpdesks means a brand on Kustomer, Gladly, or Salesforce Service Cloud can buy the same agent a Gorgias shop buys, which narrows the field considerably for brands outside Gorgias and Zendesk.
- **Change management you can audit.** Draft-then-publish, replay tests, and 10% rollouts per playbook let a support lead change refund rules on a Friday without betting the weekend queue on it.
- **Procurement-ready.** Yuma states SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and CCPA compliance and EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework certification, with failover across OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google models.

## Pricing

As published on 2026-11-26, Yuma sells annual resolution bundles, paid upfront, which it calls illustrative and sizes to your forecast:

| Plan | Annual price | Resolutions / year | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $14,400 | 12,000 | ~$1.20 |
| Growth | $40,000 | ~48,000 | ~$0.83 |
| Scale | $90,000 | ~120,000 | ~$0.75 |

Above 120,000 resolutions a year the rate falls toward a $0.55 floor. Every plan carries a 12-month commitment, the first 30 days are free, and monthly plans exist with overage at a flat $1.50 per extra resolution. The Shopify App Store listing shows monthly plans from $850 (Small Merchant) and $1,200 (Medium Merchant) without stating the included ticket count. Voice AI, Social AI, Sales AI, and custom integrations are add-ons priced by scope.

The real comparison is against the native agent you could switch on instead. At 1,200 resolutions a month (14,400 a year), Gorgias' AI Agent at $0.90 on annual billing costs about $12,960, a bundle near Yuma's Starter rate about $17,280, and Siena about $21,960 ($750 a month plus $0.90 each). At 4,000 resolutions a month, Yuma's Growth bundle ($40,000) undercuts Gorgias' native agent ($43,200) and Siena ($52,200). Below roughly 4,000 resolutions a month, Yuma has to resolve more tickets than the native agent to pay its premium back; above that, it wins on price alone at equal performance.

## Best for

Heads of CX at Shopify or multi-store DTC brands that resolve roughly 1,000 tickets a month or more, run Gorgias, Zendesk, Kustomer, or Gladly, and can commit to 12 months. The queue should be dominated by order status, returns, and subscription changes. It is the wrong pick for brands under about 500 tickets a month, for teams on Intercom or Freshdesk, and for B2B or software support.

## Watch-outs

- **Annual bundles stop, they do not overflow.** On annual plans, usage pauses when you exhaust the bundle and resumes at renewal, so a strong Black Friday can switch automation off in month nine. The guard: size the bundle to your forecast plus 20%, set a usage alert at 75%, and get the top-up price for extra resolutions written into the contract before signing.
- **Customer metrics are vendor-picked.** Yuma says most customers land between 40% and 89% automation and offers a "100% ROI guarantee", while its Shopify listing has only nine public reviews. The guard: run the free 30 days on your own tickets, measure resolved-without-human-reply on a sample of 200, ask for the guarantee's terms in writing, and take two reference calls with brands on your helpdesk.
- **Yuma 2.0 retired the 1.0 configuration.** Existing customers' Auto-Pilots, processes, and guidelines are migrated by Yuma's team into the Handbook. The guard: before and after any migration or major Handbook change, compare automation rate and reopen rate by intent for two weeks, and keep rollout at 10% until both hold.

## Alternatives, and when to pick them instead

[Gorgias](/en/tools/gorgias/) and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) are the market-share leaders, and both sell a native AI agent per resolution — about $0.90 on Gorgias annual and $1.50 on Zendesk committed volume. Pick the native agent first under about 4,000 resolutions a month, or whenever one vendor owning the ticket end to end matters more than the last 10 points of automation.

[Siena AI](/en/tools/siena-ai/) is the closest overlay rival and the fastest-growing one, with a $17M Series A in October 2026. Pick Siena when brand voice and social moderation are the priority and the $750 monthly platform fee is small next to your volume. [eesel AI](/en/tools/eesel-ai/) is the budget overlay at $0.40 per handled ticket with no commitment; pick it under 1,000 tickets a month or when support is not ecommerce. [Richpanel](/en/tools/richpanel/) replaces the helpdesk as well; pick it if you are leaving your helpdesk anyway. [Decagon](/en/tools/decagon/) fits enterprise support beyond ecommerce. If none of these fit, because volume is low or tickets need judgement rather than actions, stay with macros, a returns portal, and a self-serve order-status page.
