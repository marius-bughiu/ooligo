## What it is

Trellus is a Chrome extension that turns the sales engagement platform you already pay for into a parallel dialer with an AI coach listening on every call. It does not replace Salesloft, Outreach, HubSpot, or Apollo — it renders inside them, executes the dial tasks already sitting in your cadences, and adds up to 5 parallel lines, voicemail drop, number rotation, and live in-call guidance without a second tab. Around that core sit automatic scoring on every call, practice voice bots built from your team's own recordings, a virtual sales floor, and a consolidated LinkedIn and Sales Navigator inbox marketed as Superhuman for LinkedIn. The company is Outpost Labs Inc., trading as Trellus — a Y Combinator W22 startup in New York founded by Craig Bonnoit and Ajinkya Nene.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **It is an overlay, so the dial stack does not change.** Reps keep working the cadences they already have and the CRM stays the system of record — no migration, no second task list, no retraining on a new dialer UI. Supported surfaces include Salesloft, Outreach, HubSpot, Apollo, Salesforce, Gong Engage, Close, Attio, lemlist, Clay, Unify, Zoho, Amplemarket, and Google Sheets.
- **Coaching lands during the call, not in Monday's review.** Live guidance fires on objection handling and competitor positioning while the prospect is still on the line, from prebuilt scenarios or talk tracks you write yourself. Post-call scoring runs on 100% of calls rather than on the handful a manager spot-checks.
- **Phone numbers and spam hygiene are inside the price.** Power bundles 5 additional numbers, Parallel bundles 8, both with spam remediation, plus automated number-health checks and rotation as you dial. Dialer platforms that leave you to buy and babysit your own numbers push another $10-$15 per number per month onto the invoice.
- **Voice agents share the same surface.** The same product deploys practice bots that imitate your prospects for ramp, and inbound agents for call handling, qualification, and appointment setting — so a team that wants both practice and deflection does not buy twice.

## Pricing reality

Trellus publishes per-seat prices, which is rare in this segment. Power is $99.99 per user per month — power dialing on every supported platform, voicemail drop, 5 extra numbers with spam remediation, and the LinkedIn inbox. Parallel is $149.99 per user per month and adds multi-line dialing, unlimited AI practice calls, and 8 extra numbers. Business is quote-only and adds customizable AI research and coaching, team analytics, and custom reporting. The pricing page carries a monthly/annual toggle. The free entry point is the extension itself: install it from the Chrome Web Store and start, no demo required — the vendor's own contact form tells small teams to skip the call and buy a self-serve license.

At list, a 10-rep team on Parallel is roughly $18,000 per year. [Nooks](/en/tools/nooks/) quotes about $4,000-$5,000 per user per year with a 5-seat minimum, so the same team lands at $40,000-$50,000; [Orum](/en/tools/orum/) Launch starts near $3,000 per seat per year. Trellus runs about a third of a Nooks quote and half an Orum seat, before the phone numbers those two bill separately. Treat the band as live, not fixed: the current tier list still references a cheaper BYOD plan the pricing page no longer displays, and third-party review sites are quoting stale figures against it.

## Best for

- SDR and BDR teams of 2-25 reps already living in [Salesloft](/en/tools/salesloft/), [Outreach](/en/tools/outreach/), [HubSpot](/en/tools/hubspot/), or [Apollo](/en/tools/apollo/) that want more connects per hour and live objection coaching without buying a dialer platform or migrating cadences.
- Sales managers ramping new reps who want practice bots cloned from real recorded calls and a score on every call, at a price that survives a headcount freeze.

Do not buy Trellus if your calling list lives outside a supported platform — there is no standalone CSV-upload dialer, and Google Sheets or [Clay](/en/tools/clay/) are the only spreadsheet-shaped paths in. Do not buy it if you want one vendor to own dialing, coaching, and prospecting under an enterprise support contract; that is the argument for Nooks.

## Versus the alternatives

Nooks is the top alternative by share and the bundling play: dialer, AI coaching, salesfloor, and an AI prospector in one quote-based platform with a roughly $25,000 annual floor. Pick Nooks when you want a single vendor accountable for the whole outbound calling surface and the budget to match; pick Trellus when the dial stack already works and you are buying speed and coaching on top of it.

Orum is the dialer-first incumbent and the pick when raw connects-per-hour is the only goal — more mature dialing infrastructure, but coaching lives elsewhere and seats cost roughly double. Koncert is the third established multi-line option, worth a quote if you want higher line counts than Orum's entry tier and are price-shopping the dialer alone. On the coaching flank, Balto and Abstrakt are the established real-time-guidance vendors — Trellus publishes head-to-head pages against both — and both aim at contact-center agent assist, so they win when the job is script adherence and QA across a support floor rather than cold-call connects. Trellus is itself the fast-growing entrant in the overlay category, which is why every alternative here is either a full dialer platform or a contact-center coaching tool rather than a like-for-like competitor.

## Watch-outs

- **It is a browser extension, and that is the whole failure surface.** Everything runs inside a Chrome tab on a supported page; a managed-browser policy, a blocked extension ID, or a locked-down profile stops the product cold, and there is no mobile path. Guard: have IT allowlist the extension ID and pilot on one rep's actual managed Chrome profile before you buy seats for the team.
- **The vendor is very small.** The company's own team page lists four people and its Y Combinator profile lists three. That buys you fast, direct support and no account-management theater, but it is a real concentration risk on a system your reps use every hour. Guard: keep recordings, transcripts, and dispositions syncing back to the CRM or [Gong](/en/tools/gong/) so the call record never lives only in Trellus, and start on monthly billing until you have run a full quarter.
- **Parallel dialing trades connect quality for volume.** Stale numbers drop connect rates and dead air when two prospects answer at once burns the human connect — the same physics that applies to every multi-line dialer. Guard: verify numbers before a session, cap parallel lines at 3 when list quality is unknown, and measure connects-per-hour rather than dials.
- **The published price band has drifted.** The pricing page shows $99.99 and $149.99 while its own feature copy references a BYOD tier it no longer lists, and review sites still quote lower legacy numbers. Guard: confirm the number at the Stripe checkout and the exact annual-toggle amount before you forecast the line item, not from a comparison site.
- **Real-time coaching only helps if someone writes the talk tracks.** Prebuilt objection scenarios ship out of the box, but the guidance is generic until your positioning is loaded in. Guard: budget an hour of a sales manager's week for the first month to encode real objections and competitor responses, and treat the prebuilt set as a pilot default rather than the deployment.
