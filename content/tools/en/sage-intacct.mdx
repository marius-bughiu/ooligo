## What it is

Sage Intacct is a cloud financial management system for mid-market companies: general ledger, accounts payable and receivable, cash management, multi-entity consolidation, and add-on modules for revenue recognition, project accounting and inventory. Sage Group bought Intacct in 2017 for $850 million and still sells it under the Intacct name. It is finance-first. NetSuite started as a full ERP suite. Intacct started as an accounting system, and its operational depth (inventory, order management) was added later or comes from partners.

Its signature is the dimensional ledger. Instead of encoding department, location, project or customer into a long chart of accounts, every transaction carries tags, and reports slice on them. That is why accounting teams pick it, and it is also why the GTM tools that post to it need a clear mapping.

## Why it shows up in RevOps stacks

- **It is the second finance endpoint every comp and planning vendor supports.** [CaptivateIQ](/en/tools/captivateiq/), [Everstage](/en/tools/everstage/), [Pigment](/en/tools/pigment/) and [QuotaPath](/en/tools/quotapath/) all list Sage Intacct next to [NetSuite](/en/tools/netsuite/). QuotaPath puts its accounting connectors in a higher tier, so check the plan before you assume the sync is included.
- **Salesforce has a Sage-built connector.** The Advanced CRM Connector runs inside [Salesforce](/en/tools/salesforce/). Accounts become Intacct customers and opportunities become sales orders. Sage's integration material describes that sync as one-way, with invoice and payment status visible back in Salesforce but not editable there.
- **There is now an MCP server.** Release 2026 R2 (release notes dated May 8, 2026) introduced the Sage Intacct AI Gateway: REST API and MCP access for AI applications. The notes list it for Australia, Canada, Singapore, South Africa, the UK and the US. One UK partner describes the MCP side as read-only and usable from [Claude](/en/tools/claude/), Copilot or any MCP client. Release 2026 R3 (August 7, 2026) added AI anomaly detection in AP Automation and a marketplace of partner-built agents for Sage Copilot, with access controlled through Intacct roles.

## Pricing reality

Sage publishes no price list. Quotes are built from users, modules, entities and contract term. Vendr's Intacct guidance puts small to mid-market deployments at $15,000-$60,000 a year, with larger multi-entity deployments above $100,000. At 10-25 users it cites $75-$150 per user per month. Core financials (GL, AP, AR) are in the base price, while inventory, project accounting, consolidation and revenue recognition are priced separately. Vendr reports 15-30% off list for multi-year deals.

Some third-party guides quote $400-$600 per user per month. Those figures don't match Vendr's, so budget from the Vendr band and ask for a written quote. Vendr's median for the whole Sage portfolio is $56,902 a year across 352 purchases, with 14% average savings. That number includes other Sage products, so don't use it as an Intacct price. Implementation runs $5,000 for a simple rollout to $50,000+ for multi-entity work, through a Sage partner.

## Best for

A controller or finance-systems lead at a services, SaaS or nonprofit company with a handful to a few dozen entities, who needs dimensional reporting and fast consolidation more than inventory or manufacturing, and whose comp and planning tools need a ledger to post payouts to.

**Not for you if** you carry real inventory, run ecommerce, or want one suite for billing, orders and stock. NetSuite or Business Central covers that natively. It is also overkill for a single-entity company under a few million in revenue that QuickBooks still handles.

## Versus the alternatives

**NetSuite** and **Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central** are the two systems Intacct meets most in mid-market shortlists. Pick NetSuite when you need inventory, subscription billing and revenue recognition in one suite, and the widest set of GTM connectors. Pick Business Central when the company already runs [Dynamics 365 Sales](/en/tools/microsoft-dynamics-365-sales/) and Microsoft 365 and wants one vendor. Pick Intacct when the buyer is the accounting team and reporting by dimension matters more than operations.

**Rillet** is the fastest-growing entrant: it raised a $100M Series C at a $1B valuation in August 2026. Pick it, or **Campfire** (its Ember assistant runs on Claude), when your revenue is pure SaaS and you want an AI-native close more than a mature partner channel. Pick Intacct when you have nonprofit or fund accounting, project accounting, or several entity types that need a proven consolidation engine.

## Watch-outs

- **The Salesforce sync stops at new bookings.** One-way opportunity-to-order mapping handles a new deal, but amendments, co-terms and multi-entity pricing usually need custom work or a middleware like [Workato](/en/tools/workato/). *Guard:* before go-live, run test cases for an upgrade, a co-term, a cancellation and a multi-currency deal, and name one owner for the sync.
- **Dimensions become integration debt.** A comp tool posting payouts needs to know which department, location and employee dimensions each line carries. *Guard:* agree the dimension scheme with finance before connecting any GTM tool, and document the mapping per connector.
- **The AI Gateway's scope is not fully documented in public.** Regions exclude France and Germany in the R2 notes, and read-only access is a partner claim. *Guard:* get the scope, region and licence terms in writing, and give AI clients a dedicated read-only role.
- **Modules are where the bill grows.** Consolidation, revenue recognition and inventory each add a line, and renewals compound it. *Guard:* price every module separately, trade a multi-year term for the 15-30% discount, and cap the renewal uplift in the order form.
