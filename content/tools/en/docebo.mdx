## What it is

Docebo is a publicly listed enterprise learning platform (TSX and Nasdaq: DCBO) that sells one LMS for employee training and for what it calls "extended enterprise" — courses, certifications, and paid catalogs for customers and partners who are not on your payroll. It reported $255.1M in ARR at 30 June 2026, an average contract value of $74.8K (up 27% year over year), and a 16.4% adjusted EBITDA margin for the second quarter. It is the buyer in this market, not the target: it bought the French skills-intelligence company 365Talents on 20 January 2026 and the German knowledge-search company Zive on 2 April 2026, and it raised full-year guidance twice in 2026.

The reason it belongs on a Customer Success shortlist is the same structural fact that shapes the whole category. Northpass and Skilljar both now sit inside [Gainsight](/en/tools/gainsight/). If you want your customer academy run by a company that is not also your CS platform, the independent options are Docebo, [Intellum](/en/tools/intellum/), and [Thought Industries](/en/tools/thought-industries/) — and of those three, Docebo is the largest, the only one with public financials, and the one whose product roadmap is pulled hardest toward internal workforce learning.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **One instance, many academies.** Extended Enterprise lets you run separate branded sub-domains — a customer academy, a partner portal, an internal onboarding site — from one admin console, with per-audience catalogs, permissions, and page layouts. For a company that trains employees, resellers, and end users, that is one contract instead of two or three.
- **Paid training is built in.** The platform sells courses and catalogs directly and connects to Shopify, Stripe, and PayPal for checkout. Docebo's Q2 2026 release cites a security-services company that replaced its previous provider to launch a multilingual, customer-facing ecommerce academy on it.
- **Salesforce is the CRM bridge.** Docebo offers a premium packaged Salesforce integration that syncs users and contacts in and pushes course and enrollment data back to the account record. There is no native Gainsight connector; CS teams on Gainsight route learning data through Salesforce or an iPaaS such as Tray.ai.
- **The AI layer reaches outside the LMS.** The Docebo MCP server went to production on 24 July 2026 and exposes courses, progress, and answers inside Claude, Microsoft Copilot, and ChatGPT. Companion, a browser extension, puts the assistant and relevant courses inside any web app, and Docebo states it is free to all Docebo Learn customers. AI Tutor, Conversational Insights for admin reporting, and AI Roleplay ship today; Docebo says monthly completed roleplay simulations grew 4x between February and its August 2026 earnings call.

## Pricing reality

Docebo publishes no list prices on its own site, but its AWS Marketplace listing does. The Elevate plan is priced at **$35,900 a year for 500 yearly active users** and **$52,800 a year for 1,000**, on a 36-month agreement billed annually in advance. That works out to roughly $72 per active learner per year at 500 and $53 at 1,000. A yearly active user is anyone who logs in once during the contract year, and the listing prices overage at $150 and $110 per extra user on those two tiers.

Vendr's purchase data, as summarized by third-party pricing trackers, puts the median annual contract near **$40,000**, with most buyers between $21,000 and $86,000. Docebo's own disclosed average contract value of $74.8K sits above that because its customer base skews toward large enterprises. Onboarding is a mandatory paid line item at several resellers, and the premium Salesforce package, Extended Enterprise domains, and ecommerce are scoped separately.

The realistic first-year number for a customer academy of 1,000 to 2,000 active learners is **$55,000 to $95,000** once onboarding and the extended-enterprise and Salesforce lines are added. That is the same band as Intellum and Thought Industries, so price does not decide this shortlist; the three-year term and the learner metric do.

## Best for

A Customer Education or Enablement leader at a company with 1,000+ employees who has to run internal training *and* a customer or partner academy, wants both in one contract with one admin team, already runs Salesforce as the CRM of record, and values a vendor with public financials over a smaller pure play.

**Not for you if** customer education is the whole job and you will never train employees in the same system. Then you are paying for workforce features — skills intelligence, talent marketplace, compliance training — that the 2026 acquisitions are building out, and a customer-education pure play will fit tighter. It is also the wrong pick under roughly 500 learners: the published entry tier is $35,900 a year on a three-year commitment, and [Zendesk](/en/tools/zendesk/) help-centre content plus an in-app guide from [Whatfix](/en/tools/whatfix/) or [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/) covers more of the same questions for far less.

## Versus the alternatives

- **Skilljar by Gainsight** has the largest installed base in customer education. Pick it when Gainsight is already your CS platform and you want course completions feeding health scores without integration work — the one thing Docebo cannot do natively.
- **Intellum** is the closest match on scope: one instance for customers, partners, and employees, with certification built as an audit-grade artifact. Route on where most of your learners sit. If most of them are on your payroll, Docebo; if most of them are customers and partners, Intellum.
- **Thought Industries** is the external-only pure play. Pick it when you will never run employee learning in the same system and want a product whose entire roadmap is customer and partner education.
- **WorkRamp** is the fastest-growing modern entrant. Pick it when you want a lighter admin surface and a faster first launch more than you want Docebo's extended-enterprise depth and AI footprint.

## Watch-outs

- **Add-ons move the total, not the base fee.** Extended Enterprise domains, the premium Salesforce package, ecommerce, and onboarding are each priced separately, and the base Elevate quote you see first covers none of them. *Guard:* require a line-item order form that names each of those four, its price, and whether it renews at the same rate. Compare that total, not the platform fee, against Intellum and Thought Industries.
- **Yearly active users punish casual customer logins.** A customer who opens the academy once to download a certificate counts as a full active user for the year, and the AWS listing prices overage at $110 to $150 per user. On a self-serve product with a large free tier, that turns curiosity into cost. *Guard:* gate academy access behind SSO for paying accounts only, model your yearly active users from last year's real login data before choosing a tier, and negotiate the overage rate down in writing rather than accepting the list rate.
- **Admin depth comes with admin weight.** Reviewers on G2 and Capterra repeatedly cite configuration complexity, a steep learning curve, leftover legacy admin pages, and reporting that struggles in complex organizations. *Guard:* trial it as an administrator. Build one customer sub-domain, one certification with a renewal rule, and one report on course completion by account end to end; if any takes more than an hour, budget for a dedicated LMS admin.
- **The roadmap is pulling toward the workforce.** 365Talents and Zive add skills intelligence, a talent marketplace, and enterprise knowledge search — strong for HR and internal enablement, but not customer education — and third-party coverage puts full AgentHub availability in late 2026 rather than today. *Guard:* buy against features that are generally available on the day you sign, get the GA status of each AI item named in the order form, and do not let a three-year term rest on an AgentHub use case you cannot test.
