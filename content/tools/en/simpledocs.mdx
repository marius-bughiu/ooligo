## What it is

SimpleDocs is a contract AI company that, since 6 November 2026, sells a full contract lifecycle management platform called **SimpleDocs 360**. The platform is three products that you can buy one at a time or together:

- **SimpleAI** — drafting, review, redlining, comparison and benchmarking inside Microsoft Word, plus a browser version. It runs against your playbooks (a standard library of about 50 plus a custom builder) and against market precedent from Law Insider.
- **Workflow** — contract intake, approval routing, stakeholder coordination and signature. Signing goes out through DocuSign, Dropbox Sign or Adobe Sign; there is no native e-signature.
- **Repository** — an AI repository with bulk import, OCR, metadata extraction, search, reporting, and renewal and obligation alerts.

The distinguishing asset is **Law Insider**, the public contract and clause database SimpleDocs bought on 30 September 2025. Both companies were founded by Preston Clark, who runs the combined business, and both were bootstrapped and profitable at the time of the deal. At acquisition, Law Insider reported 5 million contracts and 20 million clauses in more than 50 languages and 1.2 million registered users. SimpleDocs uses that corpus to tell a reviewer whether a clause is common in executed agreements, not just whether it matches your own playbook. That Contract Intelligence Layer went into limited beta in February 2026.

## Why it shows up in Legal Ops stacks

- **It publishes its prices.** Most CLM vendors in this segment — [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/), [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/), [Juro](/en/tools/juro/), [LinkSquares](/en/tools/linksquares/) — sell through a demo with no list price. SimpleDocs posts per-module list prices, contract-volume tiers and implementation fee ranges. You can budget a pilot before the first sales call.
- **Modular entry.** A team can start with Word-based review for three lawyers and add Workflow or Repository later, instead of signing a full CLM rollout on day one.
- **Precedent, not just playbook.** Review against "what you accept" is table stakes. Review against "what the market signs" is the feature most competitors cannot match without a comparable corpus. See [contract benchmarking](/en/learn/contract-benchmarking/) for what that comparison can and cannot tell you.

## Pricing reality

List prices on simpledocs.com as of 11 November 2026:

- **SimpleAI:** $149 per user per month, 3-seat minimum. **SimpleAI Pro:** $199, same minimum, adds 10 hours of legal engineer support, advanced playbook sharing and live training. Law Insider sells the same SimpleAI seat at $1,790 per user per year.
- **Repository:** Core from $10,000/year and Pro from $18,000/year for up to 2,000 active contracts. Prices step to $14,000/$23,000 at 2,001–5,000 and $20,000/$30,000 at 5,001–10,000; above that it is custom. Each tier includes 3 full users and unlimited read-only users. Extra full users cost $1,200/year.
- **Workflow:** Core from $10,000/year, Pro from $18,000/year, 3 full users and unlimited business requesters included. Extra full users cost $1,200/year.
- **Implementation:** $5,000–$15,000 for a Core module and $15,000–$25,000 for a Pro module, one-time.
- **Terms:** annual subscriptions prepaid, on one- or two-year agreements. The 360 bundle itself is custom-quoted.

Real-world bands, at list price and before any bundle discount:

- **Word-only review for 3 lawyers:** $5,364/year on SimpleAI, $7,164 on Pro.
- **Full three-module build for a small legal team** (SimpleAI Pro × 3, Repository Core and Workflow Core, under 2,000 contracts): about **$27,200/year**, plus $10,000–$30,000 in year-one implementation.
- **The same on Pro modules:** about **$43,200/year**, plus $30,000–$50,000 in implementation.

For context, ooligo's [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/) page cites a Vendr average contract of about $25,300/year. Our [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) page puts typical deals at $50,000–$500,000+. So SimpleDocs 360 at Core level prices like a mid-market CLM, and it publishes the meter.

## Best for

**Best for:** a head of legal or legal ops manager at a 2–15 lawyer in-house team that negotiates most third-party paper in Word and wants playbook review first, with intake and a repository added in stages. The case is strongest when market-precedent benchmarking will change negotiating positions, for example on SaaS, vendor and commercial agreements where Law Insider coverage is deep.

**Not for you if** your hard problem is approval logic across many business units, procurement-led supplier contracting, or more than 10,000 active contracts. Those fit Ironclad or DocuSign IAM better. It is also the wrong buy if you only need Word redlining for a law firm, which a cheaper drafting tool covers.

## Versus the alternatives

- [Ironclad](/en/tools/ironclad/) is the enterprise default. Pick it when workflow design across departments and a large legal org is the constraint and budget is not.
- [DocuSign IAM](/en/tools/docusign-iam/) is the market-share incumbent where DocuSign is already the signature standard. Pick it when consolidating onto one signing vendor matters more than precedent review.
- [SpotDraft](/en/tools/spotdraft/) is the fast-growing AI-native challenger in mid-market CLM, with a vendor-reported 700+ customers. Pick it when you want one integrated platform and its VerifAI review rather than a modular buy.
- [Juro](/en/tools/juro/) fits teams that want to leave Word for a browser editor, and sales teams that self-serve NDAs and order forms.
- [Spellbook](/en/tools/spellbook/) or [LegalOn](/en/tools/legalon/) fit when you need only Word or review AI and no repository or intake.

For the full field, see [best CLM platforms](/en/vs/best-clm-platforms/).

## Watch-outs

- **The 360 platform is days old.** SimpleAI has been in market since 2025, but Workflow and Repository launched as a suite on 6 November 2026, and the launch materials mixed "available now" with an early-access waitlist. **Guard:** buy SimpleAI first on a one-year term. Run Repository through the vendor's pilot or sandbox option before prepaying, and take the two-year discount only once a pilot has gone live.
- **Pro's headline features depend on Repository.** Precedent-based benchmarking and Repository integration in SimpleAI Pro are "included with Repository", which is a separate subscription. **Guard:** if benchmarking is why you are buying, price SimpleAI Pro and Repository together and confirm in the order form that benchmarking is active.
- **Repository is metered on active contracts.** Crossing 2,000 or 5,000 active contracts moves you up a $4,000–$7,000 step. **Guard:** count active, unexpired agreements before the quote, and archive expired ones before renewal so they do not set the tier.
- **Precedent frequency is not market standard.** Clark has said publicly that separating what is common from what is truly standard still needs work. **Guard:** keep your own playbook as the deciding rule and treat the Law Insider benchmark as negotiation evidence, not as the position.
- **API access is plan-gated and there is no MCP server.** The vendor lists API access on Enterprise plans and scoped API and webhooks on Workflow Pro. Nothing published covers an MCP server. **Guard:** if your agent or reporting stack must read contract data, write API scope into the contract, or plan around [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/) or a CRM as the integration point.

Security posture as published: SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and CCPA, AWS hosting with US and EU residency, and no training on customer documents. Product pages also claim ISO 27001, though the security page does not list it, so ask for the certificate. If you want to reproduce precedent-based playbooks yourself first, the [playbook-from-precedent skill](/en/workflows/playbook-from-precedent-claude-skill/) is a no-licence way to test whether the idea pays off for your contract mix.
