## What it is

OnRamp is a B2B customer onboarding platform: a branded portal the customer logs into, a playbook engine that decides which tasks each account gets, and an internal project view that shows the onboarding team what is stalled and why. It covers the stretch between closed-won and go-live, and it is built for implementations where the customer has work to do — upload data, sign forms, attend training, connect systems — and the vendor needs that work done on a date.

The Boston company was founded in 2020 by CEO Paul Holder and Ross Lerner. On 12 November 2025 it closed a $15M Series A led by KDT, the venture arm of Koch Inc., with Contour, Javelin and Pear VC participating, bringing total funding to $27M. It reports more than 100% year-over-year growth and three Fortune 15 companies as customers; named logos include CVS Health, Bullhorn, Betterment and Qualia.

## Why it shows up in Customer Success stacks

- **Conditional playbooks, not static templates.** OnRamp's playbook layer branches on customer attributes and on answers the customer gives in the portal, so a 20-seat SMB account and a multi-entity enterprise account get different task lists from the same playbook. Reviewers consistently name the response-triggered branching as the reason they picked it.
- **The CRM starts the project.** Native Salesforce and HubSpot integrations create an onboarding project when a deal hits closed-won (or whatever trigger you map), pull custom fields into the project name, owner and variables, and write milestone data back to the CRM. That is the sales-to-CS handoff most teams still run through a Slack message and a spreadsheet.
- **Aero, the agentic layer, is in the base price.** Launched 27 May 2026, Aero puts agents in three places: in the customer portal to guide a stuck user to the next action, alongside CSMs to flag projects that are losing momentum, and in the ops layer to turn a spreadsheet, PDF or plain-language description into a draft playbook. Pending agent recommendations can be approved, redirected or rejected, and every action is logged in an Activity Record. OnRamp's pricing page states Aero is included in all plans, not sold as an add-on.

## Pricing reality

OnRamp publishes no tier prices. Its pricing page, checked on 18 September 2026, says pricing starts at $15,000 a year and scales on three meters: the number of playbooks, the number of active customer accounts, and the user types (five permission roles) you license. Tiers run from Basic to Premier. Every plan includes the branded portal, playbooks, automated reminders, the Salesforce and HubSpot integrations, reporting and SOC 2 Type II; higher tiers add API access, advanced integrations and deeper reporting. There is no self-serve trial — you get a sandbox during evaluation.

For a benchmark: GuideCX's Starter plan has been reported at $143 per license per month with a four-license minimum, about $6,900 a year before any Premium features, and third-party pricing roundups put GuideCX's median contract around $23,000. Rocketlane publishes list prices from $19 to $99 per user per month. Budget OnRamp at $15,000–$30,000 a year for a mid-market team; the account meter matters more than the seat count once you pass a few hundred onboardings a year.

## Best for

Onboarding and implementation leads at B2B SaaS and services companies with 50–2,000 new accounts a year, where each onboarding runs two to twelve weeks and depends on customer-side tasks. It fits best where segments need different paths — by plan, industry or region — and the CRM is Salesforce or HubSpot. G2 reviewers skew small-business (about 55% of its roughly 160 reviews, rated 4.4/5), but the Fortune 15 logos show it holds up at enterprise volume.

It is the wrong tool if onboarding is a self-serve in-app checklist (use [Userpilot](/en/tools/userpilot/) or [Pendo](/en/tools/pendo/)), or if your implementation team bills hours and needs resource planning and utilization — OnRamp has no PSA layer.

## Versus the alternatives

**GuideCX** is the incumbent the category is benchmarked against, with over 500,000 onboarding projects run and $35M raised. Pick it when you need time tracking, resource management and program management across many concurrent implementations, which live in its Advanced tier.

**[Rocketlane](/en/tools/rocketlane/)** is the fastest-growing and best-funded challenger, with a $60M Series C led by Insight Partners in March 2026. Pick it when onboarding is run by a professional-services team that needs PSA features — utilization, financials, time tracking — alongside the portal.

**[Arrows](/en/tools/arrows/)** is the HubSpot-native pick for lighter, sales-led onboarding plans, and **[Dock](/en/tools/dock/)** covers the case where one shared workspace should span the sales cycle and onboarding.

Pick OnRamp when branching playbooks and CRM-triggered project creation are the core requirement and you do not need PSA. If none fit — a handful of onboardings a year — run a HubSpot or Salesforce project template plus a shared doc until volume justifies a platform.

## Watch-outs

- **Projects are rigid once they start.** The most repeated review complaint is that modules and tasks are hard to change after a project is live, and sub-tasks cannot be moved between stages. **Guard:** during the sandbox, launch a test project, then change scope mid-flight — add a module, move a task, reassign an owner — and time how long it takes. If your implementations routinely change scope, weigh this above any AI feature.
- **Three meters make the renewal hard to forecast.** Playbooks, active accounts and user types all move the price, and growth moves all three. **Guard:** get the per-account and per-playbook overage rates and a renewal-uplift cap in the order form, and model year two at your projected account volume, not today's.
- **Aero's numbers are vendor-reported.** The launch claim — half the time spent tracking, twice the time engaging — comes from OnRamp and one customer quote, and the agents have been generally available only since May 2026. **Guard:** run Aero's stall detection on your live projects for one quarter and compare its flags to what your CSMs escalated manually before letting it message customers.
- **No MCP server.** Agent access runs through the open API, and the long tail of integrations goes through Zapier. **Guard:** if you want Claude or an internal agent reading onboarding status, scope API access in the contract (it is tier-gated) and build a read-only wrapper rather than routing customer data through another middleware vendor.
