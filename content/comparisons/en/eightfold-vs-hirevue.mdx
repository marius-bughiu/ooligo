[Eightfold](/en/tools/eightfold/) shipped an AI Interviewer in October 2025 and spent 2026 wrapping agents around every other step of the hiring journey. [HireVue](/en/tools/hirevue/) bought its way to the same capability — it acquired Hireguide's technology on 10 March 2026 and launched its own voice-based AI Interviewer on 18 June 2026 — and sells it on two decades of validation studies rather than on platform breadth. Both now interview. So the decision is not which interviewer is better; it is whether you are buying a talent-intelligence platform that has learned to interview, or a selection-science vendor that has learned to run agents. That difference shows up on the invoice before it shows up in the product, and the pricing section below is where this page earns its keep.

## Where Eightfold wins

- **One dataset behind sourcing, internal mobility, and interviewing.** Eightfold's skills graph — its stated basis is 1.6 billion career trajectories and 1.6 million skills — is the same asset that ranks external candidates, surfaces internal ones, and now grounds the interview. HireVue interviews against a role definition you supply; Eightfold interviews against a profile it already built. If you have 20,000 employees you cannot currently see, that is the entire argument.
- **Agent coverage is end-to-end, and the shipping cadence is real.** AI Interviewer in October 2025; AI Interview Companion plus functional and coding interview types on 8 April 2026; Candidate Agent generally available on 15 July 2026, running 24/7 in 24+ languages over SMS, WhatsApp, and voice; Avatar and 360 Interview scheduled as Talent Agents 2.5 at the end of July 2026. HireVue's shipped agent surface across the same window is one AI Interviewer.
- **A written modality exclusion.** Eightfold states that its interviewing evaluates the content of a candidate's answer, not video, biometrics, or tone. That is a narrower surface to defend under [AI screening bias](/en/learn/ai-screening-bias/) scrutiny, and it is a sentence you can hand to counsel. HireVue makes no equivalent published exclusion for its voice interviewer.
- **The certification set a regulated buyer actually asks for.** SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, and FedRAMP Moderate attested. ISO 42001 (AI management systems) and the FedRAMP attestation are the two that decide public-sector and regulated-industry deals, and they are not table stakes in this category yet.
- **AI Interview Companion augments the human panel instead of replacing it.** It runs alongside a human-led interview with real-time guidance, consistent evaluation, and documentation written back to the record. That is the missing half of [structured interviewing](/en/learn/structured-interviewing/) — most vendors automate the screen and leave the panel undisciplined.

## Where HireVue wins

- **The validation evidence is external, named, and published.** Dr. Richard Landers reviewed roughly 1,000 pages of HireVue documentation and concluded in April 2021 that the job-analysis process was "of very high quality and rigor" against established standards; HireVue then engaged DCI Consulting Group in January 2023 for an external audit of its algorithms for race, gender, and intersectional bias. Eightfold says it evaluates its models for bias. It does not point at a named external auditor's published conclusion. When defensibility is the buying criterion, that gap is the product.
- **The outcome corpus behind the rubrics is enormous.** HireVue reports 1,150+ global customers including over 60% of the Fortune 100, 180 million assessments hosted, 80 million video interviews, and 200 million chat-based candidate engagements. Its AI Interviewer scores against IO-validated rubrics built from that base rather than from a general-purpose model prompted into a scoring role.
- **You can buy interviewing without buying a workforce platform.** This is the decisive practical difference. Eightfold's interviewer is a module of a talent-intelligence platform whose commercial model assumes you want the platform. HireVue sells the screening layer as the product, drops into [Workday](/en/tools/workday/), [Greenhouse](/en/tools/greenhouse/), or [iCIMS](/en/tools/icims/), and leaves your system of record alone.
- **The entry point is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper at enterprise headcount.** See below.
- **No live class action over how it scores candidates.** Also below, and it is not a small point in 2026.

## Pricing reality

Neither vendor publishes a price, so everything here is third-party procurement data, not vendor-stated, and should be treated as a negotiating anchor rather than a quote.

Against that data, HireVue's entry tier is reported around $35,000/year with a minimum applicant commitment, enterprise contracts land roughly $75,000–$150,000/year, and Vendr's observed average deal is $49,855. Implementation is quoted separately at $15,000–$40,000 one-time, plus $5,000–$25,000 for custom ATS integration work. Eightfold is reported at roughly $7–$10 per employee per month, with large-org annual contracts of $150,000–$500,000+ and 3–6 month rollouts.

The structural point matters more than any single band: **HireVue meters on hiring volume, Eightfold meters on your employee base.** Work it. A 10,000-employee company hiring 600 people a year pays Eightfold on 10,000 employees — $840,000–$1,200,000/year at the reported PEPM — against HireVue's $75,000–$150,000 enterprise band. That is a 6–10x spread for a capability both now offer. Invert the shape: a 1,500-employee company hiring 900 a year pays Eightfold roughly $126,000–$180,000 while HireVue stays in the same band, and the gap mostly closes.

The usable rule is the ratio of annual hires to total headcount. Above roughly 8–10%, you are a high-volume hirer with a small base and the volume meter is the honest basis — buy HireVue and spend the difference on [AI resume screening](/en/learn/ai-resume-screening/) upstream. Below roughly 4%, Eightfold's PEPM is charging you for 96% of a workforce you are not hiring — which is fine if internal mobility is the actual purchase, and indefensible if interviewing is.

## Watch-outs, and what to do about each

- **Eightfold is defending a live FCRA class action about the scoring mechanism itself.** *Kistler v. Eightfold AI* (N.D. Cal., filed 20 January 2026) alleges Eightfold compiled data on over a billion workers from third-party sources, scored applicants, and functioned as an unregistered consumer reporting agency without FCRA disclosures. Eightfold's motion to dismiss argues it is not a consumer reporting agency; it was argued on 4 August 2026 and is undecided as of today. Guard: require the order form to name which third-party data sources feed a candidate score, whether candidates receive disclosure and a dispute path, and who indemnifies you if the FCRA theory survives.
- **Eightfold's bill does not fall when hiring does.** A headcount-indexed meter is a fixed cost through a hiring freeze. Guard: price the quote against your 18-month headcount plan, and negotiate a band-transition clause before signing rather than at renewal.
- **Part of the Eightfold 2026 story is roadmap, not shipped product.** Avatar and 360 Interview were announced as expected at the end of July 2026. Guard: get written GA confirmation for any capability in your business case, or exclude it from year-one pricing entirely.
- **HireVue's published validation evidence predates the product you are buying.** The Landers audit is 2021 and the DCI engagement is 2023; both cover the assessment library, not the June 2026 AI Interviewer. Guard: demand the validation study and adverse-impact analysis for the specific AI Interviewer model and the job family you intend to deploy, and make delivery a condition of go-live.
- **HireVue's AI Interviewer is voice-based with no published modality exclusion.** HireVue discontinued facial analysis in January 2021, but its AI Interviewer materials describe scoring responses without stating what acoustic features are or are not used. Guard: get that in writing before a works council or a state regulator asks you.
- **Under NYC Local Law 144 the bias-audit obligation is yours, not the vendor's.** Vendor audits do not discharge it, and Illinois HB 3773 adds its own employer-side duties. Guard: budget an annual independent audit and the published summary as a line item, and read [NYC Local Law 144](/en/learn/nyc-local-law-144/) and [Colorado's SB 205](/en/learn/colorado-ai-act-sb205/) before the deployment date, not after.

## Verdict

- **Pick Eightfold** when internal mobility, succession, or workforce planning is a board-level commitment and interviewing is one output of that investment rather than the reason for it; when you run Workday or SAP SuccessFactors and want one skills graph under sourcing, mobility, and hiring; when ISO 42001 or FedRAMP is a hard gate; or when your hires-to-headcount ratio is high enough that PEPM is not punitive.
- **Pick HireVue** when defensibility is the primary criterion and you need to hand an external auditor's published conclusion to legal; when you hire at volume against a modest employee base; when you have a system of record you are not replacing and want the screening layer to be a module, not a platform; or when the budget that exists is five figures rather than seven.
- **Pick neither** when the constraint is somewhere else. Frontline and hourly hiring is [Paradox](/en/tools/paradox/)'s category, not either of these. If you want a conversational interviewer without an enterprise platform underneath it, look at [Apriora](/en/vs/apriora-vs-hirevue/) or [Sapia.ai](/en/vs/sapia-ai-vs-hirevue/). If the real gap is sourcing rather than interviewing, [Findem](/en/vs/eightfold-vs-findem/), [Gem](/en/vs/gem-vs-eightfold/), and [hireEZ](/en/tools/hireez/) are the more direct answers, and the wider field is in [alternatives to Eightfold](/en/vs/alternatives-to-eightfold/).

**Default pick: HireVue.** A team that genuinely cannot choose between these two is buying an interviewer, and HireVue is the only one of the pair that sells you an interviewer rather than a workforce platform with an interviewer inside it — at roughly a fifth to a tenth of the cost at enterprise headcount, with external validation evidence you can put in front of counsel, and without a pending class action over the scoring mechanism. Switch to Eightfold when a specific condition forces it: internal mobility is funded and owned, your HCM gravity makes one skills graph the cheaper architecture, or a procurement gate names ISO 42001. What should not decide it is which demo felt more advanced — Eightfold's agent surface is visibly wider, and on a pure screening purchase you would be paying for most of it in perpetuity to use one part of it. If high inbound volume is the actual problem, the [high-volume recruiting stack](/en/stacks/high-volume-recruiting-stack/) and [candidate experience](/en/learn/candidate-experience/) matter more than which of these two you sign.
