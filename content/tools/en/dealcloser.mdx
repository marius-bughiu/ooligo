## What it is

DealCloser is a transaction management platform for deal lawyers: one workspace for the closing checklist, the signature packets and the closing book, so a financing or an acquisition stops running on an Excel tracker and a reply-all thread. It was founded in 2017 in Edmonton, Alberta, by corporate lawyer Amir Reshef and CTO Mike O'Connor; Jag Dhariwal is now CEO, and the company is majority-owned by Bryce Catalyst. Its practice-area pages cover M&A, banking and finance, real estate, venture capital, private equity and restructuring.

The product has three working parts. **Checklists** are customizable, importable from your existing Word or Excel list, clonable from a past deal, and shareable with clients and opposing counsel under document-level permissions. **Signatures** pulls signature pages out of the deal documents, builds a packet per signatory, places DocuSign tabs automatically (or uses DealCloser's own e-signature), tracks wet-ink pages, and sends one-click reminders. **Closing books** generate an indexed, hyperlinked, firm-branded set when the deal closes. Around that sit version control, in-app editing through Microsoft Online, redline comparison, and two-way sync with the firm's document management system.

The AI layer arrived in two steps. **Cloe**, launched 3 March 2026, is an "AI Deal Assistant" that coordinates several specialized agents to draft correspondence, create and assign tasks, chase signatures and summarize documents; it works human-in-the-loop, suggesting an action and waiting for approval before it executes. In May 2026 DealCloser embedded Thomson Reuters CoCounsel Legal document review inside the platform, building on a HighQ partnership from late 2023. On 5 November 2026 its connector went generally available in Harvey's Connector Library, built on MCP with read and write access, so a Harvey user can build or update a closing checklist, check signature status, and turn review findings into assigned tasks without leaving Harvey.

## Why legal ops teams shortlist it

- **Signature pages are the part it automates hardest.** Extracting pages, building per-signatory packets and placing tabs is the most clerical hour of any closing, and it is the step the CEO calls "nearly fully automated" since Cloe. For a firm whose paralegals spend the final week of a deal assembling packets, this is the line item that pays for the tool.
- **It sits next to the AI the firm already bought.** DealCloser is one of the few closing tools with both a CoCounsel embed and a Harvey connector. If the firm standardized on either, review findings flow into the checklist as tasks instead of being retyped.
- **Volume-tolerant mid-market economics.** The published case study is KMSC Law, which reports doubling deal capacity across 5,500+ deals and 125,000+ documents. That is a high-volume real-estate and lending profile, not a once-a-quarter mega-deal practice, and it is where the per-seat model makes sense.
- **Quick to stand up.** Importing an existing checklist and cloning past deals means the first live deal does not start from a blank template.

## Pricing

**Quote-only, checked 15 November 2026.** dealcloser.com has no pricing page (the /pricing URL returns a 404) and every call to action is "Book a Demo." Third-party directories disagree: Capterra lists $69 per user per month billed annually or $79 monthly, and ITQlick lists $49 per user per month. Neither figure is confirmed by the vendor, so treat roughly $49 to $79 per user per month as a planning band for a small team, not a quote.

The number that matters is the licensing unit. Per-user pricing is fine for a ten-person real-estate group; it gets expensive once partners, associates and paralegals across several practices all need seats. Ask whether client and opposing-counsel access is free (it should be), whether Cloe is included or an add-on, and whether the Harvey and CoCounsel connections carry any separate fee.

## Best for

**A legal ops lead or practice-group paralegal manager at a North American mid-market firm with steady deal volume (real-estate finance, lending, venture, mid-market M&A) who wants signature packets and closing books off the paralegal's desk, and whose lawyers already use Harvey or CoCounsel.**

**Skip it** if the firm closes a handful of deals a year. A shared checklist and DocuSign envelopes will do, and no closing platform earns its seat cost at that volume.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Legatics](/en/tools/legatics/)** — the UK-built platform with the larger Am Law and Magic Circle logo wall, ISO 27001 certification, a VDR-lite data room, and its own MCP server that any assistant can call. Pick Legatics for a large or international firm that wants a firm-wide checklist platform and plans to connect several AI assistants. Pick DealCloser when signature-packet automation and a built-in assistant matter more than the logo wall, and the firm is North American.
- **[Litera](/en/tools/litera/) Transact** — the incumbent module from the vendor whose drafting and comparison tools most large firms already license. Pick it when the firm wants one contract with Litera across the document lifecycle; it has no third-party MCP connector, so pick DealCloser if Harvey is the lawyers' daily surface.
- **iManage Closing Folders** — the other large incumbent. Pick it when [iManage](/en/tools/imanage/) Work is the DMS and the closing set should file itself there with no second vendor.
- **SimplyAgree** — the fast-growing US challenger built around automated signature packets and same-day binders for venture and mid-market deals. It is the closest like-for-like rival; run both on the same live deal and pick on packet-building accuracy.

## Watch-outs

- **No published security certification.** None of DealCloser's product or company pages mention SOC 2 or ISO 27001; they claim "enterprise-grade security" and audit trails only. Guard: request a SOC 2 Type II report or ISO certificate and the latest penetration-test summary before a bank or institutional client's vendor review, and treat a missing report as a blocker for those clients.
- **Write access from Harvey changes the live deal record.** An assistant that can update a checklist or assign tasks is editing the list opposing counsel and the client also read. Guard: for the first 60 days, let only a named supervising associate's Harvey account use the connector, and review DealCloser's activity report weekly before widening access.
- **Cloe's "industry first" claims are vendor press releases.** Every launch announcement for Cloe, CoCounsel and Harvey was written by DealCloser, and the 75-80% time-savings figure on its homepage has no published method. Guard: pilot on one live deal, time packet assembly and closing-book generation against your last comparable deal, and use that number in the business case instead.
- **Adoption depends on the other side.** A checklist only one firm updates is a prettier spreadsheet. Guard: name DealCloser as the system of record in the first all-parties email and give one associate ownership of list hygiene on the first two deals.

Related: if the deal needs a full sell-side data room with Q&A and buyer analytics, pair the closing tool with [Datasite](/en/tools/datasite/) or [Intralinks](/en/tools/intralinks/) rather than expecting a checklist platform to replace one.
