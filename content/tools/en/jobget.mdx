## What it is

JobGet is a Boston hiring marketplace for hourly and frontline workers that has grown mostly by acquisition. It started as a mobile job app, raised a $52M Series B in June 2022, and has since bought Heroes Jobs (May 2023), Wirkn (June 2024, retail and mall hiring), Snagajob (November 2024, the largest US hourly job marketplace), Seasoned (March 2025, restaurants) and RippleMatch (28 May 2026, early-career matching). JobGet calls RippleMatch its sixth acquisition. Its employer page now sells "Snagajob, Seasoned, and JobGet" as one network of "100M+" everyday workers. That figure is the vendor's own and almost certainly counts the same people across brands.

There are two products for two buyer sizes. **Small businesses** post for free or pay to promote a post. **Enterprise** employers "with 50+ open roles" buy two tools. **SpendSmart** reads ATS data (stage conversion, application velocity, no-show rate, interview availability) and moves the ad budget toward the roles that are short of candidates. **HireReady** pulls applicants from the ATS, careers page, paid channels and the JobGet network, screens them, lets them book their own interview, and sends SMS and email reminders. Compared with the category leaders, it sits between [Indeed](/en/tools/indeed/) as a source of applicants and [Appcast](/en/tools/appcast/) as a budget optimizer.

JobGet also bid for the CareerBuilder and Monster job boards in their 2025 bankruptcy. It raised its stalking-horse offer to $27M but lost the auction to Bold Holdings, so those brands are not part of JobGet.

## Why it shows up in Recruiting stacks

- **One contract buys several hourly networks.** Snagajob, Seasoned and the JobGet app now share one candidate pool and one employer sales team. For a quick-service or retail franchise group, that replaces three separate job-board line items.
- **SpendSmart budgets for hires, not clicks.** Most job advertising optimizes cost per click or per apply. SpendSmart uses ATS stage data, so a store with plenty of applicants but no interviews stops getting budget. That closed loop is the core argument against plain sponsored listings.
- **It writes back into the ATS hourly employers already run.** The employer page names ADP, Workday, UKG, Fountain, BambooHR, JazzHR, Greenhouse, SmartRecruiters, Oracle Recruiting, Bullhorn, TalentReef and others, and says JobGet handles the setup.
- **Reference customers are restaurant and retail franchises.** Case studies on the site cover an 85-plus-store Taco Bell franchisee, Applebee's franchisees and Potbelly. All are vendor-published.

## Pricing

- **Small business.** A post can stay free with regular visibility. Promotion is billed per applicant. The help center lists a Standard plan at $16 a day, a Pro plan at $23 a day that adds distribution to "100+" partner boards (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Appcast and Google Ads among them) and top placement, and a Flex plan with a custom budget. The first promotion gets a $50 credit. You are billed at $300 of spend, when all posts close, or monthly. These help pages carry a 2024 footer, so confirm the rates at sign-up.
- **Enterprise.** SpendSmart and HireReady are quote-only, sold through a demo, with no public price. The pitch is about moving your existing job-ad budget, so ask whether JobGet charges a markup on that media, a platform fee, or both.
- **Math check.** One promoted role at $16 a day for 30 days is $480. That is worth it if it produces at least one more hire than a free post at your [cost per hire](/en/learn/cost-per-hire/).

## Best for

Talent acquisition and operations leaders at multi-unit restaurant, retail and service franchises who hire hundreds of hourly workers a month, already advertise on Indeed, and want spend moved by store-level hiring data instead of clicks.

**Do not buy JobGet if** you hire salaried, technical or licensed roles, where its audience is thin; if you fill fewer than about ten hourly roles a month, where a free post plus Indeed covers it; or if campus recruiting is your main job, because RippleMatch is being folded into a company whose focus is hourly work.

## Versus the alternatives

- **[Indeed](/en/tools/indeed/)**: the largest audience and a self-serve sponsored-jobs budget. Pick it when you want reach without another vendor. JobGet wins when you want the budget moved by ATS hiring outcomes and an hourly-only audience.
- **[ZipRecruiter](/en/tools/ziprecruiter/)**: a subscription with AI matching and invites. Pick it for mixed hourly and salaried hiring at small businesses.
- **[Appcast](/en/tools/appcast/) or [Joveo](/en/tools/joveo/)**: programmatic platforms that spread spend across many publishers. Pick one when you need neutral distribution and do not want a network owner deciding where the budget goes.
- **[Fountain](/en/tools/fountain/) or [Workstream](/en/tools/workstream/)**: high-volume hiring systems that own the funnel after the apply. Pick one when the bottleneck is screening and onboarding, not applicant supply.
- **[Handshake](/en/tools/handshake/)**: the default for campus and early-career hiring. Pick it over RippleMatch when graduates are the program.

## Watch-outs

- **Roll-up integration risk.** Six brands bought in about three years run on systems JobGet says it will "migrate" onto shared infrastructure. *Guard:* ask which brands your posts and data will live on in 12 months, and get service levels for the ATS connector in writing.
- **RippleMatch contracts are changing.** JobGet made the RippleMatch university product free to schools on 17 September 2026. It has said nothing public about pricing or roadmap for the employer side. *Guard:* sign RippleMatch renewals for one year or less, with a right to leave if the product is merged or retired.
- **The network owner allocates your budget.** SpendSmart can move spend toward JobGet's own networks. *Guard:* require channel-level reporting of hires and cost per hire, not only applies, and compare it with your Indeed baseline for a quarter.
- **Results are vendor metrics.** Claims such as "2x hires per month" and a 40% shorter time to hire come from JobGet's case studies. *Guard:* record [time to fill and time to hire](/en/learn/time-to-fill-vs-time-to-hire/) for a test group of stores and a control group before the pilot.
